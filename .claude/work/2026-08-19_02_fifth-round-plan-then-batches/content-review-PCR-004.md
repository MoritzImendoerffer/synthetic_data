### Q1 — causal verbs that name no physical cause in their own clause

The judge grouped these in two families. First, **connectives whose "cause" is a documentary or
definitional fact**:

- "No parameter of this step reached the score at which RA-001 assigns a multivariate design, because none of them acts on a critical quality attribute." — the because-clause names a risk-tool category, no species and no direction.
- "For harvest and clarification the impact ranking was low for every parameter, because the step forms no attribute and no clearance is claimed for it." — "no clearance is claimed" is an authoring act, not a cause.
- "It is not a set parameter but a property of the clarified harvest that the other two produce, and it was characterized because it is the in-process measure of the particulate burden delivered to Protein A capture." — the because-clause gives a measurement role, not a mechanism.
- "The precision of the turbidity method matters to the conclusions of §5.4, because the response that bounds the depth filter load is turbidity itself." — circular; the clause names the response rather than what raises it.
- "No response surface model was fitted for this step and no design space is claimed for it, because the step governs no critical quality attribute and there is no attribute limit for a model to be judged against." — "governs" here is a regulatory relation with no physical content.
- "That limitation was accepted at the design stage in RA-001 on the grounds that the step governs no critical quality attribute, and it is restated in §11 as a bound on what this report claims." — same categorical "governs".
- "Those runs are also the verification of the step as it is proposed for Stage 2, since the set-point condition is what the commercial process will run." — the since-clause is a design convention, not a cause.
- "The two rows that carry the argument are the step yield and the post-clarification turbidity, because those are the responses the parameters act on." — "act on" with no named species, interaction or direction.
- "The parameter therefore trades clarity against lysis, and the field is set at 9,000 g because that is the region in which both are acceptable rather than because either is optimal." — the because-clause names an acceptability judgment.
- "It is defined for a step whose parameters act on a critical quality attribute, because it is the attribute limit that closes the region." — definitional; "act on" carries no mechanism.
- "Because there is no design space for this step, movement of a parameter outside its normal operating range is not covered by the regulatory position of ICH Q8 on movement within a design space." — purely regulatory causation.
- "Against step yield the proven acceptable range is 80 to 160 L/m2, since yield was unaffected across the characterization range." — the since-clause is an observation, not a cause with a direction.
- "They are given here because these three attributes are the ones the operation of Step 4 can affect, and not because Step 4 delivers them." — the because-clause explains the table's placement.
- "No parameter of this step meets that condition, because the step forms no attribute and no clearance is claimed for it, so the step has no critical process parameter and no well controlled critical process parameter." — same categorical because.
- "No design space is defined for this step, because it governs no critical quality attribute." — same.
- "No product quality attribute was affected, since the step alters none." — the since-clause restates the conclusion instead of giving a cause.

Second, **the mechanism verbs "governs", "sets" and "acts on" used for a register relation**
between a step and a CQA — physical vocabulary carrying documentary content:

- "Step 4 forms no quality attribute of A-Mab and sets 0 critical quality attributes of the drug substance." — "sets" names a register entry, not an action on a species.
- "Step 4 sets no critical quality attribute of A-Mab, so the register of attributes set by this step is empty." — same, twice in one sentence.
- "Step 4 governs no critical quality attribute, so no design space is defined for it and none is claimed." — categorical "governs".
- "For a step that governs a critical quality attribute, the proven acceptable range of a parameter is the range over which the attribute has been shown to stay inside its acceptance criterion." — categorical "governs".
- "Step 4 governs no such attribute, so the acceptance basis here is process performance and the quality of the feed delivered to the capture step." — same.
- "The narrower of the two is what governs, so the proven acceptable range proposed for the depth filter load is 100 to 140 L/m2 and it coincides with the normal operating range." — "governs" applied to a decision rule between two numbers.
- "The field acts on process performance through the completeness of solid removal and through shear driven lysis, and both directions were confirmed in §5.3." — names two mechanisms but defers the direction of each to another section.
- "Neither acts on a critical quality attribute." — bare category, no species or direction.
- "The response it acts on is feed quality to Protein A capture and not a critical quality attribute, so the parameter is key rather than critical." — "feed quality" is an abstraction.
- "It is a property of the clarified harvest produced by the other two parameters rather than a setting an operator selects, and it acts on no quality attribute of the antibody." — negative category claim, no physical content.

For calibration the judge listed these as passing cleanly: "because that material fouls the capture
column and shortens resin life"; "The centrifugal field sets the sedimentation velocity of cells and
debris and so decides how completely they are removed at a given feed rate, and it also sets the
shear the culture meets."; "since a higher field delivers a centrate with less solid material to the
filter train"; "since the particulate burden it measures is what fouls the capture column and
shortens resin life"; "because host cell protein released by lysis is added to the load the
purification train has to clear."

### Q2 — terms that are not terms of art

- "…were left at platform settings under SOP-2005 and SOP-2006 and were identity controlled rather than characterized." — "identity controlled" is not a term in the literature.
- "…and no setting changed the disposition of the step." — "disposition" is a QA term for a batch, lot or deviation, not for a unit operation.
- "Those are the two mechanisms the step turns on, which is why the model is scaled on them." — "the mechanisms the step turns on" is an idiom, not a scale-down term.
- "Load per unit area is what decides whether the filter is still doing its job at the end of the batch…" — "doing its job" replaces the available term.
- "Confidence in the scale-down model rests on the two mechanisms the step turns on." — same idiom repeated.

Passing: feed zone, disc stack, sedimentation velocity, relative centrifugal field, load per unit
area, flux, media grades, filter aid, nucleosomes, air liquid interface, sterilising grade filter,
virus retentive filtration, modular claim, knowledge space, proven acceptable range.

### Q3 — mechanism sentences that cannot be disagreed with on their own

- "The physical chemistry of the step explains what each parameter can do, and it is the basis on which the study was designed."
- "Aggregate is a different case." — a discourse marker with no content.
- "The direction of the two competing effects was as the physical chemistry of the separation predicts."
- "Those are the two mechanisms the step turns on, which is why the model is scaled on them."
- "Confidence in the scale-down model rests on the two mechanisms the step turns on."
- "The measurement supports that use."
- "The result confirms the platform expectation and the mechanism given in §2.1." — a verdict delivered by cross-reference.

Passing: "The mechanism is the finite capacity of the media." and "Loading beyond the point of
saturation therefore raises the turbidity of the filtrate directly, and at the extreme it forces
cells and debris through the pores."

## Disposition after run 1

Returned to the same authoring agent in one message: the four questions restated and the flagged
sentences with what each lacks, in the judge's words. Two of the judge's parentheticals were
removed before sending because they named the replacement term, and a return may never give the
author a phrase to insert. No count and no verdict was sent.

The author did not comply uniformly, which is the point of returning sentences rather than a rule.
It separated the two families itself: where the reason was documentary it dropped the causal
connective and stated the fact plainly ("A design space is closed by the acceptance limit of a
critical quality attribute, and no critical quality attribute is assigned to Step 4"); where a real
physical cause stood behind the categorical one it wrote the cause in (the depth filter load PAR
now says yield was unaffected "because the antibody passes the media unretained at every loading
studied and the only mass the filtration stage holds back is the hold up volume"). Every
"governs / sets / acts on" used for a step-to-attribute register relation is gone, verified at zero
by grep. It also corrected two of its own replacements before gating, one of which had claimed
larger debris "passes through the column", which a Protein A column does not do.

318 sentences / 7,140 words became 323 / 7,062: the sentence count rose while the word count fell,
which is the shape of splitting packed clauses and deleting filing clauses.

## What this says, against PCR-008 attempt 2

**The cycle converged here, and it did not converge there.** On `PCR-008` attempt 2 a second fresh
judge flagged more in every question than the first had (12/8/5/12 → 16/9/9/14). On `PCR-004` it
flagged fewer in every question, and two of the four now pass outright. The difference between the
two runs is the amended rule 4: this author had "the cause stands in the clause where the causal
verb stands, and where the cause is a convention or a procedure say what it holds constant"
*before* it wrote, and it used that distinction to answer the return instead of deleting sentences.

The residue is consistent and small. Every remaining Q1 flag is a sentence whose direction lives in
a later section, and every remaining Q4 flag is the "which is the X in §Y" cross-reference clause.
Both are candidates for the next rule, if the owner wants one; neither was acted on here.
