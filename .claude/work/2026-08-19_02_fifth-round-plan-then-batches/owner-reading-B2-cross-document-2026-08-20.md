## What the three documents actually say there

`PCR-008` §6, the sentence the owner quotes as correct:

> "The host cell protein criterion excludes 14.4% of the region and the two viral criteria exclude
> no part of it, so within the ranges studied the step delivers its required contribution to both
> viral claims everywhere, including at the corner least favourable to clearance."

A *criterion* excludes part of a region. That is what a criterion does.

`PCR-005` §6, the sentence the owner quotes against it:

> "Pool host cell protein rejects 26.6% of the grid on its own, and it is the only response that
> rejects any of it."

followed by "Leached Protein A rejects none" and "Only protein load and elution buffer pH change
how much of the region is rejected". An attribute does not reject a region; a criterion does. The
same paragraph also carries "The principal plane of the design space is protein load against
elution buffer pH".

`PCR-004` §6, the "weird sentences":

> "The region is closed by the acceptance limit of a critical quality attribute. A design space is
> therefore defined for a step that forms or clears one." … "The relationship between the ranges is
> the ordinary one."

## The finding: a one-cycle review can swap a flagged coinage for an unflagged one

This is the important part, and it is a property of the apparatus rather than of any one author.

**`PCR-005` run 1 flagged exactly this vocabulary.** Its judge wrote: *"Pool host cell protein is
the binding response, and it rejects 26.6% of the grid on its own." — "binding" is borrowed from
constrained optimization, not from the chromatography literature, and in a Protein A report the
word already means ligand binding.* The author's revision removed "binding response" and reported
doing so — and kept **"rejects"**, which was in the same flagged sentence. **Run 2, reading fresh,
did not flag "rejects" at all.** It flagged "the undivided ceiling", "assurance margin" and
"carboxylate contacts" instead. "The principal plane" survived both runs here, though `PCR-008`'s
run-1 judge had flagged that exact phrase in that document as belonging to optics.

**`PCR-008` attempt 3 fixed the same defect properly**, because its run-1 judge named the whole
construction — "binding attribute" *and* "an attribute that 'rejects' a region" — and the author
replaced the idea rather than the word: "the host cell protein criterion excludes 14.4 % of the
region".

So the one-cycle review is not self-correcting on vocabulary. A judge flags a coinage, the author
substitutes a neighbouring coinage from the same sentence, and the second judge — who has never
seen the first draft — has no reason to look there. The owner caught it by reading the same
paragraph across three documents, which is a comparison no single-document review performs.
