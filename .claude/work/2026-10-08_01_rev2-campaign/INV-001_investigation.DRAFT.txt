Summary

On 2026-08-03, Quality Assurance opened this investigation, INV-001, during the re-
view of the Process Characterization Master Report PCMR-001 Version 1.0 for BLA
readiness. The review found that the acceptance criteria against which each step was
judged in the proven acceptable range analysis were not in any protocol approved be-
fore the studies ran. They had been set at analysis time, after the data were known.
The investigation was classified as major.
The investigation makes six findings, numbered F1 to F6 in §4. Four concern the
criteria themselves. The criteria were defined after the data were seen (F1). For
impurities that downstream steps clear, each criterion was divided by a safety factor
that differed by step, and the factors were such that every step passed at its own set-
point (F2). The glycan and acidic variant limits at the bioreactor were computed from
the capability data they were then used to judge (F3). Acidic variants were assessed
against the upper end of their two-sided range only (F4). Finding F5 concerns the
records. Their title blocks misstated the dates on which the plans were approved, and
they gave no execution dates. Finding F6 concerns the method for fragments. SEC
read fragments high in the capture matrices, and the decision AMD-001 to measure
fragments by nrCE-SDS instead was not carried into the documents.
The root cause is that the Process Characterization Master Plan PCMP-001 Version
1.0 required a criterion for each step but gave no rule for deriving one. The criteria
were therefore derived during the analysis, by the people analysing the data (§5).
Under corrective action CA1, the investigation defined intermediate acceptance crite-
ria by a rule written before any report applied it. Each criterion is carried back from
the drug-substance criterion through the platform worst case of each downstream
step, with no margin. This campaign’s data can move a criterion only through the 95
% confidence bound of a downstream step’s worst case within its normal operating
range (NOR). The same rule set fixes how a scale-down model whose mean differs
from manufacturing scale is qualified, and that viral clearance is judged at the least
favourable combination of the other parameters. Corrective action CA1 issues all of
this in PCMP-001 Version 2.0. Corrective actions CA2 and CA3 reissue the step plans
and every other document of the package against it, with fragments measured by
nrCE-SDS. They are due on 2026-09-30 and were open at closure. The preventive ac-
tions revise SOP-1002 so that a characterization protocol cannot be approved without
its criteria (§7).
Of the 61 proven acceptable ranges that Version 1.0 reported with the other param-
eters varying within their NORs, 29 are wider in the re-analysis, 3 are narrower, 3
have moved, 3 no longer give a range and 23 are unchanged. In the re-analysis,
acidic variants at the production bioreactor step and XMuLV clearance at the low-pH
viral inactivation step do not meet their Version 2.0 criterion across the whole NOR,

bility index of the drug substance is now that of acidic variants, at 1.03, against its
lower limit. No GMP batch was dispositioned on these documents, and there is no
product impact (§6.6).

1 Event description

PCMR-001 Version 1.0, the Process Characterization Master Report, and the step
reports it consolidates were approved on 2026-07-24. On 2026-08-03, QA reviewed
PCMR-001 for use in the BLA. The review traced the in-process criteria in the control
strategy of PCMR-001 back to the step plans and step reports. It found that none
of the criteria could be traced to a plan as it was approved before execution. It also
found that the master plan, PCMP-001, gave no rule from which the criteria could
have been derived in advance. QA opened INV-001 the same day.
The 33 studies that Version 1.0 reported were executed between 2025-08-27 and
2026-05-15. Each of them closed before the Version 1.0 reports were approved.
Two immediate actions were taken on the day the investigation was opened.

  1. The Version 1.0 documents were placed on hold for BLA use. They were kept
     unchanged, as the record under investigation.
  2. No analysis that depended on the Version 1.0 step criteria was to be issued until
     the investigation had concluded.

The investigation was closed on 2026-09-18.

The Protein A plan, PCP-005, had planned 3 set-point runs for the qualification of
that model (study S-PA-SDM). The further runs were made to narrow the confidence
interval of the offset of the model for pool host cell protein. Of the Protein A pool
attributes, host cell protein comes nearest its criterion, at 82 % of it at the upper
95 % prediction limit over the NOR (Table 6.4), and it rises with lower elution buffer
pH and higher protein load. With the planned runs alone, the half-width of the 90 %
confidence interval of that offset was 1,249 ng/mg. With all runs it is 678 ng/mg. A
scale-down model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion
over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of that interval (§6.1). On the
planned runs alone and on all runs, the Protein A model is qualified for each output
with a criterion at the step, host cell protein and leached Protein A. The plan did not
foresee the further runs, so the Protein A report PCR-005 Version 2.0 records them
as PD-005-01, a discrepancy from the plan. They are not a deviation, because no
approved procedure or GMP requirement was departed from. They were part of the
re-analysis, not of the corrective actions in §7.

3 Background

3.1 The master plan

PCMP-001 Version 1.0, the master plan, required that each step be judged against a
criterion that applies to it. It did not say how that criterion was to be obtained. Its §7
left the threshold to the step plans.

     Where a threshold depends on the step, on the attribute or on the precision
     of the method, the criterion is defined in the plan of that step and the reason
     is given there.

The same section stated that the criterion for a response is the in-process limit of the
step where one has been established, and the drug-substance criterion where none
has. It gave neither a rule nor a value for an in-process limit.



3.2 The Protein A analysis

The need for step criteria became apparent during the analysis of the Protein A study.
The capture pool carries host cell protein at a level far above the drug-substance cri-
terion of 100 ng/mg. Protein A capture removes most of the host cell protein of the
harvest, and part of the remainder is carried into the eluate bound to the antibody.
That level is acceptable in an intermediate, because cation exchange and anion ex-
change remove most of it afterwards. Judged against the drug-substance criterion, no
part of the Protein A characterization range would have been acceptable. PCMR-001
Version 1.0 put the argument in one sentence.

     The drug substance limit is the wrong yardstick for an intermediate, and
     the campaign uses a criterion for each step that applies to that step.



3.3 The Version 1.0 criteria

Version 1.0 set 15 step criteria at 6 steps, by four rules. Table 3.1 gives each criterion
with its rule code, beside the Version 2.0 criterion that replaced it. The Version 2.0
columns are discussed in §6.

  1. For an impurity that downstream steps clear, the drug-substance criterion was
     carried back through the clearance the downstream steps delivered at their set-
     points in this campaign. The result was divided by a safety factor (R1, 6 criteria).



                                           10
  2. For an attribute that no downstream step changes, the criterion was an alert
     limit at the capability mean plus 2.5 SD (R2, 4 criteria, all at the bioreactor).
  3. For viral clearance at low-pH viral inactivation and anion exchange, the criterion
     was the cumulative requirement less the nominal clearance of the other credited
     steps (R3, 3 criteria).
  4. For viral clearance at virus filtration, the criterion was the nominal clearance
     claimed for the step less 0.35 log10 for assay variability (R4, 2 criteria).

The back-calculation through nominal downstream clearance, the viral criteria less
the other steps’ nominal clearance and the virus filtration criterion less the assay
allowance all rest on the nominal results of this campaign. The alert limits rest on its
capability data. Every Version 1.0 criterion was therefore set after the data were seen,
from this campaign’s nominal performance or capability, and where a safety factor
was used its size was chosen step by step. That, and not the use of this campaign’s
data as such, is what the findings in §4 concern.

4 Investigation

4.1 Approach

The sequence in which the criteria were set was reconstructed from the analysis
records, from the study chronology and from the authors of the Version 1.0 reports.
Each criterion in Table 3.1 was recomputed from its stated rule and compared with
the value printed in the documents. The capability assessment was repeated against
the drug-substance criteria as the register prints them. The execution windows of
the studies were compared with the calibration records of the equipment they used.
The record of the fragment method was reviewed against the decision AMD-001 to
measure fragments by nrCE-SDS and against the comparability study S-CEX-FRC.
Every recomputed criterion agreed with the printed value.



4.2 F1. Criteria defined after the data were seen

The step criteria were defined after the data were seen and outside the approved
protocols.
Each step plan was approved before its studies began, as Table 4.3 in §4.6 shows.
The Protein A plan PCP-005, for example, was approved on 2025-11-03, and its first
study started on 2025-11-05. None of the step criteria was in a plan as approved.
The criteria were introduced later, while the Protein A data were being analysed,
when it became clear that the capture pool could not be judged against the drug-
substance criterion (§3.2). They were then written into the text of every step plan
when the Version 1.0 package was compiled. The approved plans were not revised to
carry them. The Version 1.0 plans therefore read as if the criteria had been approved
before execution, which they had not.
The master plan PCMP-001, approved on 2025-08-04, did not prevent this. As §3.1
shows, it required a step criterion without fixing one. The record therefore holds no
protocol, approved before execution, that contains a step criterion. A criterion added
to a protocol after its data are known does not meet the purpose of a protocol (U.S.
Food and Drug Administration 2011).

4.3 F2. Safety factors chosen so that each step passed at its
    set-point

For an impurity that downstream steps clear, Version 1.0 carried each drug-substance
criterion back through the nominal downstream clearance of this campaign and then
divided it by a safety factor (R1 in Table 3.1). The factors ranged from 3.2 to 6.8, and
no document records why they differ.
Table 4.1 sets each factor against the value that the response surface model of the
step predicts at its set-point. The last column gives the largest factor at which that
set-point value would still have met the criterion. Every step meets its Version 1.0
criterion at its own set-point. Had the largest factor used, 6.8, been applied to every
step, the set-point value would have failed its criterion for 4 of the attributes in Ta-
ble 4.1. The analysis records show that a factor was reduced where the limit would
otherwise have fallen below the value the step produces at its set-point. The factors
were therefore chosen with the result in view.
The highest pool level in Table 4.1 assumes that every downstream step clears as
much as it does at its set-point. A downstream step that runs elsewhere in its NOR
may clear less, and the downstream steps can then bring a lower pool level within
the drug-substance criterion. Version 1.0 divided each criterion by a safety factor
to cover this. The factor was chosen. It was not calculated from how much less the
downstream steps clear within their NORs. The Version 1.0 viral clearance criteria
for low-pH viral inactivation and anion exchange rest on nominal clearance in the
same way. Each was the cumulative requirement less the clearance that the other
steps showed at their set-points in this campaign.

4.4 F3. Capability limits judged against the data they came
    from

For the glycans and acidic variants, Version 1.0 set 4 bioreactor criteria at the mean
plus 2.5 SD of the commercial-scale capability simulation (R2 in Table 3.1). Table 4.2
recomputes each one. The recomputed value and the printed criterion agree to the
second decimal place.
The capability simulation draws every parameter inside its NOR. The proven accept-
able range analysis then asked whether the upper 95 % prediction limit stayed within
the alert limit while the other parameters varied inside the same NOR. For a normal
distribution, about 0.62 % of the batches lie above a one-sided limit at the mean plus
2.5 SD, whatever the process. A limit of this kind is an alert limit for trending. It is
not an acceptance criterion, because it carries no information about what the product
needs.
An alert limit of this kind can be reached inside the NOR by a batch that is well within
the drug-substance criterion. The bioreactor report PCR-003 Version 1.0 reported
such a case, a corner of the NOR at which predicted afucosylation exceeded its alert
limit while meeting the drug-substance criterion by a wide margin. For the same
reason, several proven acceptable ranges at the bioreactor stopped short of the NOR
because they reached an alert limit, not the drug-substance criterion (§6.3).



4.5 F4. Acidic variants assessed against one end of a
    two-sided range

The register gives acidic variants a two-sided criterion of 18 % to 40 %, and the Ver-
sion 1.0 capability table printed that range. The capability index beside it was com-
puted against the upper end alone and printed as 4.3. The simulated drug substance
has a mean of 22.3 % and an SD of 1.39 %. Computed against both ends, the index
is 1.03, because the mean lies 3.1 SD above the lower limit and 12.8 SD below the
upper limit. The same one-sided treatment was used in the proven acceptable range

4.7 F6. Fragments by SEC in the capture matrices

In the comparability study S-CEX-FRC at cation exchange, executed from 2026-03-
02 to 2026-03-13, fragments were measured by SEC and by nrCE-SDS on the same
samples. Table 4.5 gives every replicate.
On average, SEC read fragments higher than nrCE-SDS by 0.43 to 0.53 percentage
points in the Protein A pool, the VI pool and the CEX load. From the CEX pool onward,
the mean difference between the two methods was no more than 0.06 percentage
points. The difference follows the host cell protein content of the sample. Part of
the host cell protein elutes in the low molecular weight window of SEC, where it is
counted with the clipped antibody species. The excess can therefore be no larger

about 17 % of the Protein A fragment criterion of 2.50 % (Table 6.1). SEC fragment
results from the capture matrices are therefore biased high by an amount that would
use a large part of a fragment criterion.

5 Root cause

The root cause is that PCMP-001 Version 1.0, the master plan, required a criterion for
each step but gave no rule for deriving one (finding F1). When the Protein A analysis
showed that an intermediate cannot be judged against a drug-substance criterion, the
criteria were derived during the analysis, by the people analysing the data, with the
results in view. Every rule they chose drew on the nominal results or the capability
data of the studies being judged (§3.3).
Four causes contributed.

  1. SOP-1002 did not require that the acceptance criteria and their basis be ap-
     proved in the protocol before execution. Criteria set after execution could there-
     fore be added to the plan text when the package was compiled, without a revision
     of the approved plan (finding F1, criteria defined after the data were seen).
  2. Neither the plans nor the master plan defined how much less a downstream
     step may clear within its NOR, which is what a step criterion has to allow for. A
     chosen safety factor was used instead, and its size was set step by step (finding
     F2). Capability data were used where no downstream step changes the attribute
     (finding F3).
  3. No second review checked whether a capability calculation used a one-sided or
     a two-sided criterion as the register gives it (finding F4, acidic variants). No
     second review checked the dates in the title blocks and study records against
     the approval and equipment records (finding F5).
  4. No procedure required that a method decision be assessed against the approved
     plans and put into effect by plan amendment (finding F6, fragments by SEC).

6 Impact assessment

6.1 The Version 2.0 criteria

Under corrective action CA1, the investigation defined the intermediate acceptance
criteria and the rules for judging against them. Corrective action CA1 then issues
them in PCMP-001 Version 2.0, the master plan, effective 2026-09-30. The investiga-
tion closed on 2026-09-18, before that date, and the re-analysis in this section applied
the rules as CA1 defined them.
Each drug-substance criterion is carried backward through the train, using the worst
case each downstream step showed on the platform (X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab). The
platform worst case is the lowest fold clearance or the largest increase seen on those
products. No margin is applied. The glycans keep their drug-substance criterion,
because no downstream step changes them. Each viral clearance step is held to its
platform claim. The claims sum to the cumulative clearance that the CQA register
requires of the process, taken from the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). The sums are 16.72 against a requirement of 16.7 log10 for XMuLV
and 8.62 against 8.6 log10 for MVM.
Corrective action CA1 also defined three rules that the re-analysis applies.

  1. Verification against the A-Mab data replaces a platform worst case with the 95
     % confidence bound of the A-Mab worst case within the NOR of the downstream
     step, in either direction. Where the bound is worse than the platform value, the
     criteria upstream tighten. Where it is better, they widen. The point prediction
     is never used to move a criterion.
  2. A scale-down model is qualified for an output when its offset (the mean of the
     scale-down set-point runs less the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches) is
     known precisely enough that the conclusion over the NOR is the same at either
     end of the 90 % confidence interval of the offset. A qualified model may differ
     in mean from manufacturing scale. Every Version 2.0 judgement subtracts the
     offset, so results are stated at manufacturing scale.
  3. A quality attribute is judged per batch, on the 95 % prediction interval of a single
     batch. Viral clearance is judged on the mean predicted log reduction factor (LRF)
     at the least favourable combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the
     other parameters.

Table 6.1 gives the 21 criteria with their derivation. Of these, 8 are the drug-
substance criterion applied directly, 8 are carried back through the downstream
steps, and 5 are platform viral clearance claims. When the platform worst cases
were verified (§6.2), 2 of the back-calculated criteria were tightened and 6 were

widened. The criterion for aggregates at the production bioreactor step is counted
in both, because one downstream step tightened it and another widened it.
This campaign’s data enter the Version 2.0 criteria, as they entered the Version 1.0
criteria. The difference lies in how they enter. Version 1.0 set its criteria after the
data were seen, from the nominal performance of the downstream steps, with a safety
factor chosen step by step. Version 2.0 starts from the platform worst case, by a rule
written before any report applied it. This campaign’s data can then move a criterion
only through the 95 % confidence bound of a downstream step’s worst case within its
NOR, in either direction. The data of a step never set its own criterion.

from 1.51 for MVM clearance, as PCMR-001 Version 1.0 printed it, to 1.03 for acidic

target of 1.33, which is common industry practice for a capable process. It sets a
minimum of 1.00, the point at which a spread of three standard deviations reaches the
criterion. Only the capability index of acidic variants falls below the target, and every
attribute meets the minimum. The re-analysis also gives the probability of a drug
substance batch outside specification for each attribute, from the same simulation.
For acidic variants the simulation puts one batch in 2,000 outside the range, and a
normal fit gives 971 per million. No simulated batch falls outside the criterion of any
other attribute.

6.6 Product impact

No GMP batch of A-Mab was manufactured or dispositioned on the basis of these
documents, and Version 1.0 was not used in a regulatory submission. The findings
concern how the characterization data were judged. The experiments, the process
and the drug-substance criteria that existed in Version 1.0 are unchanged. Version 2.0
adds one drug-substance criterion, for fragments, at ≤ 2.00 %. There is no product
impact.

7 Corrective and preventive actions

The actions in Table 7.1 follow from the root cause and the contributing causes in §5.
All 6 actions were open at closure and are tracked to their due dates in the CAPA sys-
tem (International Council for Harmonisation 2008). The retest of retained samples
by nrCE-SDS finished on 2026-04-17, under the decision AMD-001 and before this
investigation was opened.
Corrective action CA1, the reissue of the master plan PCMP-001 with a priori criteria,
removes the root cause for this campaign. It also answers the contributing cause that
no document defined how much less a downstream step may clear within its NOR.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 fixes every step criterion before any study is judged against it,
defines the downstream worst case each criterion protects against, and lets no step’s
data set its own criterion. Corrective action CA2, the amendment of the step plans
with the criteria and the fragment method, brings the decision AMD-001 to measure
fragments by nrCE-SDS into the plans (finding F6). Corrective action CA3 reissues
the reports. They judge the same models against the new criteria and assess the
capability of acidic variants against both ends of their range (finding F4).
Preventive action PA1, the revision of SOP-1002 so that acceptance criteria are ap-
proved in the protocol before execution, removes the root cause for future campaigns.
It also removes the contributing cause that SOP-1002 did not require this. Preventive
action PA2 adds to the report review checklist a check that every acceptance crite-
rion can be traced to the approved protocol. That check would have detected the
criteria added after approval (finding F1). Preventive action PA3 records execution
dates and calibration status in every study record. It answers the contributing cause
that no second review checked the dates in the title blocks and study records (finding
F5).
Two causes have no preventive action. No action adds a second review of whether
a capability calculation uses the one-sided or two-sided criterion that the register
gives. Corrective action CA3, which reissues the reports with a two-sided assess-
ment of acidic variants, corrects finding F4 for this campaign only. For this product,
the two-sided assessment is fixed in PCMP-001 Version 2.0, so the error cannot re-
cur in this campaign. No action creates a procedure that would require a method
decision to be assessed against the approved plans and put into effect by plan amend-
ment either. Corrective action CA2 brings the decision AMD-001 into the plans, but
a future method decision would again depend on the people involved to reach the
documents.

8 Effectiveness check

The corrective actions are verified effective when the Version 2.0 package is effec-
tive and QA has repeated the review that triggered this investigation, on PCMR-001
Version 2.0, the master report. That review checks three things. Every step crite-
rion used in a proven acceptable range analysis must appear in PCMP-001 Version
2.0 with its basis, and no criterion may be set by data from the step it judges. Every
fragment result used to judge a step must be by nrCE-SDS. Every study must carry
its execution window.
QA verifies the preventive actions by 2027-03-31. The check requires the next two
characterization protocols approved with acceptance criteria and provenance, before
execution. QA owns both checks.

9 Conclusion and closure

The investigation confirmed the classification as major. The criteria used to support
the operating ranges in a BLA were not defined before the data were seen. The
analyses that rested on them could therefore not be relied on as approved. The inves-
tigation found no product impact (§6.6).
The corrective actions are due with Version 2.0 on 2026-09-30, and the preventive
actions by 2026-11-30. INV-001 was closed on 2026-09-18, with its remaining actions
open and tracked to completion under the effectiveness check in §8.
