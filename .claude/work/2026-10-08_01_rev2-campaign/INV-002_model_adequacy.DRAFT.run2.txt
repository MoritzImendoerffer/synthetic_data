Summary

The Version 2.0 re-analysis of the A-Mab characterization campaign applied the model-
adequacy criteria of the process characterization master plan (PCMP-001 §7.2) to
every response-surface model that carries an acceptance criterion at its step. Of the
22 full quadratic models in scope, 14 did not meet the limit on overfitting, which
allows predicted R² to fall short of R² by at most 0.20. No model showed significant
lack of fit. QA opened INV-002 on 2026-08-31 and classified it as minor on these
grounds: no GMP batch was made or dispositioned on the analysis, no product quality
impact, and the finding concerns the statistical analysis of development data, not their
integrity.
The root cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the predictive
model of each design, with no model selection. The full quadratic model keeps every
term whatever the data show, and the planned designs are small for a model with that
many terms. The Version 1.0 plans also set no adequacy criterion against which the
size of a design could have been checked.
A rule for the remedy of each failing model was approved on 2026-09-02, before any
new run was made (Section 5). A failing model of an attribute of the drug substance
specification, on which the refit of the planned runs keeps an effect, is resolved by
further runs (corrective action CA1), unless the target cannot be reached within the
size of the planned design. Every other failing model is refitted by hierarchical back-
ward elimination (corrective action CA2). Viral clearance is demonstrated by spiking
studies against a cumulative claim, not by testing the drug substance, so its models
are refitted and no virus is spiked.
The rule gives 3 models an augmentation, in 2 steps. The production bioreactor de-
sign was augmented by 16 runs for high mannose and acidic variants, in study S-BRX-
AUG. The anion exchange design was augmented by 12 runs for host cell protein, in
study S-AEX-AUG. Each augmentation is the smallest D-optimal set of runs that gives
an assurance of at least 0.90. The assurance is the probability, at the effects esti-
mated from the planned runs, that the planned model, the full quadratic with a block
term, meets §7.2 on all runs (Section 6). The other 11 failing models were refitted.
Model selection was adopted on 2026-09-15 as corrective action CA3, for every
response-surface output (Section 8). Each output is modelled on the log or the raw
scale, as its Box-Cox profile decides, by the submodel of the full quadratic with the
lowest AICc, and that model is judged against §7.2. Model selection replaced the
refits. Once selected, the three models that the augmentations were made for meet
§7.2 on the planned runs alone, so the augmentations were not needed to pass that
test. They changed what two models of the production bioreactor say, those of high
mannose and aggregates, whose models selected on the planned runs predict the
new runs poorly. The reports use the models of all runs. A §7.2 check of a selected



                                           6
model is optimistic, because the selection used the same runs. After selection, 3
outputs have no adequate model (fragments at low-pH viral inactivation, fragments
at cation exchange and XMuLV clearance at virus filtration), and they are judged on
their measured results.
Model selection was adopted before INV-001 closed on 2026-09-18, so the interme-
diate acceptance criteria fixed at that closure rest on the selected models. Against
the criteria that the verification of the platform worst cases would have given on the
full quadratic models, model selection widened 4 criteria, by 4 %, 4 %, 7 % and 7 %
(Section 9.1). The verification replaces a platform worst case by the one-sided 95 %
prediction bound of a single batch, never by a confidence bound of the mean. Against
the narrower criteria every conclusion over the NOR stays the same, and one proven
acceptable range moves. The scale-down model, however, would not be qualified for
host cell protein in the Protein A pool and at cation exchange. These two qualifica-
tions rest on the criteria that model selection widened. The augmentation runs moved
no criterion.
This report gives the models of the augmented steps on all runs, how well the models
selected on the planned runs predict the new runs, and one prediction limit that
follows from them. The reports of the two augmented steps give the measured results
of the new runs, the re-evaluation of every model and the ranges.

1 Event description

INV-001, the investigation into acceptance criteria set after the data were seen, was
opened on 2026-08-03. It followed the QA review of the process characterization
master report (PCMR-001) Version 1.0 for BLA readiness, which found that the ac-
ceptance criteria of the proven acceptable range analysis had been set after the data
were seen. Its corrective actions included the reissue of every characterization report
at Version 2.0, re-analysed against criteria set a priori. Version 2.0 of the process char-
acterization master plan (PCMP-001) also states campaign thresholds for model ade-
quacy in §7.2. PCMP-001 Version 1.0 had left these thresholds to the individual study.
The Version 1.0 reports fitted the full quadratic model to every response-surface de-
sign and applied no adequacy test.
The Version 2.0 re-analysis applied the criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 to every response-
surface model with an acceptance criterion at its step. It found 14 of the 22 full
quadratic models below the limit on overfitting. QA opened INV-002 on 2026-08-31,
while INV-001 was still open. The recorded trigger is the re-analysis itself.
As an immediate action, no proven acceptable range, design space or judgement
across the normal operating range (NOR) was set from a model that did not meet
§7.2 while the investigation was open. The Version 1.0 documents, whose ranges
came from the full quadratic models, were already on hold for BLA use under INV-
001.
The rule that assigns a remedy to each failing model was approved within this investi-
gation on 2026-09-02, together with the sample sizes and the augmentation designs.
The first augmentation run started on 2026-09-03. The root cause was recorded on
2026-09-15, and model selection was adopted on the same date as corrective action
CA3. Table 1.1 gives the dates of the investigation.

3 Finding

PCMP-001 §7.2 judges a response-surface model by three tests. The model F-test
must be significant at α = 0.05. The lack-of-fit test splits the residual variation into
pure error, from the replicated centre points, and lack of fit, which is the rest. Lack
of fit must not be significant against pure error at α = 0.05. The third test limits
overfitting. The prediction error sum of squares (PRESS) adds up the squared error
of each run when it is predicted from a fit to all the other runs, and predicted R² is one
minus PRESS divided by the total sum of squares (Montgomery 2017). PCMP-001 §7.2
limits R² minus predicted R² to at most 0.20. A model that fails the lack-of-fit test or
the limit on overfitting is refitted with a justified change of terms or transformation,
or it is not used to set a range. The re-analysis applied the three tests to the full
quadratic model of every output in scope, on the planned runs.
Table 3.1 lists the 14 full quadratic models that do not meet PCMP-001 §7.2, out of
the 22 models in scope. Each step with a response-surface design has at least one.
Every model in the table fails the limit on overfitting, with R² minus predicted R² from
0.26 to 2.14 against a limit of 0.20. No model fails the lack-of-fit test, and the lowest
lack-of-fit p-value among the failing models is 0.096. The lack-of-fit test therefore
gives no sign of systematic error in the full quadratic model. Of the failing models, 5
also fail the model F-test, with p-values from 0.280 to 0.936. They are the 4 models of
attributes with no detected effect and fragments in the Protein A pool. All of them fail
the limit on overfitting as well, and that failure decides the remedy. Section 4 gives
the cause.

4 Root cause

The primary cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the pre-
dictive model of every response-surface design, with no model selection. The full
quadratic model keeps every main effect, two-factor interaction and squared term,
whatever the data show. The planned designs are small for a model with that many
terms. With four factors, the full quadratic model has 15 terms, including the inter-
cept, and the planned face-centred design has 28 runs. Low-pH viral inactivation has
three factors, 10 terms and 18 runs. Virus filtration has two factors, 6 terms and 12
runs.
Predicted R² comes from predicting each run from a fit that leaves that run out. The
weight a run has in its own fitted value is its leverage, and the average leverage equals
the number of terms divided by the number of runs. In these designs it lies between
0.50 and 0.56. PRESS divides the residual of each run by one minus its leverage, and
at the average leverage of these designs this enlarges a squared residual by a factor
of 4.0 to 5.1. R² minus predicted R² therefore grows with the share of the variation
that the model leaves unexplained, one minus R². Every full quadratic model that met
the limit has an R² of at least 0.96, and every model that failed it has an R² of at most
0.95. Predicted R² falls well below R² even where the lack-of-fit test gives no sign of
systematic error.
The sample-size simulation of Section 6 quantifies this gap for the models that the rule
augments. With the planned runs alone, the probability that the full quadratic model
meets §7.2 is 0.25 for high mannose, 0.44 for acidic variants and 0.49 for host cell
protein at anion exchange. These probabilities are computed at the effects that the
same planned runs estimated. They describe the planned design under those effects,
not what could have been known before any run. Under those effects a failure was at
least as likely as success for each of the three models.
Attributes with no detected effect within the range studied fail for a second reason.
For such an attribute the full quadratic model fits only random variation, and each
of its terms takes up part of it. R² stays between 0.27 and 0.57 while predicted R² is
below zero. With no effect, R² minus predicted R² depends on the number of terms
against the number of runs, not on the size of the noise, so it shrinks only as the runs
outgrow the terms. The fragment models of Protein A and cation exchange behave
in the same way with a small effect. Their assurance is at most 0.01 at every size
computed, up to the size of the planned design (Table 6.1).
The contributing cause is that no plan checked the size of its design against the model
it was to fit. PCMP-001 Version 1.0 left the model-adequacy threshold to the individual
study, and the Version 1.0 plans set none. The threshold of PCMP-001 Version 2.0
came later. The last planned response-surface study ended on 2026-05-15, before
INV-001 was opened.

5 Remedy and decision rule

PCMP-001 §7.2 allows two remedies for a model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the
limit on overfitting. The model may be refitted with a justified change of terms or
transformation. Where the design is too small for the model a decision needs, the
design may instead be augmented with further runs as a second block. A model that
is neither refitted nor augmented is not used to set a range. §7.2 leaves it to the
investigation of a failing model to state when a design is augmented and how the
number of runs is calculated. The A-Mab case study names the same remedy for a
design that cannot resolve the terms a decision needs, which is to augment it (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009).
The decision was to calculate the further runs needed to resolve the effects where a
model bears on the drug substance specification, and to reduce the model in every
other case. The rule was approved on 2026-09-02, before any new run was made, and
it was applied to every failing model in the same way:

  1. A failing model of an attribute of the drug substance specification, on which the
     refit of the planned runs keeps an effect, is resolved by further runs. The number
     of runs is the smallest that gives an assurance of at least 0.90 that the planned
     model, the full quadratic with a block term, meets §7.2 on all runs (Section 6).
  2. Viral clearance is not tested on the drug substance. It is a process requirement,
     demonstrated by spiking studies at worst-case conditions against a cumulative
     claim (International Council for Harmonisation 2023). Its models are refitted,
     and no augmentation spikes virus.
  3. No augmentation is larger than the planned design. Where the assurance target
     is not reached within that size, the model is refitted.
  4. Every other failing model is refitted.
  5. A response with no adequate model, full or refitted, is judged on its measured
     results. No model sets a range for it.

For an attribute of the specification with an effect, a refit on a design that is too small
can drop a real interaction or curvature, and the ranges of the step would then rest on
a model that misses it. Further runs make the estimate of every term of the full model
more precise. For an attribute with no detected effect, the refit to the mean describes
the results, and further runs would only raise the power to detect a small effect. The
target of 0.90 is the probability of success customary for a confirmatory study, 0.80 to
0.90, taken at its upper end because a further failure would leave a criterion attribute
without an adequate model. The cap on the size of an augmentation, the size of the
planned design, keeps a remedy from becoming a new study.
The rule did not foresee one case. The XMuLV model at low-pH viral inactivation
bears on a decision. On it the step claim is not met over the NOR as characterized,




                                            16
and the low-pH viral inactivation report (PCR-006) moves the NOR (Section 9.2). The
rule refitted it as a model of viral clearance.
The refit removes terms by hierarchical backward elimination at α = 0.05, and it is
corrective action CA2. At each step, the least significant term that no remaining term
contains is removed. A main effect therefore stays while an interaction or a square of
it remains, even where the main effect itself is not significant. Elimination stops when
every removable term is significant. In an augmented design the block term belongs
to the design and is never removed. The refitted model is then judged against §7.2 in
the same way as the full model. Model selection, corrective action CA3, later replaced
the refit by backward elimination for every output, the outputs that CA2 had refitted
included (Section 8). The refits remain the record of how the rule was applied, and
the sample-size calculation rests on them.
Where neither the full nor the refitted model meets §7.2, the response is judged on
its measured results, as PCMP-001 §7.2 asks. Every measured result of the designs
is compared with the criterion at manufacturing scale. The designs span the charac-
terized range, so a response whose measured results all meet the criterion is taken
to meet it over the NOR. This is a weaker standard than the one a modelled output
meets, which is the 95 % prediction interval of a single future batch at every combi-
nation of NOR low, set-point and NOR high. Section 8.5.2 gives the margin of each
such response.

the anion exchange models of XMuLV clearance and MVM clearance, and the virus

substance.

6 Sample-size calculation

6.1 Method

The number of further runs is calculated by simulation, separately for each failing
model with an effect. The simulation estimates the assurance, the probability, at the
effects estimated from the planned runs, that the planned model meets §7.2 once
the design has been augmented by a given number of runs. The assurance does not
include the uncertainty of those estimates. The planned model is the full quadratic
with a block term, on the raw scale, because that was the model the plans prescribed
when the runs were sized. Model selection, and with it the log scale for some outputs,
came later. The calculation has six steps:

  1. The refit of the planned runs by backward elimination, with all its terms (Ta-
     ble 5.1), is taken as the true model, with its fitted coefficients. Its residual stan-
     dard deviation is taken as the variation between runs. The refit is used because
     it keeps only the terms that the planned runs support. The full model would
     carry its fitted noise into every simulated data set.
  2. For a candidate size, the D-optimal augmentation of that many runs is added to
     the planned runs as a second block (Section 6.2).
  3. Responses are simulated at the planned and the augmentation runs, as the true
     model plus an error drawn from a normal distribution with the residual standard
     deviation, the same at every level. For each size, 4,000 data sets are simulated.
  4. The planned model, the full quadratic with a block term, is fitted to each simu-
     lated data set. It is judged against the lack-of-fit test at α = 0.05 and the limit of
     0.20 on R² minus predicted R², the two tests of §7.2 that decide whether a model
     may set a range. The model F-test is not simulated. Pure error comes from the
     replicated centre points of the planned design, because no augmentation repeats
     a point within its own block.
  5. The assurance is estimated as the share of simulated data sets in which the
     planned model meets both tests.
  6. Sizes are evaluated in steps of 2 runs, up to the size of the planned design. The
     number of runs needed is the smallest size with an assurance of at least 0.90.

The simulation also gives the power of each significant effect of the refit. The power
is the share of simulated data sets in which the term is significant at α = 0.05 in
the fitted planned model. The assurance does not always rise with every further run,
because the D-optimal augmentation of each size is a different set of points. In 9 of
the 10 curves it falls at least once from one size to the next (Figure 6.1).




                                           21
The assurance cannot rise much above 0.95. The lack-of-fit test at α = 0.05 rejects a
correct model in 5 % of data sets, whatever the size of the design. A target of 0.90
therefore asks that the limit on overfitting be met in about 95 % of data sets.
The calculation assumes that the refit of the planned runs is the true model, that the
error is normal with the same variance at every level, and that the two blocks differ
by a constant at most. A constant difference between the blocks is taken up by the
block term of the fitted model. A change in the effects between the blocks is not, and
no augmentation repeats a point that could show it. Model selection later put 3 of
the outputs simulated here on the log scale (high mannose and acidic variants at the
production bioreactor, and host cell protein at anion exchange). For them, the error
of constant variance on the raw scale is an assumption that the log-scale analysis
does not support (Section 8.2). An effect that the planned runs did not detect is not
in the true model, so the calculation does not size a design to find it. Each curve
uses its own stream of a pseudo-random number generator with a fixed seed, so the
calculation gives the same result each time it is run.



6.2 D-optimal augmentation

Each augmentation is drawn from the levels of the face-centred design, so every factor
takes its low, centre or high level. For four factors this gives 81 candidate points. The
runs are chosen to maximise the determinant of the information matrix of the planned
model, the full quadratic with a block term, with the planned runs held fixed in the
first block. The selection adds, one run at a time, the candidate that increases the
determinant most. It then exchanges chosen runs with candidates until no exchange
increases the determinant further, which is a Fedorov exchange (Fedorov 1972). Ties
go to the first candidate in a fixed order, so the design needs no random number.
The face-centred designs of three and four factors hold corner, axial and centre points
only, and none of their points has exactly one factor at its centre level. Their squared
terms are therefore strongly correlated with each other, because every corner run
sets all of them to one. Both D-optimal augmentations consist mostly of points at
the middle of an edge of the design cube, with one factor at its centre level and
the others at a limit (Section 7). These points reduce the correlation between the
squared terms. In the production bioreactor design the largest correlation between
two squared terms falls from 0.69 on the planned runs to 0.54 on all runs.

8 Model selection (corrective action CA3)

8.1 Procedure

Model selection was adopted as corrective action CA3 on 2026-09-15, before INV-001
closed on 2026-09-18. It replaced the analysis of every output, the refits of corrective
action CA2 included. Each report with a response-surface design applies it to every
output of the design, on all runs in which the output was measured. Where a design
was augmented, the planned runs form the first block and the augmentation runs the
second. The verification of the platform worst cases behind the intermediate accep-
tance criteria applies the same procedure (Section 8.4). PCMP-001 is not changed.
Each report records the model selection as a discrepancy from its plan (Section 9).
The procedure has four steps:

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile likelihood of the power λ is calculated for the
     full quadratic model of the output, with the block term where the runs form two
     blocks (Box and Cox 1964). The output is modelled on the log scale (λ = 0) where
     the 95 % profile-likelihood interval of λ contains 0 and excludes 1. Otherwise
     it is modelled on the raw scale (λ = 1). The rule chooses between these two
     scales only. Where the interval excludes both 0 and 1, as for afucosylation at
     the production bioreactor and acidic variants at low-pH viral inactivation, the
     raw scale is kept. Only an output whose results are all positive can take the log
     scale.
  2. The search. Every submodel of the full quadratic model that respects strong
     hierarchy is fitted on that scale. Strong hierarchy allows an interaction only
     together with both of its main effects, and a squared term only together with its
     main effect. The block term is kept in every candidate. The candidate with the
     lowest AICc is selected (Section 8.3). An output that the data give without error,
     with replicates that agree exactly at the precision the method reports, is fitted
     exactly by more than one candidate. AICc is not defined for an exact fit, so the
     smallest candidate that fits exactly is selected instead. In this campaign the rule
     applies to acidic variants at low-pH viral inactivation.
  3. The check. The selected model is judged against PCMP-001 §7.2 on its own scale,
     by the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting. A selected model with terms
     must also have a significant model F-test at α = 0.05. Only the selected model is
     judged. If it fails, no other candidate takes its place. The selection used the same
     runs that §7.2 then judges. A model chosen as the best of many candidates fits
     those runs better than a model fixed in advance, so §7.2 is an optimistic check
     of a selected model. Section 8.5.1 gives the one independent check that exists.
  4. The offset. The scale-down offset of an output is taken on the scale of its model,
     with its 90 % confidence interval. For a raw-scale output it is the mean of the




                                          29
     scale-down set-point runs minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches,
     and it is subtracted from a scale-down prediction. For a log-scale output it is the
     same difference of the mean logarithms, reported as a ratio of geometric means,
     and a scale-down prediction is divided by it.

The check has three outcomes. A selected model with terms that passes is the pre-
dictive model of the output, and it sets the ranges of its step. A selected model with
no term passes when it meets the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting. It says
that no effect of the parameters was detected within the range studied, at the power
the design gives (Section 6.4). It does not show that there is none. For a model of
the mean alone, R² is zero and R² minus predicted R² depends on the number of runs
only, 0.19 for 12 runs and 0.08 for 28, so for such a model the check reduces to the
lack-of-fit test. An output whose selected model fails has no adequate model. It is
judged on its measured results, as Section 5 describes, and it sets no range.



8.2 Why a log scale, and what it changes

Host cell protein in the Protein A pool shows what the log scale does. On the log scale
the selected model has the form


                           ln 𝑦 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + 𝑏2 𝑥2 + ⋯ + 𝜀

and, transformed back,


                             𝑦 = 𝑒𝑏0 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏1 𝑥1 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏2 𝑥2 ⋯ 𝑒𝜀

Here y is pool host cell protein in ng/mg, x₁, x₂ and so on are the parameters in coded
units, from −1 at the low end of the range studied to +1 at the high end, and ε is the
error of a single run. Each parameter multiplies the output by a fixed factor, 𝑒𝑏 per
coded unit and 𝑒2𝑏 from the low to the high end of its range. The error multiplies
it too, so the scatter in ng/mg grows with the level. Across the range studied, the
selected model multiplies pool host cell protein by a factor of 2.43 for protein load,
0.30 for elution buffer pH, 0.95 for load flow rate and 0.85 for end of pool collect,
from the low to the high end of each parameter. A factor below one is a decrease.
Load flow rate also has a squared term, so its effect is not the same along its range.
Two properties of the process and the method make host cell protein behave this way.
A purification step removes a share of what is present, so a parameter changes the
share that is removed, not a fixed amount. The precision of the host cell protein ELISA
(AMV-3012) is a percentage of the level, 9.5 %, so the scatter of the results grows with
the level. Effects that multiply look like an interaction when they are added on the
raw scale. On the raw scale the full quadratic model needs the interaction protein
load × elution buffer pH (p < 0.001). On the log scale the same interaction is not
significant (p = 0.156), and the selected model has no interaction at all.




                                          30
Least squares, and the t-tests, F-tests and prediction intervals built on it, assume
residuals that are independent and normally distributed, with mean zero and the
same variance over the whole range studied. On the raw scale the full quadratic
model of pool host cell protein has a residual SD of 638 ng/mg in the lower half of its
predictions and 947 ng/mg in the upper half. On the log scale the same model has
0.067 and 0.074, in natural-log units. The upper half is 1.48 times the lower on the
raw scale and 1.11 times on the log scale. The Box-Cox profile puts λ at 0.06, with a
95 % interval from −0.32 to 0.42, which contains 0 and excludes 1. The assumption
of equal variance holds on the log scale and does not hold on the raw scale.
The log scale changes the following, and nothing else:

  1. The criterion stays in its own unit, ng/mg, and the prediction limits are trans-
     formed back into it.
  2. Effects are fold changes, as above.
  3. The scale-down offset is a ratio of geometric means, 0.97 for pool host cell protein
     with a 90 % confidence interval from 0.89 to 1.06, and a scale-down prediction
     is divided by it.
  4. A prediction interval is calculated on the log scale and transformed back. It
     keeps its 95 % coverage, because the probability that ln y lies between two values
     is the probability that y lies between their antilogs. It is therefore suitable, and
     the least-squares assumption behind it holds on the log scale, which on the raw
     scale it does not.
  5. The interval is asymmetric, further above the predicted value than below it. At
     the least favourable combination of the NOR (protein load 39 g/L resin, elution
     buffer pH 3.4, load flow rate 150 cm/hr and end of pool collect 2.3 CV), the
     predicted pool host cell protein is 12,201 ng/mg, and the 95 % prediction interval
     of a single run runs from 9,698 to 15,349 ng/mg, 2,503 below and 3,148 above.
  6. The predicted value is the median of the output, its geometric mean, not its
     arithmetic mean. The upper prediction limit, which is judged against an upper
     criterion, is not affected by that.
  7. Near an upper criterion, where the level is high, a raw-scale model understates
     the scatter, because it pools one residual SD over all levels. The interval from
     the log scale is correctly wider there.

In the reports, 7 of the 22 outputs with a criterion are modelled on the log scale. They
are high mannose, acidic variants and aggregates at the production bioreactor, host
cell protein in the Protein A pool, aggregates and host cell protein at cation exchange,
and host cell protein at anion exchange.



8.3 How the model is searched

The goal of the search is the model with the lowest AICc among all hierarchical sub-
models of the full quadratic. AICc estimates how well a model would predict new
runs. It rewards a close fit and charges a penalty for every estimated quantity, so
that a term is kept only where it improves the fit by more than its penalty (Hurvich
and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002). AICc is Akaike’s criterion, AIC, with



                                          31
a correction for small samples. The correction raises the penalty when the runs are
few for the number of terms, as in these designs, so that the criterion does not favour
models with too many terms. For the full quadratic model on 28 runs, AIC charges
32 for its 16 estimated quantities (15 coefficients and the residual variance), and the
correction adds 49.5 to that.
The search fits every candidate, 1,337 for a design with four factors, 95 for three and
13 for two, the model of the mean included. It takes the candidate with the lowest
AICc. The candidates are listed in a fixed order, from the fewest main effects upward,
and a tie goes to the first. A stepwise search, by contrast, adds or removes one term
at a time and stops when no single change lowers AICc. It therefore stops where only
a combination of changes would lower AICc further.
On this campaign’s data the two searches were compared for 21 outputs with a cri-
terion. The output that fits exactly has no AICc and is left out. A backward stepwise
search from the full quadratic would have stopped at a model with a higher AICc than
the best candidate for 9 of them, a forward search from the mean for 6, and one or
the other for 12. For host cell protein at cation exchange, for example, the exhaustive
search selects 6 terms with an AICc of −31.2. A backward search stops at 8 terms
(−27.4) and a forward search at 3 terms (−27.9).
The backward elimination of corrective action CA2 differed from this search in three
ways. It was stepwise. It decided by p-value, which tests each term against zero and
does not compare models. It also worked on the raw scale only.



8.4 Verification of the platform worst cases

PCMP-001 Version 2.0 derives the intermediate acceptance criteria a priori. Each
drug substance criterion is carried back through the platform worst case of every
downstream step, its lowest clearance fold or its largest increase, and each platform
worst case is the result of a single run. The verification of these worst cases uses
the same model selection, on the planned runs of each downstream step. For each
worst case it predicts the output of the step at every combination of NOR low, set-
point and NOR high of the NOR as characterized. At each combination it also takes
the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch, on the scale of the model and
transformed back. Both are converted into a clearance fold or an increase, and the
least favourable value over the combinations is the bound of the step.
Where the predicted value of a step is worse than the platform worst case, the bound
replaces the platform value, and the criteria upstream of the step tighten. Where the
bound itself is better than the platform worst case, on a selected model that meets
§7.2, the bound replaces the platform value, and the criteria upstream widen. Oth-
erwise the platform value stays. Both directions use the single-batch bound, never a
confidence bound of the mean, because every output is judged per batch and the plat-
form worst case that the bound replaces was a single run. A bound from a tolerance
interval would be statistically purer, but it is too conservative, and the single-batch
prediction bound was chosen as the pragmatic rule. The bound is pointwise at each
combination, not simultaneous over them.



                                          32
A replacement does not always mean that the product did better. The platform worst
cases of the low-pH hold are increases over a hold of 180 min, while the A-Mab value is
the increase within the NOR as characterized, which allows at most 120 min. Where
the A-Mab increase replaces the platform value, the replacement reflects the shorter
hold, not a more stable product. The criterion that follows is still right, because
routine manufacture never holds longer than the NOR. Section 9.1 gives the criteria
that the bounds of the selected models changed, and the risk of each change.

12 Conclusion and closure

The investigation confirmed the classification as minor, on the grounds recorded when
it was opened: no GMP batch was made or dispositioned on the analysis, no product
quality impact, and the finding concerns the statistical analysis of development data,
not their integrity. No range was set from a failing model at Version 2.0.
The root cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the predictive
model, with no model selection, on designs that are small for it. The Version 1.0 plans
set no adequacy criterion against which the size of a design could have been checked.
A remedy was assigned to every failing model by a rule approved before any new
run, and both augmentations were run by 2026-09-24. Model selection then replaced
the refits for every output. Once selected, the models that the augmentations were
made for meet §7.2 on the planned runs alone. The augmentation runs show that the
models of high mannose and aggregates at the production bioreactor, selected on the
planned runs, did not predict new runs well, and the reports use the models of all
runs. After selection 3 outputs have no adequate model, and every measured result
of these outputs meets its criterion.
The intermediate acceptance criteria in force were fixed at the closure of INV-001 on
the selected models. Model selection made 4 of them wider. No conclusion over the
NOR depends on the widening, and one proven acceptable range moves at its lower
end, outside the NOR. The qualification of the scale-down model for host cell protein
in the Protein A pool and at cation exchange does depend on it. The augmentation
runs moved no criterion.
At closure the augmentation runs of corrective action CA1 were complete, and the
refits of corrective action CA2 and the model selection of corrective action CA3 were
complete. The preventive actions were open. INV-002 was closed on 2026-09-28.
