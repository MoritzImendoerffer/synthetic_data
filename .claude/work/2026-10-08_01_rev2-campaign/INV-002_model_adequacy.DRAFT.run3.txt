Summary

The Version 2.0 re-analysis of the A-Mab characterization campaign applied the model-
adequacy criteria of the process characterization master plan (PCMP-001 §7.2) to
every response-surface model that carries an acceptance criterion at its step. The
criteria require a significant model F-test (α = 0.05), no significant lack of fit (α =
0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most 0.20. Of the 22 full quadratic models in
scope, 14 did not meet the limit on overfitting, which allows predicted R² to fall short
of R² by at most 0.20. No model showed significant lack of fit. One more model, of
acidic variants at low-pH viral inactivation, reproduces every run exactly, so none of
the tests can be applied to it. QA opened INV-002 on 2026-08-31 and classified it as
minor, on the grounds recorded at opening (no GMP batch was made or dispositioned
on the analysis, no product quality impact, and the finding concerns the statistical
analysis of development data, not their integrity).
The root cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the predictive
model of each design, with no model selection. The full quadratic model keeps every
term whatever the data show, and the planned designs are small for a model with that
many terms. The Version 1.0 plans also set no adequacy criterion against which the
size of a design could have been checked.
A rule for the remedy of each failing model was approved on 2026-09-02, before any
new run was made (Section 5). A failing model of an attribute of the drug substance
specification, on which the refit of the planned runs keeps an effect, is resolved by
further runs (corrective action CA1), unless the target probability of success cannot
be reached within the size of the planned design. Every other failing model is refit-
ted by hierarchical backward elimination (corrective action CA2). Viral clearance is
demonstrated by spiking studies against a cumulative claim, not by testing the drug
substance, so its models are refitted and no virus is spiked.
The remedy rule gives 3 models an augmentation, in 2 steps. The production bioreac-
tor design was augmented by 16 runs for high mannose and acidic variants, in study
S-BRX-AUG. The anion exchange design was augmented by 12 runs for host cell pro-
tein, in study S-AEX-AUG. Each augmentation is the smallest D-optimal set of runs
that gives a probability of success of at least 0.90. The probability of success is the
probability, at the effects estimated from the planned runs, that the planned model,
the full quadratic with a block term, meets the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfit-
ting on all runs (Section 6). On all runs that model meets both tests for acidic variants
and host cell protein, and it does not meet the limit on overfitting for high mannose
(Section 8.5.1). The other 11 failing models were refitted.
Model selection was adopted on 2026-09-15 as corrective action CA3, for every
response-surface output (Section 8). Each output is modelled on the log or the raw
scale, as its Box-Cox profile decides, by the submodel of the full quadratic with the

lowest AICc, and that model is judged against the model-adequacy criteria. Model
selection replaced the refits. Once selected, the three models that the augmentations
were made for meet the criteria on the planned runs alone, so the augmentations
were not needed to pass them. The augmentation runs removed from two models
of the production bioreactor, those of high mannose and aggregates, terms that
the planned runs had suggested. Their models selected on the planned runs had
predicted only 13 and 11 of the 16 new runs within the 95 % prediction interval,
fewer than a one-sided binomial test at the 0.05 level allows. The reports use the
models of all runs. A check of a selected model against the model-adequacy criteria
is optimistic, because the selection used the same runs. After selection, 4 outputs
have no adequate model (acidic variants at low-pH viral inactivation, fragments at
low-pH viral inactivation, fragments at cation exchange and XMuLV clearance at
virus filtration), and they are judged on their measured results.
Model selection was adopted before the investigation into acceptance criteria set
after the data were seen (INV-001) closed on 2026-09-18, so the intermediate accep-
tance criteria fixed at that closure rest on the selected models. The verification of the
platform worst cases widened 3 of these criteria, for aggregates at the production
bioreactor, host cell protein in the Protein A pool and host cell protein at cation ex-
change, by replacing a platform worst case with the one-sided 95 % prediction bound
of a single A-Mab batch. The criterion for aggregates at the production bioreactor
was also tightened by another downstream step, and it is narrower than its a pri-
ori value. Against the criteria that the same verification gives on the full quadratic
models, model selection made 4 criteria wider, by 4 %, 4 %, 7 % and 7 %, and kept
the upper limit of acidic variants at the production bioreactor at its a priori value,

range moves. Against the criteria without model selection, however, the scale-down
model would not be qualified for host cell protein in the Protein A pool and at cation
exchange. The augmentation runs moved no criterion.

opened on 2026-08-03. It followed the QA review of the process characterization
master report (PCMR-001) Version 1.0 for BLA readiness, which found that the ac-
ceptance criteria of the proven acceptable range analysis had been set after the data
were seen. Its corrective actions included the reissue of every characterization report
at Version 2.0, re-analysed against criteria set a priori. Version 2.0 of the process
characterization master plan (PCMP-001) also states campaign thresholds for model
adequacy (PCMP-001 §7.2). Version 1.0 of the master plan had left these thresholds
to the individual study. The Version 1.0 reports fitted the full quadratic model to every
response-surface design and applied no adequacy test.
The Version 2.0 re-analysis applied the model-adequacy criteria of the master plan to
every response-surface model with an acceptance criterion at its step. The criteria
require a significant model F-test (α = 0.05), no significant lack of fit (α = 0.05) and R²
minus predicted R² of at most 0.20. The re-analysis found 14 of the 22 full quadratic
models above the limit on overfitting. QA opened INV-002 on 2026-08-31, while INV-
001 was still open. The recorded trigger is the re-analysis itself.
As an immediate action, no proven acceptable range, design space or judgement
across the normal operating range (NOR) was set from a model that did not meet
the model-adequacy criteria while the investigation was open. The Version 1.0 docu-
ments, whose ranges came from the full quadratic models, were already on hold for
BLA use under INV-001.
The remedy rule, which assigns a remedy to each failing model, was approved within
this investigation on 2026-09-02, together with the sample sizes and the augmen-

3 Finding

The process characterization master plan judges a response-surface model by three
tests (PCMP-001 §7.2). The model F-test must be significant at α = 0.05. The lack-of-
fit test splits the residual variation into pure error, from the replicated centre points,
and lack of fit, which is the rest. Lack of fit must not be significant against pure error
at α = 0.05. The third test limits overfitting. The prediction error sum of squares
(PRESS) adds up the squared error of each run when it is predicted from a fit to
all the other runs, and predicted R² is one minus PRESS divided by the total sum of
squares (Montgomery 2017). The master plan limits R² minus predicted R² to at most
0.20. For a model that fails, it states the remedy in these words. “A model that fails
the lack-of-fit test or the limit on overfitting is either refitted with a justified change
of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a range.” The re-analysis applied
the three tests to the full quadratic model of every output in scope, on the planned
runs.
Table 3.1 gives the full quadratic model of each of the 22 outputs in scope, with its
verdict. Each step with a response-surface design has at least one model that fails.
The 14 failing models all fail the limit on overfitting, with R² minus predicted R² from
0.26 to 2.14 against a limit of 0.20. No model fails the lack-of-fit test, and the lowest
lack-of-fit p-value among the failing models is 0.096. The lack-of-fit test therefore
gives no sign of systematic error in the full quadratic model. With 4 centre points,
however, pure error has 3 degrees of freedom, so the test has little power to find such
an error. Of the failing models, 5 also fail the model F-test, with p-values from 0.280
to 0.936. They are the 4 models of attributes with no detected effect and fragments
in the Protein A pool. All of them fail the limit on overfitting as well, and that failure
decides the remedy. Section 4 gives the cause.

4 Root cause

The primary cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the pre-
dictive model of every response-surface design, with no model selection. The full
quadratic model keeps every main effect, two-factor interaction and squared term,
whatever the data show. The planned designs are small for a model with that many
terms. With four factors, the full quadratic model has 15 terms, including the inter-
cept, and the planned face-centred design has 28 runs. Low-pH viral inactivation has
three factors, 10 terms and 18 runs. Virus filtration has two factors, 6 terms and 12
runs.
Predicted R² comes from predicting each run from a fit that leaves that run out. The
weight a run has in its own fitted value is its leverage. The average leverage equals the
number of terms divided by the number of runs, and in these designs it lies between
0.50 and 0.56. PRESS divides the residual of each run by one minus its leverage.
Leverage differs from run to run, from 0.12 to 0.79 in these designs, so the enlarge-
ment at the average leverage understates the effect. In expectation, PRESS is the
residual sum of squares times the sum of 1/(1 − leverage) over the runs, divided by
the residual degrees of freedom. That factor is 5.2 to 7.0 for these designs. R² minus
predicted R² equals PRESS minus the residual sum of squares, divided by the total
sum of squares. It is therefore expected to be 4.2 to 6.0 times the share of the varia-
tion that the model leaves unexplained, one minus R². The limit of 0.20 is then met,
in expectation, only where R² is above 0.95 to 0.97. Every full quadratic model that
met all three tests (a significant model F-test (α = 0.05), no significant lack of fit (α =
0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most 0.20) has an R² of at least 0.96, and every
model that failed the limit on overfitting has an R² of at most 0.95.
The sample-size simulation of Section 6 quantifies this gap for the models that the
remedy rule augments. With the planned runs alone, the probability that the full
quadratic model meets the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting is 0.251 for
high mannose, 0.444 for acidic variants and 0.493 for host cell protein at anion ex-
change. These probabilities are computed at the effects that the same planned runs
estimated.
Attributes with no detected effect within the range studied fail for a second reason.
For such an attribute the full quadratic model fits only random variation, and each
of its terms takes up part of it. R² stays between 0.27 and 0.57 while predicted R² is
below zero. With no effect, R² minus predicted R² depends on the number of terms
against the number of runs, not on the size of the noise, so it shrinks only as the runs
outgrow the terms. The fragment models of Protein A and cation exchange behave
in the same way with a small effect. Their probability of success is at most 0.005 at
every size computed, up to the size of the planned design (Table 6.1).

5 Remedy and decision rule

The process characterization master plan (PCMP-001 §7.2) judges a model by a sig-
nificant model F-test (α = 0.05), no significant lack of fit (α = 0.05) and R² minus
predicted R² of at most 0.20. For a model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the limit on
overfitting, it allows a refit with a justified change of terms or transformation, and
otherwise the model is not used to set a range (Section 3). The master plan names no
augmentation. Augmentation is this investigation’s rule, for a design too small for the
model a decision needs. The A-Mab case study names the same remedy for a design
that cannot resolve the terms a decision needs, which is to augment it (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009).
QA approved the remedy rule on 2026-09-02, before any new run was made, and it
was applied to every failing model in the same way:

  1. A failing model of an attribute of the drug substance specification, on which the
     refit of the planned runs keeps an effect, is resolved by further runs. The number
     of runs is the smallest that gives a probability of success of at least 0.90 that the
     planned model, the full quadratic with a block term, meets the lack-of-fit test
     and the limit on overfitting on all runs (Section 6).
  2. Viral clearance is not tested on the drug substance. It is a process requirement,
     demonstrated by spiking studies at worst-case conditions against a cumulative
     claim (International Council for Harmonisation 2023). Its models are refitted,
     and no augmentation spikes virus.
  3. No augmentation is larger than the planned design. Where the target probability
     of success is not reached within that size, the model is refitted.
  4. Every other failing model is refitted.
  5. A response with no adequate model, full or refitted, is judged on its measured
     results. No model sets a range for it.

For an attribute of the specification with an effect, a refit on a design that is too small
can drop a real interaction or curvature, and the ranges of the step would then rest on
a model that misses it. Further runs make the estimate of every term of the full model
more precise. For an attribute with no detected effect, the refit to the mean describes
the results, and further runs would only raise the power to detect a small effect. The
target of 0.90 is the probability of success customary for a confirmatory study, 0.80
to 0.90, taken at its upper end because a further failure would leave an attribute with
a criterion without an adequate model. The cap on the size of an augmentation, the
size of the planned design, keeps a remedy from becoming a new study.
The remedy rule did not foresee a viral clearance model that bears on a decision. The
XMuLV model at low-pH viral inactivation is such a model. On it the step claim is not




                                            17
met over the NOR as characterized, and the low-pH viral inactivation report (PCR-
006) moves the NOR (Section 9.2). Under rule 2, which refits every viral clearance
model and spikes no virus, the XMuLV model at low-pH viral inactivation was refitted,
because viral clearance is not tested on the drug substance.
The refit removes terms by hierarchical backward elimination at α = 0.05, and it is
corrective action CA2. At each step, the least significant term that no remaining term
contains is removed. A main effect therefore stays while an interaction or a square
of it remains, even where the main effect itself is not significant. Elimination stops
when every removable term is significant. In an augmented design the block term be-
longs to the design and is never removed. The refitted model is then judged against
the model-adequacy criteria in the same way as the full model. Model selection, cor-
rective action CA3, later replaced the refit by backward elimination for every output,
the outputs that CA2 had refitted included (Section 8). The sample-size calculation
rests on the refits.
Where neither the full nor the refitted model meets the model-adequacy criteria, the
response is judged on its measured results, as the master plan asks for a response with
no adequate model. Every measured result of the screening and response-surface
designs of the step is compared with the criterion, at manufacturing scale where
the scale-down qualification gives an offset for the output. The designs span the
characterized range, so a response whose measured results all meet the criterion is
taken to meet it over the NOR. A modelled output must instead meet its criterion
with the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch at every combination of NOR low,
set-point and NOR high. Section 8.5.2 gives the margin of each such response.

6 Sample-size calculation

6.1 Method

The number of further runs is calculated by simulation, separately for each failing
model with an effect. The model-adequacy criteria of the process characterization
master plan (PCMP-001 §7.2) require a significant model F-test (α = 0.05), no signifi-
cant lack of fit (α = 0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most 0.20. The simulation
estimates the probability of success, which is the probability, at the effects estimated
from the planned runs, that the planned model meets the second and the third of
these once the design has been augmented by a given number of runs. It does not
include the uncertainty of those estimates. The planned model is the full quadratic
with a block term, on the raw scale, because that was the model the plans prescribed
when the runs were sized. Model selection, and with it the log scale for some outputs,
came later. The calculation has six steps:

  1. The refit of the planned runs by backward elimination, with all its terms (Ta-
     ble 5.1), is taken as the true model, with its fitted coefficients. Its residual stan-
     dard deviation is taken as the variation between runs. The refit is used because
     it keeps only the terms that the planned runs support. The full model would
     carry its fitted noise into every simulated data set.
  2. For a candidate size, the D-optimal augmentation of that many runs is added to
     the planned runs as a second block (Section 6.2).
  3. Responses are simulated at the planned and the augmentation runs, as the true
     model plus an error drawn from a normal distribution with the residual standard
     deviation, the same at every level. For each size, 4,000 data sets are simulated.
  4. The planned model, the full quadratic with a block term, is fitted to each simu-
     lated data set. It is judged against the lack-of-fit test at α = 0.05 and the limit
     of 0.20 on R² minus predicted R², the two tests of the master plan that decide
     whether a model may set a range. The model F-test is not simulated. Pure er-
     ror comes from the replicated centre points of the planned design, because no
     augmentation repeats a point within its own block.
  5. The probability of success is estimated as the share of simulated data sets in
     which the planned model meets both tests.
  6. Sizes are evaluated in steps of 2 runs, up to the size of the planned design. The
     number of runs needed is the smallest size with a probability of success of at
     least 0.90.

The simulation also gives the power of each significant effect of the refit. The power
is the share of simulated data sets in which the term is significant at α = 0.05 in the
fitted planned model, the full quadratic with a block term. The probability of success
does not always rise with every further run, because the D-optimal augmentation of



                                           22
each size is a different set of points. In 9 of the 10 curves it falls at least once from
one size to the next (Figure 6.1).
The probability of success cannot rise much above 0.95. The lack-of-fit test at α =
0.05 rejects a correct model in 5 % of data sets, whatever the size of the design. A
target of 0.90 therefore asks that the limit on overfitting be met in about 95 % of data
sets.
The calculation assumes that the refit of the planned runs is the true model, that
the error is normal with the same variance at every level, and that the two blocks
differ by a constant at most. A constant difference between the blocks is taken up
by the block term of the fitted model. A change in the effects between the blocks
is not. The augmentations repeat some corner points of the planned designs, and
Section 8.5.1 compares the results there. On the planned runs that the simulation
used, model selection put 2 of the outputs simulated here on the log scale (acidic
variants at the production bioreactor and host cell protein at anion exchange), and
on all runs also high mannose at the production bioreactor. For them, the error of
constant variance on the raw scale is an assumption that the log-scale analysis does
not support (Section 8.2). An effect that the planned runs did not detect is not in the
true model, so the calculation does not size a design to find it. Each curve uses its own
stream of a pseudo-random number generator with a fixed seed, so the calculation
gives the same result each time it is run.
The calculation has two further limitations. First, it simulates the planned runs again
together with the augmentation runs, instead of holding the planned results at their
measured values. It therefore estimates how often a new design of planned and aug-
mentation runs would meet the tests, not how often the measured planned results
would meet them together with new runs. Second, the terms of the true model were
kept because they were significant on the planned runs. Coefficients kept for that
reason tend to lie further from zero than the effects they estimate, so the probability
of success is optimistic.



6.2 D-optimal augmentation

Each augmentation is drawn from the levels of the face-centred design, so every factor
takes its low, centre or high level. For four factors this gives 81 candidate points. The
runs are chosen to maximise the determinant of the information matrix of the planned
model, the full quadratic with a block term, with the planned runs held fixed in the
first block. The selection adds, one run at a time, the candidate that increases the
determinant most. It then exchanges chosen runs with candidates until no exchange
increases the determinant further, which is a Fedorov exchange (Fedorov 1972). Ties
go to the first candidate in a fixed order, so the design needs no random number.
The face-centred designs of three and four factors hold corner, axial and centre points
only, and none of their points has exactly one factor at its centre level. Their squared
terms are therefore strongly correlated with each other, because every corner run
sets all of them to one. Both D-optimal augmentations consist mostly of points at
the middle of an edge of the design cube, with one factor at its centre level and



                                           23
the others at a limit (Section 7). These points reduce the correlation between the
squared terms. In the production bioreactor design the largest correlation between
two squared terms falls from 0.69 on the planned runs to 0.54 on all runs.

8 Model selection (corrective action CA3)

8.1 Procedure

Model selection was adopted as corrective action CA3 on 2026-09-15, before the in-
vestigation into acceptance criteria set after the data were seen (INV-001) closed on
2026-09-18. It replaced the analysis of every output, the refits of corrective action
CA2 included. Each report with a response-surface design applies it to every output
of the design, on all runs in which the output was measured. Where a design was
augmented, the planned runs form the first block and the augmentation runs the sec-
ond. The verification of the platform worst cases behind the intermediate acceptance
criteria applies the same procedure (Section 8.4). The process characterization mas-
ter plan (PCMP-001) is not changed. Each report records the model selection as a
discrepancy from its plan (Section 9). The procedure has four steps:

  1. The scale. The output is raised to a power λ, and the full quadratic model is
     fitted to the result, with the block term where the runs form two blocks, over
     a range of powers (Box and Cox 1964). A power of λ = 1 leaves the output on
     its raw scale, and λ = 0 is read as the natural logarithm. For each power the
     profile likelihood measures how well the model fits the transformed output with
     residuals that are normal and of equal spread. Its 95 % interval is the set of
     powers that the data do not reject at the 5 % level. The output is modelled on
     the log scale (λ = 0) where this interval contains 0 and excludes 1. Otherwise it
     is modelled on the raw scale (λ = 1). The rule chooses between these two scales
     only, and only an output whose results are all positive can take the log scale.
     Where the interval excludes both 0 and 1, the raw scale is kept. On all runs this
     applies to afucosylation at the production bioreactor. On its planned runs alone
     the interval of afucosylation contains 0 and excludes 1, and the model selected
     there is on the log scale. The scale is chosen on the full quadratic before the
     search, so that the search compares every candidate on one scale. An output
     whose results the full quadratic reproduces exactly has no interval, because its
     profile likelihood is then unbounded, and no scale is chosen for it.
  2. The search. Every submodel of the full quadratic model that respects strong
     hierarchy is fitted on that scale. Strong hierarchy allows an interaction only
     together with both of its main effects, and a squared term only together with its
     main effect. The block term is kept in every candidate. The candidate with the
     lowest AICc is selected (Section 8.3). Where the full quadratic reproduces every
     run exactly, AICc is not defined, and the output has no adequate model (step 3).
  3. The check. The selected model is judged on its own scale against the model-
     adequacy criteria of the master plan, which require a significant model F-test (α
     = 0.05), no significant lack of fit (α = 0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most




                                          31
     0.20. A selected model with no term has no model F-test and is judged by the
     other two tests. A model that reproduces every run exactly is not adequate. With
     no residual there is no pure error for the lack-of-fit test and no residual mean
     square for the model F-test, so the criteria cannot be applied, and its prediction
     interval would have zero width. Only the selected model is judged. If it fails,
     no other candidate takes its place. The selection used the same runs that the
     criteria then judge. A model chosen as the best of many candidates fits those
     runs better than a model fixed in advance, so the check of a selected model
     against the model-adequacy criteria is optimistic. Section 8.5.1 gives the one
     independent check that exists.
  4. The offset. The scale-down offset of an output is taken on the scale of its model,
     with its 90 % confidence interval. For a raw-scale output it is the mean of the
     scale-down set-point runs minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches,
     and it is subtracted from a scale-down prediction. For a log-scale output it is the
     same difference of the mean logarithms, reported as a ratio of geometric means,
     and a scale-down prediction is divided by it. An output with no adequate model
     takes its offset on the scale chosen in step 1, or on the raw scale where no scale
     is chosen.

The check of a selected model against the model-adequacy criteria has three out-
comes. A selected model with terms that passes is the predictive model of the output,
and it sets the ranges of its step. A selected model with no term passes when it meets
the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting. It says that no effect of the parame-
ters was detected within the range studied. It does not show that there is none. For
a model of the mean alone on a single block, R² is zero and R² minus predicted R²
depends on the number of runs only, 0.19 for 12 runs and 0.08 for 28, so for such a
model the check reduces to the lack-of-fit test. With 3 degrees of freedom for pure
error, that test has little power, and such a model passes it almost always. An output
whose selected model fails, or fits exactly, has no adequate model. It is judged on its
measured results, as Section 5 describes, and it sets no range.



8.2 Why a log scale, and what it changes

Host cell protein in the Protein A pool shows what the log scale does. On the log scale
the selected model has the form


                           ln 𝑦 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + 𝑏2 𝑥2 + ⋯ + 𝜀

and, transformed back,


                             𝑦 = 𝑒𝑏0 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏1 𝑥1 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏2 𝑥2 ⋯ 𝑒𝜀

Here y is pool host cell protein in ng/mg, x₁, x₂ and so on are the parameters in coded
units, from −1 at the low end of the range studied to +1 at the high end, and ε is the
error of a single run. One coded unit is therefore half the range studied. A parameter




                                          32
that enters the model as a main effect alone multiplies the output by a fixed factor, 𝑒𝑏
per coded unit and 𝑒2𝑏 from the low to the high end of its range. The error multiplies
it too, so the scatter in ng/mg grows with the level.
Protein load shows how this reads in real units. Its range studied is 10 to 50 g/L
resin, so one coded unit is 20 g/L resin. Its coefficient on the log scale is b = 0.443.
Pool host cell protein therefore rises by a factor of 𝑒𝑏 = 1.56 for every 20 g/L resin of
protein load, and by 𝑒2𝑏 = 2.43 from 10 to 50 g/L resin. Across the range studied, the
selected model multiplies pool host cell protein by a factor of 2.43 for protein load,
0.30 for elution buffer pH and 0.85 for end of pool collect, from the low to the high
end of each parameter. A factor below one is a decrease. Load flow rate also has a
squared term, so its factor depends on where along the range the change is made.
From 100 to 200 cm/hr pool host cell protein changes by a factor of 0.88, and from
200 to 300 cm/hr by a factor of 1.08.
Two properties of the process and the method make host cell protein behave this way.
A purification step removes a share of what is present, so a parameter changes the
share that is removed, not a fixed amount. The precision of the host cell protein ELISA
(AMV-3012) is a percentage of the level, 9.5 %, so the scatter of the results grows with
the level. Effects that multiply look like an interaction when they are added on the
raw scale. On the raw scale the full quadratic model needs the interaction protein
load × elution buffer pH (p < 0.001). On the log scale the same interaction is not
significant (p = 0.156), and the selected model has no interaction at all.
Least squares needs no assumption about the distribution of the residuals to estimate
the coefficients. The t-tests, F-tests and prediction intervals built on it assume resid-
uals that are independent and normally distributed, with mean zero and the same
variance over the whole range studied. The residuals of the full quadratic model of
pool host cell protein show how the raw scale departs from this. Its runs were split at
the median of the predicted values, 14 runs in the lower half and 14 in the upper half.
On the raw scale the standard deviation of the residuals is 638 ng/mg in the lower
half of the predictions and 947 ng/mg in the upper half, 1.48 times as large. On the
log scale the same split gives 0.067 and 0.074 in natural-log units, 1.11 times as large.
On the log scale, therefore, the spread no longer grows with the level. Two halves of
this size cannot show on their own that the raw-scale spread differs, and the Box-Cox
profile is the evidence for the scale. It puts λ at 0.06, with a 95 % interval from −0.32
to 0.42. The interval contains 0 and excludes 1, so the data support the log scale
and reject the raw scale. This evidence concerns the full quadratic model, on which
the scale is chosen, and not the smaller model selected afterwards. A test can fail
to reject the assumption of equal variance, but it cannot show that the assumption
holds.
The log scale changes the following:

  1. The criterion stays in its own unit, ng/mg, and the prediction limits are trans-
     formed back into it.
  2. Effects are fold changes, as above.
  3. The scale-down offset is a ratio of geometric means, 0.97 for pool host cell protein
     with a 90 % confidence interval from 0.89 to 1.06, and a scale-down prediction
     is divided by it.



                                          33
  4. A prediction interval is calculated on the log scale and transformed back. It
     keeps its 95 % coverage, because the probability that ln y lies between two values
     is the probability that y lies between their antilogs.
  5. The interval is asymmetric, further above the predicted value than below it. At
     the least favourable combination of the NOR (protein load 39 g/L resin, elution
     buffer pH 3.4, load flow rate 150 cm/hr and end of pool collect 2.3 CV), the model
     predicts ln y = 9.382 in the scale-down model. The 95 % prediction interval of
     a single batch is two-sided. On the log scale it is the prediction ± t·s·√(1 + h),
     where t = 2.074 is the 97.5th percentile of the t distribution with 22 degrees of
     freedom, s = 0.105 is the residual standard deviation on the log scale, and h =
     0.107 is the leverage of this combination. This gives 9.382 ± 0.230. Transformed
     back, the interval runs from 9,440 to 14,941 ng/mg around a median of 11,876
     ng/mg in the scale-down model. Divided by the scale-down offset of 0.97, it runs
     from 9,698 to 15,349 ng/mg around a median of 12,201 ng/mg at manufacturing
     scale, 2,503 below and 3,148 above. The conclusion over the NOR uses this two-
     sided interval. The verification of the platform worst cases uses the one-sided
     95 % bound instead (Section 8.4).
  6. The predicted value is the median of the output, its geometric mean, not its
     arithmetic mean. The upper prediction limit, which is judged against an upper
     criterion, is not affected by that.
  7. Near an upper criterion, where the level is high, a raw-scale model pools one
     residual standard deviation over all levels and so understates the scatter, which
     the log-scale interval does not.

In the reports, 7 of the 22 outputs with a criterion are modelled on the log scale. They
are high mannose, acidic variants and aggregates at the production bioreactor, host
cell protein in the Protein A pool, aggregates and host cell protein at cation exchange,
and host cell protein at anion exchange.



8.3 How the model is searched

The goal of the search is the model with the lowest AICc among all hierarchical sub-
models of the full quadratic. AICc estimates how well a model would predict new runs
(Hurvich and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002). It adds a fit term to a penalty.
The fit term is n·ln(SSE/n), where n is the number of runs and SSE the residual sum
of squares, and it falls as the fit improves. The penalty is 2K, where K counts the
estimated quantities, the coefficients and the residual variance, plus a correction for
small samples, 2K(K + 1)/(n − K − 1). A constant that is the same for every candidate
is left out. A term is therefore kept only where it lowers the fit term by more than it
raises the penalty. AICc is Akaike’s criterion, AIC, with that correction. The correc-
tion raises the penalty when the runs are few for the number of terms, as in these
designs, so that the criterion does not favour models with too many terms. For the
full quadratic model on 28 runs, K is 16 (15 coefficients and the residual variance),
so the penalty is 32 and the correction adds 49.5 to it.
Pool host cell protein in the Protein A pool shows the trade. On the log scale the fit
term is −148.6 for the full quadratic model and −132.8 for the selected model. The



                                          34
selected model has 5 terms, so K is 7, its penalty is 14 and its correction 5.6. The full
quadratic fits better by 15.8, but its penalty is larger by 61.9, so the selected model
has the lower AICc, by 46.1.
The search fits every candidate, 1,337 for a design with four factors, 95 for three and
13 for two, the model of the mean included. It takes the candidate with the lowest
AICc. The candidates are listed in a fixed order, from the fewest main effects upward,
and a tie goes to the first. A stepwise search, by contrast, adds or removes one term
at a time and stops when no single change lowers AICc. It therefore stops where only
a combination of changes would lower AICc further.
On this campaign’s data the two searches were compared for 21 outputs with a cri-
terion. The output that fits exactly has no AICc and is left out. A backward stepwise
search from the full quadratic would have stopped at a model with a higher AICc than
the best candidate for 9 of them, a forward search from the mean for 6, and one or
the other for 12. For host cell protein at cation exchange, for example, the exhaustive
search selects 6 terms with an AICc of −31.2. A backward search stops at 8 terms
(−27.4) and a forward search at 3 terms (−27.9).
The backward elimination of corrective action CA2 differed from this search in three
ways. It was stepwise. It decided by the p-value of each term. That p-value compares
the model with and without the term, but it does not weigh the fit against the number
of terms, and it cannot compare models that are not nested. It also worked on the
raw scale only.



8.4 Verification of the platform worst cases

The process characterization master plan (PCMP-001) Version 2.0 derives the inter-
mediate acceptance criteria a priori. Each drug substance criterion is carried back
through the platform worst case of every downstream step, its lowest clearance fold
or its largest increase, and each platform worst case is the result of a single run. The
verification of these worst cases uses the same model selection, on the planned runs
of each downstream step. For each worst case it predicts the output of the step at
every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the NOR as characterized.
At each combination it also takes the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single
batch, on the scale of the model and transformed back. Both are converted into a
clearance fold or an increase, and the least favourable value over the combinations
is the bound of the step.
Where the predicted value of a step is worse than the platform worst case, the bound
replaces the platform value, and the criteria upstream of the step tighten. Where the
bound itself is better than the platform worst case, on a selected model that meets
the model-adequacy criteria (a significant model F-test (α = 0.05), no significant lack
of fit (α = 0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most 0.20), the bound replaces the
platform value, and the criteria upstream widen. Otherwise the platform value stays.
Both directions use the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch, because
every output is judged per batch and the platform worst case that the bound replaces
was a single run. A bound from a tolerance interval covers a stated share of all batches



                                           35
with a stated confidence, and it is wider. The bound is pointwise at each combination,
not simultaneous over them.
The platform worst cases of the low-pH hold are increases over a hold of 180 min. The
A-Mab value is the increase within the NOR as characterized, which allows a hold of at
most 120 min. At low pH the antibody forms aggregate, and the aggregate grows with
the time at that pH. At the edges of the range studied, which include a hold of 180 min,
A-Mab adds up to 0.84 percentage points of aggregate, more than the platform value
of 0.70. Its single-batch bound of 0.559 within the NOR as characterized is smaller
than the platform value because that NOR ends at a shorter hold. The selected model
of aggregate at low-pH viral inactivation has terms in hold time and temperature only,
and none in inactivation pH. The NOR in force ends at the same longest hold time,
120 min, and the same highest temperature, 23 °C, as the NOR as characterized.
The bound over the NOR as characterized therefore also covers the NOR in force.
For acidic variants the model at low pH fits exactly and is not adequate, so its bound
replaces no platform value (Section 8.5.3). Section 9.1 gives the criteria that the
bounds of the selected models changed, and the risk of each change.

significant (p = 0.053), and its R² minus predicted R² of 0.24 exceeds the limit of 0.20.
XMuLV clearance at virus filtration is therefore judged on its measured results. The
two models differ by 1.0 in AICc. Models within two units of each other have about
equal support (Burnham and Anderson 2002), so the outcome for this output rests on
a small difference.


8.5.3 An output that fits exactly

Acidic variants at low-pH viral inactivation have results with no scatter. Every run
at the same hold time returned the same value, in the screening and the response-
surface designs alike, the replicated centre points included. The full quadratic model,
which contains hold time and its square, therefore reproduces every run exactly, and
so does the model with hold time alone. With no residual there is no pure error for
the lack-of-fit test and no residual mean square for the model F-test, so the model-
adequacy criteria cannot be applied. A prediction interval would have zero width.
The Box-Cox profile likelihood is unbounded, so no scale is chosen and no interval
is reported. The output therefore has no adequate model, and it is judged on its
measured results (Table 8.4). Its bound cannot replace a platform value in the verifi-
cation of the platform worst cases (Section 8.4). The upper limit of acidic variants at
the production bioreactor therefore stays at its a priori value of 39.0 %.

9.2 Conclusions over the NOR

The conclusion over the NOR of an output, whether it meets its criterion at every

met on either model, and both rested on full quadratic models that fail the limit on
overfitting:

  1. XMuLV clearance at low-pH viral inactivation is judged on the mean LRF at the
     least favourable combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high, because viral
     clearance is validated at worst-case conditions (PCMP-001 §6.4). The criterion is
     the platform claim of the step, 6.6 log10. The claims of the three viral clearance

     steps add up to 16.72 log10, against a cumulative requirement of 16.7 log10.
     The selected model has the main effects of inactivation pH, hold time and tem-
     perature only. On it, the lowest mean LRF within the NOR as characterized is
     6.16, at inactivation pH 3.6, hold time 60 min and temperature 19 °C, so the

     the same conclusion. The low-pH viral inactivation report (PCR-006) moves the

  2. Acidic variants at the production bioreactor are judged on the 95 % prediction
     interval of a single batch at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR
     high. The lower limit of the criterion is 18 %, the lower limit of the drug sub-
     stance specification carried to the step unchanged. In the selected model acidic
     variants fall as culture pH rises, as in the model of the A-Mab case study (CMC

11 Effectiveness check

The actions are checked for effectiveness by 2027-06-30. The check is recorded in
three parts:

  1. For the next two characterization plans, the sample-size calculation of preventive
     action PA1 documented for every planned model before approval.
  2. At their first analysis, for every attribute with a criterion, a selected model that
     meets the model-adequacy criteria and whose 95 % prediction intervals contain
     the step’s confirmation runs at least as often as a one-sided binomial test at the
     0.05 level allows.
  3. If no plan is approved by the due date, the check is made on the next character-
     ization study of A-Mab.

The second part tests the prediction of new runs. Under a correct model each con-
firmation run falls inside its 95 % prediction interval with a probability of 0.95, and
the number inside follows a binomial distribution. The test rejects the model when
that number is so low that a correct model would give it with a probability below 0.05.
With 16 confirmation runs at least 14 must fall inside, and with 12 at least 10. In the
second part, a selected model meets the model-adequacy criteria of the process char-
acterization master plan (PCMP-001 §7.2) when it shows a significant model F-test
(α = 0.05), no significant lack of fit (α = 0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most
0.20. Applied to the augmentation runs of this investigation, the binomial test rejects
the models of high mannose and aggregates at the production bioreactor that were
selected on the planned runs (Section 8.5.1).

12 Conclusion and closure

The investigation confirmed the classification as minor, on the grounds recorded when
it was opened (no GMP batch was made or dispositioned on the analysis, no product
quality impact, and the finding concerns the statistical analysis of development data,
not their integrity). No range was set from a failing model at Version 2.0.
The root cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the predictive
model, with no model selection, on designs that are small for it. The Version 1.0 plans
set no adequacy criterion against which the size of a design could have been checked.
A remedy was assigned to every failing model by a rule approved before any new
run, and both augmentations were run by 2026-09-24. Model selection then replaced
the refits for every output. Once selected, the models that the augmentations were
made for meet the model-adequacy criteria (a significant model F-test (α = 0.05), no
significant lack of fit (α = 0.05) and R² minus predicted R² of at most 0.20) on the
planned runs alone. On all runs the full quadratic model with a block term, which the
augmentations were sized for, meets the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting for
acidic variants and host cell protein, and not for high mannose. The models of high
mannose and aggregates at the production bioreactor, selected on the planned runs,
predicted fewer of the new runs within their intervals than a binomial test allows, and
the reports use the models of all runs. After selection 4 outputs have no adequate
model, and every measured result of these outputs meets its criterion.
The intermediate acceptance criteria in force were fixed on the selected models at the
closure of the investigation into acceptance criteria set after the data were seen (INV-
001). Against the criteria that the full quadratic models give, model selection made 4
intermediate acceptance criteria wider and kept the upper limit of acidic variants at
the production bioreactor at its a priori value. No conclusion over the NOR depends
on that difference, and one proven acceptable range moves at its lower end, outside
the NOR. The qualification of the scale-down model for host cell protein in the Protein
A pool and at cation exchange does depend on it. The augmentation runs moved no
criterion.
At closure the augmentation runs of corrective action CA1 and the refits of corrective
action CA2 were complete. The re-evaluation on all runs of CA1 and the application of
model selection, corrective action CA3, in the Version 2.0 reports were open, due on
2026-09-30. The preventive actions were open. INV-002 was closed on 2026-09-28.
