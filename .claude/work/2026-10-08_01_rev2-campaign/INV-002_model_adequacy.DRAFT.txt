Summary

The Version 2.0 re-analysis of the A-Mab characterization campaign applied the model-
adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 to every response-surface model that carries an
acceptance criterion at its step. Of the 22 full quadratic models in scope, 14 did not
meet the limit on overfitting, which allows predicted R² to fall short of R² by at most
0.20. No model showed significant lack of fit. QA opened INV-002 on 2026-08-31 and
classified it as minor.
The root cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the predictive
model of each design, with no model selection. The full quadratic model keeps every
term whatever the data show, and the planned designs are small for a model with that
many terms. The Version 1.0 plans also set no adequacy criterion against which the
size of a design could have been checked.
A rule for the remedy of each failing model was approved on 2026-09-02, before any
new run was made (Section 5). A failing model of an attribute of the drug substance
specification, on which the refit of the planned runs keeps an effect, is resolved by
further runs (corrective action CA1). Every other failing model is refitted by hierar-
chical backward elimination (corrective action CA2). Viral clearance is not a drug
substance test, so its models are refitted and no virus is spiked. No augmentation is
larger than the planned design.
The rule gives 3 models an augmentation, in 2 steps. The production bioreactor de-
sign was augmented by 16 runs for high mannose and acidic variants, in study S-BRX-
AUG. The anion exchange design was augmented by 12 runs for host cell protein, in
study S-AEX-AUG. Each augmentation is a D-optimal set of runs. Its size gives an
assurance of at least 0.90 that the model of the plan, the full quadratic with a block
term, meets §7.2 on all runs (Section 6). The other 11 failing models were refitted.
Model selection was adopted on 2026-09-15 as corrective action CA3, for every
response-surface output (Section 8). Each output is modelled on the scale that its
Box-Cox profile supports, by the submodel of the full quadratic with the lowest AICc,
and that model is judged against §7.2. Model selection replaced the refits. Once
selected, the three models that the augmentations were made for meet §7.2 on the
planned runs alone. The augmentations were therefore not needed for adequacy,
and they sharpened the estimates. After selection, 3 outputs have no adequate model
(fragments at low-pH viral inactivation, fragments at cation exchange and XMuLV
clearance at virus filtration), and they are judged on their measured results.
Model selection was adopted before INV-001 closed on 2026-09-18, so the intermedi-
ate acceptance criteria fixed at that closure rest on the selected models. The same
verification on the full quadratic models gives different values for 4 criteria (Sec-
tion 9.1). The anion exchange host cell protein model meets §7.2 only once selected,

1 Event description

for BLA readiness found that the acceptance criteria of the proven acceptable range
analysis had been set after the data were seen. Its corrective actions included the
reissue of every characterization report at Version 2.0, re-analysed against criteria set
a priori. PCMP-001 Version 2.0 also states campaign thresholds for model adequacy

Version 1.0 reports fitted the full quadratic model to every response-surface design

3 Finding

PCMP-001 §7.2 judges a response-surface model by three tests (Montgomery 2017).
The model F-test must be significant at α = 0.05. The lack-of-fit test splits the residual
variation into pure error, from the replicated centre points, and lack of fit, which is the
rest. Lack of fit must not be significant against pure error at α = 0.05. The third test
limits overfitting. The prediction error sum of squares (PRESS) adds up the squared
error of each run when it is predicted from a fit to all the other runs, and predicted
R² is one minus PRESS divided by the total sum of squares. R² minus predicted R²
may not exceed 0.20. A model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the limit on overfitting
is refitted with a justified change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set
a range. The re-analysis applied the three tests to the full quadratic model of every
output in scope, on the planned runs.
Table 3.1 lists the 14 full quadratic models that do not meet PCMP-001 §7.2, out of the
22 models in scope. Each step with a response-surface design has at least one. Every
model in the table fails the limit on overfitting. R² minus predicted R² ranges from
0.26 to 2.14, against a limit of 0.20. No model fails the lack-of-fit test, and the lowest
lack-of-fit p-value among the failing models is 0.096. The lack-of-fit test therefore
gives no sign of systematic error in the full quadratic model. The model fails because
it fits the runs it was estimated from much better than it predicts a run left out of the
fit.

4 Root cause

The primary cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the pre-
dictive model of every response-surface design, with no model selection. The full
quadratic model keeps every main effect, two-factor interaction and squared term,
whatever the data show. The planned designs are small for a model with that many
terms. With four factors, the full quadratic model has 15 terms, including the inter-
cept, and the planned face-centred design has 28 runs. Low-pH viral inactivation has
three factors, 10 terms and 18 runs. Virus filtration has two factors, 6 terms and 12
runs.
Predicted R² comes from predicting each run from a fit that leaves that run out. The
weight a run has in its own fitted value is its leverage, and the average leverage equals
the number of terms divided by the number of runs. In these designs it lies between
0.50 and 0.56. A run with that much weight in its own fit is predicted poorly once it
is left out. Predicted R² therefore falls well below R², even where the lack-of-fit test
shows no systematic error.
The simulation of Section 6 quantifies this for the models that the rule augments.
Simulated from what the planned runs show, the planned design alone gives the full
quadratic model an assurance of meeting §7.2 of 0.25 for high mannose, 0.44 for
acidic variants and 0.49 for host cell protein at anion exchange. A failure was there-
fore the more likely outcome for each of these models before any run was made.
Attributes with no effect within the range studied fail for a second reason. For such
an attribute the full quadratic model fits only random variation. Each of its terms
takes up part of that variation, so R² stays between 0.27 and 0.57 while predicted R²
is below zero. More runs narrow the gap only slowly, because the model has nothing
to estimate but noise. The fragment models of Protein A and cation exchange show
the same behaviour with a small effect. Their assurance stays at or below 0.005 at
every size computed, up to the size of the planned design (Table 6.1).
The contributing cause is that no plan checked the size of its design against the model
it was to fit. PCMP-001 Version 1.0 left the model-adequacy threshold to the individual
study, and the Version 1.0 plans set none. The threshold of PCMP-001 Version 2.0
came later. The last planned response-surface study ended on 2026-05-15, before
INV-001 was opened.
The root cause was recorded on 2026-09-15. The corrective action for it is model
selection for every response-surface output, adopted on the same date as corrective
action CA3 (Section 8). The rule of Section 5 and the augmentations it gave had been
decided before that date, under the model of the plan.

5 Remedy and decision rule

PCMP-001 §7.2 allows two remedies for a model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the
limit on overfitting. The model may be refitted with a justified change of terms. Where
the design is too small for the model a decision needs, the design may instead be
augmented with further runs as a second block. A model that is neither refitted nor
augmented is not used to set a range. §7.2 leaves it to this investigation to state when
a design is augmented and how the number of runs is calculated. The A-Mab case
study names the same remedy for a design that cannot resolve the terms a decision
needs, which is to augment it (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
The decision was to calculate the further runs needed to resolve the effects where a
model bears on the drug substance specification, and to reduce the model in every
other case. The rule below puts this into effect. It was approved on 2026-09-02,
before any new run was made, and it was applied to every failing model in the same
way:

  1. A failing model of an attribute of the drug substance specification, on which the
     refit of the planned runs keeps an effect, is resolved by further runs. The number
     of runs is the smallest that gives an assurance of at least 0.90 that the planned
     model, the full quadratic with a block term, meets §7.2 on all runs (Section 6).
  2. Viral clearance is not a drug substance test. It is a process requirement, shown
     by spiking studies against a cumulative claim (International Council for Harmon-
     isation 2023). Its models are refitted, and no augmentation spikes virus.
  3. No augmentation is larger than the planned design. Where the assurance target
     is not reached within that size, the model is refitted.
  4. Every other failing model is refitted.
  5. A response with no adequate model, full or refitted, is judged on its measured
     results. No model sets a range for it.

The rule spends runs where they can change a decision about the drug substance.
For an attribute of the specification with an effect, a refit on a design that is too small
can drop a real interaction or curvature, and the ranges of the step would then rest
on a model that misses it. Further runs let the full model estimate every term. For an
attribute with no effect, the refit to the mean describes the result fully, and further
runs would not change it. The cap on the size of an augmentation keeps a remedy
from becoming a new study.
The refit removes terms by hierarchical backward elimination at α = 0.05, and it is
corrective action CA2. At each step, the least significant term that no remaining term
contains is removed. A main effect therefore stays while an interaction or a square
of it remains. Elimination stops when every removable term is significant. In an
augmented design the block term belongs to the design and is never removed. The




                                           14
refitted model is then judged against §7.2 in the same way as the full model. Model
selection (corrective action CA3) later replaced this analysis for every output, the
outputs that CA2 had refitted included (Section 8). The refits remain the record of
how the rule was applied, and the sample-size calculation rests on them.
Where neither the full nor the refitted model meets §7.2, the response is judged on
its measured results, as PCMP-001 §7.2 asks. Every measured result of the designs
is compared with the criterion at manufacturing scale. The designs span the charac-
terized range, so a response whose measured results all meet the criterion meets it
over the NOR. No model draws a boundary for such a response.

6 Sample-size calculation

6.1 Method

The number of further runs is calculated by simulation, separately for each failing
model with an effect. The simulation asks how likely the planned model is to meet
§7.2 once the design has been augmented by a given number of runs. The planned
model is the full quadratic with a block term. The assurance concerns this model
because it was the model the plans prescribed when the runs were sized, before
model selection was adopted. The simulation is built from what the planned runs
show, and from nothing else. The calculation has six steps:

  1. The refit of the planned runs by backward elimination (Section 5) is taken as the
     true model, with its fitted coefficients. Its residual standard deviation is taken
     as the variation between runs. The refit is used because it keeps only the terms
     that the planned runs support. The full model would carry its fitted noise into
     every simulated data set.
  2. For a candidate size, the D-optimal augmentation of that many runs is added to
     the planned runs as a second block (Section 6.2).
  3. Responses are simulated at the planned and the augmentation runs, as the true
     model plus normal error with the residual standard deviation. For each size,
     4,000 data sets are simulated.
  4. The planned model, the full quadratic with a block term, is fitted to each simu-
     lated data set. It is judged against the lack-of-fit test at α = 0.05 and the limit
     of 0.20 on R² minus predicted R². Pure error comes from the replicated centre
     points of the planned design, because no augmentation repeats a point within
     its own block.
  5. The assurance is the share of simulated data sets in which the planned model
     meets both criteria.
  6. Sizes are evaluated in steps of 2 runs, up to the size of the planned design. The
     number of runs needed is the smallest size with an assurance of at least 0.90.

The simulation also gives the power of each effect of the refit. The power is the share
of simulated data sets in which the term is significant at α = 0.05 in the fitted planned
model. A step’s augmentation has the largest number of runs that any of its models
needs, so a model of the same step that needs fewer runs has an assurance above the
target at that size.
The calculation assumes that the refit of the planned runs is the true model, that the
error is normal, and that the two blocks do not differ. A real difference between the
blocks is taken up by the block term of the fitted model. An effect that the planned
runs did not detect is not in the true model, so the calculation does not size a design



                                           18
to find it. Each curve uses its own random stream of the seeded process model, so
the calculation gives the same result each time it is run.



6.2 D-optimal augmentation

Each augmentation is drawn from the levels of the face-centred design, so every factor
takes its low, centre or high level. For four factors this gives 81 candidate points. The
runs are chosen to maximise the determinant of the information matrix of the planned
model, the full quadratic with a block term, with the planned runs held fixed in the
first block. The selection adds, one run at a time, the candidate that increases the
determinant most. It then exchanges chosen runs with candidates until no exchange
increases the determinant further, which is a Fedorov exchange (Fedorov 1972). Ties
go to the first candidate in a fixed order, so the design needs no random number.
The face-centred design holds corner, axial and centre points only. It has no point
at which exactly one factor sits at its centre level. Its squared terms are therefore
strongly correlated with each other, because every corner run sets all of them to one.
Both D-optimal augmentations consist mostly of points at the middle of an edge of the
design cube, with one factor at its centre level and the others at a limit (Section 7).
These points separate the squared terms from each other.

8 Model selection (corrective action CA3)

8.1 Procedure

Model selection was adopted as corrective action CA3 on 2026-09-15, before INV-001
closed on 2026-09-18. It replaced the analysis of every output, the refits of corrective
action CA2 included. Each report with a response-surface design applies it to every
output of the design, on all runs in which the output was measured. Where a design
was augmented, the planned runs form the first block and the augmentation runs the
second. The verification of the platform worst cases behind the intermediate accep-
tance criteria applies the same procedure (Section 8.2). PCMP-001 is not changed.
Each report records the model selection as a discrepancy from its plan (Section 9).
The procedure has four steps:

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile likelihood of the power λ is calculated for the
     full quadratic model of the output, with the block term where the runs form two
     blocks (Box and Cox 1964). The output is modelled on the log scale (λ = 0) where
     the 95 % profile-likelihood interval of λ contains 0 and excludes 1. Otherwise
     it is modelled on the raw scale (λ = 1). Only an output whose results are all
     positive can take the log scale.
  2. The search. Every submodel of the full quadratic model that respects strong
     hierarchy is fitted on that scale. Strong hierarchy allows an interaction only
     together with both of its main effects, and a squared term only together with
     its main effect. The block term is kept in every candidate. A design with four
     factors has 1,337 candidate models, the model of the mean included, a design
     with three factors 95 and a design with two factors 13. The candidate with the
     lowest AICc is selected (Hurvich and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002).
     The candidates are listed in a fixed order, from the fewest main effects upward,
     and a tie goes to the first. An output that the data give without error, with
     replicates that agree exactly at the precision the method reports, is fitted exactly
     by more than one candidate. AICc is not defined for an exact fit, so the smallest
     candidate that fits exactly is selected instead. In this campaign the rule applies
     to acidic variants at low-pH viral inactivation.
  3. The check. The selected model is judged against PCMP-001 §7.2 on its own scale,
     by the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting. A selected model with terms
     must also have a significant model F-test at α = 0.05. Only the selected model
     is judged. If it fails, no other candidate takes its place.
  4. The offset. The scale-down offset of an output is taken on the scale of its model,
     with its 90 % confidence interval. For a raw-scale output it is the mean of the
     scale-down set-point runs minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches,
     and it is subtracted from a scale-down prediction. For a log-scale output it is the




                                          25
     same difference of the mean logarithms, reported as a ratio of geometric means,
     and a scale-down prediction is divided by it.

The check has three outcomes. A selected model with terms that passes is the pre-
dictive model of the output, and it sets the ranges of its step. A selected model with
no term passes when it meets the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting. It says
that the output does not depend on the parameters within the range studied. An out-
put whose selected model fails has no adequate model. It is judged on its measured
results, as Section 5 describes, and it sets no range.
The log scale matters for an output such as host cell protein. A process parameter
changes the level of host cell protein by a factor, not by a fixed amount, and the
spread between runs grows with the level. On the log scale these effects add, and
the spread is the same at every level, which is what a least-squares fit assumes. The
Box-Cox profile supports the log scale for host cell protein at every step that has a
criterion for it. A prediction interval calculated on the log scale and transformed back
keeps its coverage, because the transformation preserves order. In natural units the
interval is asymmetric, wider above the prediction than below it. A back-transformed
mean is the median of the output, not its mean. In the reports, 7 of the 22 outputs
with a criterion are modelled on the log scale. They are high mannose, acidic variants
and aggregates at the production bioreactor, host cell protein in the Protein A pool,
aggregates and host cell protein at cation exchange, and host cell protein at anion
exchange.
The search is exhaustive because a stepwise search changes one term at a time and
stops when no single change improves the criterion. It can therefore stop at a model
whose AICc is higher than that of the best candidate. The backward elimination of
corrective action CA2 was such a search, on p-values and on the raw scale only. AICc
is used because these designs are small. It is Akaike’s information criterion with a
correction for small samples, in which the residual variance counts as a parameter
(Hurvich and Tsai 1989). The correction grows as the number of terms approaches
the number of runs. The planned designs hold between 12 and 28 runs for up to 15
terms of the full quadratic model.



8.2 Verification of the platform worst cases

PCMP-001 Version 2.0 derives the intermediate acceptance criteria a priori, from the
platform worst case of each downstream step. The verification of these worst cases
uses the same model selection, on the planned runs of each downstream step. For
each worst case it predicts the clearance fold or the increase of the downstream step
at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the NOR as characterized.
The worst prediction is taken at its one-sided 95 % confidence bound. On a log-scale
model the bound is calculated on the log scale and transformed back. A bound worse
than the platform value replaces it, and the criteria upstream of the step tighten. A
bound better than the platform value replaces it only where the selected model meets
§7.2, and the criteria upstream widen. Section 9.1 gives what this changed.

9.2 Conclusions over the NOR

The conclusion over the NOR of an output does not depend on the choice between the
full quadratic model of the planned runs and the model that the report uses. For every
output with a criterion the two give the same conclusion, at the NOR as characterized
and at the NOR in force. Two of these conclusions are adverse on both models, and
both rested on full quadratic models that fail §7.2:

  1. XMuLV clearance at low-pH viral inactivation is judged on the mean LRF at the
     least favourable combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high, because viral
     clearance is validated at worst-case conditions (PCMP-001 §6.4). The selected
     model has the main effects of inactivation pH, hold time and temperature only.
     On it, the lowest mean LRF within the NOR as characterized is 6.16, at inactiva-
     tion pH 3.6, hold time 60 min and temperature 19 °C, and the step claim of 6.6 is
     not met. The full quadratic model gives a lowest mean of 6.43 and the same con-
     clusion. PCR-006 moves the NOR of inactivation pH, hold time and temperature,
     and it states the conclusion over the NOR in force.
  2. Acidic variants at the production bioreactor are judged on the 95 % prediction
     interval of a single batch at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR
     high. On the selected model of all runs, the lower prediction limit at culture pH
     6.95, culture temperature 34.5 °C, culture duration 18 days and dissolved CO2
     (pCO2) 130 mmHg is 16.2 % at manufacturing scale, below the lower limit of
     18 %. The predicted median at the same settings is 18.4 %, within the criterion.
     The full quadratic model of the planned runs does not meet the criterion either.
     PCR-003 states how this result is handled.

Fragments at low-pH viral inactivation, fragments at cation exchange and XMuLV
clearance at virus filtration have no adequate model and are judged on their measured
results. Every measured result meets its criterion.

12 Conclusion and closure

The investigation confirmed the classification as minor. No range was set from a
failing model at Version 2.0, and there is no product impact.
The root cause is that the plans prescribed the full quadratic model as the predictive
model, with no model selection, on designs that are small for it. The Version 1.0 plans
set no adequacy criterion against which the size of a design could have been checked.
A remedy was assigned to every failing model by a rule approved before any new run,
and both augmentations were run by 2026-09-24. Model selection then replaced the
refits for every output. Once selected, the models that the augmentations were made
for meet §7.2 on the planned runs alone, and the augmentations sharpened their
estimates. After selection 3 outputs have no adequate model, and every measured
result of these outputs meets its criterion.
The intermediate acceptance criteria in force were fixed at the closure of INV-001
on the selected models. On the full quadratic models 4 criteria would have different
values. The augmentation runs moved no criterion.
At closure the augmentation runs of corrective action CA1 were complete, and correc-
tive actions CA2 and CA3 were complete. The preventive actions were open. INV-002
was closed on 2026-09-28.
