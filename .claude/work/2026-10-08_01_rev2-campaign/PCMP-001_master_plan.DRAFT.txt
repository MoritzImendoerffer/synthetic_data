1 Purpose and scope

This plan governs the Stage 1 process characterization of the A-Mab drug substance
process. It sits under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-001, which moves the process
from the development site in Cambridge, MA, to the commercial drug substance facil-
ity in Grafton, WI. The characterization studies provide the evidence for the operating
ranges and the control strategy that are transferred.
The plan covers the 8 steps of the drug substance train listed in Table 1.1, from the
production bioreactor (Step 3) to ultrafiltration / diafiltration (Step 10). The seed
expansion steps and the drug product process are outside its scope.

This plan sets out the critical quality attribute (CQA) framework (Section 3), the risk
basis (Section 4), the common approach to scale-down models (Section 5) and to
statistics (Section 6), and the framework of acceptance criteria (Section 7) for the
whole train. Each Process Characterization Plan (PCP-003 to PCP-010) applies this
framework to one step. It adds what is specific to that step, such as the scale-down
model, the factors and their ranges, and the sampling plan. Where a step plan departs
from this plan, it should state the departure and the reason for it.
The documents of the package build on each other in sequence. The risk assessment
RA-001 sets the scope of each study. The Process Characterization Plan of the step

1.1 Changes in this revision

Version 1.0 of this plan was approved on 2025-08-04, before the first characterization
study started on 2025-08-27. Version 2.0 replaces it and is issued under INV-001. QA
opened the investigation on 2026-08-03 after its review of PCMR-001 Version 1.0 for
BLA readiness, and closed it on 2026-09-18 with the classification major. INV-001
found that the criteria used to judge each step in the proven acceptable range (PAR)
analysis were not in any approved protocol and had been set after the data were seen
(INV-001 finding 1). Each criterion had been carried back from the drug substance
limit through the nominal clearance of this campaign and then divided by a margin
that differed by step (INV-001 finding 2). Version 1.0 of this plan defined no criteria
for step outputs.
This revision makes the following changes.

  1. It defines intermediate acceptance criteria (IACs) for the step outputs on which
     a drug substance criterion depends (Section 7.3). The rule that derives them
     uses no data from the studies it judges. They replace the Version 1.0 step crite-
     ria described above (INV-001 findings 1 and 2). They also replace the bioreactor
     limits for the glycans and acidic variants, which were computed from the capa-
     bility data they were then used to judge (INV-001 finding 3).
  2. It defines how each report verifies the platform worst cases that the criteria rely
     on, and when that verification may change a criterion (Section 7.5). Verification
     tightens a criterion where a step does worse within its NOR than the platform
     worst case. It widens a criterion only where the 95 % confidence bound of the
     A-Mab worst case is better than the platform value and the model behind it is
     adequate. In both directions the bound, not the point prediction, replaces the
     platform value.
  3. It qualifies each scale-down model by its offset from manufacturing scale, not by
     a test of equivalence, and states every judgement against a criterion at manu-
     facturing scale (Section 7.1).
  4. It sets non-reduced CE-SDS (nrCE-SDS) as the method for fragments at every
     step, following the decision of 2026-03-20 to change the fragment method to
     nrCE-SDS (AMD-001, Section 7.8). This answers INV-001 finding 6, that size
     exclusion chromatography (SEC) reads fragments high in samples that still carry
     host cell protein.
  5. It assesses capability for acidic variants against both ends of the criterion (Sec-
     tion 6.5). Version 1.0 assessed it against the upper end only (INV-001 finding
     4).

2 Stage 1 characterization strategy

Process validation follows a lifecycle, and its first stage is process design (U.S. Food
and Drug Administration 2011). In Stage 1 the commercial process is defined from
the knowledge gained in development, and the studies that support it are documented.
This campaign follows the enhanced approach of ICH Q8 and ICH Q11 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012). Prior knowledge and risk assessment identify
which parameters may affect which attributes. Characterization studies then mea-
sure those effects and bound them. The result is a set of normal operating ranges
(NORs), PARs and parameter classes, from which the control strategy is built (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009).
A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 antibody developed on the Novacyte platform. The down-
stream train of Protein A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion
exchange, virus filtration and UF/DF was used for three earlier antibodies, X-Mab, Y-
Mab and Z-Mab. Their history establishes the mechanism of each step and the range
of performance to expect from it. Characterization of A-Mab therefore confirms and
bounds known behaviour. The same history supplies the worst case of each down-
stream step, on which the intermediate acceptance criteria are built (Section 7.3).
Criticality is treated as a continuum, as described in ICH Q8(R2) (International Coun-
cil for Harmonisation 2009). RA-001 ranks each parameter and assigns it to multivari-
ate study, to univariate study or to no new study (Section 4). Parameters assigned
to multivariate study are characterized by designed experiments in a qualified scale-
down model. Such designs are planned for 6 steps, from the production bioreactor
to virus filtration. The harvest (Step 4) and UF/DF (Step 10) steps are characterized
by univariate and qualitative studies only, because RA-001 assigned none of their
parameters to multivariate study.
For 32 of the 37 parameters the characterized range is wider than the NOR at both
ends (Table 4.2). For 4 parameters (post-clarification turbidity, filtration volume
(load), number of diavolumes and transmembrane pressure) the low end of the charac-
terized range equals the low end of the NOR, and only the high end extends beyond it.
For A-Mab concentration the NOR equals the characterized range. Where the range
is wider, it shows how the process behaves near and beyond the edges of routine
operation. It also gives the knowledge space within which a PAR can be claimed. A
PAR is claimed only where the data support it, and never beyond the range studied.
Viral safety rests on modular and orthogonal clearance across low-pH inactivation,
anion exchange and virus filtration (International Council for Harmonisation 2023).
Each of these steps is studied in its own spiking model, and each step plan claims
only the log reduction of its own step. The cumulative claim is consolidated in PCMR-
001.

3 Critical quality attribute framework

The CQAs and their drug substance acceptance criteria are taken from the A-Mab
case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Several of the criteria were set there
from clinical experience with X-Mab, the closest platform antibody. They are held in
the drug substance CQA register, and Table 3.1 lists all 11 attributes. Criticality was
assigned with Tool #1 of the case study, as the product of impact and uncertainty. The
levels run from very low (VL) to very high (VH), and the Tool #1 score stands beside
each level. For every attribute except viral clearance, a higher score gives an equal
or higher level. The two viral clearance attributes were assigned very high criticality
by rule, because a viral contamination would directly harm the patient, whatever the
uncertainty term of the score gives.
The last column of Table 3.1 names the step at which each attribute is formed or set.
Most attributes are formed in the production bioreactor. Afucosylation, galactosy-
lation and high mannose are formed there, and no downstream step changes them.
Protein A binds the Fc region at the interface of the CH2 and CH3 domains, so it
captures every glycan variant alike. The two ion exchange steps do not separate gly-
can variants under platform conditions (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Leached
Protein A enters the process at capture. Fragments are formed in culture and dur-
ing the harvest hold, by proteases released from lysed cells that clip the antibody.
The register therefore assigns them to harvest. No criterion for fragments is set at
harvest, and they are judged at the downstream steps (Table 7.1). They are not an
attribute of the production bioreactor designs (Table 7.3).
The two viral clearance attributes are cumulative. The register assigns each to one
step, but each requirement is met by the sum of the claims of several steps (Sec-
tion 7.4).
The attributes of very low criticality (acidic charge variants (deamidation) and resid-
ual DNA) remain in the register, and their criteria apply.
The output of a step that removes an impurity, or that adds to a variant, is judged
against an intermediate acceptance criterion derived from the drug substance crite-
rion (Section 7.3).

4 Risk-based prioritization

RA-001 assessed every process parameter of the train before characterization began.
Each parameter was ranked on its potential impact on the CQAs and on process per-
formance, and on its potential to interact with other parameters. Parameters ranked
high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to multivariate study. Parameters
ranked lower, whose range could be supported one factor at a time, were assigned
to univariate study. RA-001 gives the scores and the rationale for each assignment,
and they are not repeated here.
Table 4.1 shows the result across the train. Of the 37 parameters, 22 are studied in
multivariate designs and 15 one factor at a time. All 6 parameters of harvest (Step 4)
and UF/DF (Step 10) are studied univariately.
Table 4.2 lists every parameter with its set-point, NOR and characterized range. The
NOR shown is the NOR in force. At Version 2.0 PCR-005 narrowed the NOR of protein
load at Protein A and PCR-006 narrowed the NOR of hold time and temperature at low-
pH viral inactivation, and the table footnotes the narrowed rows. The class column
gives the classification assigned in each PCR-00N under the parameter classification
procedure SOP-4001, by the rules in Section 7.7.
The range of a parameter studied univariately is supported by its own data, and its
class is decided by the same rules as for a parameter studied in a design.

5 Scale-down model strategy

Every characterization study is run in a scale-down model. The model is qualified
under the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001 before its first characterization run.
It is designed on the scaling principles of its unit operation. Parameters that do not
depend on scale are held at their commercial values. Parameters that depend on scale
are set so that the model matches the performance of the manufacturing scale.
For the chromatography steps the model keeps the bed height and the residence time
of the manufacturing column. Load, wash and elution volumes are normalized to col-
umn volumes, and column efficiency is checked by plate count and peak asymmetry.
For the production bioreactor the model is a stirred tank with equivalent mixing, aer-
ation and process control. In each study, the parameters not under study are held
at their commercial set-points. The designs vary culture pH, culture temperature,
dissolved CO2 (pCO2), osmolality and culture duration, and univariate studies vary
dissolved oxygen, initial viable cell conc., basal medium concentration and nutrient
feed-1 volume. Agitation and gas flow are set to match the oxygen transfer and car-
bon dioxide stripping of the commercial vessel. For virus filtration the model uses the
same filter type as manufacturing, and load is scaled by membrane area. For UF/DF,
membrane loading and cross flow are scaled by membrane area.

6 Common statistical approach

6.1 Designs

Each step with a multivariate scope is studied in two stages, under the design of
experiments procedure SOP-1002. A screening design comes first. It is a two-level
factorial design, full or fractional as the number of factors requires, with replicated
centre points. A response-surface design follows. It is a central composite design
over the factors that screening showed, or RA-001 expected, to affect a CQA with a
criterion at the step. It also has replicated centre points. Table 6.1 gives the structure
of each design. Every design has at least 3 centre points. The centre points estimate
pure error and allow a test for curvature and for lack of fit.
Runs are executed in random order within each design. Where a run is lost or inval-
idated, the PCR-00N should report it and state whether the design was repaired or
re-executed.



6.2 Models, and what each model is for

The screening data are fitted with a model of main effects and two-factor interactions
in coded units. The response-surface data are fitted with a full quadratic model. Ef-
fects are reported as the change in the response over the coded range of the factor,
and a model term is significant when its p-value is below α = 0.05. This is the con-
ventional level. Its consequence is that about one term in twenty with no real effect
is called significant by chance. An attribute is called affected by a parameter when a
term that contains the parameter is significant at that level.
The screening model identifies effects. Its centre points detect curvature but cannot
attribute it to a factor, and the model still interpolates linearly between the corners of
the design. In the production bioreactor and small-virus retentive filtration screening
designs the interaction model also leaves only 1 degree of freedom for lack of fit
(Table 6.1), so its fit statistics overstate how well it fits. The response-surface model
is the predictive model. Every prediction, every PAR and every design space in the
package comes from it. No report should claim prediction from a screening model.
A response-surface model predicts the mean response over the range studied. At
the edge of an acceptable region defined on mean predictions, a single batch meets
its criterion about half the time if variability is symmetrical. For this reason a quality
attribute is judged on a single batch, not on the mean prediction, in two analyses. The
NOR-propagated proven acceptable range (Section 6.4) is computed by Monte Carlo,
and its interval includes the model residual, the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients

6.3 Model adequacy

Each fitted model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R², the model F-
test, and the lack-of-fit test against pure error from the centre points. Residuals are
inspected against predicted values and on a normal probability plot. A model is used
for prediction only if it meets the adequacy criteria in Section 7.2.



6.4 Proven acceptable ranges

A PAR is computed for each CQA that has a criterion at the step and for each factor
of the response-surface model.

  1. The set-point analysis is a univariate proven acceptable range with the other pa-
     rameters at their set-points. It scans the factor across its characterized range.




                                              20
     The PAR is the contiguous range around the set-point over which the mean pre-
     diction meets the criterion.
  2. The NOR-propagated analysis is the same range with the other parameters vary-
     ing within their NORs. It scans the factor across 81 grid points. The PAR is the
     contiguous range around the set-point over which the predicted result meets the
     criterion.

What the NOR-propagated analysis compares with the criterion depends on the kind
of attribute.

  • For a quality attribute it compares the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch,
    because a single batch is what must meet the criterion. At each grid point the
    analysis draws 2,000 operating points for the other factors. Each factor is drawn
    from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a standard deviation of
    one sixth of the width of its NOR, and clipped to the characterized range. This
    assumes that the NOR spans ±3 standard deviations of the routine variation
    of the parameter. The interval includes the model residual and the uncertainty
    of the fitted coefficients. It runs from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile of the
    simulated batches. For an upper limit the 97.5th percentile must not exceed
    the limit, for a lower limit the 2.5th percentile must not fall below it, and for a
    two-sided criterion both apply.
  • For viral clearance it compares the worst case within the NOR. At each grid point
    the other factors are set to every combination of their NOR low, set-point and
    NOR high. The lowest mean predicted log reduction among these combinations
    must meet the claim of the step. Viral clearance is validated at worst-case con-
    ditions, so a claim that held only on average over the NOR would not support a
    validated log reduction.

The NOR-propagated PAR is the one that supports a claim. It is in general narrower
than the set-point PAR, because the range must hold while the rest of the operating
point moves within its NOR.



6.5 Capability, and the integrated model

Capability at commercial scale is estimated from an integrated model of the train. The
model runs 2,000 simulated batches through every step in sequence. Each process
parameter of each batch is drawn from the same normal distribution as in the NOR-
propagated analysis of a quality attribute (Section 6.4). The drug substance attributes
of each batch are recorded.
Capability is reported as Cpk against the drug substance criterion. For a criterion
with one limit, Cpk is computed against that limit. For a two-sided criterion it is the
lower of the two one-sided indices. Acidic variants have a two-sided criterion (18 to
40 %), and their capability is assessed against both ends. The criteria for capability
are in Section 7.9.
The same simulation gives the probability of an out-of-specification drug substance for
each attribute. Two figures are reported, because a few thousand simulated batches



                                          21
cannot resolve a rare event. These are the count of simulated batches outside the
criterion, and the tail probability of a normal distribution with the simulated mean and
standard deviation, one- or two-sided as the criterion is. The intermediate acceptance
criteria judge each step separately, and the integrated model shows whether the steps
together deliver the drug substance.

7.1 Scale-down model qualification

A scale-down model is qualified for each output separately. It is qualified by the offset
between the scales, not by a test of equivalence. A model may differ in mean from
manufacturing scale, provided the difference is known precisely enough. The rule
has four parts.

  1. The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the
     mean of the manufacturing-scale batches. It is reported with its two-sided 90
     % confidence interval. Each end of that interval is a one-sided 95 % confidence
     bound, which is the level used to compare two means at 5 % on each side.
  2. Where the qualification compared an output at both scales, every judgement of
     a scale-down result for that output against a criterion first subtracts its offset.
     Ranges and conclusions are therefore stated at manufacturing scale.
  3. The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion
     over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its confidence interval.
     The conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination of
     NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the response-surface factors.
  4. An output with no criterion at the step, such as step yield, has its offset recorded
     only. No conclusion depends on it.

Where the cause of an offset is known, the report states it. A model whose conclusion
for an output changes between the two ends of the offset interval is not used for that
output. The qualification studies do not compare fragments or viral clearance at both
scales. Their offset is unknown, not zero. These outputs are judged as the model
predicts them because the qualification did not compare them, and their representa-
tiveness rests on what each step plan states, which is the mechanism of the step for
fragments and the spiking model for viral clearance.
Each PCP-00N names the outputs, the number of runs at each scale and the
manufacturing-scale data set before its first characterization run. Each PCR-00N



                                           23
tabulates the offsets of its step, and PCMR-001 summarises them for the whole
train.



7.2 Model adequacy

A response-surface model is adequate for prediction when the following hold.

  1. The model F-test is significant at the level set for model terms, α = 0.05 (Sec-
     tion 6).
  2. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. This is a limit on overfitting,
     because a model that fits its own runs much better than it predicts left-out runs
     has more terms than the data support.
  4. The residuals show no pattern against predicted values or run order, and no
     single run dominates the fit.

A model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the limit on overfitting is either refitted with
a justified change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a range.
Where no adequate model results, the PCR-00N should report the response descrip-
tively, against the criterion, over the runs of the design.



7.3 Intermediate acceptance criteria

A drug substance criterion does not say what the output of an upstream step must
meet. Protein A, for example, leaves host cell protein in its pool that cation exchange
and anion exchange will remove. The Protein A pool is expected to carry far more host
cell protein than the drug substance limit of 100 ng/mg, so that limit cannot judge it.
Each step output is therefore judged against an intermediate acceptance criterion.
The rule is the following. Each drug substance criterion is carried backward through
the train, step by step, using the worst case that each downstream step showed on the
platform. For a step that removes an attribute, the worst case is the lowest clearance
fold observed for X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab. For a step that adds to an attribute, it is
the largest increase observed. No margin is applied. An attribute that no downstream
step changes keeps its drug substance criterion. This is the case for afucosylation,
galactosylation and high mannose.
For host cell protein the rule gives the Protein A pool an a priori criterion of 16,000
ng/mg. The drug substance limit of 100 ng/mg is carried back through the platform
worst case of anion exchange (4-fold) and then of cation exchange (40-fold). The
cation exchange pool is judged against the limit carried back through anion exchange
alone, 400 ng/mg a priori. For A-Mab, the 95 % confidence bounds of the host cell pro-
tein clearance were 40.9-fold for cation exchange and 4.84-fold for anion exchange.
Both are better than the platform worst cases. The cation exchange bound replaced
its platform value (Section 7.5). The anion exchange host cell protein model does not




                                            24
meet the adequacy criteria of Section 7.2, so its platform value stands. The Protein A
pool criterion is therefore 16,379 ng/mg, and the cation exchange pool criterion stays
at 400 ng/mg.

7.5 Verification of the platform worst cases

Each intermediate acceptance criterion assumes that every downstream step per-
forms at least as well as its platform worst case. That is, the step clears at least
the lowest fold, or adds no more than the largest increase, seen for X-Mab, Y-Mab
and Z-Mab. Each report checks the platform worst cases of its own step. The check
uses the response-surface model of the step. The model gives the worst clearance
fold, or the largest increase, that it predicts within the NOR, and the one-sided 95
% confidence bound of that worst case, on its unfavourable side. An assumption is
confirmed when the predicted worst case is no worse than the platform worst case.
The values observed at the edges of the characterized range are reported beside it
for information.
Verification may change a criterion in either direction, by the following rule.

  1. Where the A-Mab worst case predicted within the NOR is worse than the plat-
     form worst case, its 95 % confidence bound replaces the platform value. Every
     criterion upstream of that step is recalculated with it and becomes stricter.
  2. Where the 95 % confidence bound of the A-Mab worst case within the NOR is bet-
     ter than the platform value, and the response-surface model behind it meets the
     adequacy criteria of Section 7.2, the bound replaces the platform value. Every
     criterion upstream of that step is recalculated with it and becomes wider.
  3. Otherwise the platform value stands.

In both directions the bound, on its unfavourable side, replaces the platform value,
and the point prediction never does.
A report that relies on a widened criterion gives the argument for it, with the bound,
the runs behind it, and the reason why the performance of the downstream step for
A-Mab is known well enough to replace the platform value. The rule was written into




                                          27
this plan before any Version 2.0 step report used it. This reissue of the plan also
records what the rule gave, in Section 7.3 and below.
The criteria assume 9 platform worst cases. Of these, 7 are responses of the response-
surface designs of low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange and anion exchange, and
they can be checked. The other 2 are the leached Protein A clearance of cation ex-
change and of anion exchange. Leached Protein A is not a response of those designs,
so the platform values stand. The 95 % confidence bound was better than the plat-
form worst case for the aggregate increase of low-pH viral inactivation, the acidic
variants increase of low-pH viral inactivation and the host cell protein clearance of
cation exchange, and these bounds replaced the platform values. For the fragments
increase of low-pH viral inactivation, the fragments clearance of cation exchange and
the host cell protein clearance of anion exchange the bound was also better, but the
model does not meet the adequacy criteria, so the platform values stand. Verification
found that the aggregate clearance of cation exchange within its NOR is lower than
the platform worst case. That tightened the aggregate criteria upstream of cation
exchange (Section 7.3). The cation exchange report PCR-007 gives the values and
their analysis.



7.6 Proven acceptable ranges

A range of a parameter is proven acceptable for an attribute when the NOR-
propagated analysis (Section 6.4) meets the criterion of that attribute across it. The
criterion is the IAC where Table 7.1 defines one, and the drug substance criterion
otherwise. A parameter that affects several attributes has a PAR for each, and the
range carried into the control strategy is their intersection. Where the output does
not meet its criterion even at the set-point, the PCR-00N reports that no PAR exists
for the attribute and states the consequence for the NOR.
A PAR that reaches the edge of the characterized range is reported as reaching it,
and no claim is made beyond it.



7.7 Parameter classification

Parameters are classified under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001 by
the decision logic of the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).

  1. A critical process parameter (CPP) has an effect on a CQA that is significant and
     practically relevant within the characterized range, and a high risk of falling
     outside its acceptable range in operation.
  2. A well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP) has the same kind of ef-
     fect on a CQA but a low risk of falling outside its acceptable range, because it is
     readily controlled and its PAR is wide against its NOR.
  3. A key process parameter (KPP) affects process performance, such as yield or
     concentration, and has no effect on a CQA within its range.




                                          28
  4. A general process parameter (GPP) has no relevant effect on a CQA or on process
     performance within its range.

A significant effect that is too small to move the attribute appreciably within the PAR
does not by itself make a parameter critical. The PCR-00N should state the size of
the effect beside its significance and give the reason for each class. The classification
at this revision counts 1 CPP, 20 WC-CPP, 10 KPP and 6 GPP, and is consolidated in
PCMR-001.



7.8 Method for fragments

From 2026-03-20, fragments are measured by nrCE-SDS (AMV-3020, under SOP-
3015) in every study at every step. This follows the decision to change the fragment
method to nrCE-SDS (AMD-001). One method across the train is needed because
fragment results are compared across steps, and a comparison between two meth-
ods would mix method bias with step performance. Designs executed before that
date measured fragments by SEC (Table 7.3). The screening designs of Protein A,
low-pH viral inactivation and cation exchange and the invalidated first execution of
the anion exchange design have SEC results only. The response-surface designs of
Protein A and low-pH viral inactivation were measured by SEC, and their retained
samples were later re-tested by nrCE-SDS. The cation exchange response-surface de-
sign ran across the decision date and measured fragments by both methods on every
run.
The decision followed the comparability study S-CEX-FRC (2026-03-02 to 2026-03-
13), in which SEC (AMV-3011) and nrCE-SDS were run on the same samples. SEC
read fragments high wherever the sample still carried host cell protein, because the
part of the host cell protein that elutes in the low molecular weight window of SEC is
counted as fragment. The excess can never exceed the host cell protein present. It
was a few tenths of a percent in the Protein A and viral inactivation pools and close to
zero from the cation exchange pool onward. From the cation exchange pool onward
the two methods agreed (INV-001 finding 6). SEC remains the method for aggregate
(HMW), where no such interference applies.
Table 7.3 shows which method measured fragments in which study. Retained
response-surface samples of Protein A and low-pH viral inactivation were re-tested
by nrCE-SDS between 2026-04-06 and 2026-04-17. Screening samples were not
retained. Their SEC fragment results are kept in the record but are not used for

7.9 Capability and the probability of an out-of-specification
    drug substance

Capability is estimated as in Section 6.5 and judged for each CQA in PCMR-001
against two values.

  1. A Cpk of at least 1.33 is expected of a CQA in routine manufacture. At that value
     the nearest limit lies 3.99 standard deviations from the process mean, and a
     normal process would put about 33 batches per million beyond that limit.
  2. A CQA with a Cpk below 1 needs an action in the control strategy. At that value
     the process spread, at three standard deviations on the limiting side, just reaches
     the criterion. The action may be a tighter NOR, an added in-process control or
     added testing. PCMR-001 should name the action.

A Cpk between the two values is acceptable. PCMR-001 should then state the side
of the criterion that limits it and the parameters that drive it. For the probability of
an out-of-specification drug substance, PCMR-001 should report the count of simu-
lated batches outside each criterion and the tail probability, and should discuss every
attribute with a simulated batch outside its criterion.
