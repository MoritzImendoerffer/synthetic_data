1 Purpose and scope

This plan describes the Stage 1 characterization of the production bioreactor, Step 3
of the A-Mab drug substance process (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). The
production bioreactor is a fed-batch culture with a working volume of 15,000 L at
commercial scale. The step forms the glycan, charge variant and aggregate quality
attributes of A-Mab, and no later step of the process changes the glycan profile (Sec-
tion 4.1). The plan applies the strategy, the statistical approach and the framework
of acceptance criteria of the Process Characterization Master Plan PCMP-001 to this
step, following the approach of the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009).
The studies are run in a scale-down model of the production bioreactor, whose qual-
ification for each output against manufacturing scale is planned in Section 5.1. The
studies cover the 9 process parameters of the step that the Pre-Characterization Pro-
cess Risk Assessment RA-001 brought into scope (Table 6.1), and the 5 quality at-
tributes that are measured on every run (Section 4.2). The plan also states the crite-
ria against which each result will be judged, and the analysis from which the proven
acceptable ranges (PARs) of the step will be derived. The results will be reported in
the paired Process Characterization Report PCR-003, which rolls up into the Process
Characterization Master Report PCMR-001.
The characterization supports the transfer of the process from the development site in
Cambridge, MA, to the commercial drug substance facility in Grafton, WI, under the
Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. The results of the characterization are the evidence
for the operating ranges of the production bioreactor and for the part of the control
strategy that this step carries.
Some related work lies outside the scope of this plan. The seed expansion steps that
precede the production bioreactor are not characterized here. Harvest and clarifi-
cation (Step 4) is characterized under its own plan, PCP-004. Fragments, host cell
protein and residual DNA are formed in the culture, but they are not responses of the
bioreactor studies, for the reasons given in Section 4.2.

2 Objectives

The study has the following objectives.

  1. To estimate, before the first characterization run, the offset between the scale-
     down bioreactor and manufacturing scale for each of the 5 measured attributes,
     and to decide at analysis for which attributes the scale-down model is qualified
     (Section 5.1).
  2. To identify which of the 5 parameters of the screening design (culture pH, culture
     temperature, CO2 partial pressure (pCO2), osmolality and culture duration) af-
     fect the measured attributes, as main effects or through two-factor interactions
     (Section 6.2).
  3. To fit a response-surface model of each measured attribute in the 4 factors of the
     response-surface design, and to test each model against the adequacy criteria
     of PCMP-001 §7.2 (Section 6.3, Section 7).
  4. To determine, for each measured attribute and each response-surface factor, the
     proven acceptable range against the intermediate acceptance criterion of the
     step at manufacturing scale, and to compare it with the NOR (Section 8).
  5. To assess the 4 remaining parameters (dissolved oxygen, initial viable cell con-
     centration, basal medium concentration and nutrient feed-1 volume) one factor
     at a time at both ends of their ranges studied (Section 6.4).
  6. To provide the evidence on product quality from which each parameter of the
     step is classified under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001, and
     from which the bioreactor report PCR-003 and the master report PCMR-001
     justify the ranges and controls that are carried into Stage 2.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

The production bioreactor is a fed-batch culture in which a recombinant Chinese ham-
ster ovary (CHO) cell line secretes A-Mab into the culture medium. The culture starts
in basal medium at the initial viable cell concentration of the run and receives nutri-
ent feed-1 while it grows. The pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen and pCO2 of the
culture are controlled throughout the run. At the set-point the culture lasts 17 days.
The output of the step is the culture fluid at the end of the run, which passes to harvest
and clarification (Step 4).
Glycosylation is completed in the Golgi apparatus before the antibody is secreted, and
no downstream step changes the glycan profile. Protein A binds the Fc region at the
interface of the CH2 and CH3 domains, so it captures every glycoform alike (PCMP-
001 §3). The two ion exchange steps separate by charge. Core fucose, galactose and
mannose carry no charge, so glycoforms that differ only in these residues have the
same charge, and the ion exchange steps cannot separate them (CMC Biotech Work-
ing Group 2009). Only sialic acid carries a charge, and sialylated forms are counted
among the acidic variants. The glycan profile of the drug substance is therefore de-
cided in this step. Charge variants and aggregate are also formed in the culture.
Cation exchange removes part of the aggregate later in the process, and no down-
stream step is credited with removing acidic variants (Table 7.1).
The production bioreactor runs on the Novacyte fed-batch platform. The behaviour
of the platform is established from three earlier humanized IgG1 antibodies, X-Mab,
Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and from A-Mab clinical and engineering experience. This history
shows the direction and the typical size of the effects to expect. The characterization
of A-Mab tests these expectations and bounds the effects for this cell line and this
molecule. The expected effects are summarized below, and the sign and size of each
effect will be read from the fitted models.
Core fucose is added to the N-glycan in the Golgi by a fucosyltransferase, from the
donor GDP-fucose. A larger supply of GDP-fucose, or a longer residence of the glycan
in the Golgi, lets the enzyme fucosylate a larger share of the antibody, and afucosy-
lation falls. Culture temperature and culture pH set the growth rate and the intra-
cellular pH, pCO2 lowers the intracellular pH, and osmolality changes the supply of
nucleotide sugars. Each of these parameters can therefore change afucosylation, in a
direction that the models will show. A-Mab acts mainly through antibody-dependent
cellular cytotoxicity. Antibody without core fucose binds more strongly to the Fcγ
receptor IIIa (FcγRIIIa), which triggers that cytotoxicity, so afucosylation is linked
directly to the efficacy of the molecule.




                                           13
Terminal galactose is added by a galactosyltransferase, from the donor UDP-
galactose, with manganese as its cofactor. Late in a fed-batch culture UDP-galactose
and manganese are depleted, and the harvested pool accumulates antibody made
under those conditions. Galactosylation is therefore expected to fall as the culture
ages. A high osmolality slows growth and raises the specific productivity of each
cell. More antibody then passes through the Golgi in the same time, and galacto-
sylation is expected to fall. Culture temperature, culture pH and pCO2 act on the
galactosyltransferase and on the supply of UDP-galactose through the growth rate
and the intracellular pH, in directions that the models will show. When the supply
of UDP-galactose limits the reaction, a parameter that speeds the enzyme can raise
galactosylation only as far as the donor allows. The effect of one parameter can then
depend on the level of another. For this reason the designs test the interactions
between them.
High mannose glycans, mainly Man5, remain when processing in the Golgi stops
before N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase I adds the first N-acetylglucosamine. Their
level rises when transit through the Golgi is fast relative to the processing enzymes,
or when those enzymes are inhibited. A parameter that raises the specific productiv-
ity of the cell, such as a high osmolality, shortens the transit and is expected to raise
high mannose. Culture pH, culture temperature and pCO2 act both on the processing
enzymes and on the rate of secretion, and the models will show the direction of their
effects. High mannose species are cleared faster from the circulation, which is why
the attribute has very high criticality.
Aggregate forms while the antibody sits in the culture fluid at culture temperature.
Its rate of formation rises with the antibody concentration and with the time at tem-
perature. It also rises as the culture pH approaches the isoelectric point of the an-
tibody, which lies above the culture pH. Closer to that point the antibody carries
less net charge, the repulsion between molecules is weaker, and they associate more
readily. A longer culture duration and a higher culture pH are therefore expected
to raise aggregate. A higher initial viable cell concentration gives a higher antibody
concentration in the culture fluid, and it is expected to raise aggregate for the same
reason.
Acidic variants arise mainly from asparagine deamidation, with smaller contributions
from sialylated glycans and glycated lysines. Deamidation is faster at higher temper-
ature and at higher pH, and it accumulates with the time that the antibody spends in
the culture fluid. Through deamidation alone, a higher culture temperature, a higher
culture pH and a longer culture duration are each expected to raise acidic variants.
Culture pH, pCO2 and osmolality also act on the intracellular pH and on sialylation,
and the direction of that route is not known from the chemistry. For these three
parameters the net direction of the effect on acidic variants is not predicted here.
Host cell protein and residual DNA are released when cells die and lyse. Viability
falls late in a fed-batch culture, so a longer culture carries a larger load of both into
the harvest. The production bioreactor sets this load, and the purification steps clear
it.
Culture duration decides how much late-culture antibody the harvested pool contains
and how far viability has fallen at the end of the run. A longer culture is therefore



                                           14
expected to lower galactosylation and to raise aggregate, host cell protein and resid-
ual DNA. A higher culture temperature or a higher culture pH speeds deamidation
and aggregation. The effects of temperature and pH on the glycans pass through
the glycosyltransferases and the growth rate, and the effect of each may depend on
the level of the other and on the culture duration. Dissolved carbon dioxide crosses
the cell membrane and lowers the intracellular pH. A high pCO2 therefore acts on
the pH-sensitive Golgi enzymes as a low culture pH would, and it also slows growth.
pCO2 also raises the osmolality of the medium as bicarbonate accumulates. In rou-
tine manufacture osmolality follows the base and feed additions and the accumulation
of metabolites.
The risk assessment RA-001 ranked the 4 remaining parameters lower (Section 4.3),
but each has a route to the quality attributes. Dissolved oxygen is held by sparging,
and the more gas is sparged, the more carbon dioxide is stripped from the culture.
The initial viable cell concentration acts on aggregate through the antibody concen-
tration. The basal medium concentration and the nutrient feed-1 volume set the sup-
ply of glucose, amino acids and the precursors of UDP-galactose. A lower supply is
expected to deplete the galactose donor earlier and to lower galactosylation.



4.2 Quality attributes in scope

Table 4.1 lists the 7 quality attributes that the drug substance CQA register assigns
to the production bioreactor. Criticality was assigned with Tool #1 of the A-Mab case
study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The Tool #1 score in Table 4.1 is the
product of impact and uncertainty, and criticality is treated as a continuum (Interna-
tional Council for Harmonisation 2009). The levels run from very low (VL) to very
high (VH), and the attributes of high (H) and very high criticality are called critical.
For this step the critical attributes are afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose
and aggregate. Host cell protein has moderate to high criticality (M-H). Acidic vari-
ants and residual DNA have very low criticality, and their drug substance criteria still
apply (PCMP-001 §3).

Of the 7 attributes, the 5 measured on every run of the bioreactor studies are afuco-
sylation, galactosylation, high mannose, acidic variants and aggregate. The register
gives the criterion for acidic variants as measured by CEX or icIEF. This plan mea-
sures acidic variants by icIEF only (Section 5.3).
Host cell protein and residual DNA are released into the culture as cells lyse, and a
longer culture is expected to carry more of both into the harvest (Section 4.1). They
are not responses of the bioreactor studies, because neither has a criterion at this step.
Host cell protein is first judged in the Protein A pool, against a criterion carried back
from the drug substance limit, and residual DNA is judged on the drug substance
only (PCMP-001 §7.3). The clearance of both is characterized in the plans of the
purification steps. The classification of the bioreactor parameters therefore rests on
the 5 measured attributes, and an effect of culture duration on the load of host cell
protein is not assessed in this plan.
Fragments do not appear in Table 4.1. Fragments are formed in the culture and
during the hold of the harvest, by proteases released from lysed cells that clip the
antibody. The CQA register assigns them to harvest. Fragments have intermediate
acceptance criteria from the Protein A pool onward and none at this step (PCMP-001
§7.3). They are therefore not an attribute of the bioreactor studies.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The risk assessment RA-001 ranked every parameter of the step before characteri-
zation began. Each parameter was ranked on its potential impact on the CQAs and
on its potential to interact with other parameters (International Council for Harmon-
isation 2023). Parameters ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to
multivariate study. Parameters ranked lower, whose range could be supported one
factor at a time, were assigned to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). RA-001 gives the
scores and the rationale for each assignment, and they are not repeated here.
For the production bioreactor, 5 of the 9 parameters were assigned to multivariate
study and 4 to univariate study (Table 6.1). The multivariate parameters act on the
galactosyltransferase and the fucosyltransferase, on the supply of UDP-galactose and
GDP-fucose, and on the rates of deamidation and aggregation (Section 4.1). The effect
of one of them may therefore depend on the level of another, and they are studied in
designs that can estimate interactions.
The range studied of every parameter extends beyond its NOR at both ends. The
width of the range studied is between 1.5 and 2.5 times the width of the NOR. The

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model is a stirred-tank bioreactor built on the same design principles
as the commercial vessel. It has the same controls for pH, dissolved oxygen, temper-
ature and nutrient addition. Mixing and the removal of carbon dioxide differ between
a bench vessel and a commercial vessel, and the qualification study measures the off-
set that such differences give each output. Parameters that do not depend on scale
are run at their commercial values. These include culture pH, culture temperature,
dissolved oxygen, the initial viable cell concentration, the culture duration and the
media and feeds. Agitation and gas flow depend on scale, and they are set so that
oxygen transfer matches that of the commercial vessel (PCMP-001 §5). The model
is operated under the bioreactor operating procedure SOP-2003, and the media and
feeds are prepared under the media and feed procedure SOP-2004. The scale-down
system runs several bioreactors at the same time, so the runs of a study overlap in
time (Section 11).
pCO2 depends on scale, because it is set by the balance between the carbon dioxide
that the cells produce and the carbon dioxide that the gas flow strips. A large vessel
strips less carbon dioxide per unit volume than a bench vessel, so pCO2 tends to rise
with scale. In the scale-down model pCO2 is a factor of the designs and is controlled
at the level of each run. The carbon dioxide stripping of the commercial vessel is
therefore not matched. The pCO2 of each run is measured by the pCO2 probe of the
scale-down bioreactor system (EQ-BRX-205), which is calibrated and under change
control (Section 12).
The qualification study S-BRX-SDM is run under the scale-down model procedure
SOP-1001. It compares 3 runs of the scale-down model at the set-points in Table 6.1
with 5 batches at manufacturing scale. The outputs compared are the 5 measured at-
tributes, afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose, acidic variants and aggregate.
The qualification runs are made, and the offset of each output is estimated, before
the first characterization run. A scale-down model need not reproduce the manufac-
turing mean. The difference between the two scales must instead be known precisely
enough that no conclusion depends on its exact size. The model is qualified for each
output separately, by the following rule (PCMP-001 §7.1).

  1. The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of the
     manufacturing-scale batches. It is reported with its two-sided 90 % confidence
     interval, computed from Student’s t with the pooled standard deviation of the
     two scales. Each end of that interval is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound.




                                         18
  2. Every judgement of a scale-down result against a criterion first subtracts the
     offset of that output, in every study of this plan. Ranges and conclusions are
     therefore stated at manufacturing scale.
  3. For each output, the judgement over the NOR is whether the output meets its
     criterion at all 81 combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the 4
     response-surface factors. Each combination is judged on the exact 95 % predic-
     tion interval of a single run from the response-surface model.
  4. The model is qualified for an output when the judgement over the NOR gives the
     same conclusion with the offset at either end of its confidence interval.

The decision whether the model is qualified for an output is made at analysis, because
the judgement over the NOR needs the response-surface model. If the model is not
qualified for an output, the report will state so and will state the consequence (PCMP-
001 §5). PCMP-001 sets no qualification rule for an output that has no adequate
response-surface model, and this plan sets none. The report states its approach for
such an output. The offset is estimated at the set-points only. Its use across the range
studied is an assumption of this plan (Section 12).



5.2 Cell line, media and culture operation

Each run starts from cells of the A-Mab production cell line, expanded through the
seed train of the platform process. The bioreactor is filled with basal medium at
the concentration of the run and inoculated at the initial viable cell concentration of
the run. Nutrient feed-1 is added during the culture as a percentage of the working
volume. pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen and pCO2 are controlled at the levels of
the run throughout the culture. The run ends at the culture duration of the run, when
the culture fluid is sampled for analysis.
Each factor of a design is held at the level that the design gives for the run (Ap-
pendices A and B). Every parameter that is not under study in a run is held at its
set-point (Table 6.1). The basal medium and nutrient feed-1 are prepared to platform
specification under SOP-2004 and are released by identity and quality testing. The
composition of the media and feeds is not a factor of this study, and the lot of each
medium and feed used in each run is recorded.
Viable cell concentration, viability and titer are monitored during each run as the
bioreactor operating procedure SOP-2003 describes. Titer is measured by Protein A
affinity HPLC under SOP-3016, validated under AMV-3021. These measures of pro-
cess performance are recorded with each run, but they are not responses of the de-
signs, and no model of process performance is fitted. This plan sets no rule by which
these studies show a parameter to be a key process parameter (KPP), and the biore-
actor report PCR-003 states its approach (Section 7).

5.5 Statistical methods

The designs are analysed under the design of experiments procedure SOP-1002, with
the methods of the master plan PCMP-001 (PCMP-001 §6) (Montgomery 2017). Each
factor is coded from −1 at the low end to +1 at the high end of its range studied. An
effect is reported as the change in the response over the coded range of the factor,
which is twice the coded coefficient. A model term is significant when its p-value is
below α = 0.05.
The screening data are fitted by least squares with a model of the 5 main effects
and the 10 two-factor interactions. The model has 16 terms, and the design has 19
runs. This leaves 2 degrees of freedom for pure error from the centre points and 1
for lack of fit. With so few residual degrees of freedom, R² describes the fit to these
runs, but it overstates how well the model would predict new runs. The centre points
show whether curvature is present but cannot attribute it to a factor. The screening
model identifies effects and their direction, and it is not used for prediction (PCMP-
001 §6.2).
The response-surface data are fitted by least squares with the full quadratic model
in the 4 factors of the response-surface design. The model has 15 terms, which are
the intercept, 4 main effects, 6 two-factor interactions and 4 quadratic terms. With
28 runs the model leaves 3 degrees of freedom for pure error and 10 for lack of fit.
Each model is reported with R², adjusted R², predicted R² from the prediction error
sum of squares (PRESS), the model F-test and the test of lack of fit against pure
error. The residuals are plotted against the predicted values and against the start
order of the runs, and on a normal probability plot. A model is used for prediction
only if it meets the adequacy criteria in Section 7. The response-surface model is the




                                           21
predictive model of this step. Every prediction, every PAR and the design space of
the step come from it.
A response-surface model predicts the mean response. At the edge of a region drawn
on mean predictions, a single batch meets its criterion only about half the time if
the variation is symmetrical (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). For this reason
each quality attribute is judged on the 95 % prediction interval of a single future
batch, not on the mean prediction (PCMP-001 §6.2). The PAR computed with the other
factors varied over their NORs uses such an interval from a Monte Carlo simulation
(Section 8). The judgement over the NOR in the qualification uses the exact 95 %
prediction interval of a single run (Section 5.1).
The results of the univariate study are reported against the criteria of Table 7.1 and
beside the set-point runs of the qualification study. The difference between the two
ends of each range is stated together with the replicate variation at each end.
Capability at commercial scale is estimated from the integrated process model (PCMP-
001 §6.5). The model simulates 2,000 batches, with every process parameter varying
about its set-point as in the PAR analysis (Section 8), and it records the drug substance
attributes of each batch. Cpk is computed against the drug substance criterion. The
criteria of afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose and acidic variants have two
limits, and the Cpk of each is the lower of the two one-sided indices. The same simu-
lation gives, for each attribute, the probability that the drug substance falls outside
its criterion.

6.2 Screening design

The screening study S-BRX-SCR varies the 5 multivariate parameters in a two-level
fractional factorial design. The design is a resolution V half fraction of the full facto-
rial, with 16 factorial runs and 3 centre points, 19 runs in all. The runs are started
in a random order. Appendix A gives this start order, which is the run order of the
design. Several runs are in progress at the same time (Section 5.1).
At resolution V no main effect or two-factor interaction is aliased with another main
effect or two-factor interaction. Each two-factor interaction is aliased only with a
three-factor interaction, which is assumed to be negligible. The design therefore
estimates all 5 main effects and all 10 two-factor interactions. The screening design
does not resolve curvature by factor. The centre points show whether curvature is
present, and the response-surface design then resolves it for its own factors.
Osmolality and pCO2 are separate factors of the screening design, and each run holds
each of them at the level that the design gives (Section 5.2). The screening design
therefore estimates the effect of pCO2 at constant osmolality, and the effect of os-
molality at constant pCO2. At manufacturing scale a higher pCO2 also raises the
osmolality, as bicarbonate accumulates (Section 4.1). The effect of a pCO2 excursion
at manufacturing scale is then the effect of pCO2 in the models plus the effect of the
rise in osmolality that comes with it.



6.3 Response-surface design

The master plan PCMP-001 plans the response-surface design of this step in 4 of its
5 multivariate parameters (PCMP-001 §6.1). The response-surface study S-BRX-RSM
varies culture pH, culture temperature, culture duration and CO2 partial pressure
(pCO2) in a face-centred central composite design. The design has 16 factorial runs,
8 axial runs at the centres of the faces of the design cube, and 4 centre points, 28




                                           24
runs in all. The runs are started in a random order, which Appendix B gives as the
start order. Because the axial runs lie on the faces of the cube, every run stays inside
the range studied, and each factor is run at 3 levels. The design supports the full
quadratic model of Section 5.5.
Osmolality is a factor of the screening design only, and it is held at its set-point in every
run of the response-surface design. The response-surface model therefore does not
predict the effect of osmolality, and no PAR is computed for it (PCMP-001 §6.4). Its
effects are estimated from the screening model. This plan sets no rule for judging the
NOR of osmolality from the screening model. The bioreactor report PCR-003 reports
what the screening showed, and it records the later osmolality study S-BRX-OSM as
discrepancy PD-003-01 from this plan.
The response-surface study starts after Process Development and Statistics have re-
viewed the results of the screening study. This plan sets no rule by which that review
could change the factors of the response-surface design.



6.4 Univariate assessment

The univariate study S-BRX-UNI assesses dissolved oxygen, initial viable cell concen-
tration, basal medium concentration and nutrient feed-1 volume one factor at a time.
Each parameter is run at the low and at the high end of its range studied, in 2 repli-
cate runs at each end, with every other parameter at its set-point. The univariate
study has 16 runs. In the runs of dissolved oxygen, pCO2 is held at its set-point like
every other parameter, so the study shows the effect of dissolved oxygen at constant
pCO2.
The set-point reference of the univariate study is the 3 set-point runs of the qualifica-
tion study S-BRX-SDM, made in the same scale-down system. They were run before
the univariate study, not at the same time. A difference between them and the uni-
variate runs can therefore include an effect of time and of the media lot.
An assessment of one factor at a time cannot detect an interaction between the pa-
rameter studied and the other parameters. The assessment therefore rests on the
conclusion of the risk assessment RA-001 that the ranges of these parameters can be
supported one factor at a time (Section 4.3). Runs at both ends of each range studied
show the largest change that each parameter causes within that range, provided its
effect does not reverse inside the range (Section 12).

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

The criteria below apply the acceptance criteria framework of the master plan PCMP-
001 (PCMP-001 §7) to the production bioreactor. None of them uses data from the
bioreactor studies. The criteria decide when the scale-down model is qualified for an
output, when a model is adequate, when a step output is acceptable, when a range is
proven and how a parameter is classified.

  1. The scale-down model is qualified for an output when the judgement over the
     NOR gives the same conclusion with the offset at either end of its 90 % confi-
     dence interval (Section 5.1). The judgement over the NOR is whether the output
     meets its criterion at all 81 combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR high
     of the 4 response-surface factors. A scale-down model whose conclusion for an
     output changes between the two ends of the offset interval is not used for that
     output (PCMP-001 §7.1).
  2. A response-surface model is adequate for prediction when all of the following
     hold (PCMP-001 §7.2).
      a. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
      b. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
      c. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20.
      d. The residuals show no pattern against the predicted values or the start order,
         and no single run dominates the fit. There is no pattern when a regression
         of the absolute residuals on the predicted values, and a regression of the
         residuals on the start order, each show no slope significant at α = 0.05. No
         single run dominates when no run has a Cook’s distance above 4/n, where
         n is the number of runs of the design.
     A model is used for prediction only if it meets all four criteria (PCMP-001 §6.3).
     A model that fails the lack of fit test or the limit on overfitting is either refitted
     with a justified change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a
     range (PCMP-001 §7.2). Where no adequate model results, the report states the
     response descriptively, against its criterion, over the runs of the designs.
  3. Each step output is judged against its intermediate acceptance criterion in
     Table 7.1, at manufacturing scale. A quality attribute is judged on the 95 %
     prediction interval of a single future batch. Afucosylation, galactosylation and
     high mannose keep their drug substance criteria, because no downstream step
     changes them. The criteria of afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose and
     acidic variants are judged at both limits, and the criterion for aggregate at its
     upper limit.
  4. A range of a response-surface factor is proven acceptable for an attribute when
     the analysis of Section 8 with the other factors varied over their NORs meets the
     criterion of that attribute across it. A parameter has a PAR for each attribute,



                                           26
     and the range carried into the control strategy is their intersection. A PAR that
     reaches the end of the range studied is reported as reaching it, and no claim is
     made beyond it. Where that analysis does not meet the criterion even with the
     parameter at its set-point, the report states that no PAR exists for the attribute
     and states the consequence for the NOR (PCMP-001 §7.6). Neither PCMP-001
     nor this plan sets a rule for a PAR that is narrower than the NOR, and the report
     states its approach.
  5. Osmolality and the univariate parameters have no PAR from the response-
     surface model (Section 8). The range of a parameter studied one factor at a
     time is supported by its own data (PCMP-001 §4). Neither PCMP-001 nor this
     plan sets a decision rule for the univariate study or for the NOR of osmolality,
     and the bioreactor report PCR-003 states its approach.
  6. The design space of the step comes from the response-surface model, like every
     prediction and every PAR (PCMP-001 §6.2). A quality attribute is judged on a
     single batch, not on the mean prediction (Section 5.5). The master report PCMR-
     001 consolidates the design spaces of the steps (PCMP-001 §9).
  7. Each parameter is classified under the parameter classification procedure SOP-
     4001, by the decision logic of PCMP-001 §7.7 (CMC Biotech Working Group
     2009). A critical process parameter (CPP) has an effect on a CQA that is signifi-
     cant and practically relevant within the range studied, and a high risk of falling
     outside its acceptable range in operation. A well-controlled critical process pa-
     rameter (WC-CPP) has the same kind of effect on a CQA but a low risk of falling
     outside its acceptable range, because it is readily controlled and its PAR is wide
     against its NOR. A key process parameter (KPP) affects process performance,
     such as yield or concentration, and has no effect on a CQA within its range. A
     general process parameter (GPP) has no relevant effect on a CQA or on process
     performance within its range. A significant effect that is too small to move the
     attribute appreciably within the PAR does not by itself make a parameter criti-
     cal. The report states the size of each effect beside its significance and gives the
     reason for each class.
  8. Capability is judged in the master report PCMR-001 against two values that
     PCMP-001 §7.9 sets. A Cpk of at least 1.33 is expected of a CQA in routine
     manufacture. A Cpk below 1 requires an action in the control strategy. A Cpk
     between the two is acceptable, and the master report then states the side of
     the criterion that limits it and the parameters that drive it. This plan sets no
     capability criterion of its own.

Each criterion in force assumes that every downstream step performs at least as
well as the worst case from which the criterion was derived. The report of each

worst case, the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch there replaces the
platform value, and every criterion upstream becomes stricter. Where that bound is
better than the platform value, and the model behind it meets the adequacy criteria
above, the bound replaces the platform value, and every criterion upstream becomes
wider.
That rule changed the criterion of this step for aggregate. The cation exchange model
predicted a lower aggregate clearance within its NOR than the platform worst case
of 2.00-fold. The one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch for that clearance,
1.40-fold, tightened the criterion (cation exchange report PCR-007). The low-pH vi-
ral inactivation model predicted a smaller aggregate increase than the platform worst
case of 0.70 percentage points. The bound for that increase, 0.56 percentage points,

widened the criterion (low-pH viral inactivation report PCR-006). The resulting ag-
gregate criterion of ≤ 6.46 % HMW is stricter than the a priori criterion of ≤ 9.30 %.
The widening does not reflect a more stable product. The platform worst case of
the low-pH hold is an increase over a hold of 180 min, and the range studied of the
A-Mab hold time reaches the same 180 min. At the edges of the range studied, the
A-Mab aggregate increase was 0.84 percentage points, more than the platform worst
case of 0.70. Within the hold time NOR as characterized, which ends at 120 min,
the model predicts an increase of 0.53 percentage points, with a bound of 0.56. The
widening therefore reflects the shorter A-Mab hold. The NORs in force for hold time
and temperature end at the same values as the NORs as characterized, and the low-
pH aggregate model has no pH term. The bound therefore also covers the NORs in
force, and routine manufacture does not hold longer than the NOR.
The change moves risk between the producer and the patient. A stricter criterion low-
ers the risk to the patient and raises the risk to the producer, because more batches
may fail it. A wider criterion does the opposite. The aggregate criterion in force is
stricter than the a priori criterion, although the low-pH bound widened it. The biore-
actor report PCR-003 judges the aggregate results against the changed criterion. For
the widening it gives the bound, the runs behind it, and the reason why the perfor-
mance of low-pH viral inactivation for A-Mab is known well enough to replace the
platform value. It also argues the change by its risk, as PCMP-001 §7.5 requires.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A PAR is computed for each of the 5 measured attributes and each of the 4 response-
surface factors, 20 combinations in all. Each PAR is computed from the response-
surface model of the attribute, with the offset of the attribute subtracted so that the
PAR is stated at manufacturing scale. The criterion is the intermediate acceptance
criterion in Table 7.1. Two analyses are run for each combination (PCMP-001 §6.4).
The first analysis gives the set-point PAR, the PAR with the other factors at their set-
points. It holds the other response-surface factors at their set-points in Table 6.1
and scans the parameter of interest across its full range studied, evaluating the fitted
response-surface model at 201 grid points. The set-point PAR is the contiguous range
around the set-point over which the mean prediction meets the criterion.
The second analysis gives the NOR-propagated PAR, the PAR with the other factors
varied over their NORs. At each of 81 grid points across the range studied of the
parameter of interest, 2,000 operating points are drawn for the other factors. Each
factor is drawn from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a standard
deviation equal to the width of its NOR divided by 6, so that 99.7 % of draws fall within
the NOR. The draws are clipped to the range studied. The draw assumes that each
half of the NOR spans 3 standard deviations of routine variation. Each simulated
batch adds a residual drawn from a normal distribution with the residual standard
deviation of the model. It also takes its own set of coefficients, drawn from the multi-
variate normal distribution given by the fitted coefficients and their covariance matrix.
The 95 % prediction interval of a single future batch runs from the 2.5th to the 97.5th
percentile of the simulated batches. For an upper limit the 97.5th percentile must not
exceed the limit. For a lower limit the 2.5th percentile must not fall below it. For the
two-sided criteria of afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose and acidic variants
both conditions apply. The NOR-propagated PAR is the contiguous range around the
set-point over which the interval meets the criterion.
The NOR-propagated PAR is in general narrower than the set-point PAR, for two rea-
sons. It is judged on the interval of a single batch, which includes the residual and the
uncertainty of the coefficients, while the set-point PAR is judged on the mean predic-
tion. It must also hold while the rest of the operating point varies about its set-point.
The NOR-propagated PAR is the one that supports a claim.
The report will give both PARs for every combination in one table, with the criterion
and the basis of each. For each attribute the report will also show, as a figure, the
PAR of the factor with the largest main effect on that attribute. The figure has one
panel for each analysis, with the acceptable region of the parameter shaded green
and the set-point and the NOR marked.

9 Data management and integrity

Records of the studies are kept to the ALCOA+ principles. Each run has a batch
record under the bioreactor operating procedure SOP-2003 that gives its run num-
ber, its design levels and the settings that the control system of the scale-down biore-
actor recorded. Analytical results are recorded under the SOP of each method, and
Analytical Development reviews them before they are released to the analysis.
Each study record states the execution window of the study and the calibration status
of the instrumented system that it used. The preventive action PA3 of the QA inves-
tigation INV-001 requires this of every study record. A run whose recorded setting
lies outside the control band that SOP-2003 sets for that parameter is recorded as a
deviation, and its impact is assessed in the report. The data set is locked before the
statistical analysis starts, and Statistics performs the analysis under SOP-1002.
Work that this plan does not foresee, such as an added study or a changed analysis, is
recorded in the report as a discrepancy from the plan, with its reason and its effect on
the conclusions. A departure from an approved procedure or from a GMP requirement
during a run is recorded as a deviation.

12 Risks and assumptions

The main risks to the study are the following.

  1. The scale-down model may not represent manufacturing scale for an output. The
     qualification by offsets controls this risk (Section 5.1), and an output for which
     the model is not qualified sets no range.
  2. pCO2 depends on scale and is measured by the pCO2 probe of the scale-down
     bioreactor system (EQ-BRX-205). A drift of the probe between calibrations would
     move the true pCO2 of a run away from its design level, while the controller still
     holds the probe reading at that level, so the recorded setting would not show
     it. The calibration interval of the probe limits this risk, and every study window
     closed before the next calibration of the probe was due (Section 11).
  3. Each run lasts the full culture duration, so a lost run cannot be repeated quickly.
     A lost or invalidated run is reported, and the report states whether the design
     was repaired or re-executed (PCMP-001 §6.1).
  4. A change of lot of a medium or feed between runs could add variation that the
     analysis attributes to error. The lot used in each run is recorded so that such an
     effect can be traced.

The study rests on the following assumptions.

  1. The offset between the scales, estimated at the set-points, holds across the range
     studied.
  2. No downstream step changes afucosylation, galactosylation or high mannose, so
     their drug substance criteria apply to the output of this step (PCMP-001 §3).
  3. Interactions of three or more factors are negligible, as the screening design
     requires (Section 6.2).
  4. The univariate parameters do not interact with the other parameters within their
     ranges, and their effects do not reverse between the two ends of each range
     studied.
  5. The effects of pCO2 and of osmolality, each estimated at a constant level of the
     other, add when both change together at manufacturing scale (Section 6.2).
  6. In routine operation each parameter varies about its set-point as a normal distri-
     bution whose standard deviation is the width of its NOR divided by 6 (Section 8).
  7. Parameters that are not studied are held at their set-points, and the media and
     feeds meet platform specification.
