1 Purpose and scope

This plan describes the Stage 1 characterization of the production bioreactor, Step 3
of the A-Mab drug substance process (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). The
production bioreactor is a fed-batch culture with a working volume of 15,000 L at
commercial scale. The step forms the glycan, charge variant and aggregate quality
attributes of A-Mab, and no later step of the process changes the glycan profile (Sec-
tion 4.1). The plan applies the strategy, the statistical approach and the framework of
acceptance criteria of the master plan PCMP-001 to this step, following the approach
of the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
The studies are run in a scale-down model of the production bioreactor that is qual-
ified against manufacturing scale (Section 5.1). The studies cover the 9 process pa-
rameters of the step that the risk assessment RA-001 brought into scope (Table 6.1)
and the 5 quality attributes that are measured on every run (Section 4.2). The plan
also states the criteria against which each result will be judged, and the analysis from
which the proven acceptable ranges (PARs) of the step will be derived. The results
will be reported in the paired report PCR-003, which rolls up into the master report
PCMR-001.
The characterization supports the transfer of the process from the development site in
Cambridge, MA, to the commercial drug substance facility in Grafton, WI, under the
Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. The results of the characterization are the evidence
for the operating ranges of the production bioreactor and for the part of the control
strategy that this step carries.
Some related work lies outside the scope of this plan. The seed expansion steps that
precede the production bioreactor are not characterized here. Harvest and clarifi-
cation (Step 4) is characterized under its own plan, PCP-004. Fragments are not an
attribute of the bioreactor studies (Section 4.2). Host cell protein and residual DNA
are formed in the culture, but they are not responses of the bioreactor studies either
(Section 4.2).

2 Objectives

The study has the following objectives.

  1. To qualify the scale-down bioreactor as a model of manufacturing scale for each
     of the 5 measured attributes, by the offset between the two scales (Section 5.1).
  2. To identify which of the 5 parameters of the screening design (culture pH, cul-
     ture temperature, dissolved CO2 (pCO2), osmolality and culture duration) affect
     the measured attributes, as main effects or through two-factor interactions (Sec-
     tion 6.2).
  3. To fit a predictive response-surface model of each measured attribute in the 4
     factors of the response-surface design, and to show that each model is adequate
     by the criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 (Section 6.3).
  4. To determine, for each measured attribute and each response-surface factor,
     the proven acceptable range against the intermediate acceptance criterion of
     the step at manufacturing scale, and to show where the NOR lies within it (Sec-
     tion 8).
  5. To assess the 4 remaining parameters (dissolved oxygen, initial viable cell con-
     centration, basal medium concentration and nutrient feed-1 volume) one factor
     at a time at both ends of their ranges studied (Section 6.4).
  6. To provide the evidence from which each parameter of the step is classified un-
     der SOP-4001, and from which PCR-003 and PCMR-001 justify the ranges and
     controls that are carried into Stage 2.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

The production bioreactor is a fed-batch culture in which a recombinant Chinese ham-
ster ovary (CHO) cell line secretes A-Mab into the culture medium. The culture starts
in basal medium at the initial viable cell concentration of the run and receives nutri-
ent feed-1 while it grows. The pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen and pCO2 of the
culture are controlled throughout the run. At the set-point the culture lasts 17 days.
The output of the step is the culture fluid at the end of the run, which passes to harvest
and clarification (Step 4).
Glycosylation is completed in the Golgi apparatus before the antibody is secreted. No
downstream step changes the glycan profile, because neither Protein A nor the two
ion exchange steps separate glycan variants under platform conditions (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009). The glycan profile of the drug substance is therefore decided
in this step. Charge variants and aggregate are also formed in the culture. Cation
exchange removes part of the aggregate later in the process, and no downstream step
is credited with removing acidic variants (Table 7.1).
The production bioreactor runs on the Novacyte fed-batch platform. The behaviour
of the platform is established from three earlier humanized IgG1 antibodies, X-Mab,
Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and from A-Mab clinical and engineering experience. The mech-
anisms by which the culture conditions act on the quality attributes are therefore
known from the platform. The characterization of A-Mab confirms and bounds these
effects for this cell line and this molecule. The expected effects are summarized be-
low. The sign and size of each effect will be read from the fitted models.
Afucosylation depends on the fucosyltransferase that adds core fucose in the Golgi,
on the supply of its nucleotide sugar donor, and on how long the glycan stays in the
Golgi before secretion. Conditions that change the growth rate, the intracellular pH
or the nucleotide sugar pool are therefore expected to move afucosylation. A-Mab
acts mainly through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. Afucosylation governs
binding to the Fcγ receptor, so it is linked directly to the efficacy of the molecule.
Galactosylation is expected to fall as the culture ages. Late in a fed-batch culture
the galactose donor and the manganese cofactor of the galactosyltransferase are de-
pleted, and the harvested pool accumulates antibody made under those conditions.
Culture temperature, culture pH, pCO2 and osmolality act on the same enzymes and
the same donor pool, through the growth rate, the intracellular pH and the metabolic
state of the cell. The effects of these parameters on galactosylation are therefore
expected to interact.




                                           12
High mannose glycans are species on which processing in the Golgi never proceeded.
The level of high mannose rises when transit through the Golgi is fast relative to the
processing enzymes, or when those enzymes are inhibited. Culture pH and dissolved
oxygen are expected to act on high mannose only weakly. High mannose species are
cleared faster from the circulation, which is why the attribute has very high critical-
ity.
Aggregate forms while the antibody sits in the culture fluid at culture temperature.
The formation of aggregate depends on the antibody concentration, on the time at
temperature and on the colloidal stability of the molecule at the culture pH. A longer
culture and a higher culture pH are therefore expected to raise aggregate.
Acidic variants arise mainly from asparagine deamidation, with smaller contributions
from sialylated glycans and glycated lysines. Culture temperature and culture pH set
the rates of these reactions. pCO2 and osmolality act through the intracellular pH
and the sialylation machinery, and the culture duration sets the time at temperature.
Because each parameter acts on several reactions at once, the net direction of its
effect on acidic variants cannot be predicted from mechanism. The net direction of
each effect will be read from the fitted models.
Host cell protein and residual DNA are released when cells die and lyse. Viability
falls late in a fed-batch culture, so a longer culture carries a larger load of both into
the harvest. The production bioreactor sets this load, and the purification steps clear
it.
The parameters act on these attributes in different ways. Culture duration decides
how much late-culture antibody the harvested pool contains and how far viability
has fallen at the end of the run. Culture duration is therefore expected to act on
the glycans that change with culture age, on aggregate and on the impurity load.
Culture pH and culture temperature act on the glycosyltransferases and on the rates
of deamidation and aggregation, and their effects on the glycans are coupled to each
other and to the culture duration. pCO2 acidifies the cytosol, so it acts on the cell in
the same way as culture pH but from inside. pCO2 also raises the osmolality of the
medium as bicarbonate accumulates. Osmolality is set by the base and feed additions
and by the accumulation of metabolites. High osmolality slows growth and shifts the
balance of the nucleotide sugar pool.
The 4 remaining parameters are expected to act on the culture mainly through growth
and titer. Dissolved oxygen acts only weakly on the glycans within its range, and it
is controlled by sparging and agitation independently of the other parameters. The
initial viable cell concentration sets where on the growth curve the culture starts, and
it changes the titer more than any quality attribute. The basal medium concentration
and the nutrient feed-1 volume set the supply of nutrients and glycosylation precur-
sors. Within the ranges studied the culture is fed to excess, so neither is expected to
move the glycans appreciably.

4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The risk assessment RA-001 ranked every parameter of the step before characteri-
zation began. Each parameter was ranked on its potential impact on the CQAs and
on its potential to interact with other parameters (International Council for Harmon-
isation 2023). Parameters ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to
multivariate study. Parameters ranked lower, whose range could be supported one
factor at a time, were assigned to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). RA-001 gives the
scores and the rationale for each assignment, and they are not repeated here.
For the production bioreactor, 5 of the 9 parameters were assigned to multivariate
study and 4 to univariate study (Table 6.1). The multivariate parameters act on the
same Golgi enzymes, the same nucleotide sugar pool and the same reaction rates,
and their effects are therefore expected to interact (Section 4.1). The univariate
parameters act mainly through growth and titer, or are controlled independently of
the other parameters.
The range studied of every parameter extends beyond its NOR at both ends. The
width of the range studied is between 1.5 and 2.5 times the width of the NOR. The
wider range shows how the culture behaves near and beyond the edges of routine
operation, and it sets the knowledge space within which a PAR can be claimed. No
PAR is claimed beyond the range studied.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model is a stirred-tank bioreactor built on the same design principles
as the commercial vessel. Mixing, aeration, mass transfer and process control of pH,
dissolved oxygen, temperature and nutrient addition are equivalent at both scales.
Parameters that do not depend on scale are run at their commercial values. These
include culture pH, culture temperature, dissolved oxygen, the initial viable cell con-
centration, the culture duration and the media and feeds. Agitation and gas flow
depend on scale, and they are set so that oxygen transfer and carbon dioxide strip-
ping match those of the commercial vessel (PCMP-001 §5). The model is operated
under SOP-2003, and the media and feeds are prepared under SOP-2004.
pCO2 also depends on scale, because it is set by the balance between the carbon
dioxide that the cells produce and the carbon dioxide that the gas flow strips. In the
scale-down model pCO2 is a factor of the designs and is controlled at the level of
each run. The pCO2 of each run is measured by the pCO2 probe of the scale-down
bioreactor system (EQ-BRX-205), which is under calibration and change control.
The qualification study S-BRX-SDM is run under SOP-1001 before the first charac-
terization run. The qualification study compares 3 runs of the scale-down model at
the set-points in Table 6.1 with 5 batches at manufacturing scale. The outputs com-
pared are the 5 measured attributes, afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose,
acidic variants and aggregate. A scale-down model need not reproduce the manufac-
turing mean. The difference between the two scales must instead be known precisely
enough that no conclusion depends on its exact size. The model is qualified for each
output separately, by the following rule (PCMP-001 §7.1).

  1. The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of
     the manufacturing-scale batches. The offset is reported with its two-sided 90 %
     confidence interval.
  2. Every judgement of a scale-down result against a criterion first subtracts the
     offset of that output. Ranges and conclusions are therefore stated at manufac-
     turing scale.
  3. The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion
     over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its confidence interval.
     The conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every one of the 81
     combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface
     factors. Each combination is judged on the exact 95 % prediction interval of a
     single run from the response-surface model.

If the model is not qualified for an output, the report will state so, and no range will
be set for that output from the scale-down data. The offset is estimated at the set-
points only. The use of the offset across the range studied is an assumption of this
plan (Section 12).



5.2 Cell line, media and culture operation

Each run starts from cells of the A-Mab production cell line, expanded through the
seed train of the platform process. The bioreactor is filled with basal medium at
the concentration of the run and inoculated at the initial viable cell concentration of
the run. Nutrient feed-1 is added during the culture as a percentage of the working
volume. pH, temperature, dissolved oxygen and pCO2 are controlled at the levels of
the run throughout the culture. The run ends at the culture duration of the run, when
the culture fluid is sampled for analysis.
Each factor of a design is held at the level that the design gives for the run (Ap-
pendices A and B). Every parameter that is not under study in a run is held at its
set-point (Table 6.1). The basal medium and nutrient feed-1 are prepared to platform
specification under SOP-2004 and are released by identity and quality testing. The
composition of the media and feeds is not a factor of this study, and the lot of each
medium and feed used in each run is recorded.
Viable cell concentration, viability and titer are monitored during each run as SOP-
2003 describes. These measures of process performance are not responses of the
designs.



5.3 Analytical methods

The 5 responses are measured by 3 validated methods (Table 5.1). Released N-glycan
mapping gives afucosylation, galactosylation and high mannose as percentages of the
total glycans. Imaged capillary isoelectric focusing (icIEF) gives acidic variants as a
percentage of the charge variant profile. Size exclusion chromatography (SEC) gives
aggregate as the percentage of high molecular weight species (HMW). Within a study,
each response is measured by one validated method under its SOP.

5.5 Statistical methods

The designs are analysed under SOP-1002 with the methods of PCMP-001 §6 (Mont-
gomery 2017). Each factor is coded from −1 at the low end to +1 at the high end of
its range studied. An effect is reported as the change in the response over the coded
range of the factor, which is twice the coded coefficient. A model term is significant
when its p-value is below α = 0.05.
The screening data are fitted by least squares with a model of the 5 main effects
and the 10 two-factor interactions. The model has 16 terms, and the design has 19
runs. This leaves 2 degrees of freedom for pure error from the centre points and 1
for lack of fit. The fit statistics of the screening model therefore overstate how well
it fits. The centre points show whether curvature is present but cannot attribute it to
a factor. The screening model identifies effects and their direction, and it is not used
for prediction (PCMP-001 §6.2).
The response-surface data are fitted by least squares with the full quadratic model
in the 4 factors of the response-surface design. The model has 15 terms, which are
the intercept, 4 main effects, 6 two-factor interactions and 4 quadratic terms. With
28 runs the model leaves 3 degrees of freedom for pure error and 10 for lack of fit.
Each model is reported with R², adjusted R², predicted R² from the prediction error
sum of squares (PRESS), the model F-test and the test of lack of fit against pure error.
The residuals are inspected against predicted values and run order, and on a normal
probability plot. A model is used for prediction only if it meets the adequacy criteria
in Section 7.
The response-surface model is the predictive model of this step. Every prediction,
every PAR and the design space of the step come from it.
A response-surface model predicts the mean response. At the edge of a region drawn
on mean predictions, a single batch meets its criterion only about half the time if the
variation is symmetrical (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). For this reason each
quality attribute is judged on a single batch (PCMP-001 §6.2). The NOR-propagated
PAR uses the 95 % predictive interval of a single batch (Section 8). The judgement
over the NOR in the qualification uses the exact 95 % prediction interval of a single
run (Section 5.1). Before either judgement the offset of the output is subtracted, so
that the result is stated at manufacturing scale.
The results of the univariate study are reported against the criteria of Table 7.1 and
beside the set-point runs of the qualification study. The difference between the two
ends of each range is stated together with the replicate variation at each end. A
study of one factor at a time cannot show an interaction with other parameters (Sec-
tion 6.4).
Capability at commercial scale is estimated from the integrated model of the whole
train (PCMP-001 §6.5). The model simulates 2,000 batches with every process pa-
rameter varying within its NOR, and it records the drug substance attributes of each
batch. Cpk is computed against the drug substance criterion. The criterion for acidic
variants has two limits, and its Cpk is the lower of the two one-sided indices. Both




                                          19
ends of the criterion are therefore assessed. The same simulation gives, for each
attribute, the probability that the drug substance falls outside its criterion.

6.2 Screening design

The screening study S-BRX-SCR varies the 5 multivariate parameters in a two-level
fractional factorial design. The design is a resolution V half fraction of the full facto-
rial, with 16 factorial runs and 3 centre points, 19 runs in all. The runs are made in
random order (Appendix A).
At resolution V no main effect or two-factor interaction is aliased with another main
effect or two-factor interaction. Each two-factor interaction is aliased only with a
three-factor interaction, which is assumed to be negligible. The design therefore
estimates all 5 main effects and all 10 two-factor interactions. The screening design
does not resolve curvature by factor. The centre points show whether curvature is
present, and the response-surface design then resolves it for its own factors. The
screening design identifies effects. For the 4 factors that the response-surface design
also varies, the size of each effect is refined by that design.



6.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study S-BRX-RSM varies culture pH, culture temperature, cul-
ture duration and pCO2 in a face-centred central composite design. The design has
16 factorial runs, 8 axial runs at the centres of the faces of the design cube, and 4 cen-
tre points, 28 runs in all. The runs are made in random order (Appendix B). Because
the axial runs lie on the faces of the cube, every run stays inside the range studied,
and each factor is run at 3 levels. The design supports the full quadratic model of
Section 5.5.
Osmolality is not a factor of the response-surface design, and it is held at its set-
point in every run of that design. Osmolality is in part a consequence of pCO2 and
of the base and feed additions (Section 4.1), and its own effects are estimated by the
screening design. The response-surface model therefore does not predict the effect




                                             22
of osmolality, and no PAR is computed for osmolality from it (PCMP-001 §6.4). The
report will state the effects of osmolality from the screening model, which identifies
effects but does not predict.
The response-surface study starts after Process Development and Statistics have re-
viewed the results of the screening study.



6.4 Univariate assessment

The univariate study S-BRX-UNI assesses dissolved oxygen, initial viable cell concen-
tration, basal medium concentration and nutrient feed-1 volume one factor at a time.
Each parameter is run at the low and at the high end of its range studied, in 2 repli-
cate runs at each end, with every other parameter at its set-point. The univariate
study has 16 runs. The set-point reference is the 3 set-point runs of the qualification
study S-BRX-SDM, made in the same scale-down system.
An assessment of one factor at a time cannot detect an interaction between the pa-
rameter studied and the other parameters. The assessment therefore rests on the
conclusion of RA-001 that the ranges of these parameters can be supported one fac-
tor at a time (Section 4.3). Runs at both ends of each range studied show the largest
change that each parameter causes within that range, provided its effect does not
reverse inside the range (Section 12).

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

The criteria below apply PCMP-001 §7 to the production bioreactor. None of them
uses data from the bioreactor studies. The criteria decide when the scale-down model
is qualified for an output, when a model is adequate, when a step output is acceptable,
when a range is proven and how a parameter is classified.

  1. The scale-down model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against
     its criterion over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its 90 %
     confidence interval (Section 5.1). No range is set for an output for which the
     model is not qualified.
  2. A response-surface model is adequate for prediction when all of the following
     hold (PCMP-001 §7.2).
      a. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
      b. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
      c. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20.
      d. The residuals show no pattern against predicted values or run order, and no
         single run dominates the fit.
     A model that fails the lack of fit test or the limit on overfitting is not used to set
     a range. Where no adequate model results, the response is reported against its
     criterion over the runs of the designs.
  3. Each step output is judged against its intermediate acceptance criterion in Ta-
     ble 7.1, at manufacturing scale. A quality attribute is judged on a single batch.
     Afucosylation, galactosylation and high mannose keep their drug substance cri-
     teria, because no downstream step changes them. Acidic variants are judged
     against both the lower and the upper limit of their criterion.
  4. A range of a parameter is proven acceptable for an attribute when the NOR-
     propagated analysis of Section 8 meets the criterion of that attribute across it.
     A parameter has a PAR for each attribute, and the range carried into the control
     strategy is their intersection. A PAR that reaches the end of the range studied is
     reported as reaching it, and no claim is made beyond it. Where an attribute does
     not meet its criterion even at the set-point, the report states that no PAR exists
     for that attribute and states the consequence for the NOR (PCMP-001 §7.6).
  5. The design space of the step is the region of the 4 response-surface factors,
     within the range studied, in which every measured attribute meets its criterion
     on the mean predictions of the adequate models. The report will show it on the
     principal planes of the models. Assurance for a single batch comes from the
     PARs and not from the edge of the design space (Section 5.5).
  6. Each parameter is classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001
     §7.7 (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). A critical process parameter (CPP)
     has a significant and practically relevant effect on a CQA and a high risk of



                                           24
     falling outside its acceptable range in operation. A well-controlled critical pro-
     cess parameter (WC-CPP) has such an effect but a low risk, because it is readily
     controlled and its PAR is wide against its NOR. A key process parameter (KPP)
     affects process performance and has no effect on a CQA within its range. A
     general process parameter (GPP) has no relevant effect on either. A significant
     effect that is too small to move the attribute appreciably does not by itself make
     a parameter critical, and the report states the size of each effect beside its sig-
     nificance.
  7. Capability is judged in PCMR-001 against the values of PCMP-001 §7.9. A Cpk
     of at least 1.33 is expected of a CQA in routine manufacture, and a Cpk below 1
     requires an action in the control strategy. This plan sets no capability criterion
     of its own.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A PAR is computed for each of the 5 measured attributes and each of the 4 response-
surface factors, 20 combinations in all. Each PAR is computed from the response-
surface model of the attribute, with the offset of the attribute subtracted so that the
PAR is stated at manufacturing scale. The criterion is the intermediate acceptance
criterion in Table 7.1. Two analyses are run for each combination (PCMP-001 §6.4).
The first analysis holds the other response-surface factors at their set-points in Ta-
ble 6.1 and scans the parameter of interest across its full range studied, evaluating
the fitted response-surface model at 201 grid points. The set-point PAR is the contigu-
ous range around the set-point over which the mean prediction meets the criterion.
The second analysis lets the other response-surface factors vary within their NORs.
At each of 81 grid points across the range studied of the parameter of interest, 2,000
operating points are drawn for the other factors. Each factor is drawn from a normal
distribution centred on its set-point, with a standard deviation of one sixth of the width
of its NOR, and it is clipped to its range studied. The draw assumes that the NOR
spans three standard deviations of routine variation on either side of the set-point.
Each simulated batch carries the model residual and the uncertainty of the fitted
coefficients. The 95 % predictive interval of a single batch runs from the 2.5th to the
97.5th percentile of the simulated batches. For an upper limit the 97.5th percentile
must not exceed the limit. For a lower limit the 2.5th percentile must not fall below
it. For acidic variants, whose criterion is two-sided, both conditions apply. The NOR-
propagated PAR is the contiguous range around the set-point over which the interval
meets the criterion.
In general the NOR-propagated PAR is narrower than the set-point PAR, because the
range must hold while the rest of the operating point moves within its NOR. The
NOR-propagated PAR is the one that supports a claim.
The report will give both PARs for every combination in one table, with the criterion
and the basis of each. For each attribute the report will also show, as a figure, the
PAR of the factor with the largest main effect on that attribute. The figure has one
panel for each analysis, with the acceptable region of the parameter shaded green
and the set-point and the NOR marked.
Osmolality and the 4 univariate parameters are not factors of the response-surface
model, and no PAR is computed for them from it. The range of a univariate parameter
is supported by its own data (PCMP-001 §4). The effects of osmolality are reported
from the screening model (Section 6.3).

12 Risks and assumptions

The main risks to the study are the following.

  1. The scale-down model may not represent manufacturing scale for an output. The
     qualification by offsets controls this risk (Section 5.1), and an output for which
     the model is not qualified sets no range.
  2. pCO2 depends on scale and is measured by the pCO2 probe of the scale-down
     bioreactor system (EQ-BRX-205). A drift of the probe between calibrations would
     move the true pCO2 of a run away from its design level. The probe is under cali-
     bration and change control, and a recorded setting that departs from its design
     level is recorded and assessed (Section 9).
  3. Each run lasts the full culture duration, so a lost run cannot be repeated quickly.
     A lost or invalidated run is reported, and the report states whether the design
     was repaired or re-executed (PCMP-001 §6.1).
  4. A change of lot of a medium or feed between runs could add variation that the
     analysis attributes to error. The lot used in each run is recorded so that such an
     effect can be traced.

The study rests on the following assumptions.

  1. The offset between the scales, estimated at the set-points, holds across the range
     studied.
  2. No downstream step changes afucosylation, galactosylation or high mannose, so
     their drug substance criteria apply to the output of this step (PCMP-001 §3).
  3. Interactions of three or more factors are negligible, as the screening design
     requires (Section 6.2).
  4. The univariate parameters do not interact with the other parameters within their
     ranges, and their effects do not reverse between the two ends of each range
     studied.
  5. In routine operation each parameter varies about its set-point as a normal distri-
     bution whose standard deviation is one sixth of the width of its NOR (Section 8).
  6. Parameters that are not studied are held at their set-points, and the media and
     feeds meet platform specification.
