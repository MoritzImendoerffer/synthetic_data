1 Purpose and scope

This plan defines the process characterization study of harvest and clarification,
Step 4 of the A-Mab drug substance process. It states which parameters are studied,
how the study is run and analysed, and the criteria by which its results are judged.
Version 2.0 amends Version 1.0 after the study ran (Section 1.1).
Harvest separates the antibody from the cells and the cell debris of the production cul-
ture. At commercial scale the step is run once per batch on the contents of a 15,000 L
production bioreactor. The culture passes a continuous disk-stack centrifuge, a train
of depth filters and a sterilizing-grade filter, in that order. The centrifuge and the fil-
ters are not expected to change a quality attribute of the antibody. Fragments form
in the culture and continue to form while the clarified harvest is held before capture
(Section 4.2). The step determines the quality of the feed to Protein A capture. That
feed should carry little intact cell material and fine debris, and the operation should
break few cells, because broken cells release host cell protein and DNA into it.
In scope are the parameters of the step listed in Section 6.1. Each is studied one
factor at a time on a scale-down model of the harvest train (Section 5.1), over a range
that extends beyond its normal operating range (NOR). The responses are the impu-
rity burden of the clarified harvest, its particulate content, the pressure that the filter
train develops, the size-variant profile of the antibody across the step and the step
yield. In the turbidity series they also include the column pressure and the cycle yield
of a Protein A load challenge. The study supports the transfer of the drug substance
process from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug sub-
stance site in Grafton, WI. It forms part of the Stage 1 process design record of the
commercial process (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011).
Five things lie outside this plan:

  1. The culture conditions, which set the viability of the culture and with it the host
     cell protein and DNA that the culture carries into harvest. They are character-
     ized under the production bioreactor plan PCP-003 and are not varied here.
  2. The parameters of Protein A capture, which are characterized under the Protein
     A plan PCP-005. In this study capture serves only as a Protein A load challenge
     at set-point conditions (Section 6.2).
  3. The number and the arrangement of the depth filter stages, which the depth and
     sterile filtration procedure SOP-2006 fixes.
  4. Viral clearance. No clearance is claimed for this step, and no spiking study is
     included (International Council for Harmonisation 2023).
  5. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture. Its duration and its temperature
     are not parameters of this study.

2 Objectives

The study has five objectives.

  1. Qualify a scale-down model of the commercial harvest train and show that it rep-
     resents the centrifugation and depth filtration performance of that train (Sec-
     tion 5.1).
  2. Establish the effect of the centrifugal field, over 6,000 g to 12,000 g, on the
     clarity of the centrate and on the host cell protein and DNA released by cell
     breakage.
  3. Establish the effect of depth filter load, over 80 L/m2 to 160 L/m2, on the particu-
     late content of the clarified harvest and on the pressure the filter train develops.
  4. Establish whether the step alters the size-variant profile of the antibody, and
     establish the effect of post-clarification turbidity, over 1 NTU to 20 NTU, on the
     material presented to capture.
  5. Define a proven acceptable range for each parameter, confirm that the normal
     operating range lies inside it, and provide the evidence on which each parameter
     will be classified under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001.

Classification is an outcome of the study and is reported in PCR-004. No parameter
is classified in this plan, and no result is stated in it.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

At commercial scale the culture is fed continuously to a disk-stack centrifuge. Cells
and debris settle under the centrifugal field, at a velocity that rises with the field and
with the difference between their density and the density of the medium. In the disc
stack a particle settles across the narrow channel between two discs, is captured
on the disc surface and slides outward to the bowl wall, from which the solids are
discharged at intervals. A higher feed rate shortens the time the fluid spends in the
stack, so that more of the small particles escape capture. The centrate therefore still
carries the fine debris and the colloidal material that are too small to settle in the
time available.
The depth filter train removes that material. Its media are positively charged, so they
adsorb part of the DNA and fine debris as well as retaining particles by size. The
filtrate then passes the sterilizing-grade filter into the harvest vessel, where it is held
until capture.
A higher centrifugal field clarifies more completely, because cells and debris settle
faster and fewer escape capture. It can also break more cells. The culture is accel-
erated to the speed of the bowl in the feed zone, and the shear there rises with that
speed, while the flow between the discs is laminar and low in shear. Broken cells
release host cell protein and DNA into the centrate, and they leave fine debris that is
harder to capture. The set-point balances clarification against cell breakage.
A depth filter has a finite capacity for the solids and colloids that the centrifuge passes.
As retained solids fill the void space of the matrix and occupy its charged sites, the
pressure drop across the filter rises and more fine material passes. At the extreme,
cells and debris are forced through the pores. How far the media are saturated de-
pends on the volume of centrate that each unit of media area has received and on
the solids that this volume carries. For this reason the parameter is expressed as the
volume of centrate loaded per unit of media area, in L/m2.
Post-clarification turbidity measures the fine particulate and colloidal material that
the filters passed. It is not a set parameter. It falls as the centrifugal field rises,
because fewer fine particles reach the filters, and it rises as the filter load approaches
the capacity of the media. It is monitored on every batch as an in-process control. It
is listed among the parameters of the step because the particulate and colloidal load
that it measures reaches the Protein A column, where it fouls the bed and shortens
the working life of the resin.




                                            13
Harvest by continuous centrifugation and depth filtration is the platform recovery
operation at Novacyte Biologics. It was used for three earlier antibodies (X-Mab, Y-
Mab and Z-Mab), and the equipment, the grade of the filter media and the control
scheme are unchanged for A-Mab. The mechanisms above are therefore expected to
hold for A-Mab, and the platform experience gives the approximate position of the
operating point. Host cell protein and DNA enter harvest at a concentration that
rises as the viability of the culture falls, because cells that died before harvest have
already released their contents into the medium. Viability at the end of culture is a
property of the A-Mab culture and is characterized under the production bioreactor
plan PCP-003. The ranges in this plan are therefore established on A-Mab material.
The A-Mab case study assumes, for simplicity, that harvest has no impact on product
quality, and it notes that this might not hold in a real process (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). This plan keeps that assumption, and the criteria of PCMP-001 Version
2.0 rest on it (Section 7). The study tests it where cell breakage could release host cell
protein and DNA, and where air-liquid interfaces could add aggregate. Fragments
continue to form while the clarified harvest is held before capture, and that hold is
outside this plan.

Host cell protein, DNA and aggregate enter harvest in the culture fluid and leave
it in the clarified harvest. The step is not credited with clearing them. The depth
filter media adsorb part of the DNA (Section 4.1), but no clearance is claimed for it.
Aggregate is reduced mainly at cation exchange. The three are in scope because the



                                               14
operation can add to them. Cell breakage adds host cell protein and DNA, and an
air-liquid interface in the feed zone or in a foaming transfer can unfold the antibody
and add aggregate. Aggregate has a higher criticality than host cell protein, but
an effect on it is less expected, because the antibody meets such an interface only
briefly. Residual DNA has very low criticality. It is measured because DNA fragments
and nucleosomes foul depth filters and Protein A resin, so it describes the condition
of the feed even where its own criterion is far from binding.
Fragments are clipped antibody, mostly cleaved in or near the hinge into Fab and
Fab+Fc species. Clipping starts in the reactor, where proteases released by dead
and lysing cells act on the secreted antibody. It continues in the clarified harvest,
because clarification removes the cells but not the proteases already in solution, and
its rate rises with temperature. A longer culture ends at lower viability and carries
more protease into harvest. The master plan PCMP-001 sets no criterion for frag-
ments at this step, and they are judged at the downstream steps (PCMP-001 Table
7.1). Fragments are not a response of the study in this plan.
The drug substance criteria in Table 4.1 are met by the clearance that the purification
train delivers after this step, and they are not in-process limits for the clarified harvest.
The criteria by which this step is judged are those of Section 7.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The parameters of this study and the study type of each follow the pre-characterization
risk assessment RA-001. RA-001 ranked each parameter on its potential to affect
a quality attribute and on its potential to interact with another parameter, and it
assigned a study type from the combined ranking. It brought 3 parameters of this
step into scope and assigned each of them to univariate study (Table 6.1). No param-
eter was ranked high enough to require a multivariate design, so no screening or
response-surface design is planned for this step (PCMP-001 §2). The other operating
conditions are held constant (Section 5.2). The feed rate follows the ratio of flow to
sigma factor, the depth filtration runs at constant flux, and the temperature is held
at the commercial set-point.
The centrifuge and the filters can raise a quality attribute in two ways. Cell breakage
releases host cell protein and DNA into the centrate, and an air-liquid interface can
unfold the antibody and raise the high molecular weight (HMW) content. The depth
filter media also adsorb part of the DNA, and the share that they take up can change
with the filter load. Clipping by the proteases in solution continues during the oper-
ation, as it does in the reactor and in the hold (Section 4.2). Protein A capture and
the two ion exchange steps clear the host cell protein and DNA that cell breakage
releases. A poorly chosen setting is therefore expected to load capture more heavily
without changing the drug substance.
The range studied extends beyond the NOR at both ends for centrifugation (rcf) and
depth filter load. For post-clarification turbidity the lower edge of the range studied
is the lower limit of the NOR, and only the upper edge extends beyond it. A range
that stops at the NOR limits cannot show where the operation begins to fail, and an



                                            15
excursion beyond a NOR limit would then be a deviation with no data behind it. The
wider ranges also cover settings that the receiving site may need if its equipment
differs from the development equipment.

5 Materials and methods

The materials and methods are those of Version 1.0. Where this amendment applies

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The characterization runs will be executed on a scale-down model of the harvest train,
qualified under the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001 before the first character-
ization run.
The model reproduces the commercial operation in three parts, and each part is
matched on the quantity that drives its own mechanism and not on the size of the
equipment. Matching on volume or on bowl diameter alone would leave the shear in
the feed zone, the settling in the disc stack and the loading of the filter media free to
differ between the scales. Cell damage in the feed zone will be reproduced in a bench
shear device operated at the energy dissipation rate calculated for the commercial
feed zone. Sedimentation will be reproduced in a laboratory centrifuge operated at
the same relative centrifugal field as the commercial bowl and at a matched ratio of
flow to sigma factor. The centrifugal field rises with the radius in the bowl, and the
sigma factor expresses the settling area of the disc stack, so a setting in g carries
across only together with the ratio of flow to sigma factor. Depth filtration will be
reproduced with disks of the commercial filter media, run at the same flux and to the
same load per unit area, so that a setting in L/m2 carries across in the same way.
Qualification will compare the model with the commercial train at the set-point condi-
tion on the same culture. The comparison will cover centrate turbidity, solids removal,
host cell protein and DNA in the clarified harvest, filter pressure at the end of the load,
and step yield. Replicate runs will be performed at each scale, which at commercial
scale are separate batches.
PCMP-001 §7.1 judges the comparison by the offset of each output, the mean of the
scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean of the commercial batches, with its two-
sided 90 % confidence interval. Step yield, centrate turbidity, solids removal and filter
pressure have no criterion at this step, so their offsets would only be recorded. Host
cell protein and DNA in the clarified harvest are judged against criterion 1 of Sec-
tion 7, and the turbidity of the clarified harvest against criterion 2, an absolute limit.
PCMP-001 §7.1 decides the qualification for such an output on the response-surface
model of the step, and this step has none. This plan therefore sets no qualification
rule for these three outputs, and PCR-004 states its approach. PCMP-001 §7.1 also




                                           17
asks each step plan to name the number of runs at each scale and the manufacturing-
scale data set. Version 1.0 named neither, and this amendment, issued after the study,
does not add them.
The comparison does not cover the turbidity of the clarified harvest, the size vari-
ants, or the column pressure and the cycle yield of the Protein A load challenge (Sec-
tion 6.2). Fragments are not a response of this study (Section 4.2).
A commercial disk-stack centrifuge discharges solids periodically and recirculates
part of its contents, and the number of passes an element of fluid makes through
the feed zone is not reproduced at bench scale. The model therefore represents the
field and the residence time well and the cumulative shear history less well, and it
is expected to under-represent lysis rather than to over-represent it. This study uses
the model to bound ranges and to establish the direction and the approximate size of
each effect. It does not use the model to predict the absolute impurity concentration
of a commercial batch.



5.2 Operation

Each characterization run will be executed as a single pass of the whole harvest
through the model train, in the order the commercial operation runs it. Runs will
be executed against the centrifugation procedure SOP-2005 and the depth and ster-
ile filtration procedure SOP-2006.
The culture will be taken from the production bioreactor scale-down model at the
end of culture, at the conditions characterized under the production bioreactor plan
PCP-003. Culture from the qualified bioreactor model matters here, because a culture
that ends at lower viability enters harvest with more host cell protein already released
into the medium and with more fragile cells. Both raise the impurity concentration
of the clarified harvest, whatever the centrifuge does. Where the volume allows, one
culture will be split between the levels of a parameter, so that the levels of a series
are compared on the same material.
The culture will be fed to the centrifuge model at the field setting for the run and at
the matched ratio of flow to sigma factor. The centrate will be collected, sampled, and
passed over the depth filter train at constant flux to the load per unit area for the run,
and then over a sterilizing-grade filter into the harvest container. Temperature will
be held at the commercial set-point throughout. The time between the end of culture
and the end of clarification will be held constant across the runs, since a longer hold
on unclarified culture allows further lysis and would confound the comparison the
study is making. Every parameter not varied in a run will be held at its set-point
(Table 6.1), apart from the bracket of the depth filter load series (Section 5.5).

5.3 Analytical methods

Four of the validated methods in Table 3.2 report the responses of the study. Turbid-
ity by nephelometry (AMV-3015) measures the fine particulate and colloidal material
that the filters pass, and it is the in-process measurement by which clarified harvest
is released to capture. Host cell protein by ELISA (AMV-3012) measures the impurity
burden of the clarified harvest, and it is the response through which the release of
host cell protein by cell breakage is tested (Section 4.1). Residual DNA by qPCR (AMV-
3014) measures the second product of cell breakage and the material that the depth
filter media adsorb. Size variants by SEC-HPLC (AMV-3011) measure the high molec-
ular weight content of the antibody before and after the step. Version 1.0 named no
method for the antibody concentration on which step yield rests, and this amendment
names none.

5.4 Sampling plan

Samples will be drawn at four points in every run. The points, the methods applied
at each and the purpose of each are listed in Appendix B (Table B.1).



                                                  19
The sample of unclarified culture is the reference for the whole study. Impurity re-
leased by the operation can only be seen as a difference from what entered it. That
sample will therefore be drawn from the production bioreactor scale-down model at
the end of culture, immediately before the feed to the centrifuge starts, and it will
be assayed in the same campaign as the samples that follow it. The centrate sample
separates the two stages of the operation. It shows what the centrifuge passed and
therefore what the depth filter was asked to remove, which the depth filter load series
varies through centrate produced at 6,000 g and at 12,000 g. The sample after depth
filtration and the sample after sterilizing-grade filtration are both clarified harvest,
and the second of them is the material capture receives.
Each level will be sampled once at each point. The set-point condition will be run in
replicate across the execution of the study, and those replicates provide the estimate
of run to run variability against which every effect is tested. Version 1.0 set no number
of replicates, and this amendment sets none.
Samples for size variants will be drawn at the first and the last point only, since the
step is not expected to change the size-variant profile. The centrate is not tested for
size variants, so a change found at the last point could not be placed at the centrifuge
or at the filters.



5.5 Statistical methods

Each response will be analysed against the parameter that was varied, by linear re-
gression on the levels run, with the replicates at the set-point condition providing an
independent estimate of error. Effects will be tested at α = 0.05, the campaign level
of PCMP-001 §6.2. Where the relationship is not linear over the range, a quadratic
term may be fitted, and the fit will be reported with the terms that were needed rather
than with the terms that were planned. Version 1.0 named no test for curvature, and
this amendment adds none.
Statistical significance and practical significance will be reported separately. A sig-
nificant slope will be reported together with the change it predicts across the normal
operating range of that parameter, and that change will be compared with the pre-
cision of the method that measured the response (Section 5.3). An effect smaller
than the method can resolve will be reported as detected and not material, with the
comparison shown.
No adequacy threshold is set for the regressions. The decisions of Section 7 rest
on the significance of a change, on its size against the resolution of the method, or
on an absolute limit, and not on the fit of a predictive model. Each regression will
therefore be reported with its coefficient of determination and its residual standard
error and will be judged on those. The model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2
apply to response-surface models, and none is fitted at this step.
Results below the limit of quantitation of a method will be reported as such and ex-
cluded from the regression, and the number excluded will be given with the fit.




                                           20
The design is one factor at a time and cannot estimate an interaction between two
parameters. An interaction between centrifugation (rcf) and depth filter load is ex-
pected, because the centrifugal field sets the solids that the centrate carries to the
depth filter. A higher field passes fewer cells and less coarse debris, but it may break
more cells into fine debris. That interaction will be bracketed and not estimated. The
depth filter load series will be run on centrate produced at 6,000 g and again on cen-
trate produced at 12,000 g, the two edges of the range studied for centrifugation (rcf).
The bracket bounds the filter response between the centrates of those two fields, and
it yields no coefficient for the interaction, so none will be reported.
No response-surface model will be fitted for this step and no Monte Carlo propaga-
tion of a fitted model will be performed, because no multivariate design is run here.
A range reported from this study therefore holds with the other parameters at their
set-points, and it says nothing about two of them moving away from their set-points
together. The single-batch prediction interval of PCMP-001 §6.2 belongs to analy-
ses built on a response-surface model and is not used. Each level is judged on its
measured results (Section 7).

6.2 Univariate assessment

Each parameter will be assessed at five levels. They are the lower edge of the range
studied, the lower limit of the normal operating range, the set-point, the upper limit
of the normal operating range and the upper edge of the range studied, and they
are listed in Appendix A (Table A.1). Three of the five are values that the control
strategy uses, so the study reports directly on the limits it is meant to justify. For



                                           22
post-clarification turbidity the lower edge of the range studied and the lower limit of
the NOR coincide, so its series has four distinct levels.
Runs will be executed in randomized order within each parameter series. A drift in
the culture, in the equipment or in an assay over the execution of a series would
otherwise be read as an effect of the parameter. Replicates at the set-point condition
will be distributed across a series rather than run together, for the same reason.
The centrifugation (rcf) series will be run first, because its outcome supplies the two
centrate qualities used to bracket the depth filter load series (Section 5.5). The depth
filter load series will follow. The turbidity series will be run last, because the material
for it is produced by the two series before it. Clarified harvest at a target turbidity
will be prepared by blending clarified harvest with retained centrate to the target
turbidity for the level, which allows the turbidity presented to capture to be varied
independently of the settings that produced it. Blended material carries the particle
size distribution of the centrate it was made from, and that is not identical to the
distribution a depth filter at the end of its capacity would pass.
The turbidity series will be loaded onto a scale-down Protein A column held at the
set-point conditions of the Protein A plan PCP-005, as a Protein A load challenge at
set-point conditions. The responses are the pressure developed across the column
over the load and the yield of the cycle. Version 1.0 set no criterion for these two
responses, and this amendment sets none. No conclusion about the capture step will
be drawn from these runs, and the characterization of capture itself is reported in
PCR-005.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

The master plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0 derives an intermediate acceptance criterion
for a step output by carrying the drug substance criterion back through the platform
worst case of every downstream step, with no margin (PCMP-001 §7.3). This step has
no intermediate acceptance criterion. The derivation records no platform worst case
for harvest, so it credits the step with no change in any attribute, and the criteria
of the production bioreactor are carried through harvest unchanged. For the same
reason the verification of platform worst cases (PCMP-001 §7.5) has no value to check
at this step. The criteria therefore rest on the assumption that harvest changes no
attribute (Section 4.1), and criteria 1 and 3 below test it.
The decision criteria below are those of Version 1.0, in its words. The impurity release
criterion (1) and the product quality criterion (3) compare results within a run, and
the clarification criterion (2) is an absolute limit. No result of this study enters them.

  1. Impurity release is the first criterion. Host cell protein and residual DNA in the
     clarified harvest will be compared with the same measurements on the unclari-
     fied culture that produced it. A level passes when no increase is detected at α =
     0.05, or when a detected increase is smaller than the resolution of the method
     that measured it (Section 5.3). A setting that releases more impurity than that
     lies outside the acceptable range of its parameter.
  2. Clarification is the second criterion and the absolute one. Post-clarification tur-
     bidity of the clarified harvest will be compared with the upper limit of its normal
     operating range, 10 NTU. A centrifuge setting or a filter load that delivers clari-
     fied harvest above that value has failed to clarify, whatever the impurity results
     show.
  3. Product quality is the third. High molecular weight content will be compared
     before and after the step, and a level passes when no increase is detected at α
     = 0.05.
  4. Step yield is reported but is not gated. Yield will be given for every level and
     compared with the set-point replicates. No numeric yield limit is set in this plan,
     because none follows from a product quality requirement at this step. A loss of
     yield at a level will be reported with its cause and will be weighed in the choice
     of the operating range. It will not on its own place a level outside the proven
     acceptable range.

A parameter meets the criteria over a range when every level in that range passes the
first three criteria. The proven acceptable range of the parameter is then that range,
bounded at the levels tested (Section 8). Where a level fails, the proven acceptable
range is bounded at the last level that passed, and the failure should be investigated
before the range is reported.




                                           24
A failure at a level inside the normal operating range means the operating range in
force is not supported by the data, and it should be raised to Manufacturing Science
and to Quality Assurance before the study continues, so that the normal operating
range or the control strategy can be changed under SOP-4001. Deviations from this
plan during execution will be recorded, investigated and reported in PCR-004 with
their impact on the conclusions, whether or not the affected runs are retained.
Work that this plan does not foresee, done without a departure from this plan or from
a procedure, is not a deviation. The paired report records it as a discrepancy from
the plan, the term this campaign uses for such work.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

The two PAR analyses of PCMP-001 §6.4, the set-point analysis and the NOR-
propagated analysis, both scan a factor of a response-surface model, and no such
model is fitted at this step. PCMP-001 §4 states that the range of a parameter studied
univariately is supported by its own data. The PAR of each parameter of this step will
therefore be determined from the levels tested, by the rule of Version 1.0 below.
A proven acceptable range is the range of a parameter over which the step has been
shown to meet its criteria, with every other parameter held at its set-point (Inter-
national Council for Harmonisation 2009). No combination of two parameters away
from their set-points is claimed for this step (Section 5.5). The bracketing arrange-
ment of the depth filter load series is the only place where the study looks at two
parameters together, and it bounds the response between the centrates of two fields
rather than modelling it.
A proven acceptable range is also bounded by the levels tested. The analysis will not
extrapolate beyond the edges given in Table 6.1. Where every level of a parameter
meets the criteria, the proven acceptable range will be reported as the whole range
studied and not as anything wider.
For each parameter, PCR-004 will give the range studied, the criterion applied, the
response that came closest to that criterion, and the proven acceptable range that
follows. The normal operating range will be shown nested inside it. Where the two
coincide at an edge, that will be stated, because a normal operating limit sitting on
the edge of the proven range leaves no margin for the ordinary variability of the
commercial operation.
No design space is claimed for this step. A design space is a multivariate region,
and this study runs no multivariate design. What Step 4 contributes to the control
strategy is a proven acceptable range for each parameter, a normal operating range
nested inside it, and an in-process control on the turbidity of the clarified harvest.
The classification of each parameter under the parameter classification procedure
SOP-4001 will be made in PCR-004 on the evidence this study produces, by the deci-
sion logic of PCMP-001 §7.7, and it will roll up into the master report PCMR-001 with
the other steps of the train. A critical process parameter (CPP) and a well-controlled
critical process parameter (WC-CPP) both affect a CQA, and they differ in the risk that
the parameter falls outside its acceptable range in operation. A key process parame-
ter (KPP) affects process performance and no CQA, and a general process parameter
(GPP) affects neither. Neither PCMP-001 nor SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold
for a practically relevant effect. For each parameter, PCR-004 will therefore state




                                          26
the change in each response across the NOR against the resolution of its method,
and the margin between the PAR and the NOR. It will give the reason for each class
from those two quantities.

12 Risks and assumptions

The following risks and assumptions bear on the conclusions of the study. Each is
given with its mitigation or its bound.

  1. The scale-down model. A continuous disk-stack centrifuge is the hardest unit in
     this process to represent at bench scale. The commercial centrifuge discharges
     solids periodically and recirculates part of its contents, so the fluid passes the
     feed zone more than once, and the model is expected to under-represent cumu-
     lative shear (Section 5.1). The mitigation is that the study reports effects and
     ranges and not absolute impurity levels. The qualification of Section 5.1 will com-
     pare the model with the commercial train on centrate turbidity, solids removal,
     host cell protein, DNA, filter pressure and step yield.
  2. The culture. A culture that ends at lower viability carries more debris and more
     broken cells into harvest, which raises both the solids load on the centrifuge and
     the host cell protein released before the run begins. Culture will therefore be
     taken from the qualified bioreactor scale-down model at its set-point condition,
     and the viability and the cell density of every culture used will be recorded with
     the run and reported in PCR-004.
  3. Material supply. One culture may not provide enough volume for all five levels
     of a parameter at the scale of the model, so a series may span more than one
     culture. Where it does, the set-point replicates run on each culture provide the
     reference for the levels run on that culture, and the analysis will fit the culture
     as a block.
  4. The blended material of the turbidity series. It carries the particle size distri-
     bution of centrate and not that of a saturated depth filter (Section 6.2). The
     turbidity range will therefore be reported as a range of turbidity delivered to
     capture, and no claim will be made about the specific material a filter at the end
     of its capacity would pass.
  5. Assay variability. It bounds the smallest effect the study can see. Criterion 1
     of Section 7 is judged against the resolution of the host cell protein and DNA
     methods (Table 5.1).
  6. The assumption that harvest has no impact on product quality. This plan keeps
     the assumption of the A-Mab case study (Section 4.1), and the criteria of PCMP-
     001 Version 2.0 rest on it. Criteria 1 and 3 of Section 7 test it for host cell protein
     and DNA released by cell breakage and for aggregate added at air-liquid inter-
     faces. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture, during which fragments
     continue to form, is outside this plan (Section 1).
