1 Purpose and scope

This plan defines the process characterization study of harvest and clarification,
Step 4 of the A-Mab drug substance process. It states which parameters are studied,
how the study is run and analysed, and the criteria by which its results are judged.
Version 2.0 amends Version 1.0 after the study ran (Section 1.1).
Harvest separates the antibody from the cells and the cell debris of the production cul-
ture. At commercial scale the step is run once per batch on the contents of a 15,000 L
production bioreactor. The culture passes a continuous disk-stack centrifuge, a train
of depth filters and a sterilizing-grade filter, in that order. The centrifuge and the fil-
ters are not expected to change a quality attribute of the antibody. Fragments form
in the culture and continue to form in the clarified harvest (Section 4.2). The step
determines the quality of the feed to Protein A capture. That feed should carry little
intact cell material and fine debris, and the operation should break few cells, because
broken cells release host cell protein and DNA into it.
In scope are the parameters of the step listed in Section 6.1. Each is studied one
factor at a time on a scale-down model of the harvest train (Section 5.1), over a range
that extends beyond its normal operating range (NOR). The responses are the impu-
rity burden of the clarified harvest, its particulate content, the pressure that the filter
train develops, the size-variant profile of the antibody across the step and the step
yield. In the turbidity series they also include the column pressure and the cycle yield
of a Protein A load challenge. The study supports the transfer of the drug substance
process from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug sub-
stance site in Grafton, WI. It forms part of the Stage 1 process design record of the
commercial process (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011).
Five things lie outside this plan:

  1. The culture conditions, which set the viability at the end of culture. Lower viabil-
     ity releases more host cell protein and DNA into the culture fluid before harvest.
     The culture conditions are characterized under the production bioreactor plan
     PCP-003 and are not varied here.
  2. The parameters of Protein A capture, which are characterized under the Protein
     A plan PCP-005. In this study capture serves only as a Protein A load challenge
     at set-point conditions (Section 6.2).
  3. The number and the arrangement of the depth filter stages, which the depth and
     sterile filtration procedure SOP-2006 fixes.
  4. Viral clearance. No clearance is claimed for this step, and no spiking study is
     included (International Council for Harmonisation 2023).
  5. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture. Its duration and its temperature
     are not parameters of this study.

2 Objectives

The study has five objectives.

  1. Qualify a scale-down model of the commercial harvest train and show that it rep-
     resents the centrifugation and depth filtration performance of that train (Sec-
     tion 5.1).
  2. Establish the effect of the centrifugal field, over 6,000 g to 12,000 g, on the
     clarity of the centrate and on the host cell protein and DNA released by cell
     breakage.
  3. Establish the effect of depth filter load, over 80 L/m2 to 160 L/m2, on the particu-
     late content of the clarified harvest and on the pressure the filter train develops.
  4. Establish whether the step alters the size-variant profile of the antibody, and
     establish the effect of post-clarification turbidity, over 1 NTU to 20 NTU, on the
     material presented to capture.
  5. Define a proven acceptable range for each parameter, determine whether the
     normal operating range lies inside the proven acceptable range, and provide
     the evidence on which each parameter will be classified under the parameter
     classification procedure SOP-4001 (corrected, change 7).

Classification is an outcome of the study and is reported in PCR-004.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

At commercial scale the culture is fed continuously to a disk-stack centrifuge. Cells
and debris settle under the centrifugal field, at a velocity that rises with the field and
with the difference between their density and the density of the medium. In the disc
stack a particle settles onto a disc, is captured there and slides outward to the bowl
wall, from which the solids are discharged at intervals (corrected, change 7). A higher
feed rate shortens the time the fluid spends in the stack, so that more of the small
particles escape capture. The centrate therefore still carries the fine debris and the
colloidal material that are too small to settle in the time available.
The depth filter train removes that material. Its media are positively charged, so they
adsorb part of the DNA and fine debris as well as retaining particles by size. The
filtrate then passes the sterilizing-grade filter into the harvest vessel, where it is held
until capture.
A higher centrifugal field clarifies more completely, because cells and debris settle
faster and fewer escape capture. It can also break more cells. The culture is ac-
celerated to the speed of the bowl in the feed zone, and the shear there rises with
that speed, while the flow between the discs is laminar and low in shear (corrected,
change 7). Broken cells release host cell protein and DNA into the centrate, and they
leave fine debris that is harder to capture. The set-point balances clarification against
cell breakage.
A depth filter has a finite capacity for the solids and colloids that the centrifuge passes.
As retained solids fill the void space of the matrix and occupy its charged sites, the
pressure drop across the filter rises and more fine material passes. At the extreme,
cells and debris are forced through the pores. How far the media are saturated de-
pends on the volume of centrate that each unit of media area has received and on
the solids that this volume carries. For this reason the parameter is expressed as the
volume of centrate loaded per unit of media area, in L/m2 (corrected, change 7).
Post-clarification turbidity measures the fine particulate and colloidal material that
the filters passed. It is not a set parameter. A higher centrifugal field lowers it,
because fewer cells and less debris reach the filters, and it can also raise it, because
more broken cells leave fine debris (corrected, change 7). It rises as the filter load
approaches the capacity of the media. It is monitored on every batch as an in-process
control. It is listed among the parameters of the step because the particulate and
colloidal load that it measures reaches the Protein A column, where it fouls the bed
and shortens the working life of the resin.




                                            13
Harvest by continuous centrifugation and depth filtration is the platform recovery
operation at Novacyte Biologics. It was used for three earlier antibodies (X-Mab, Y-
Mab and Z-Mab), and the equipment, the grade of the filter media and the control
scheme are unchanged for A-Mab. The mechanisms above are therefore expected to
hold for A-Mab, and the platform experience gives the approximate position of the
operating point. Host cell protein and DNA enter harvest at a concentration that
rises as the viability of the culture falls, because cells that died before harvest have
already released their contents into the medium. Viability at the end of culture is a
property of the A-Mab culture and is characterized under the production bioreactor
plan PCP-003. The ranges in this plan are therefore established on A-Mab material.
The A-Mab case study assumes, for simplicity, that harvest has no impact on product
quality, and it notes that this might not hold in a real process (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). This plan keeps that assumption, and the criteria of PCMP-001 Version
2.0 rest on it (Section 7). The study tests it where cell breakage could release host
cell protein and DNA, and where air-liquid interfaces could add aggregate.

Host cell protein, DNA and aggregate enter harvest in the culture fluid and leave
it in the clarified harvest. The step is not credited with clearing them. The depth
filter media adsorb part of the DNA (Section 4.1), but no clearance is claimed for
it. Aggregate is reduced mainly at cation exchange. The three are in scope because
shear and air-liquid interfaces in the centrifuge and the filters can break cells, which
adds host cell protein and DNA, and because an air-liquid interface in the feed zone or



                                               14
in a foaming transfer can unfold the antibody and add aggregate (corrected, change
7). Aggregate has a higher criticality than host cell protein, but an effect on it is less
expected, because the antibody meets such an interface only briefly. Residual DNA
has very low criticality. It is measured because DNA fragments and nucleosomes foul
depth filters and Protein A resin, so it describes the condition of the feed even where
its own criterion is far from binding.
Fragments are clipped antibody, mostly cleaved in or near the hinge into Fab and
Fab+Fc species. Clipping starts in the reactor, where proteases released by dead
and lysing cells act on the secreted antibody. It continues in the clarified harvest,
because clarification removes the cells but not the proteases already in solution, and
its rate rises with temperature. A longer culture ends at lower viability and carries
more protease into harvest. The master plan PCMP-001 sets no criterion for frag-
ments at this step, and they are judged at the downstream steps (PCMP-001 Table
7.1). Fragments are not a response of the study in this plan.
The drug substance criteria in Table 4.1 are to be met by the clearance that the purifi-
cation train delivers after this step, and they are not in-process limits for the clarified
harvest (corrected, change 7). The criteria by which this step is judged are those of
Section 7.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The parameters of this study and the study type of each follow the pre-characterization
risk assessment RA-001. RA-001 ranked each parameter on its potential to affect
a quality attribute and on its potential to interact with another parameter, and it
assigned a study type from the combined ranking. It brought 3 parameters of this
step into scope and assigned each of them to univariate study (Table 6.1). No param-
eter was ranked high enough to require a multivariate design, so no screening or
response-surface design is planned for this step (PCMP-001 §2). The other operating
conditions are held constant (Section 5.2). The feed rate follows the ratio of flow to
sigma factor, the depth filtration runs at constant flux, and the temperature is held
at the commercial set-point.
The depth filter media adsorb part of the DNA, and the share that they take up can
change with the filter load. Protein A capture and the two ion exchange steps clear
the host cell protein and DNA that cell breakage releases (Section 4.2). A poorly
chosen setting is therefore expected to load capture more heavily without changing
the drug substance.
The range studied extends beyond the NOR at both ends for centrifugation (rcf) and
depth filter load. For post-clarification turbidity the lower edge of the range studied
is the lower limit of the NOR, and only the upper edge extends beyond it (corrected,
change 7). A range that stops at the NOR limits cannot show where the operation
begins to fail, and an excursion beyond a NOR limit would then be a deviation with
no data behind it. The wider ranges also cover settings that the receiving site may
need if its equipment differs from the development equipment.

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The characterization runs will be executed on a scale-down model of the harvest train,
qualified under the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001 before the first character-
ization run.
The model reproduces the commercial operation in three parts, and each part is
matched on the quantity that drives its own mechanism and not on the size of the
equipment. Matching on volume or on bowl diameter alone would leave the shear
at the feed zone, the settling in the bowl and the loading of the filter media free to
differ between the scales. Cell damage in the feed zone will be reproduced in a bench
shear device operated at the energy dissipation rate calculated for the commercial
feed zone. Sedimentation will be reproduced in a laboratory centrifuge operated at
the same relative centrifugal field as the commercial bowl and at a matched ratio of
flow to sigma factor. At each field of the series the bench feed rate will be set so
that its ratio of flow to sigma factor equals that of the commercial centrifuge at the
same field and the commercial feed rate (corrected, change 7). Depth filtration will
be reproduced with disks of the commercial filter media, run at the same flux and to
the same load per unit area, so that a setting expressed in L/m2 carries across in the
same way.
Qualification will compare the model with the commercial train at the set-point condi-
tion on the same culture. The comparison will cover centrate turbidity, solids removal,
host cell protein and DNA in the clarified harvest, filter pressure at the end of the load,
and step yield. Replicate runs will be performed at each scale. At commercial scale
each replicate is a separate batch.
PCMP-001 §7.1 replaces the Version 1.0 acceptance criterion of this comparison
(change 2). It judges each output by its offset, the mean of the scale-down runs
at set-point minus the mean of the commercial batches, with its two-sided 90 %
confidence interval. Step yield, centrate turbidity, solids removal and filter pressure
have no criterion at this step, so their offsets would only be recorded. Host cell
protein and DNA are judged against the impurity release criterion (criterion 1 of
Section 7). PCMP-001 §7.1 decides the qualification of an output with a criterion
on the response-surface model of the step, which this step does not have. This plan
therefore sets no qualification rule, and PCR-004 states its approach. PCMP-001

manufacturing-scale data set. Version 1.0 named neither, and this amendment,
issued after the study, does not add them.
The turbidity of the clarified harvest, which the clarification limit of 10 NTU judges
(criterion 2), is not in the comparison, so its offset is unknown, not zero. The size
variants and the column pressure and the cycle yield of the Protein A load challenge
are not in the comparison either.
A continuous disk-stack centrifuge passes the feed through its feed zone once. The
cell damage that a bench model does not fully reproduce comes from the acceleration
of the feed in the feed zone, which the shear device matches only in its energy dissipa-
tion rate, and from the periodic discharge of solids (corrected, change 7). The model
therefore represents the field and the residence time well and that cell damage less
well, and it is expected to under-represent lysis rather than to over-represent it. This
study uses the model to bound ranges and to establish the direction and the approx-
imate size of each effect. It does not use the model to predict the absolute impurity
concentration of a commercial batch.



5.2 Operation

Each characterization run will be executed as a single pass of the whole harvest
through the model train, in the order the commercial operation runs it.
The culture will be taken from the production bioreactor scale-down model at the
end of culture, at the conditions characterized under the production bioreactor plan
PCP-003. Culture from the qualified bioreactor model matters here, because a culture
that ends at lower viability enters harvest with more host cell protein already released
into the medium and with more fragile cells. Both raise the impurity concentration
of the clarified harvest, whatever the centrifuge does. Where the volume allows, one
culture will be split between the levels of a parameter, so that the levels of a series
are compared on the same material.
The culture will be fed to the centrifuge model at the field setting for the run and at
the matched ratio of flow to sigma factor. The centrate will be collected, sampled, and
passed over the depth filter train at constant flux to the load per unit area for the run,
and then over a sterilizing-grade filter into the harvest container. Temperature will
be held at the commercial set-point throughout. The time between the end of culture
and the end of clarification will be held constant across the runs, since a longer hold
on unclarified culture allows further lysis and would confound the comparison the
study is making.
Every parameter not varied in a run will be held at its set-point, and the set-points
are those given in Table 6.1. Runs will be executed against the procedures listed in
Table 3.2.

5.4 Sampling plan

Samples will be drawn at four points in every run. The points, the methods applied
at each and the purpose of each are listed in Appendix B (Table B.1).
The sample of unclarified culture is the reference for the whole study. Impurity re-
leased by the operation can only be seen as a difference from what entered it, so that
sample will be drawn from the production bioreactor scale-down model at the end of
culture, immediately before the feed to the centrifuge starts, and it will be assayed in
the same campaign as the samples that follow it. The centrate sample separates the
two stages of the operation. It shows what the centrifuge passed and therefore what
the depth filter was asked to remove, which is the quantity that the depth filter load
runs on centrate from 6,000 g and from 12,000 g vary (Section 5.5). The sample af-
ter depth filtration and the sample after sterilizing-grade filtration are both clarified
harvest, and the second of them is the material capture receives.




                                                 18
Each level will be sampled once at each point. The set-point condition will be run in
replicate across the execution of the study, and those replicates provide the estimate
of run to run variability against which every effect is tested. Version 1.0 set no number
of replicates, and this amendment sets none.
Samples for size variants will be drawn at the first and the last point only. The antibody
meets air-liquid interfaces only briefly in the operation, so the step is not expected
to change the size-variant profile (Section 4.2). The centrate is not tested for size
variants, so a change found at the last point could not be placed at the centrifuge or
at the filters (corrected, change 7).



5.5 Statistical methods

Each response will be analysed against the parameter that was varied, by linear re-
gression on the levels run, with the replicates at the set-point condition providing an
independent estimate of error. Effects will be tested at α = 0.05, the significance
level set for the campaign (PCMP-001 §6.2). Where the relationship is not linear over
the range, a quadratic term may be fitted, and the fit will be reported with the terms
that were needed rather than with the terms that were planned. Version 1.0 named
no test for curvature, and this amendment adds none.
Statistical significance and practical significance will be reported separately. A sig-
nificant slope will be reported together with the change it predicts across the normal
operating range of that parameter, and that change will be compared with the pre-
cision of the method that measured the response (Section 5.3). An effect smaller
than the method can resolve will be reported as detected and not material, with the
comparison shown.
No adequacy threshold is set for the regressions. The decisions of Section 7 rest
on the significance of a change, on its size against the resolution of the method, or
on an absolute limit, and not on the fit of a predictive model (corrected, change 7).
Each regression will therefore be reported with its coefficient of determination and
its residual standard error and will be judged on those.
Results below the limit of quantitation of a method will be reported as such and ex-
cluded from the regression, and the number excluded will be given with the fit.
The design is one factor at a time. It cannot determine an interaction between two
parameters, where the effect of one parameter on a response depends on the level
of the other. One interaction is expected on mechanistic grounds, since a higher
centrifugal field passes fewer solids to the depth filter and so changes the load that
filter has to handle. That interaction will be bracketed and not estimated. The depth
filter load series will be run on centrate produced at 6,000 g and again on centrate
produced at 12,000 g, so that the filter is challenged with both extremes of the solids
load it can receive. The bracket bounds the filter response between the two solids
loads the centrifuge can deliver, and it yields no coefficient for the interaction, so
none will be reported.

range reported from this study therefore holds with the other parameters at their
set-points, and it says nothing about two of them moving away from their set-points
together.

6.2 Univariate assessment

Each parameter will be assessed at five levels. They are the lower edge of the range
studied, the lower limit of the normal operating range, the set-point, the upper limit
of the normal operating range and the upper edge of the range studied, and they are

listed in Appendix A (Table A.1). Three of the five are values that the control strategy
uses, so the study reports directly on the limits it is meant to justify (corrected, change
7). For post-clarification turbidity the lower edge of the range studied and the lower
limit of the NOR coincide, so its series has four distinct levels.
Runs will be executed in randomized order within each parameter series. A drift in
the culture, in the equipment or in an assay over the execution of a series would
otherwise be read as an effect of the parameter. Replicates at the set-point condition
will be distributed across a series rather than run together, for the same reason.
The centrifugation (rcf) series will be run first, because its outcome supplies the cen-
trate produced at 6,000 g and at 12,000 g on which the depth filter load series is run
(Section 5.5). The depth filter load series will follow. The turbidity series will be run
last, because the material for it is produced by the two series before it. Clarified har-
vest at a target turbidity will be prepared by blending clarified harvest with retained
centrate to the target turbidity for the level, which allows the turbidity presented to
capture to be varied independently of the settings that produced it. Blended material
carries the particle size distribution of the centrate it was made from, and that is not
identical to the distribution a depth filter at the end of its capacity would pass.
The turbidity series will be loaded onto a scale-down Protein A column held at the
set-point conditions of the Protein A plan PCP-005, as a Protein A load challenge. The
responses are the pressure developed across the column over the load and the yield
of the cycle. Version 1.0 set no criterion for these two responses, and this amendment
sets none. No conclusion about the capture step will be drawn from these runs, and
the characterization of capture itself is reported in PCR-005.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

The master plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0 derives an intermediate acceptance criterion
for a step output by carrying the drug substance criterion back through the platform
worst case of every downstream step, with no margin (PCMP-001 §7.3). PCMP-001
Table 7.1 sets no intermediate acceptance criterion for the clarified harvest, because
the derivation credits harvest with no change in any attribute, so the criteria of the
production bioreactor output pass through it unchanged. For the same reason the
verification of platform worst cases (PCMP-001 §7.5) has no value to check at this step.
Those criteria therefore rest on the assumption that harvest changes no attribute
(Section 4.1), which the impurity release criterion (1) and the product quality criterion
(3) below test.
The decision criteria below are those of Version 1.0, in its words. The impurity release
criterion (1) and the product quality criterion (3) compare results within a run, and
the clarification criterion (2) is an absolute limit. No result of the studies under this
plan was used to set or change these criteria.

  1. Impurity release is the first criterion. Host cell protein and residual DNA in the
     clarified harvest will be compared with the same measurements on the unclari-
     fied culture that produced it. A level passes when no increase is detected at α =
     0.05, or when a detected increase is smaller than the resolution of the method
     that measured it (Section 5.3). A setting that releases more impurity than that
     lies outside the acceptable range of its parameter.
  2. Clarification is the second criterion and the absolute one. Post-clarification tur-
     bidity of the clarified harvest will be compared with the upper limit of its normal
     operating range, 10 NTU. A centrifuge setting or a filter load that delivers clari-
     fied harvest above that value has failed to clarify, whatever the impurity results
     show.
  3. Product quality is the third. High molecular weight content will be compared
     before and after the step, and a level passes when no increase is detected at α
     = 0.05.
  4. Step yield is reported but is not gated. Yield will be given for every level and
     compared with the set-point replicates. No numeric yield limit is set in this plan,
     because none follows from a product quality requirement at this step. A loss of
     yield at a level will be reported with its cause and will be weighed in the choice
     of the operating range. It will not on its own place a level outside the proven
     acceptable range.

A parameter meets the criteria over a range when every level in that range passes the
first three criteria. The proven acceptable range of the parameter is then that range,
bounded at the levels tested (Section 8). Where a level fails, the proven acceptable




                                           23
range is bounded at the last level that passed, and the failure should be investigated
before the range is reported.
A failure at a level inside the normal operating range means the operating range in
force is not supported by the data, and it should be raised to Manufacturing Science
and to Quality Assurance before the study continues, so that the normal operating
range or the control strategy can be changed under SOP-4001. Deviations from this
plan during execution will be recorded, investigated and reported in PCR-004 with
their impact on the conclusions, whether or not the affected runs are retained.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

PCMP-001 §6.4 computes proven acceptable ranges from the response-surface model
of a step, once with the other parameters held at their set-points and once with them
drawn at random around their set-points. That method needs a response-surface
model (Section 5.5). PCMP-001 §4 states that the range of a parameter studied uni-
variately is supported by its own data, so the proven acceptable range of each param-
eter of this step will be determined from the levels tested, by the rule of Version 1.0
below.
A proven acceptable range is the range of a parameter over which the step has been
shown to meet its criteria, with every other parameter held at its set-point (Interna-
tional Council for Harmonisation 2009). A range established by varying one param-
eter at a time holds while the other parameters are at their set-points (Section 5.5),
and no combination of two parameters away from their set-points is claimed for this
step.
A proven acceptable range is also bounded by the levels tested. The analysis will not
extrapolate beyond the edges given in Table 6.1. Where every level of a parameter
meets the criteria, the proven acceptable range will be reported as the whole range
studied and not as anything wider.
Version 1.0 gave no size for the resolution of a method in the impurity release crite-
rion, no threshold for judging the regressions on their coefficient of determination
and residual standard error, no rule for the proven acceptable range of the post-
clarification turbidity series, and no rule for how the proven acceptable range of depth
filter load follows from its two series, on centrate from 6,000 g and from 12,000 g.
This amendment sets none of them, and PCR-004 states its approach to each.
For each parameter, PCR-004 will give the range studied, the criterion applied, the
response that came closest to that criterion, and the proven acceptable range that
follows. It will determine whether the normal operating range lies inside the proven
acceptable range (corrected, change 7). Where the two coincide at an edge, that will
be stated, because a normal operating limit sitting on the edge of the proven range
leaves no margin for the ordinary variability of the commercial operation.
No design space is claimed for this step. A design space is a multivariate region, and
this study runs no multivariate design. What the study is to provide to the control
strategy is a proven acceptable range for each parameter, the position of the normal
operating range against it, and an in-process control on the turbidity of the clarified
harvest (corrected, change 7).




                                          25
The classification of each parameter under SOP-4001 will be made in PCR-004 on the
evidence this study produces, and it will roll up into PCMR-001 with the other steps
of the train.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 sets the rule for that classification (change 5). An attribute is
affected by a parameter when the effect of the parameter on it is significant at the
significance level set for the campaign (PCMP-001 §6.2). A critical process parameter
(CPP) and a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP) both affect a CQA,
and they differ in the risk that the parameter falls outside its acceptable range in
operation. A key process parameter (KPP) affects process performance and no CQA,
and a general process parameter (GPP) affects neither (PCMP-001 §7.7). Neither
PCMP-001 nor SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold for a practically relevant effect.
PCR-004 will therefore state, for each parameter, the size of each effect across the
proven acceptable range and the margin of that range to the normal operating range,
and it will give the reason for each class from those two quantities.

12 Risks and assumptions

Five things could weaken the conclusions of this study, and each carries a mitigation
or a bound (Version 1.0). A sixth item states the assumption on which the study
rests.

  1. The scale-down model is the largest of them. A continuous disk-stack centrifuge
     is the hardest unit in this process to represent at bench scale, for the reason
     given in Section 5.1, and the model is expected to under-represent lysis (cor-
     rected, change 7). The mitigation is that the study reports effects and ranges
     rather than absolute impurity levels, and that the qualification of Section 5.1
     compares the model with the commercial train on the responses that matter
     here.
  2. The study assumes the culture is representative. A culture that ends at lower
     viability carries more debris and more broken cells into harvest, which raises
     both the solids load on the centrifuge and the host cell protein released before
     the run begins. Culture will therefore be taken from the qualified bioreactor
     scale-down model at its set-point condition, and the viability and the cell density
     of every culture used will be recorded with the run and reported in PCR-004.
  3. Material supply constrains what can be compared within a series. One culture
     may not provide enough volume for all five levels of a parameter at the scale
     of the model, so a series may span more than one culture. Where it does, the
     set-point replicates run on each culture provide the reference for the levels run
     on that culture, and the analysis will fit the culture as a block.
  4. The blended material of the turbidity series carries the particle size distribution
     of centrate and not that of a saturated depth filter (Section 6.2). The turbidity
     range will therefore be reported as a range of turbidity delivered to capture, and
     no claim will be made about the specific material a filter at the end of its capacity
     would pass.
  5. Assay variability bounds the smallest effect the study can see (Table 5.1).
  6. The assumption that harvest has no impact on product quality (Section 4.1). The
     impurity release criterion and the product quality criterion of Section 7 test it,
     for host cell protein and DNA released by cell breakage and for aggregate added
     at air-liquid interfaces.
