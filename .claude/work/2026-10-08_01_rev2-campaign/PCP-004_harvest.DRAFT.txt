1 Purpose and scope

This plan defines the process characterization study of harvest and clarification,
Step 4 of the A-Mab drug substance process. It states which parameters are studied,
how the study is run and analysed, and the criteria by which its results are judged.
Version 2.0 amends Version 1.0 after the study ran (Section 1.1).
Harvest separates the antibody from the cells and the cell debris of the production cul-
ture. At commercial scale the step is run once per batch on the contents of a 15,000 L
production bioreactor. The culture passes a continuous disk-stack centrifuge, a train
of depth filters and a sterilizing-grade filter, in that order. The step forms no quality
attribute of the antibody. What it decides is the condition of the feed to Protein A
capture. That condition is the amount of intact cell material and fine debris in the
clarified harvest, and the amount of host cell protein and DNA released by cells that
break during the operation and not in the reactor.
In scope are the parameters of the step listed in Section 6.1. Each is studied one
factor at a time on a scale-down model of the harvest train (Section 5.1), over a range
that extends beyond its normal operating range (NOR). The responses are the impu-
rity burden of the clarified harvest, its particulate content, the size-variant profile of
the antibody across the step and the step yield. The study supports the transfer of the
drug substance process from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the commer-
cial drug substance site in Grafton, WI. It forms part of the Stage 1 process design
record of the commercial process (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011).
Five things lie outside this plan:

  1. The culture conditions that set the impurity burden entering harvest. They are
     characterized under the production bioreactor plan PCP-003 and are not varied
     here.
  2. The parameters of Protein A capture, which are characterized under the Protein
     A plan PCP-005. Capture is used in this study only as a fixed test of the material
     that harvest delivers (Section 6.2).
  3. The number and the arrangement of the depth filter stages, which the clarifica-
     tion procedure SOP-2006 fixes.
  4. Viral clearance. No clearance is claimed for this step, and no spiking study is
     included (International Council for Harmonisation 2023).
  5. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture. Its duration and its temperature
     are not parameters of this study.

2 Objectives

The study has five objectives.

  1. To compare the scale-down model with the commercial harvest train at set-point,
     and to record the offset of each output compared (Section 5.1).
  2. To measure the effect of centrifugation (rcf), from 6,000 to 12,000 g, on the
     turbidity of the centrate and on the host cell protein and DNA released by cell
     breakage.
  3. To measure the effect of depth filter load, from 80 to 160 L/m2, on the particulate
     content of the clarified harvest and on the pressure that the filter train develops.
  4. To establish whether the step changes the high molecular weight (HMW) content
     of the antibody, and to measure the effect of post-clarification turbidity, from 1
     to 20 NTU, on the material presented to capture.
  5. To determine a PAR for each parameter, to check that the NOR lies inside it,
     and to provide the evidence on which each parameter is classified under the
     parameter classification procedure SOP-4001.

Classification is an outcome of the study and is reported in PCR-004. This plan clas-
sifies no parameter and states no result.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

At commercial scale the culture is fed continuously to a disk-stack centrifuge. A cell
settles toward the bowl wall at a velocity proportional to the centrifugal field and to
the difference between its own density and the density of the medium. A higher field
therefore moves every cell and every particle of debris outward faster. A higher feed
rate shortens the time the fluid spends in the bowl, and a particle that has not reached
the wall when the fluid leaves is carried out in the centrate. The centrate therefore
still carries the fine debris and the colloidal material that are too small to settle in the
time available.
The depth filter train removes that material. Its media are positively charged, so
they adsorb DNA and fine debris as well as retaining particles by size. The filtrate
then passes the sterilizing-grade filter into the harvest vessel, where it is held until
capture.
Two mechanisms limit the centrifuge, and they act in opposite directions. Shear in
the feed zone and at the disc stack rises with the speed of the bowl. Cells damaged by
that shear release host cell protein and DNA into the centrate. A higher field therefore
clarifies more completely and also breaks more cells. The set-point is a compromise
between the two, and the centrifugation (rcf) series of Section 6.2 is designed to show
where each side of that compromise begins to matter.
A depth filter has a finite capacity for the solids and colloids that the centrifuge passes.
As retained solids fill the void space of the matrix and occupy its charged sites, the
pressure drop rises and more fine material passes. At the extreme, cells and debris
are forced through the pores. How far the media are saturated depends on the mass
of solids that each unit of media area has received. For this reason the parameter is
expressed as load per unit area, in L/m2.
Post-clarification turbidity measures the fine particulate and colloidal material that
the filters passed. It is not a set parameter. It is a property of the clarified harvest,
which the centrifugal field and the filter load produce together. It is monitored on
every batch as an in-process control, and it is listed among the parameters of the
step because its value bounds what capture receives. The material it measures fouls
the Protein A column and shortens the working life of the resin.
Harvest by continuous centrifugation and depth filtration is the platform recovery
operation at Novacyte Biologics. It was used for three earlier licensed antibodies
(X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab), and the equipment, the grade of the filter media and the




                                            12
control scheme are unchanged for A-Mab. The mechanisms above are therefore ex-
pected to hold for A-Mab, and the platform experience gives the approximate position
of the operating point. Host cell protein and DNA enter harvest at a concentration
that rises as the viability of the culture falls, because cells that died before harvest
have already released their contents into the medium. Viability at the end of culture
is a property of the A-Mab culture and is characterized under PCP-003. The ranges
in this plan are therefore established on A-Mab material.
The A-Mab case study assumes, for simplicity, that harvest has no impact on product
quality, and it notes that this might not hold in a real process (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). This plan keeps that assumption. The intermediate acceptance criteria
of PCMP-001 Version 2.0 rest on it as well, because their derivation credits this step
with no change in any attribute (Section 7). The study tests the assumption at the
two points where the mechanisms above could make it untrue. These are host cell
protein and DNA released by lysis, and aggregate formed by shear.

None of the four is formed by the centrifuge or the filters. Host cell protein and DNA
enter harvest in the culture fluid and leave it in the clarified harvest. The step does
not clear them and is not credited with clearing them. Aggregate is formed upstream
and is reduced mainly at cation exchange. Host cell protein, DNA and aggregate are
nevertheless in scope, because the operation can add to the first two by lysis and,
in principle, to the third by shear at the feed zone or at an air-liquid interface in a
foaming transfer.
Fragments are listed because the drug substance CQA register assigns them to this
step. Fragments are clipped antibody, mostly cleaved in or near the hinge into Fab




                                                  13
and Fab+Fc species. Clipping starts in the reactor, where proteases released by dead
and lysing cells act on the secreted antibody. It continues in the clarified harvest, be-
cause clarification removes the cells but not the proteases already in solution, and
its rate rises with temperature. A longer culture ends at lower viability and carries
more protease into harvest. The master plan PCMP-001 sets no criterion for frag-
ments at this step, and they are judged at the downstream steps (PCMP-001 Table
7.1). Fragments are not a response of the study in this plan.
The criteria in Table 4.1 apply to the drug substance. They are met by the clearance
that the purification train delivers after this step, and they are not in-process limits
for the clarified harvest. PCMP-001 derives no intermediate acceptance criterion for
an output of this step. The criteria by which the step is judged are given in Section 7,
and only one of them is an absolute limit.
Host cell protein and aggregate carry most of the weight. Host cell protein is the
response through which the lysis mechanism of Section 4.1 is tested, and it is the
attribute that rises most when cells break during the operation. Aggregate carries
a higher criticality than host cell protein but a much lower expectation of an effect,
since the exposure to shear at this step is brief. Residual DNA has very low criticality
as a drug substance attribute. It is measured because DNA fragments and nucleo-
somes foul depth filters and Protein A resin, so it describes the condition of the feed
even where its own criterion is far from binding.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The parameters of this study and the study type of each follow the pre-characterization
risk assessment RA-001. RA-001 ranked each parameter of the step on its potential
to affect a quality attribute and on its potential to interact with another parameter,
and it assigned a study type from the combined ranking. All 3 parameters of the step
were assigned univariate study, as the study type column of Table 6.1 records. No
parameter was ranked high enough to require a multivariate design. For this reason
no screening or response-surface design is planned for this step (PCMP-001 §2).
The step forms no quality attribute, so a parameter of the step can reach an attribute
in only two ways. It can break cells, which raises host cell protein and DNA in the clar-
ified harvest. It can also unfold the antibody at a shear or air-liquid interface, which
raises the HMW content. Both mechanisms act in one direction only, and neither can
lower an attribute that the step does not form. In addition, Protein A capture and
the two ion exchange steps clear the impurities that lysis releases. A poorly chosen
setting is therefore expected to load capture more heavily without changing the drug
substance.
The range studied extends beyond the NOR at both ends for centrifugation (rcf) and
depth filter load. For post-clarification turbidity the lower edge of the range studied
is the lower limit of the NOR, and only the upper edge extends beyond it. A range
that stops at the NOR limits cannot show where the operation begins to fail, and an
excursion beyond a NOR limit would then be a deviation with no data behind it. The
wider ranges also cover settings that the receiving site may need if its equipment

differs from the development equipment, which the transfer requires of this study
(International Council for Harmonisation 2008).

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The characterization runs will be made on a scale-down model of the harvest train. Its
qualification runs are made under the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001 before
the first characterization run.
The model reproduces each stage of the commercial operation on the quantity that
drives its mechanism, not on the size of the equipment. Matching on volume or on
bowl diameter alone would leave the shear in the feed zone, the settling in the bowl
and the loading of the filter media free to differ between the scales. Cell damage in
the feed zone is reproduced in a bench shear device run at the energy dissipation
rate calculated for the commercial feed zone. Settling is reproduced in a laboratory
centrifuge run at the same relative centrifugal field as the commercial bowl and at a
matched ratio of flow to sigma factor. A setting in g therefore means the same thing at
both scales. Depth filtration is reproduced with disks of the commercial filter media,
run at the same flux and to the same load per unit area, so that a setting in L/m2
carries across in the same way.
The qualification compares the model with the commercial train at the set-point con-
dition, on the same culture, with replicate runs at each scale. The outputs compared
are the turbidity of the centrate, the removal of solids, host cell protein and DNA in
the clarified harvest, the filter pressure at the end of the load and the step yield.
The comparison is judged by the offset rule of the master plan PCMP-001 §7.1. The
offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean
of the commercial batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval. Under that
rule a model is qualified for an output when its conclusion against the criterion of
that output over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of the interval.
No output of this step has an intermediate acceptance criterion (Section 7). For an
output with no criterion at the step, PCMP-001 §7.1 requires only that the offset be
recorded, and no conclusion depends on it. The offset of each output compared is
therefore recorded, and the paired report PCR-004 states its cause where the cause
is known.
The qualification does not compare three outputs at both scales. These are the tur-
bidity of the clarified harvest, the HMW content and fragments, and their offsets are
unknown, not zero. Fragments are not a response of this study (Section 4.2). The
HMW content is compared before and after the step within each run (Section 7), so
its representativeness rests on the shear device and the energy dissipation rate at




                                          16
which it is run. The turbidity of the clarified harvest is judged against an absolute cri-
terion as the model gives it. Its representativeness rests on the depth filtration stage,
which uses the commercial media at the commercial flux and load per unit area.
A commercial disk-stack centrifuge discharges solids at intervals and recirculates
part of its contents. The number of passes that the fluid makes through the feed zone
is therefore not reproduced at bench scale. The model represents the field and the
residence time well and the cumulative shear history less well, and it is expected
to understate lysis. This study uses the model to bound ranges and to establish the
direction and the approximate size of each effect. It does not use the model to predict
the absolute impurity concentration of a commercial batch.



5.2 Operation

Each characterization run will be a single pass of the whole harvest through the model
train, in the order of the commercial operation. The runs follow the centrifugation
procedure SOP-2005 and the clarification procedure SOP-2006.
The culture will be taken from the production bioreactor scale-down model at the end
of culture, at the conditions characterized under PCP-003. The source of the culture
matters, because a culture that ends at lower viability enters harvest with more host
cell protein already in the medium and with more fragile cells. Both raise the impu-
rity concentration of the clarified harvest, whatever the centrifuge does. Where the
volume allows, one culture will be split between the levels of a parameter, so that the
levels of a series are compared on the same material.
The culture will be fed to the centrifuge model at the field of the run and at the
matched ratio of flow to sigma factor. The centrate will be collected and sampled. It
will then pass the depth filter train at constant flux to the load per unit area of the run,
and finally a sterilizing-grade filter into the harvest container. Temperature will be
held at the commercial set-point throughout. The time from the end of culture to the
end of clarification will be the same in every run, since a longer hold of unclarified
culture allows further lysis and would confound the comparison. Every parameter
not varied in a run will be held at its set-point (Table 6.1).



5.3 Analytical methods

Four of the validated methods in Table 3.2 report the responses of the study. Turbidity
by nephelometry (AMV-3015) measures the fine particulate and colloidal material that
the filters pass. It is also the in-process measurement by which clarified harvest is
released to capture. Host cell protein by ELISA (AMV-3012) measures the impurity
burden of the clarified harvest, and it is the response that tests the lysis mechanism
of Section 4.1. Residual DNA by qPCR (AMV-3014) measures the second product of
lysis and the material that the depth filter media adsorb. Size variants by SEC-HPLC
(AMV-3011) measure the HMW content of the antibody before and after the step.

The sample of unclarified culture is the reference for the whole study. Impurity re-

sample will therefore be drawn from the production bioreactor scale-down model at
the end of culture, immediately before the feed to the centrifuge starts. It will be

Each level will be sampled once at each point. The set-point condition will be run in

will be drawn at the first and the last point only. The step is not expected to change
the size-variant profile, and the intermediate points would not locate a change if one
were found.



5.5 Statistical methods

Each response will be analysed against the parameter that was varied, by linear re-
gression over the levels run. The replicates at the set-point condition give an inde-
pendent estimate of error. Effects are tested at α = 0.05, the campaign level of the
master plan PCMP-001 §6.2. Where the relationship is not linear over the range, a
quadratic term may be fitted, and the fit will be reported with the terms that were
needed.
Statistical significance and practical significance will be reported separately. A sig-
nificant slope will be reported with the change it predicts across the NOR of the
parameter. That change will be compared with the precision of the method that mea-
sured the response (Section 5.3). An effect smaller than the method can resolve will
be reported as detected and not material, with the comparison shown.
No adequacy threshold is set for these regressions. The decisions of Section 7 rest on
the size of an effect against the precision of its method, not on the fit of a predictive
model. Each regression will therefore be reported with its coefficient of determina-
tion and its residual standard error. The model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2
apply to response-surface models, and none is fitted at this step.
Results below the LOQ of a method will be reported as “< LOQ” and excluded from the
regression, and the number excluded will be given with the fit. Residual DNA is the
response most likely to be affected, because the depth filter media adsorb DNA and
the assay then reports a small quantity. Where one culture does not provide enough
volume for a whole series, the series will span more than one culture (Section 12).
The culture will then be fitted as a block, and the set-point replicates run on each
culture will be the reference for the levels run on it.
The design is one factor at a time. It cannot determine an interaction between two
parameters, where the effect of one parameter on a response depends on the level of




                                           19
the other. One interaction is expected from the mechanism. A higher centrifugal field
passes fewer solids to the depth filter, and so it changes the load that the filter has to
handle. That interaction will be bracketed and not estimated. The depth filter load
series will be run on centrate produced at 6,000 g and again on centrate produced at
12,000 g, the two edges of the range studied for centrifugation (rcf). The filter is then
challenged with both extremes of the solids load it can receive. The bracket bounds
the response of the filter between those two loads and yields no coefficient for the
interaction, so none will be reported.
No response-surface model is fitted for this step, and no fitted model is propagated
by Monte Carlo, because no multivariate design is run. The single-batch prediction
interval of PCMP-001 §6.2 belongs to analyses built on a response-surface model and
is not used here. Each level is judged on its measured results (Section 7). A range
reported from this study therefore holds with the other parameters at their set-points.
It says nothing about two parameters moving away from their set-points together.

Runs will be made in random order within each parameter series. Replicates at the
set-point condition will be spread across a series and not run together. A drift in the
culture, the equipment or an assay during a series would otherwise be read as an
effect of the parameter.

qualities that bracket the depth filter load series (Section 5.5). The depth filter load

will be prepared by blending clarified harvest with retained centrate to the turbidity
of the level. The turbidity presented to capture can then be varied independently of
the settings that produced it. Blended material carries the particle size distribution
of the centrate it was made from. That distribution is not the same as the one that a
depth filter at the end of its capacity would pass.
The post-clarification turbidity series will be loaded onto a scale-down Protein A col-
umn held at the set-point conditions of the Protein A plan PCP-005. The responses
are the pressure developed across the column over the load and the yield of the cycle.
No conclusion about the capture step will be drawn from these runs. Capture is used
here as a fixed test of the material that harvest delivers, and its own characterization
is reported in PCR-005.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

This step has no intermediate acceptance criterion. The master plan PCMP-001 Ver-
sion 2.0 derives intermediate acceptance criteria by carrying each drug substance
criterion back through the platform worst case of every downstream step, with no
margin (PCMP-001 §7.3). It derives none for an output of this step. The derivation
records no platform worst case for this step, so it credits the step with no change in
any attribute. The criteria of the production bioreactor are therefore carried through
harvest unchanged, and the verification of platform worst cases (PCMP-001 §7.5) has
nothing to check at this step. The drug substance criteria of Table 4.1 are not in-
process limits for the clarified harvest (Section 4.2).
The decision criteria below are those of Version 1.0. Criteria 1 and 3 compare re-
sults within a run, and criterion 2 is absolute. Step yield is reported, but it is not a
criterion.

  1. Impurity release. Host cell protein and residual DNA in the clarified harvest
     will be compared with the same measurements on the unclarified culture that
     produced it. A level passes when no increase is detected at α = 0.05, or when
     a detected increase is smaller than the precision of the method that measured
     it (Section 5.3). A setting that releases more impurity than that lies outside the
     acceptable range of its parameter.
  2. Clarification. The turbidity of the clarified harvest will be compared with the up-
     per NOR limit of post-clarification turbidity, 10 NTU. A setting of centrifugation
     (rcf) or of depth filter load that delivers clarified harvest above that value has
     failed to clarify, whatever the impurity results show.
  3. Product quality. The HMW content will be compared before and after the step,
     and a level passes when no increase is detected at α = 0.05. A null result is
     required here, not a bounded one. Aggregate formed at harvest would be carried
     into capture and would use up part of the aggregate clearance with which the
     criteria credit cation exchange.
  4. Step yield. Yield will be reported for every level and compared with the set-point
     replicates. No numerical yield limit is set, because none follows from a product
     quality requirement at this step. A loss of yield at a level will be reported with
     its cause and weighed in the choice of the operating range. It will not by itself
     place a level outside the PAR.

The criteria use the NOR of Table 6.1, the significance level of PCMP-001 and the
validated precision of each method. No result of this study enters them.
A parameter meets the criteria over a range when every level in that range passes
criteria 1 to 3. The PAR of the parameter is then that range, bounded at the levels




                                          23
tested (Section 8). Where a level fails, the PAR is bounded at the last level that passed,
and the failure should be investigated before the range is reported.
A failure at a level inside the NOR means that the data do not support the NOR in
force. It should be raised to Manufacturing Science and to Quality Assurance before
the study continues, so that the NOR or the control strategy can be changed under
the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001. A deviation during execution will
be recorded, investigated and reported in PCR-004 with its impact on the conclusions,
whether or not the affected runs are retained. Work that this plan does not foresee,
made without a departure from a procedure, is not a deviation. PCR-004 records it
as a discrepancy from the plan.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

The two PAR analyses of the master plan PCMP-001 §6.4 evaluate a response-surface
model, and no such model is fitted at this step. PCMP-001 §4 states that the range of
a parameter studied univariately is supported by its own data. The PAR of each pa-
rameter of this step will therefore be determined from the levels tested, as follows.
A PAR is the range of a parameter over which the step has been shown to meet its
criteria, with every other parameter held at its set-point (International Council for
Harmonisation 2009). A range found by varying one parameter at a time holds only
while the other parameters are at their set-points (Section 5.5). No combination of
two parameters away from their set-points is claimed for this step. The bracketing in
the depth filter load series is the only place where the study looks at two parameters
together, and it bounds the response between two solids loads without modelling it.
A PAR is also bounded by the levels tested. The analysis will not extrapolate beyond
the edges of the range studied (Table 6.1). Where every level of a parameter meets the
criteria, the PAR will be reported as the whole range studied and as nothing wider.
This agrees with PCMP-001 §7.6, under which a PAR that reaches the edge of the
characterized range is reported as reaching it.
For each parameter, PCR-004 will give the range studied, the criterion applied, the
response that came closest to its criterion and the PAR that follows. The NOR will be
shown inside the PAR. Where the two meet at an edge, PCR-004 will say so, because
a NOR limit on the edge of the PAR leaves no margin for the ordinary variation of the
commercial operation.
No design space is claimed for this step. A design space is a multivariate region, and
this study runs no multivariate design. The contribution of the step to the control
strategy is a PAR for each parameter, a NOR inside it, and an in-process control on
the turbidity of the clarified harvest.
Each parameter will be classified in PCR-004 under the parameter classification pro-
cedure SOP-4001, by the decision logic of PCMP-001 §7.7. A critical process param-
eter (CPP) and a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP) both affect a
CQA, and they differ in the risk that the parameter falls outside its acceptable range
in operation. A key process parameter (KPP) affects process performance and no
CQA, and a general process parameter (GPP) affects neither. Neither PCMP-001 nor
SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold for a practically relevant effect. For each pa-
rameter, PCR-004 will therefore state the change in each response across the NOR
against the precision of its method, and the margin between the PAR and the NOR.

12 Risks and assumptions

The following risks and assumptions bear on the conclusions of the study. Each is
given with its mitigation or its bound.

  1. The scale-down model. A continuous disk-stack centrifuge is the hardest unit
     of this process to represent at bench scale, for the reason given in Section 5.1,
     and the model is expected to understate cumulative shear. The study therefore
     reports effects and ranges and not absolute impurity levels. The qualification
     compares the model with the commercial train on the turbidity of the centrate,
     host cell protein, DNA, filter pressure and step yield, and the offset of each is
     recorded.
  2. The culture. A culture that ends at lower viability carries more debris and more
     broken cells into harvest. That raises both the solids load on the centrifuge and
     the host cell protein released before the run begins. The culture will therefore be
     taken from the production bioreactor scale-down model at its set-point condition.
     The viability and the cell density of every culture used will be recorded with the
     run and reported in PCR-004.
  3. Material supply. One culture may not provide enough volume for every level of a
     parameter at the scale of the model, so a series may span more than one culture.
     The set-point replicates run on each culture are then the reference for the levels
     run on it, and the culture is fitted as a block (Section 5.5).
  4. The blended material of the post-clarification turbidity series. It carries the parti-
     cle size distribution of centrate, not that of a saturated depth filter (Section 6.2).
     The turbidity range will therefore be reported as a range of turbidity delivered
     to capture. No claim will be made about the material that a filter at the end of
     its capacity would pass.
  5. Assay variation. It bounds the smallest effect that the study can see. The host cell
     protein ELISA is the less precise of the two methods in Table 5.1, and criterion
     1 of Section 7 is written against its precision, not against a fixed percentage.
     Residual DNA falls close to the LOQ of the qPCR assay once the depth filter
     media have adsorbed part of it, and a result below the LOQ carries no magnitude
     to regress.
  6. The assumption that harvest has no impact on product quality. This plan keeps
     the assumption of the A-Mab case study (Section 4.1), and the intermediate ac-
     ceptance criteria of PCMP-001 rest on it. Criteria 1 and 3 of Section 7 test it
     for host cell protein and DNA released by lysis and for aggregate formed by
     shear. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture, during which fragments
     continue to form, is outside this plan (Section 1).
