1 Purpose and scope

This plan is the protocol for the process characterization of the Protein A chromatog-
raphy step (Step 5) of the A-Mab drug substance process. It applies the Process
Characterization Master Plan PCMP-001 to this step. It adds what is specific to the
step, such as the scale-down model, the factors and their ranges, the sampling plan
and the values of the acceptance criteria. Where this plan says nothing of its own,
PCMP-001 applies.
Protein A chromatography is the capture step of the A-Mab purification train. The
resin binds the antibody from the clarified harvest, and most of the host cell protein
and DNA leave the column in the flow-through and the wash. The antibody is then
eluted at low pH and passed to low-pH viral inactivation (Step 6) without neutraliza-
tion. The step adds one impurity of its own, which is Protein A ligand that leaches
from the resin.
The characterization covers all 6 process parameters of the step. Of these, 4 are
studied in a screening design followed by a response-surface design, and 2 are studied
one factor at a time. The plan also covers a hold study of the Protein A eluate at
elution pH. All studies are run in a scale-down model of the commercial column. For
pool host cell protein and leached Protein A, which the qualification of the model
compares at both scales, each result is translated to manufacturing scale before it is
judged against a criterion (Section 5.1). The commercial process is run at the 15,000
L scale at the receiving site in Grafton, WI, which is the scale this characterization
supports under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-001.
PCR-005, the Process Characterization Report for this step, will report the results.
It will give the proven acceptable ranges (PARs) of the multivariate parameters for
each attribute of the Protein A pool with a criterion, the data for the classification of
every parameter of the step, and the conditions under which the eluate may be held.
Although Protein A chromatography removes some virus, no viral clearance claim is
made for this step (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The viral clearance claim of
the process rests on low-pH inactivation, anion exchange and virus filtration (PCMP-
001, viral clearance claims), and no spiking study is planned here. The life cycle of
the resin (its re-use limit, cleaning and sanitization) is governed by the resin life-cycle
procedure SOP-2008 and is outside the scope of this plan.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

Protein A capture is an affinity step operated in bind and elute mode. Staphylococcal
Protein A, immobilized on the resin, binds the Fc region of the antibody at neutral
pH. The contact sits at the interface of the second and third constant domains of
the heavy chain. Host cell protein, DNA and most other components of the clarified
harvest do not bind and are removed in the load flow-through and the wash. When
the pH is lowered, histidine residues at the Fc–ligand interface become protonated,
the interaction weakens, and the antibody is released into a small volume of low-pH
eluate.
The same capture step has been used to purify the platform antibodies X-Mab, Y-Mab
and Z-Mab, and A-Mab through clinical manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). Five mechanisms set the expectations for this study.

  1. Host cell protein reaches the eluate by two routes. Some binds to the resin back-
     bone or to the ligand. Some associates with the antibody through electrostatic
     and hydrophobic interactions, which the wash does not disrupt while the anti-
     body stays bound to the ligand. A lower elution pH disrupts more of both kinds
     of interaction and releases more host cell protein with the product, so pool host
     cell protein is expected to rise as elution buffer pH falls. In the A-Mab case study
     pool host cell protein rose with protein load and as elution pH fell (CMC Biotech
     Working Group 2009), and the same directions are expected here. Pool host cell
     protein is reported per mass of antibody, and host cell protein that travels with
     the antibody rises in proportion to it, so that route alone does not explain a rise
     with load. No mechanism for the rise with load is assumed in this plan. Both
     parameters act on the host cell protein that is eluted, so an interaction between
     them is possible, and the designs can estimate it.
  2. Step yield is lost when antibody breaks through during the load or remains on
     the column at the end of collection. Breakthrough rises as the load approaches
     the dynamic binding capacity and as the residence time falls, because binding is
     limited by diffusion into the pores of the bead. A higher load flow rate shortens
     the residence time and lowers the dynamic binding capacity. At high load the
     bed is then close to its capacity, breakthrough starts earlier and yield falls. At
     low load the bed has capacity to spare and flow rate has little effect. Yield is
     therefore expected to fall with flow rate mainly at high load. Extending the end
     of pool collect recovers the tail of the elution peak and raises yield.
  3. Leached Protein A comes from hydrolysis of the coupling chemistry and from
     proteolysis of exposed ligand. The ligand fragments still bind Fc and elute with
     the antibody. The amount depends on the coupling chemistry, the cycle number



                                          12
     and the sanitization history of the resin, which are properties of the resin over
     its life and not of a single run. It also depends on how long the load is held before
     capture, because proteolytic activity in the clarified harvest is expected to rise
     while it is held, so that a load held longer cleaves more ligand from the resin.
     Leached Protein A is reported per mass of antibody (ppm). If a similar amount
     of ligand leaches in each cycle, leached Protein A in ppm falls as protein load
     rises, and it is expected to depend little on the other parameters within their
     ranges.
  4. An antibody fragment is captured only if it still carries Fc. Fab fragments flow
     through with the load, and the pool therefore carries less fragment than the
     feed. A lower elution pH may add a little acid-catalysed hinge hydrolysis during
     elution. The pool also still carries host cell protein, and the part of it that elutes
     in the low molecular weight window of SEC is read as fragment. For this reason
     SEC overstates fragments in the Protein A pool, and nrCE-SDS does not (INV-001
     finding 6).
  5. DNA is polyanionic and does not bind Protein A. Its clearance is large and is not
     expected to depend on the operating parameters within their ranges.

Protein A, when operated under platform conditions, does not separate glycan vari-
ants or charge variants, because it binds the Fc region independently of both (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). Glycan variants and charge variants are therefore not
responses of the designs.
Aggregate and monomer both carry Fc and both bind Protein A. Aggregates bind with
higher avidity and can be partly resolved on elution, but under platform conditions
the aggregate level of the pool follows that of the load. Low pH can partly unfold
the antibody and promote aggregation, and the amount formed grows with the time
spent at low pH. In the column the antibody is at elution pH only for the duration of
the elution. In the Protein A characterization of the case study no process parameter
had a significant effect on aggregate (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Aggregate
formation at low pH is characterized in the low-pH viral inactivation study (PCP-006).
That study spans pH 3.2–4.0 for 60–180 min, which covers the elution pH range of
this step for a much longer time. For these reasons aggregate is not a response of
the designs of this plan. It is measured on every pool by SEC for information. The
time the eluate waits at elution pH before inactivation is covered by the hold study
(Section 6.5).

The drug substance criteria do not apply to the Protein A pool directly. Cation ex-

step, so a pool that the later steps bring within the drug substance limit can itself lie
far above it. Host cell protein, leached Protein A and fragments in the Protein A pool

is polyanionic and does not bind Protein A, so it leaves the column in the flow-through

for DNA from continuous cell lines (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Aggregate
is measured by SEC for information only. Step yield is a performance attribute. It
has no acceptance criterion and is used for the classification of parameters that act
on process performance.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The pre-characterization risk assessment RA-001 assessed every parameter of the
step before characterization began. Each parameter was ranked on its potential im-
pact on the CQAs and on process performance, and on its potential to interact with
other parameters.
Protein load, elution buffer pH, load flow rate and end of pool collect were assigned to
multivariate study. Pool host cell protein is expected to rise with protein load and as
elution buffer pH falls, and the two may interact (Section 4.1). Step yield is expected
to fall as load flow rate rises and to rise with end of pool collect. Load flow rate is
expected to interact with protein load, because a shorter residence time lowers the
dynamic binding capacity and costs yield only when the load is close to it. A design
that varies the four parameters together is needed to estimate these interactions.



                                             14
Operating temperature was assigned to univariate study. A higher temperature
speeds the diffusion of the antibody into the pores, which raises the dynamic binding
capacity, and it strengthens the hydrophobic contacts at the Fc–ligand interface,
which makes elution slightly less complete. A lower temperature acts the opposite
way on both. Because temperature acts on the dynamic binding capacity, it could
interact with protein load in the same way as load flow rate does. Over the range
studied, however, its effect on binding capacity is expected to be small against that
of residence time, and the resin manufacturer specifies binding capacity across that
range. An interaction of temperature with protein load is therefore expected to be
small, and on that basis temperature is studied one factor at a time. The univariate
study runs at the set-point load, so an interaction of temperature with a high load is
not estimated (Section 6.4).
Bed height was also assigned to univariate study, although it too can interact with pro-
tein load. At constant linear velocity bed height sets the residence time, and a shorter
residence time lowers the dynamic binding capacity (Section 4.1). The designs study
the interaction of residence time with protein load through load flow rate, at the set-
point bed height. Bed height itself is fixed for each packed column by the packing
specification and does not vary from run to run in manufacture, so it is studied one
factor at a time. The shortest bed of the range, 10 cm at the set-point velocity, gives
a residence time of 3 min, shorter than any run of the designs. A short bed combined
with a high protein load, where breakthrough is most likely, is therefore not studied,
and no claim is made for it.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model is a laboratory column packed with the commercial Protein A
resin. It is designed on the scaling principles that PCMP-001 sets for scale-down mod-
els. The bed height and the residence time of the commercial column are kept. Load,
wash and elution volumes are normalized to column volumes, and column efficiency
is checked by plate count and peak asymmetry after packing. Buffers are made to
the commercial recipes. The model is operated at the set-point of every parameter of
Table 6.1 unless a design sets it otherwise.
The model is qualified under the scale-down model qualification procedure SOP-1001
in study S-PA-SDM, the scale-down model qualification, before the first run of the
screening design. In that study, 3 runs of the model at set-point are compared with
5 manufacturing-scale batches of A-Mab run at set-point. The quantities compared
are pool host cell protein, leached Protein A, step yield and column efficiency (plate
count and peak asymmetry).
The model is qualified by the rule that PCMP-001 sets for scale-down model qualifica-
tion, which judges the model by its offset from manufacturing scale. The scale-down
model need not reproduce the manufacturing mean, but the difference between the
two scales must be known well enough that no conclusion depends on its exact size.
The offset of a quantity is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of the
manufacturing-scale batches. It is estimated with its two-sided 90 % confidence in-
terval, each end of which is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound, the level used to com-
pare two means at 5 % on each side. For a quantity that is compared at both scales
and has a criterion, each scale-down result is translated to manufacturing scale by
subtracting the offset before it is judged. The model is qualified for such a quantity
when its conclusion over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of the con-
fidence interval. That conclusion is whether the Protein A pool meets the criterion
at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface
factors. A model whose conclusion for a quantity changes between the two ends of
the interval is not used for that quantity.
For this step the rule applies as follows.

  1. Pool host cell protein and leached Protein A have a criterion at the step (Ta-
     ble 7.1). Their offsets are subtracted before each result is judged, and the model
     is qualified or not for each of them by the rule above.
  2. Step yield and column efficiency have no criterion at the step. Their offsets are
     recorded in PCR-005, and no conclusion depends on them.




                                             16
  3. Fragments are not measured in the qualification. AMD-001 was taken on 2026-
     03-20, after the scale-down model qualification S-PA-SDM had closed on 2025-
     11-14. The qualification therefore has no fragment result at either scale, and
     fragments carry no offset. They are judged as the response-surface model pre-
     dicts them.

Leached Protein A is expected to be lower in the laboratory column than at manufac-
turing scale. The manufacturing load is held longer before capture, so it is expected
to cleave more ligand from the resin (Section 4.1, item 3). This difference is the offset
of leached Protein A, and it is subtracted as every offset is. Where the cause of an
offset is known, PCMP-001 asks the report to state it.
Fragment results from the scale-down model are accepted on mechanism. The cap-
ture of Fc-bearing species depends on bed height and residence time, which the model
keeps at their commercial values. The low-pH hinge hydrolysis depends on pH, time
at low pH and temperature, and the model uses the commercial elution buffer at the
same temperature and the same residence time. The plan has no fragment result at
manufacturing scale with which to confirm the mechanism directly. The confirmation
it relies on is indirect. The qualification compares the plate count and peak asym-
metry of the laboratory column with those of the commercial column, which shows
whether the laboratory bed behaves as the commercial bed does. PCR-005 tabulates
the offsets of this step, and PCMR-001 summarises the offsets of the whole train.



5.2 Operation

Each run follows the Protein A operating procedure SOP-2007 at laboratory scale.
The column is equilibrated, loaded with clarified harvest to the target protein load,
washed and eluted with the elution buffer at the pH set by the design. The eluate is
collected from the rise of the absorbance at 280 nm until the end of pool collect set
by the design, expressed in column volumes. The column is then stripped, cleaned
and stored as the resin life-cycle procedure SOP-2008 specifies. The pool is not neu-
tralized, because the commercial eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation at elution
pH.
Each design is loaded from one pool of clarified harvest produced at set-point con-
ditions and held frozen in aliquots. A single feed keeps feed variation out of the
estimated effects. The effect of feed quality on the step is therefore not estimated in
this plan. Runs are executed in random order within each design.

5.5 Statistical methods

The designs are built and analysed under the DoE procedure SOP-1002 with the com-
mon statistical approach of PCMP-001. Factors are coded from −1 to +1 over their
range studied, with 0 at the centre. For each of the 4 design factors the centre of the
range studied is the set-point, so the centre-point runs of both designs are set-point
runs.
The screening data are fitted with a model of main effects and two-factor interactions,
which has 11 terms. Effects are reported as the change in the response over the
coded range of the factor, which is twice the coefficient, and significance is judged at
α = 0.05. The screening model identifies effects. A two-level design cannot resolve
curvature, so the screening model is not used to predict between its corners.
The response-surface data are fitted with a full quadratic model of 15 terms. The
response-surface model is the predictive model of the step, and every prediction and
every PAR in PCR-005 comes from it. Each fitted model is reported with R², adjusted
R² and predicted R², the model F-test, and the test of lack of fit against pure error
from the centre points. Residuals are plotted against predicted values, against run




                                               19
order and on a normal probability plot. Where a model fails the adequacy criteria
of Section 7, a transformation of the response or a justified change of terms may be
applied, and the change is reported with its reason.
A prediction of pool host cell protein or leached Protein A is translated to manufac-
turing scale by subtracting the offset of that attribute before it is compared with a
criterion (Section 5.1). The PAR analysis is described in Section 8. Where no ade-
quate model results for a response, that response is reported descriptively against its
criterion over the runs of the design, and no PAR is claimed from it.

6.2 Screening design

The screening design (study S-PA-SCR) is a full two-level factorial design in the 4
factors of Table 6.2, with 16 factorial runs and 3 centre points, 19 runs in all. Appendix
A gives the planned matrix in coded units. Because the design is a full factorial, no
effect is aliased with another. Every main effect and every two-factor interaction can
be estimated. An interaction of protein load with elution buffer pH is possible and
one with load flow rate is expected (Section 4.1), so both must be estimable.
The interaction model leaves 8 residual degrees of freedom. Of these, 2 come from
the replicated centre points as pure error and 6 are available to test lack of fit. The
centre points also allow a test for curvature. The responses are pool host cell protein,
step yield and leached Protein A. Fragments by SEC are recorded for every run for
information.
All 4 factors are carried into the response-surface design whatever the screening
result. Each of them carries an effect expected in Section 4.1, and 4 factors are few
enough for a central composite design. The screening design therefore serves to
identify effects and interactions and to check the ranges before the larger design is
run.



6.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface design (study S-PA-RSM) is a face-centred central composite
design in the same 4 factors. It has 16 factorial runs, 8 axial runs at the faces of the
cube (coded −1 and +1) and 4 centre points, 28 runs in all. Appendix B gives the
planned matrix. A face-centred design keeps every run inside the range studied, so
no axial run exceeds a range that RA-001 and the platform history support.
The full quadratic model leaves 13 residual degrees of freedom, with 3 for pure error
and 10 for lack of fit. The responses are pool host cell protein, step yield, leached
Protein A and fragments by nrCE-SDS. Fragments are measured on the retained pools
in study S-PA-RET (Section 5.4).

cannot detect an interaction. The ranges of operating temperature and bed height are
therefore supported only with the other parameters at their set-points, and PCR-005
states this beside each result.



6.5 Hold study of the Protein A eluate

The Protein A eluate is held at elution pH before viral inactivation, and the hold can
change the product. Over days at low pH, partial unfolding can raise aggregate and

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

These criteria decide when the scale-down model is qualified, when a model may be
used for prediction, when a range is proven acceptable, how long the eluate may be
held and how a parameter is classified. Criteria 2 to 5 are the model adequacy criteria
of PCMP-001. Their limits are the campaign thresholds that PCMP-001 sets for every
step alike. These are the conventional 5 % significance level for the tests, and a limit
of 0.20 on how far predicted R² may fall short of R², which guards against a model
with more terms than the data support.

  1. Scale-down model qualification. For pool host cell protein and for leached Pro-
     tein A, the model is qualified when the conclusion of criterion 6, whether the
     Protein A pool meets its criterion across the NOR, is the same with the offset at
     either end of its 90 % confidence interval. A model that is not qualified for an
     attribute is not used for that attribute. Step yield and column efficiency have
     no criterion at the step, so their offsets are recorded only. Fragments carry no
     offset, because the qualification has no fragment result, and they are accepted
     on the mechanism stated in Section 5.1.
  2. Model significance. A response-surface model is used for prediction only if the
     model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Lack of fit. The test of lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  4. Predictive power. The predicted R², computed from the prediction error sum of
     squares (PRESS), falls short of the R² of the same model by no more than 0.20.
  5. Residuals. A regression of the absolute residuals on the predicted values, and
     a regression of the residuals on run order, each show no slope significant at α
     = 0.05. No run has a Cook’s distance above 4/n, a common screening cut-off
     for influential runs that scales with the number of runs, which is 0.14 for the 28
     runs of the response-surface design.
  6. Protein A pool and PARs. Each attribute of the Protein A pool with a criterion
     is judged against the criterion of Table 7.1 by the analysis of Section 8, after
     the offset is subtracted where one applies. A range is proven acceptable for an
     attribute when the NOR-propagated analysis (a simulation in which the other
     factors vary within their NOR, Section 8.2) meets the criterion across it.
  7. Hold. A hold time is supported at a temperature when, at every sample point up
     to that time, the increase in aggregate and in acidic variants over the hold, added
     to the highest level expected in the Protein A pool in manufacture, stays within
     the criterion (Section 6.5). The same applies to fragments by nrCE-SDS on the
     days tested by nrCE-SDS. SEC fragment results are not used for this decision. A
     trend is reported even where the criterion is met.
  8. Classification. Each parameter is classified under the parameter classification
     procedure SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001. A parameter whose
     effect on a CQA is significant and practically relevant is critical, and it is well

     controlled when it has a low risk of leaving its acceptable range in operation.

     the NOR-propagated PAR of the parameter is narrower than its range studied.

8.2 The two analyses

A PAR is computed for each attribute of Table 7.1 that has a criterion and for each
of the 4 factors of the response-surface model. Two analyses are run, as PCMP-001
defines them for proven acceptable ranges. In both, a prediction of pool host cell
protein or leached Protein A is translated to manufacturing scale by subtracting its
offset before it is compared with the criterion (Section 5.1).

  1. The set-point analysis is a one-factor scan of the model’s mean prediction. It
     holds the other factors at their set-points and scans the factor across its range
     studied. The PAR is the contiguous range around the set-point over which the
     mean prediction of the response-surface model meets the criterion.
  2. The NOR-propagated analysis is a Monte Carlo simulation in which the other
     factors vary as they would in routine operation. It scans the factor at 81 grid
     points across its range studied. At each grid point it draws 2,000 operating
     points for the other factors. Each factor is drawn from a normal distribution
     centred on its set-point, with a standard deviation of one sixth of the width of its
     NOR, and clipped to the range studied. The model residual and the uncertainty
     of the fitted coefficients are included. The PAR is the contiguous range around




                                               29
     the set-point over which the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch meets the
     criterion.

The NOR-propagated PAR is the one that supports a claim. It is in general narrower
than the set-point PAR, because the range must hold while the rest of the operating
point moves within its NOR. A response-surface model predicts the mean response,
and at the edge of a region defined on mean predictions a single batch meets its
criterion only about half the time (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).



8.3 Decision rule and reporting

A parameter that affects several attributes has a PAR for each, and the range car-
ried into the control strategy is their intersection. Where the set-point itself fails a
criterion, PCR-005 reports that no PAR exists for that attribute and states the conse-
quence for the NOR. A PAR that reaches the edge of the range studied is reported as
reaching it, and no claim is made beyond it.
PCR-005 reports both analyses for every attribute and factor in one table. For each
attribute with a criterion it shows, as a two-panel figure, the PAR of the factor with
the largest main effect on that attribute in the response-surface model. The left panel
shows the set-point analysis and the right panel the NOR-propagated analysis, with
the region that meets the criterion shaded green. The univariate parameters have
no model-based PAR. The ranges of operating temperature and bed height are sup-
ported by the measured responses against the same criteria at each end of the range
studied.

12 Risks and assumptions

The studies carry the following risks and rest on the following assumptions.

  1. The scale-down model represents the commercial column within a known offset.
     For pool host cell protein and leached Protein A this is tested by the qualification
     rule of Section 5.1. With 3 scale-down runs and 5 manufacturing batches the
     confidence interval of an offset may be wide. If the conclusion for one of these
     attributes then differs between the two ends of its interval, the model is not used
     for it, and its ranges cannot be set from the designs. For fragments the model
     rests on mechanism, because capture depends on bed height and residence time,
     and hinge hydrolysis on pH, time and temperature, all of which the model keeps
     at commercial values. The qualification has no fragment result at either scale
     with which to confirm this directly.
  2. Leached Protein A is expected to be lower in the laboratory column than at man-
     ufacturing scale, because the manufacturing load is held longer before capture
     (Section 4.1, item 3). This difference is the offset of leached Protein A, and it is
     subtracted before any result for leached Protein A is judged.
  3. Each design uses one feed. The effect of feed host cell protein, titre and viability
     on the step is not estimated here. The case study reports that Protein A capture
     is robust over a wide range of feed inputs (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009),
     and the feed is characterized in PCP-003 and PCP-004.
  4. The criteria of this step rest on the downstream values of Table 8.2. If the veri-
     fication of a downstream step changes one of them before PCR-005 is approved,
     PCR-005 uses the criterion then in force.
  5. Fragments by nrCE-SDS rest on retained samples, which are the pools of the
     response-surface design and the day 0 and 7 samples of the hold study. If a
     retained pool is lost or found unsuitable, that run has no nrCE-SDS result. The
     fragment model is then fitted to the remaining runs only if the design still sup-
     ports it, and otherwise fragments are reported descriptively.
  6. Supply of clarified harvest and resin may limit the number of runs. The designs
     are executed in full before analysis, and no run is dropped to save material.
