1 Purpose and scope

This plan sets out the Stage 1 characterization of the low-pH viral inactivation step,
Step 6 of the A-Mab drug substance process (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011;
CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). It applies the Process Characterization Master
Plan PCMP-001 to this step and adds what is specific to the step, which is its scale-
down and spiking models, its designs, its sampling plan and the values of its criteria.
The studies are run in a scale-down model at the development site in Cambridge, MA.
Their results support the operating ranges and the control strategy that the Process
Transfer Plan PTP-001 moves to the commercial drug substance facility in Grafton,
WI. The commercial process runs at the 15,000 L scale.
Low-pH viral inactivation holds the Protein A eluate at acid pH for a set time and
then neutralizes it. The hold inactivates enveloped viruses. The step contributes
a log reduction of xenotropic murine leukemia virus (XMuLV) to the modular viral
clearance claim of the process (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a). The
same conditions act on the antibody and can raise the levels of aggregate, fragments
and acidic variants in the pool.
The plan covers the four parameters of the step, which are inactivation pH, hold time,
temperature and A-Mab concentration. It covers the attributes that the step sets or
can change, the scale-down model and its qualification, and the spiking model for
XMuLV. It sets out the designed experiments, the univariate study and the hold study
of the neutralized pool. It states the acceptance criteria and the planned analysis,
including the proven acceptable ranges (PARs). The Protein A step that produces
the load of this step is characterized under PCP-005. The results of this plan are
reported in PCR-006, which rolls up into the Process Characterization Master Report
PCMR-001.

2 Objectives

The studies under this plan have the following objectives.

  1. To quantify the effects of inactivation pH, hold time and temperature, and of their
     interactions, on XMuLV clearance, aggregate, fragments and acidic variants.
  2. To fit response-surface models that predict these outputs over the range studied,
     and to test each model against the adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2.
  3. To determine, for each attribute that has a criterion at this step, the PAR of
     inactivation pH, hold time and temperature, and to establish whether the NOR
     of each lies within it.
  4. To verify the platform worst cases of this step on which the criteria upstream of
     it rely.
  5. To assess the effect of A-Mab concentration on aggregate and acidic variants
     one factor at a time, and to support its range against the criteria for those two
     attributes.
  6. To establish how long the neutralized pool may be held before cation exchange.
  7. To provide the evidence for the classification of each parameter under SOP-4001,
     and for the contribution of the step to the control strategy in PCMR-001.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

Low-pH viral inactivation is the step after Protein A capture and before cation ex-
change chromatography. The Protein A eluate leaves the column at low pH. It is
adjusted to the inactivation pH and held for the hold time at the set temperature.
Base is then added to neutralize the pool. The neutralized pool is the load of cation
exchange. The step is operated under SOP-2009.
Low pH inactivates an enveloped virus by denaturing the glycoproteins of its envelope
and by disrupting the envelope itself. The virus needs the envelope glycoproteins to
bind to a cell and to fuse with its membrane. Inactivation follows first-order kinet-
ics. Its rate constant rises as the pH falls and as the temperature rises, and the log
reduction is proportional to that rate integrated over the hold time. Inactivation pH
is expected to govern the step, because the envelope glycoproteins denature much
faster with each small fall in pH near the operating range. A longer hold gives a larger
log reduction at any pH and temperature, and a warmer hold speeds the denaturation
of the envelope glycoproteins.
The same conditions act on the antibody. At acid pH the second constant domain
of the heavy chain of the antibody is partially unfolded, which exposes hydrophobic
surface. Molecules with exposed hydrophobic surface associate, so aggregate forms
for as long as the pool is held at low pH. Aggregate forms faster in a warmer pool,
because the unfolding and the association both speed up with temperature, and in a
more concentrated pool (Section 4.3). On the platform, time at low pH adds a small
amount of acidic variants. This plan measures that rise in every run of the designs,
and PCR-006 verifies it against the platform worst case (Section 7.4). The low-pH hold
also causes a small amount of acid-catalysed cleavage in the hinge region, which adds
fragments. Over a hold of the length studied, the rise in acidic variants is expected
to be small against the level formed in the production bioreactor, and the rise in
fragments small against the level the load already carries. Aggregate formed in the
hold is carried into cation exchange, the step that removes it.
The low-pH step belongs to the Novacyte downstream platform and was used for three
earlier antibodies, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab. Their history establishes the mechanism
of the step and the range of performance to expect from it (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). The same history supplies the platform claim for XMuLV clearance
and the largest increases in aggregate, fragments and acidic variants that the step
has shown. The intermediate acceptance criteria in Section 7.2 are built on these
values.

The step is not expected to change the glycan attributes, which are formed in the
production bioreactor and which no downstream step changes (PCMP-001 §3). It
makes no claim for the parvovirus minute virus of mice (MVM), because a parvovirus
has no envelope and so no envelope glycoproteins for low pH to denature. The criteria
also assume no change in host cell protein across the step, and no clearance of host
cell protein is claimed for it.

4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

Every parameter of this step was assessed in RA-001 before characterization began.
Each parameter was ranked on its potential impact on the CQAs and on its potential
to interact with other parameters, as ICH Q9 describes (International Council for Har-
monisation 2023b). RA-001 gives the scores and the rationale for each assignment.
Inactivation pH, hold time and temperature were assigned to multivariate study. A
lower pH, a longer hold and a higher temperature each raise the log reduction of
XMuLV, and each also raises the aggregate that forms in the hold. The three are
also expected to interact. The log reduction is the product of a rate that pH and
temperature set and the time over which that rate acts. A design that varies the three
parameters together is therefore needed to estimate the clearance and the aggregate
at their combinations.
A-Mab concentration was assigned to univariate study. A higher antibody concentra-
tion raises the rate at which partially unfolded molecules meet, so aggregate forms
faster in a more concentrated pool. The mechanism of the step gives no reason to
expect an effect of the antibody concentration on the inactivation of the virus, which
acts on the viral envelope. This plan does not test that expectation, and Section 12
lists it as an assumption. A-Mab concentration is also a property of the Protein A
pool and not a setting of the hold. It is therefore assessed one factor at a time, for
aggregate and acidic variants (Section 6.4).

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model holds the Protein A eluate in a temperature-controlled vessel
at laboratory scale. The eluate is adjusted to the inactivation pH, held and neutralized
in that vessel as in manufacture. The parameters of the step do not depend on scale.
Inactivation pH, hold time, temperature and A-Mab concentration are therefore set
to the same values as in manufacture, and the additions of acid and base are scaled
with the volume of the pool.
The qualification runs are made under SOP-1001 in study S-VI-SDM, before the first
characterization run. The study compares three runs of the scale-down model at set-
point with five manufacturing-scale batches, for aggregate and acidic variants in the
neutralized pool. These runs give the offset of each of the two outputs. The decision
whether the scale-down model is qualified for an output rests on whether the output
meets its criterion at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high, and
that judgement needs the response-surface model. The decision is therefore made at
the analysis, in PCR-006.
The model is qualified for each output separately, by its offset from manufacturing
scale and not by a test of equivalence (PCMP-001 §7.1). The rule has four parts.

  1. The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the
     mean of the manufacturing-scale batches. It is reported with its two-sided 90 %
     confidence interval.
  2. Every scale-down result for aggregate or acidic variants is translated to manu-
     facturing scale by subtracting the offset before it is compared with a criterion.
     Ranges and conclusions for these two outputs are therefore stated at manufac-
     turing scale.
  3. The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion
     over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its confidence interval.
     The conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination
     of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the three factors of the response-surface
     design.
  4. A scale-down model whose conclusion for an output changes between the two
     ends of the confidence interval of the offset is not used for that output.

Where the cause of an offset is known, PCR-006 will state it.
Fragments and XMuLV clearance are not compared at both scales, so their offsets
are unknown, not zero. These two outputs are judged as the model predicts them,
with no translation to manufacturing scale. For fragments, representativeness rests



                                          15
on the mechanism of the step. Cleavage in the hinge region at low pH depends on
pH, time and temperature, which the scale-down model sets to the manufacturing
values, and not on the size of the vessel. The plan therefore assumes that the scale-
down model forms fragments at the rate of manufacture (Section 12). For XMuLV
clearance, representativeness rests on the spiking model.
Virus cannot be introduced into the manufacturing process, so XMuLV clearance is
measured in a spiking model of the step (International Council for Harmonisation
2023a). The spiking model is the scale-down model, run on the same Protein A eluate
and at the same pH, hold time and temperature. Each run of the designs is spiked
with XMuLV before the pH is adjusted. The XMuLV titre is measured in the spiked
load and in the neutralized pool at the end of the hold. The inactivation rate depends
on the pH and the temperature of the pool and on its matrix, which is the buffer of
the Protein A eluate. It does not depend on the volume of the pool. The spiking
runs use Protein A eluate produced at the set-points of the Protein A step, so the
matrix is that of manufacture, and they hold A-Mab concentration at its set-point. The
small-scale result therefore represents the manufacturing step at that concentration,
provided that the whole pool reaches the inactivation pH. Whether the clearance
changes across the range of A-Mab concentration is not tested, and the plan assumes
that it does not (Section 12).



5.2 Operation

Each run starts from Protein A eluate produced at the set-points of the Protein A
step. The eluate is adjusted to the inactivation pH of the run and brought to the
temperature of the run. The hold time is counted from the moment the inactivation
pH is reached. At the end of the hold the pool is neutralized with base. Neutralization
stops the inactivation and slows the reactions that the acid drives on the antibody. In
the designs, A-Mab concentration is held at its set-point. In the univariate study it is
set to each level before the pH is adjusted.
The acid and base solutions and the other inputs of the step are prepared to their
platform specifications. They are controlled for identity and quality and are not char-
acterized in this plan.

3015 and imaged capillary isoelectric focusing (icIEF) under SOP-3013. The log re-

5.5 Statistical methods

The designs and their analysis follow SOP-1002 and PCMP-001 §6. Each factor is
coded −1 at the low end of its range studied, +1 at the high end and 0 at the midpoint
(Montgomery 2017). An effect is the change in the response over the coded range of
the factor, which is twice its coefficient in coded units. A model term is significant
when its p-value is below α = 0.05.
The screening data will be fitted by least squares with a model of main effects and
two-factor interactions. For three factors this model has seven terms. The 11 runs of
the design leave four residual degrees of freedom. The three centre points give two
of them for pure error, and the other two test lack of fit, which combines curvature
and the three-factor interaction. The screening model identifies effects. Its centre




                                             18
points can detect curvature but cannot attribute it to a factor, and no prediction will
be made from it.
The response-surface data will be fitted with a full quadratic model, which adds a
squared term for each factor to the main effects and the two-factor interactions. The
model has ten terms. The 18 runs of the design leave eight residual degrees of free-
dom, three for pure error from the four centre points and five for lack of fit. The
response-surface model is the predictive model. Every prediction, every PAR and the
verification of the platform worst cases in PCR-006 will come from it.
Each response-surface model will be reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R²,
the model F-test and the lack-of-fit test against pure error. Predicted R² is computed
from the prediction error sum of squares (PRESS), in which each run is predicted
by the model fitted without it. Residuals will be plotted against the predicted values
and the run order, and on a normal probability plot, and the Cook’s distance of each
run will be computed. The criteria that a model must meet, including the tests on its
residuals, are in Section 7.1.
Aggregate, fragments and acidic variants must meet their criteria in every batch,
and a response-surface model predicts the mean response. These three attributes
are therefore judged through a 95 % prediction interval of a single batch wherever a
judgement supports a claim. Such judgements are the PAR computed while the other
factors vary as in routine operation (Section 8) and the judgement over the NOR in
the qualification of the scale-down model (Section 5.1), which uses the exact predic-
tion interval of a single run. The PAR analysis that holds the other parameters at their
set-points judges the mean prediction (PCMP-001 §6.4). XMuLV clearance is judged
on the mean predicted LRF at the least favourable combination of inactivation pH,
hold time and temperature within their NORs, because viral clearance is validated
at worst-case conditions. Predictions of aggregate and acidic variants are translated
to manufacturing scale by subtracting their offsets (Section 5.1) before they are com-
pared with a criterion. Predictions of fragments and XMuLV clearance are compared
as the model gives them.
The results of the univariate study are compared with the set-point runs of the quali-
fication study and with the criteria for aggregate and acidic variants in Table 7.1. A
difference from the set-point runs within the precision of the method is attributed to
the assay. The hold study is evaluated as the change from day 0 at each temperature
(Section 7.5).

6.2 Screening design

The screening design (S-VI-SCR) is a two-level full factorial design in inactivation pH,
hold time and temperature, with three centre points, in 11 runs. Every combination
of the low and high ends of the three ranges is run, so the design estimates all main
effects and all two-factor interactions without aliasing. Its purpose is to identify which
factors and interactions act on each output before the response-surface design is run.
All three factors are carried into the response-surface design, because each of them is
expected to act on XMuLV clearance (Section 4.3). The runs are executed in random
order. The planned design is in Appendix A.



6.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface design (S-VI-RSM) is a face-centred central composite design
in the same three factors (Montgomery 2017). It has eight factorial runs, six axial
runs and four centre points, 18 runs in all. The axial runs lie at the centres of the
faces of the design cube, at the ends of each range studied, so every run stays inside
the range studied. Together with the centre points, the axial runs allow a squared
term to be estimated for each factor, which the full quadratic model needs. The runs
are executed in random order. The planned design is in Appendix B.
Every output in Table 5.1 is measured on every run. Fragments are judged on the
nrCE-SDS results of the retained samples (Section 5.3).



6.4 Univariate assessment

A-Mab concentration is assessed one factor at a time in the univariate study (S-VI-
UNI). The low-pH hold is run at the low and the high end of the range studied, 5 and
35 g/L, in duplicate. Inactivation pH, hold time and temperature are held at their
set-points. Aggregate and acidic variants are measured in the neutralized pool, and
the set-point runs of the qualification study (S-VI-SDM) serve as the reference at set-
point. The study does not measure XMuLV clearance or fragments, so it supports a
range of A-Mab concentration for aggregate and acidic variants only. That A-Mab
concentration does not change XMuLV clearance or fragments within the range stud-
ied is an assumption of this plan (Section 12). A study of one factor at a time cannot
show whether the effect of A-Mab concentration depends on the other parameters.

The neutralized pool may be held before it is loaded onto cation exchange. During a
hold, aggregate can form and asparagine residues can deamidate, which raises the
acidic variants (PCMP-001 §5). The hold study (S-VI-HLD) is run to establish how

7.1 Scale-down model and model adequacy

  1. The scale-down model is qualified for aggregate and for acidic variants when
     the conclusion against the criterion over the NOR is the same with the offset at
     either end of its 90 % confidence interval (Section 5.1). The decision is made at
     the analysis, with the response-surface model.
  2. A response-surface model is adequate for prediction when all four of the follow-
     ing conditions hold (PCMP-001 §7.2).
       • The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
       • Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
       • Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.2, the limit on overfitting of
         PCMP-001 §7.2.
       • The residuals show no trend, and no run dominates the fit. A regression
         of the absolute residuals on the predicted values, and a regression of the
         residuals on run order, each has no slope significant at α = 0.05. No run has
         a Cook’s distance above 4/n, where n is the number of runs in the fit (0.22
         for the 18 runs of the response-surface design).
  3. PCMP-001 §7.2 states that a model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the limit on
     overfitting is either refitted with a justified change of terms or transformation, or
     it is not used to set a range. This plan applies that rule to a model that fails any of
     the four conditions (the model F-test, the lack-of-fit test, the limit on overfitting
     and the tests on the residuals). The change is fixed here, before the data are
     seen, and one refit is allowed.
       • Where the regression of the absolute residuals on the predicted values has a
         positive slope significant at α = 0.05, the spread of the residuals grows with
         the level of the response. The response is then replaced by its logarithm,
         and the full quadratic model is fitted again.
       • Otherwise the terms that are not significant at α = 0.05 are removed one
         at a time, the least significant first, until every remaining term is significant
         or is needed to keep the model hierarchical. A model is hierarchical when

            every main effect contained in one of its interaction or squared terms stays
            in it.
          • A model that still fails any of the four conditions after this one refit sets no
            range. PCR-006 should then report the output against its criterion over the

Each step that clears a virus is held to its platform claim, which is the log reduction
that the step has delivered for that virus on the platform (PCMP-001 §7.4). For XMuLV
the claims are 6.6 log10 for this step, 5.5 log10 for anion exchange and 4.62 log10 for
virus filtration. They sum to 16.72 log10 , which is 0.02 log10 above the cumulative
requirement of 16.7 log10 . A shortfall at this step therefore cannot be made up by the
claims of the other two steps. Anion exchange and virus filtration are characterized
under PCP-008 and PCP-009, and PCMR-001 consolidates the cumulative claim.
The XMuLV clearance of this step is judged at the worst case within the NOR. The
lowest mean predicted LRF among all combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR
high of inactivation pH, hold time and temperature must reach 6.6 log10 .

PCR-006 will classify each parameter by the rule of PCMP-001 §7.7, which reads as
follows.

    Parameters are classified under the parameter classification procedure
    SOP-4001 by the decision logic of the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech
    Working Group 2009).

      1. A critical process parameter (CPP) has an effect on a CQA that is sig-

         high risk of falling outside its acceptable range in operation.
      2. A well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP) has the same
         kind of effect on a CQA but a low risk of falling outside its acceptable

      3. A key process parameter (KPP) affects process performance, such as

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A PAR will be computed for each attribute that has a criterion at this step and for each
factor of the response-surface model. Two analyses will be run (PCMP-001 §6.4). The
first holds the other parameters at their set-points and scans the parameter of interest
across its range studied. Its PAR is the contiguous range around the set-point over
which the mean prediction meets the criterion.
The second analysis computes the PAR while the other two factors of the response-
surface model vary about their set-points as in routine operation. PCMP-001 §6.4
calls it the NOR-propagated analysis. It scans the parameter across 81 grid points
over its range studied. How the other factors vary, and what is compared with the
criterion, depend on the attribute.

  • For aggregate, fragments and acidic variants it compares the 95 % prediction
    interval of a single batch, because a single batch is what must meet the criterion.
    At each grid point, 2,000 operating points are drawn for the other two factors.
    Each factor is drawn from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a
    standard deviation of one sixth of the width of its NOR, so that the NOR spans
    three standard deviations on either side of the set-point. A draw that falls outside
    the range studied is set to the nearer end of that range. The interval holds the
    central 95 % of the simulated batches, and it includes the model residual and
    the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients. For an upper limit the upper end of the
    interval must not exceed the limit, for a lower limit the lower end must not fall
    below it, and for a two-sided criterion both apply.
  • For XMuLV clearance it compares the worst case within the NOR. At each grid
    point the other two factors are set to every combination of their NOR low, set-
    point and NOR high, and the lowest mean predicted LRF must reach the claim.

The PAR of the second analysis is the contiguous range around the set-point over
which the comparison passes. It is in general narrower than the PAR of the first anal-
ysis, because the range must hold while the other factors vary. The PAR of the second
analysis is the one that supports a claim. The decision rules are the following.

  1. A range of a parameter is proven acceptable for an attribute when the second
     analysis meets the criterion of that attribute across it.
  2. A parameter that affects several attributes has a PAR for each attribute, and the
     range carried into the control strategy is their intersection.
  3. A PAR that reaches the end of the range studied is reported as reaching it, and
     no claim is made beyond it.




                                          29
  4. Where an output does not meet its criterion even at the set-point, PCR-006 re-
     ports that no PAR exists for that attribute and states the consequence for the
     NOR.
  5. The range of A-Mab concentration is supported by its own univariate data for
     aggregate and acidic variants, the two attributes that the univariate study mea-
     sures. It is supported when every result at both levels meets the criterion for
     aggregate and for acidic variants in Table 7.1, at manufacturing scale. The uni-
     variate study supports no range of A-Mab concentration for fragments or XMuLV
     clearance (Section 12).

PCR-006 will tabulate both PARs for each attribute and each parameter. For each
attribute it will also show a figure against the parameter with the largest main effect in
the response-surface model. The figure plots the prediction across the range studied,
with the region that meets the criterion shaded green.

12 Risks and assumptions

The studies carry the following risks.

  1. The scale-down model may differ in mean from manufacturing scale for aggre-
     gate and acidic variants. The offsets and the qualification rule of Section 5.1
     address this, and every conclusion for these two outputs is stated at manufactur-
     ing scale.
  2. Fragments and XMuLV clearance are not compared at both scales, so their off-
     sets are unknown (Section 5.1). Their judgements rest on the spiking model and
     on the assumptions below.
  3. An infectivity titre carries the variability of a cell-based assay. The XMuLV claim
     is therefore judged on the mean prediction of the response-surface model and
     not on a single titre. The centre points estimate the variability of the LRF at the
     design centre.
  4. The design centre is not the set-point (Section 6.1). The models predict the
     outputs at the set-point and do not measure them there. Replicate runs at set-
     point are made only in the qualification study.
  5. The screening design has no fragment results that can be used for a decision
     (Section 5.3). Effects on fragments are therefore estimated from the response-
     surface design only.
  6. The designs and the hold study need Protein A eluate produced at the Protein A
     set-points. A shortage of eluate would delay the studies, but it would not change
     the designs.

The plan rests on the following assumptions.

  1. Each downstream step performs at least as well as the platform worst case on
     which the criteria of this step rely. Each such assumption is checked in the report
     of that step. The verification of the cation exchange aggregate clearance has
     already changed the aggregate criterion of this step. Cation exchange cleared
     less aggregate within its NOR than its platform worst case, and the one-sided 95
     % prediction bound of a single batch for that clearance is part of the aggregate
     criterion (Section 7.2).
  2. The step does not change the glycan attributes or host cell protein, and no credit
     is taken for either.
  3. The scale-down model forms fragments at the rate of manufacture, because
     cleavage in the hinge region depends on pH, time and temperature and not on
     scale (Section 5.1).
  4. A-Mab concentration within its range studied does not change XMuLV clearance
     or the fragments formed in the hold. The designs hold A-Mab concentration at
     its set-point, and the univariate study measures neither output (Section 6.4). For
     XMuLV clearance the mechanism of the step gives no reason to expect such an



                                          34
   effect (Section 4.3). Cleavage in the hinge region is a reaction of each molecule
   with the solvent, so the fraction of fragments formed is not expected to depend
   on the antibody concentration either.
5. Each NOR spans the routine variation of its parameter, as the second PAR anal-
   ysis assumes (Section 8).
