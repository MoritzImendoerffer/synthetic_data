1 Purpose and scope

The A-Mab drug substance process is being transferred from the development site
in Cambridge, MA, to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI, under the
Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. This plan describes the Stage 1 characterization of
cation exchange chromatography, which is Step 7 of the drug substance train and its
principal polishing step (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). The studies are
run on a qualified scale-down model of the commercial column. At the receiving site
the process runs at a production bioreactor scale of 15,000 L.
The studies measure how far the aggregate, host cell protein and fragment content of
the eluate pool, and the step yield, move as each parameter is varied across its range
studied (Table 6.1). The results define the region in which the pool meets its criteria,
and they justify the ranges and controls applied in commercial manufacture.
The plan covers the 5 process parameters of the step. The risk assessment RA-001
assigned 4 of them to a multivariate design and elution flow rate to a univariate as-
sessment. The plan also covers the size variants and process impurities that the step
clears, and the step yield. The step is fed with the neutralized pool of low-pH viral
inactivation. The attributes of that pool are inputs to the study, not factors of it.
Three activities lie outside this plan. Resin life cycle, sanitization and repacking are
qualified under the resin life cycle procedure SOP-2008, and the studies use resin
within its qualified life. The analytical methods are validated under the method val-
idations listed in Table 3.2, and this plan uses them as validated. Confirmation of
the ranges at commercial scale belongs to Stage 2 and to the process performance
qualification (PPQ) batches that follow the transfer.
No viral clearance claim is made for cation exchange. Clearance of enveloped virus
is claimed at low-pH viral inactivation, anion exchange and virus filtration, and
clearance of parvovirus at anion exchange and virus filtration. The master report
PCMR-001 consolidates the cumulative claim (International Council for Harmonisa-
tion 2023a). Cation exchange contributes clearance of host cell protein, residual
DNA and leached Protein A, and these contributions are in scope.

The study has six objectives.

  1. To quantify the relationship between each studied parameter and each response
     of the step, over ranges wider than those in which the step will be operated.
  2. To define the multivariate region over which the fitted models predict that every

     the parameter is controlled.
  6. To state the ranges, in-process controls and monitoring that this step contributes
     to the control strategy, and to justify them for Stage 2.

The report PCR-007 presents the outcome against each of these objectives.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

Cation exchange is operated in bind and elute mode and is the principal aggregate
polishing step of the purification train. Below its isoelectric point the antibody carries
a net positive charge and binds the negatively charged ligand of the resin. Raising the
pH of the elution buffer weakens this electrostatic attraction and elutes the antibody.
Aggregate presents more positive charge to the ligand than monomer does, because
a dimer carries twice the charged surface. Aggregate is therefore retained more
strongly and elutes after the monomer. The clearance of aggregate depends on how
well the two peaks are resolved and on where collection of the pool stops.
The process impurities leave the column during the load and the wash. Most host
cell proteins are acidic at the operating pH and do not bind the resin, so they leave in
the load flow-through. The weakly bound remainder is displaced in the wash. DNA is
strongly negatively charged and is repelled by the ligand, so it passes through in the
load. Its clearance is large and is not expected to depend on the operating parameters.
Leached Protein A and its fragments are acidic and do not bind under load conditions.
The part that is complexed with the antibody is released when the antibody binds the
ligand, and it passes through as well. The step clears leached Protein A by a modest
factor that is a property of the chemistry and not of any parameter studied here.
Fragments carry less positive charge than intact antibody at the binding pH and elute
on the leading side of the main peak, so the leading fractions are rich in fragments.
The points at which collection starts and stops change the share of fragments in the
pool. The step is expected to clear part of the fragment load, not all of it. Yield is lost
at the two edges of the pool. Monomer that elutes after the stop collect point stays
in the tail, and monomer that elutes with the aggregate that is cut away is lost with
it.
The step has been run on the Novacyte humanized immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) plat-
form for three earlier products, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and throughout A-Mab
clinical and engineering manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The resin
chemistry, the buffer system and the mode of operation are unchanged for A-Mab.
This experience establishes the mechanisms above and identifies the parameters
worth studying. The studies are therefore designed to confirm and bound platform
behaviour, not to discover it. Prior knowledge enters the plan in two further ways.
It gives the expected direction of each effect, listed below. It also supplies the plat-
form worst cases of the downstream steps, from which the criteria of this step are
derived (Section 7.3), and the platform worst cases of this step, on which the criteria




                                           14
upstream rely (Section 7.6). Every range reported in PCR-007 rests on data from the
studies of this plan.
The platform experience gives four expectations, and the multivariate design is built
to test them.

  • Protein load sets how much of the ligand is occupied. As the load approaches the
    dynamic binding capacity, the monomer and aggregate peaks broaden and over-
    lap more, and weakly bound host cell protein finds fewer free sites from which
    the wash would displace it. Aggregate clearance, host cell protein clearance and
    yield are therefore expected to fall together as the load rises.
  • Load/wash conductivity sets the ionic strength that shields the charges on the
    ligand and on the bound species. A higher conductivity is expected to strip more
    weakly bound host cell protein during the wash, while the antibody, which binds
    more strongly, stays on the column. Its effect is expected on impurity clearance
    and not on the aggregate separation, which takes place during elution.
  • Elution buffer pH sets the net charge of the antibody at the moment of elution. A
    higher pH within the range weakens binding and brings monomer and aggregate
    off earlier and closer together. It is expected to reduce the resolution on which
    aggregate clearance depends.
  • Elution stop collect decides how much of the descending edge of the peak enters
    the pool. Aggregate elutes on that edge, so a later stop is expected to recover
    more monomer and to take in the front of the aggregate peak, and an earlier
    stop the reverse. It is the direct handle on the balance between aggregate and
    yield.

Elution flow rate sets the residence time during elution and with it the sharpness of
the peaks. Over the range studied, and at the particle size of the platform resin, the
change in sharpness is expected to be too small to move the aggregate content of the
pool. For this reason it is assessed one factor at a time (Section 4.3).



4.2 Quality attributes in scope

Cation exchange forms no quality attribute of its own. Aggregate is formed in the
production bioreactor and increases during the low-pH hold. Host cell protein and
residual DNA enter with the harvested cell culture fluid, leached Protein A is released
from the capture resin, and fragments are formed in culture and during the harvest
hold. This step removes part of each. Table 4.1 lists the attributes in scope with their
drug substance criteria and their criticality. Criticality was assigned with Tool #1 of
the A-Mab case study, as the product of impact and uncertainty, on a continuum from
very low (VL) to very high (VH) (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009; International
Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Attributes of high (H) and very high criticality are
called critical.

Aggregate is the attribute the step exists to control, and no step after cation ex-
change changes it in the derivation of the criteria (Section 7.3). Host cell protein
is cleared here and again at anion exchange, so this step must hand on a pool that
anion exchange can bring to the drug substance criterion. Fragments are a response
of the response-surface design, measured by nrCE-SDS (Section 5.3), and are judged
against the drug substance criterion.
Residual DNA and leached Protein A are measured across the step so that their clear-
ance is confirmed. Neither is a response of the designs. DNA is repelled by the ligand
at every conductivity and pH in Table 6.1, and leached Protein A stays unbound over
the same range. PCR-007 reports the clearance of both and claims a dependence on
a parameter for neither unless the data show one.
Step yield is a measure of process performance, not a quality attribute. It is measured
on every run because the monomer cut away with the aggregate is lost from the pool,
so the aggregate clearance and the yield of a setting have to be read together. It has
no acceptance criterion.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The risk assessment RA-001 set the scope of this study. Every process parameter of
the step was ranked for its potential impact on a quality attribute and, separately, for
its potential to interact with other parameters. The two rankings were combined into
a study assignment (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Where no data or rationale
were available, a parameter was ranked at the highest level.
The assignment gave three groups. The multivariate design carries 4 parameters.
Protein load, elution buffer pH and elution stop collect each move the aggregate peak
relative to the point at which collection stops. Protein load also acts with load/wash
conductivity to set how much weakly bound host cell protein survives the wash. For
this reason their effects on the pool cannot be assumed to add. Elution flow rate
was assigned to a univariate assessment, because it changes the residence time of




                                              16
the antibody in the pores of the resin and not the strength with which the antibody
binds the ligand. Its expected effect on the separation is small. Parameters fixed
by equipment design or held to platform specification, such as bed height, column
packing quality and buffer composition, were not assigned a new study. Their ranges
rest on the platform knowledge space and on the operation and life cycle procedures
in Table 3.2.
The range studied of every parameter is wider than its NOR at both ends (Table 6.1).
Each range was set from the control capability of the commercial equipment and from
the range over which the platform has been operated. The study therefore bounds the
step beyond the region in which it will run, and it supports later movement within the
region it defines (International Council for Harmonisation 2009). Ranges were not
widened past the point at which the step would fail for a reason already understood.
The range of elution buffer pH stops short of the pH at which the antibody would
no longer bind reproducibly. A run at such a pH would confirm the mechanism and
would say nothing about the operating region.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies are run on a laboratory column packed with the same resin and to the
same bed height as the commercial column, and operated at the same linear velocities.
Protein load is set in grams of antibody per litre of resin, and the equilibration, wash,
elution and strip volumes in column volumes. In this way every parameter of the study
is expressed at its commercial equivalent. Packing quality is verified before each
campaign by plate count and peak asymmetry, and a column that fails either test is
repacked before use. The scale-down model procedure SOP-1001 and the operating
procedure SOP-2010 describe the model and its operation.
The qualification runs come first. Before the first characterization run of the step,
the model is run at the set-point of every parameter in Table 6.1 and its outputs
are compared with manufacturing-scale batches (SOP-1001). The comparison covers
the attributes that enter and leave the step, which are step yield, pool aggregate,
pool host cell protein, residual DNA and leached Protein A. Plate count and peak
asymmetry, which describe the efficiency of the column, are compared as well (PCMP-
001 §5). The qualification uses 3 scale-down runs at set-point and 5 A-Mab batches
at manufacturing scale.
The offset of each output is estimated from these runs, also before the first charac-
terization run. The offset is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of the
manufacturing-scale batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval. Each end
of that interval is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound. Whether the model is qualified
for an output is decided later, at analysis. The decision compares the conclusion over
the NOR with the offset at either end of its interval, and that conclusion comes from
the response-surface model (Section 7.1).
Pool aggregate and pool host cell protein are compared at both scales and have a
criterion at this step. Every judgement of them against their criterion first subtracts
the offset, so their ranges and conclusions are stated at manufacturing scale. Outputs
with no criterion at this step have their offsets recorded, and no conclusion depends
on them (PCMP-001 §7.1).
The qualification does not compare fragments at both scales. Their offset is therefore
unknown, not zero. Fragments are judged as the model predicts them, and no offset
is subtracted. That judgement rests on an assumption about the mechanism. The
fragment content of the pool depends on the shape of the elution peak and on the
absorbance at which collection starts and stops. The scale-down model keeps the bed
height, the linear velocity and the absorbance thresholds of the commercial column,
and the qualification compares plate count and peak asymmetry, which describe the



                                          18
shape of the peak. Fragments are therefore expected to separate in the same way at
both scales.
The centre points of both designs are run at the set-point of every factor. Their spread
measures the reproducibility of the model under study conditions and gives the pure
error against which lack of fit is tested (Section 5.5).
The claims of this study are bounded by the model. A scale-down column reproduces
the chromatography of the commercial column. It does not reproduce the hold times,
the pool volumes or the mixing of the commercial suite. Conclusions drawn here
apply to the commercial step through the qualification above, and they are confirmed
at scale in Stage 2.



5.2 Operation

The step is loaded with the neutralized pool of low-pH viral inactivation. The pool is
adjusted to the load conductivity under study and loaded onto the equilibrated column
at the protein load under study. The column is then washed at the same conductivity
as the load. One buffer therefore sets the ionic strength of both operations, and the
two cannot be varied independently. Elution is a step change to the elution buffer at
the pH under study, at the elution flow rate set for the run. The pool is collected from
a threshold on the ascending edge of the elution peak to the stop collect threshold
on the descending edge, both read as ultraviolet absorbance (OD280). After collec-
tion the column is stripped, sanitized and re-equilibrated under the resin life cycle
procedure SOP-2008.
Buffer preparation, column packing and resin storage are held to platform specifica-
tion under the procedures in Table 3.2. They are controlled inputs to the study, not
factors of it. Temperature is held constant and is not varied, because the platform
runs the step at controlled room temperature at both sites and the transfer intro-
duces no difference in that control. A parameter that a run does not vary is held at
its set-point (Table 6.1).



5.3 Analytical methods

Each response and each attribute in scope is measured by a validated method (Ta-
ble 3.2). Pool aggregate is measured by size exclusion chromatography (SEC, AMV-
3011) and reported as the percentage of high molecular weight species. Host cell
protein is measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, AMV-3012) and
reported per milligram of antibody, so that the result does not move with the con-
centration of the pool. Residual DNA is measured by quantitative polymerase chain
reaction (qPCR, AMV-3014), and leached Protein A by ELISA (AMV-3016). Step yield
is calculated from the antibody mass in the pool and in the load, each determined by
ultraviolet absorbance against the extinction coefficient of A-Mab.

Assay variability enters the analysis at two points. It is part of the residual variance of
each fitted model, and it is part of the centre point spread that estimates pure error.
Consequently, a response measured by a less precise assay has a wider prediction
interval and a less sensitive test for lack of fit. Where a predicted value lies within
assay variability of its criterion, PCR-007 should attribute the result to the assay and
should not claim an effect. Host cell protein is expected to be the least reproducible
of the responses, and the centre points will show whether that expectation holds.



5.4 Sampling plan

Each run is sampled at four points.

  1. The load is sampled after conditioning, so that the aggregate, host cell protein,
     residual DNA and leached Protein A entering the step are known for every run.
  2. The flow-through and the wash are each sampled as a single pooled fraction, so
     that the host cell protein mass balance across the step can be closed.




                                                 20
  3. The eluate pool is sampled after collection and mixing are complete. It carries
     every response of the study.
  4. A strip fraction is sampled and retained. It is tested only where a run fails to
     close its mass balance.

Replication is concentrated at the centre point. The screening design carries 3 centre
points and the response-surface design 4, each run at the set-point of every factor.
Samples are assigned to analytical runs in an order that does not follow the design
order, so that drift in an assay cannot align with a factor. Retained samples are held
under the conditions and for the period stated in the operating procedure SOP-2010,
so that a result can be retested where it is questioned.



5.5 Statistical methods

All factors are analysed in coded units. Each factor is coded so that the centre of its
range studied is 0 and its ends are −1 and +1. At this step the centre of each range
is the set-point (Table 6.1). Coding makes the estimated effects comparable across
factors with different units, and it makes the intercept the predicted response at the
set-point. The letter codes of Table 6.2 are used for the terms of both models.
The screening design is analysed by ordinary least squares with all main effects and
all two-factor interactions. An effect is reported as twice the coded coefficient, which
is the change in the response across the full range studied of that factor. A term is
significant when its p-value is below α = 0.05, the level that PCMP-001 §6.2 sets for
model terms. The screening analysis identifies which factors and which interactions
change each response, and by how much. It is not the predictive model of the step.
Its centre points detect curvature but cannot attribute it to a factor, and a model
without quadratic terms interpolates linearly between the corners of the design. The
response-surface model is the predictive model. The operating region, the PARs, the
judgement over the NOR, the verification of the platform worst cases and the capa-
bility estimate all come from it.
The response-surface design is analysed with a full quadratic model in the 4 factors,
with main effects, two-factor interactions and pure quadratic terms. With the inter-
cept the model has 15 terms. Each fitted model is reported with R², adjusted R² and
predicted R², the last computed from the prediction error sum of squares (PRESS). It
is also reported with the model F-test and with the test for lack of fit against the pure
error from the centre points. The residuals are plotted against the predicted values
and against the run order, and on a normal probability plot. The influence of each
run is given by its Cook’s distance. Section 7.2 states the tests a model must pass
before it is used for prediction.
A factor with no significant term in either model is reported as having no demon-
strated effect on that response. That statement is bounded by the range studied and
by the power of the design. PCR-007 states it in those terms, not as an absence of
effect in general.




                                           21
A prediction interval for a single run is exact. It uses the t distribution and includes
both the residual variance and the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients. For pool
aggregate and pool host cell protein it is computed at manufacturing scale, by sub-
tracting the offset of Section 5.1.
Capability is estimated by Monte Carlo simulation. The simulation draws 2,000
batches. Each parameter is drawn from the same normal distribution as in the
NOR-propagated analysis of Section 8, the fitted models are evaluated at each draw,
and a one-sided process capability index (Cpk) is computed for each output with a
criterion in Table 7.1. This plan sets no minimum capability index for the outputs
of this step. The decision rules are those of Section 7. The capability estimate is
reported beside them as supporting evidence, with the margin between the predicted
distribution and the criterion. The capability of the drug substance is estimated
from the integrated model of the train. PCMR-001 judges it against the campaign
thresholds of PCMP-001 §7.9, a target Cpk of 1.33 and a minimum of 1.0.
Both analyses of the PAR determination (Section 8) use the response-surface model,
and its Monte Carlo part uses a fixed random seed, so that a repeat of the analysis on
the recorded data returns the same ranges.

6.2 Screening design

The screening study is a two-level full factorial design in the 4 multivariate factors,
with 16 factorial runs and 3 centre points, 19 runs in total. A full factorial was chosen



                                           23
over a fraction for two reasons. The number of factors is small, and protein load
is expected to interact with each of the other factors through the peak broadening
that rising ligand occupancy produces. A resolution IV fraction would alias these two-
factor interactions with each other in pairs, and the analysis could not say which of
them is active. The full factorial estimates every main effect and every two-factor
interaction independently.
The runs are made in random order. The centre points are spread through the cam-
paign and not grouped, so that drift in the model or in the feed material shows in
the centre point spread and not in a factor effect. All runs are made on a single con-
ditioned feed lot where the available material allows. Where more than one lot is
needed, the lot is recorded with the run and the centre points are balanced across
lots. Appendix A gives the planned coded design.



6.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study is a face-centred central composite design in the 4 factors
of the screening study. It has 16 factorial runs, 8 axial runs and 4 centre points, 28
runs in total. The axial runs sit on the faces of the cube, so that no run is made outside
the range studied of any factor. The ranges studied are already wider than the NORs,
and a rotatable design would need settings outside them for which the step has no
prior support.
The design adds the quadratic terms that the screening design cannot estimate. Cur-
vature is expected for elution stop collect and elution buffer pH. Both change where
the pool is cut relative to the aggregate peak. Because that peak has a maximum,
equal steps in the cut point add unequal amounts of aggregate to the pool. Appendix
B gives the planned coded design.
The two designs are made in sequence and analysed separately. Should the screening
analysis identify an active factor that is not carried into the response-surface design,
the design should be augmented before the operating region is declared. The aug-
mentation should then be documented as a change to this plan under Section 13.



6.4 Univariate assessment

Elution flow rate is assessed one factor at a time. Runs are made at the low end, the
set-point and the high end of its range studied (Table 6.1), with every other parameter
at its set-point. Pool aggregate, pool host cell protein and step yield are measured on
each run. The assessment establishes whether a longer residence time sharpens the
elution peak enough to change the aggregate content or the yield of the pool over the
range studied. It also supports the range within which the flow rate is controlled in
manufacture.
A univariate assessment cannot determine whether the flow rate interacts with the
other parameters. That limitation is accepted here for a physical reason. The flow



                                           24
rate changes the residence time of the antibody in the pores of the resin, and with it
the sharpness of the elution peak. It changes neither the net charge of the antibody
nor the ionic strength that sets how strongly the antibody is held. An interaction
with protein load, load/wash conductivity, elution buffer pH or the collection window
is therefore not expected. Should the assessment show an effect on any response
comparable in size with the effects estimated in the screening study, the flow rate
should be carried into a multivariate study before the operating region is declared.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

The criteria in this section decide whether the scale-down model is qualified for an
output, whether a model is adequate, whether an output meets its criterion, where the
operating region lies, whether a platform worst case holds and how each parameter
is classified. The proven acceptable ranges follow in Section 8.



7.1 Qualification of the scale-down model

The scale-down model is qualified for each output separately, by its offset from manu-
facturing scale and not by a test of equivalence (PCMP-001 §7.1). Pool aggregate and
pool host cell protein are compared at both scales and have a criterion at this step.
For each of them the model is qualified when the conclusion against the criterion
over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its 90 % confidence interval.
The conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination of NOR
low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 factors (Section 7.4). A model whose conclusion
changes between the two ends of the interval is not used for that output. Where the
cause of an offset is known, PCR-007 states it. Where the model is not qualified for
an output, PCR-007 says so and states the consequence.



7.2 Model adequacy

A response-surface model is used for prediction only if it passes the four tests of
PCMP-001 §7.2.

  1. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05, the level set for model terms.
  2. Lack of fit against the pure error from the centre points is not significant at α =
     0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. This limits overfitting, be-
     cause a model that fits its own runs much better than it predicts runs left out of
     the fit has more terms than the data support.
  4. The residuals show no pattern against the predicted values or the run order, and
     no single run dominates the fit. The residuals are regressed on the predicted
     values and on the run order, and neither slope may be significant at α = 0.05.
     No run may have a Cook’s distance above 4/n, where n is the number of runs in
     the fit. Cook’s distance measures how far all fitted values move when one run
     is left out of the fit. The cut-off 4/n is the conventional one, and it falls as the
     design grows.




                                          26
The level α = 0.05 is the conventional level for a statistical test. The same level applies
to model terms, to lack of fit and to the regressions of the residuals.
A model that fails the test for lack of fit or the limit on overfitting is either refitted with
a justified change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a range (PCMP-
001 §7.2). Where lack of fit is significant, PCR-007 should state whether the cause
is a missing term or an outlying run. It should establish that from the residuals and
not from the summary statistics alone. Where no adequate model results, PCR-007
reports the output descriptively, against its criterion, over the runs of the designs.
Such an output sets no operating region and no PAR. It meets its criterion only if
every measured result of the designs meets it at manufacturing scale. For fragments
the measured results are the nrCE-SDS results.

7.4 Judgement of an output against its criterion

Every output with a criterion at this step is a quality attribute. Its criterion applies
to each batch, so each output is judged on a single batch. A response-surface model
predicts the mean response, and a single batch scatters around that mean. For this
reason the judgement uses a prediction interval or bound of a single batch, which adds
the scatter of a batch and the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients to the predicted
mean. Three analyses judge an output in this way. They are the judgement over the
NOR, which the qualification uses (Section 7.1), the NOR-propagated PAR (Section 8)
and the verification of the platform worst cases (Section 7.6).
The judgement over the NOR asks whether the exact 95 % prediction interval of a
single run, at manufacturing scale, lies within the criterion at every one of the 81




                                               28
combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 factors. All criteria at this
step are upper limits, so the upper end of the interval decides.
No viral clearance claim is made for this step. The rule that PCMP-001 §6.4 sets for
viral clearance, the mean predicted log reduction at the least favourable combination
of the other parameters, does not apply here.



7.5 Operating region

The operating region is declared where the fitted models predict, at manufacturing
scale, that every output with a criterion in Table 7.1 meets it, over the ranges studied
of the 4 factors. The declared region lies inside the ranges studied, and the NORs lie
inside the declared region. PCR-007 states the margin between the two and identifies
the output and the parameter that bound the region most tightly. Where the models
predict that a criterion is met across the whole range studied of a parameter, PCR-
007 reports that as the finding. The range is not narrowed to create a margin that
the data do not require.
The region is defined on mean predictions. At its edge a single batch meets its crite-
rion only about half the time if variability is symmetrical. For this reason the range
that supports a claim is the NOR-propagated PAR of Section 8.
Step yield has no criterion and does not bound the operating region. PCR-007 reports
it across the region as a measure of process performance. A setting that meets every
criterion at a cost in yield is described as such and is not excluded.

7.7 Parameter classification

Parameters are classified under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001 by
the decision logic of the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009) and
of PCMP-001 §7.7. A parameter is classified from two things together. The first is the
size of the effect the study demonstrates on a quality attribute. The second is the risk
that the parameter moves outside the declared region in manufacture.

  1. A parameter linked to a quality attribute and held well inside the region by the
     commercial control system is a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-
     CPP).
  2. A parameter linked to a quality attribute whose control is narrow against the
     region is a critical process parameter (CPP).
  3. A parameter that changes step yield or another measure of process performance
     without changing a quality attribute is a key process parameter (KPP).
  4. A parameter that changes neither over the range studied is a general process
     parameter (GPP).

Neither PCMP-001 nor SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold for “held well inside” or
“narrow”. PCR-007 therefore states, for each parameter, the size of its effect across
its PAR and the margin of the PAR to the NOR, and it gives the reason for each class on
these two values. A statistically significant effect does not by itself make a parameter
critical. The classes are outcomes of the study and are reported in PCR-007.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A proven acceptable range is determined for each output with a criterion at this step
and for each factor of the response-surface model. The outputs are pool aggregate,
pool host cell protein and fragments. The criterion is the intermediate acceptance
criterion of Table 7.1, 5 % for aggregate, 427 ng/mg for host cell protein and 2 % for
fragments. At this step a PAR is the range of a parameter over which the eluate pool
meets the level it must hand on to anion exchange.
Two analyses are run for every pair of factor and output, and both are reported.

  1. The set-point analysis scans the factor across its range studied with the other
     factors at their set-points. At this step the set-points are also the centre of the
     design. The PAR is the contiguous range around the set-point over which the
     mean prediction, at manufacturing scale, meets the criterion. It shows what
     one parameter can do while the rest of the step is at target.
  2. The NOR-propagated analysis scans the factor across 81 grid points of its range
     studied. At each grid point it draws 2,000 operating points for the other factors.
     Each factor is drawn from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a
     standard deviation of one sixth of the width of its NOR, and clipped to the range
     studied. This assumes that the NOR spans ±3 standard deviations of the rou-
     tine variation of the parameter. Each draw also takes its own set of coefficients
     from the estimated sampling distribution of the fitted coefficients, and its own
     residual. The interval therefore includes the model residual and the uncertainty
     of the coefficients. The 95 % prediction interval of a single batch runs from the
     2.5th to the 97.5th percentile of the simulated batches. A grid point is accept-
     able when the 97.5th percentile, at manufacturing scale, does not exceed the
     criterion. The PAR is the contiguous range of acceptable grid points around the
     set-point.

The NOR-propagated PAR is the one that supports a claim. It is in general narrower
than the set-point PAR, because the range must hold while the rest of the operating
point moves around its set-point.
Where the acceptable settings are not contiguous, the contiguous part that contains
the set-point is reported, and PCR-007 states that this has been done. Where the
criterion is met across the whole range studied, the PAR is the range studied, and the
claim stops at that range. A PAR that reaches the end of the range studied is reported
as reaching it. Where an output does not meet its criterion even at the set-point, PCR-
007 reports that no PAR exists for that output and states the consequence for the NOR.
A parameter that affects several outputs has a PAR for each, and the range carried




                                          32
into the control strategy is their intersection (PCMP-001 §7.6). An output without an
adequate model sets no range (Section 7.2).
Each output with an adequate model is shown in a plot of its predicted response
against the parameter with the largest main effect on it. The plot shades the accept-
able region green and marks the set-point and the NOR. The Monte Carlo analysis
uses the fixed seed 20240724, so a repeat of the analysis on the recorded data returns
the same ranges.
A PAR is a statement about one parameter while the others are held at their set-points
or drawn around them. The operating region of Section 7.5 is a statement about the 4
factors together. A combination of settings that each lie inside their PAR may still fall
outside the region where an interaction is active. Both are reported, and the control
strategy is built on the region.

Data generated under this plan are recorded and kept under the ALCOA+ princi-
ples. Every result is attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate,
and the record is complete, consistent, enduring and available. Run conditions and
observations are recorded in the electronic laboratory notebook at the time of execu-
tion, with the date of each run, so that the execution window of each study is part
of the record (Section 11). The calibration status of each instrumented system on
the dates of use is recorded with the study. Chromatograms and their integration
are kept in the chromatography data system under audit trail. Analytical results are
transferred from the validated instrument systems into the study data set without
manual transcription wherever the systems allow, and a second person verifies any
manual entry.
The analysis data set, the analysis scripts and the fitted models are kept with the
report, so that every number in PCR-007 can be traced to the run that produced it.
A recorded value may be changed after review only under the correction procedure,
with the original value, the reason and the person who made the change retained.
A departure from an approved procedure or a good manufacturing practice (GMP)
requirement, or an unplanned event during execution, is recorded as a deviation when
it occurs. PCR-007 reports each deviation with its investigation, its root cause and
its impact on the conclusions, whether or not a conclusion changes. Work that this
plan does not foresee is not a deviation. PCR-007 records it as a discrepancy from
the plan, with what the plan foresaw, what was done and why.

12 Risks and assumptions

Four risks to the study are recognized, and each has a response.

  1. The scale-down model may differ from the commercial column in a respect the
     qualification does not test. Under the offset rule a difference in mean is accept-
     able when it is known precisely enough (Section 7.1). The qualification does
     not compare the flow distribution inside the two columns, which plate count and
     peak asymmetry bound only indirectly. Should a difference in packing quality
     or flow distribution be found later, the affected conclusions would have to be
     revisited at scale.
  2. The feed material may not be representative. The load is the neutralized pool
     of low-pH viral inactivation, and its aggregate and host cell protein content set
     the starting point of every clearance the study measures. Load attributes are
     measured on every run and reported with the results, so that a clearance can be
     told apart from a low starting level. Where a lot proves out of trend on any load
     attribute, the runs made on it should be identified in PCR-007 and their influence
     on the fitted models assessed.
  3. The resin may behave differently at another point in its life cycle. All character-
     ization runs are made on resin within the cycle count qualified under the resin
     life cycle procedure SOP-2008, and the conclusions are bounded by that cycle
     count. Performance at the end of the qualified life is established by the life cycle
     study, not here.
  4. Assay variability may hide a small effect. The centre points give the reproducibil-
     ity of each response under study conditions, and an effect smaller than that
     spread is not claimed.

Five assumptions are made.

  1. The platform mechanisms apply to A-Mab at this step. The qualification and the
     centre point performance test this assumption, and the study does not take it as
     given.
  2. The parameters of Table 6.1 are the parameters that matter. RA-001 established
     this, and the study can test it only within its own factors.
  3. A quadratic model describes the responses over the ranges studied. The tests of
     Section 7.2 check this for each response.
  4. The NOR of each parameter spans ±3 standard deviations of its routine variation.
     The NOR-propagated analysis rests on this assumption (Section 8).
  5. Fragments separate in the same way at both scales. The qualification does not
     compare them, so their judgement rests on the mechanism stated in Section 5.1.
