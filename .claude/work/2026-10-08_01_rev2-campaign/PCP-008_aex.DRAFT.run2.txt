1 Purpose and scope

This plan sets out the Stage 1 process characterization of the anion exchange chro-
matography step of the A-Mab drug substance process (U.S. Food and Drug Adminis-
tration 2011). The step is Step 8 of the train and the last chromatography step before
virus filtration. It is operated in flow-through mode. The characterization supports
the transfer of the process from the development site to the commercial drug sub-
stance site under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. It is executed on a scale-down
model of the commercial column (Section 5.1).
The study covers the five process parameters that the risk assessment RA-001 carried
forward for this step. Of these, four are studied in a screening design and a response
surface design, and operating flow rate is assessed one factor at a time. The responses
measured on the flow-through pool are host cell protein, the log reduction factors of
the two model viruses, step yield and fragments. Residual DNA and leached Protein
A are measured on the pool of every response surface run and are monitored, not
modelled (Section 5.4). The commercial process is operated at a bioreactor scale of
15,000 L, and the anion exchange step processes the cation exchange pool derived
from a single batch at that scale.
Three activities lie outside this plan. Resin packing, sanitization and life cycle are
qualified under the resin procedure SOP-2008, and none of them is varied here. Per-
formance at commercial scale is confirmed in Stage 2, during process performance
qualification (PPQ), whose batches run inside the NORs and do not test the edges
of the ranges. The small-virus retentive filter is characterized under its own plan,
PCP-009, and this study claims no clearance that belongs to that filter.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

Anion exchange chromatography is the last chromatography step of the A-Mab pro-
cess and is operated in flow-through mode (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The
resin carries a quaternary amine ligand with a fixed positive charge. At the load
pH the antibody carries a net positive charge, because its isoelectric point lies well
above the pH of the load, and it passes through the bed. Species that carry a net
negative charge at that pH bind to the ligand. These are residual DNA, the acidic
part of the host cell protein population, leached Protein A and virus particles. Yield
in flow-through mode is high, because the product is not retained. Product is lost
only where part of the antibody comes close enough to neutral charge at the load pH
to bind, and the range of load pH is set to avoid that.
Whether an impurity binds depends on its net charge at the load pH and on how
strongly the ionic strength of the buffer screens that charge. At a higher load pH the
pH lies above the isoelectric point of more of the host cell proteins, so more of them
carry a net negative charge and bind. A higher conductivity of the equilibration and
first wash buffer, the Equil/Wash-1 conductivity, screens the electrostatic interaction
and lets weakly bound host cell protein pass with the antibody. A higher load pH also
gives each acidic host cell protein more net negative charge, so more salt is needed
to screen it. The effect of conductivity therefore depends on the load pH, and an
interaction between the two is expected. The design must be able to estimate it free
of confounding (Section 6.2).
The conductivity of the load acts by the same screening while the bed is being loaded.
A higher load conductivity weakens the binding of the more weakly charged impuri-
ties, and as the ligand fills, more strongly charged species displace them, so weakly
charged host cell protein is the first to pass into the pool. A higher protein load brings
more impurity mass onto each litre of resin. Pool host cell protein is expressed per
milligram of antibody, so a higher load raises it only where the bound impurity ap-
proaches the capacity of the ligand and starts to break through. Within the range
studied the impurity mass is expected to stay below that capacity. Flow rate sets the
residence time in the bed, and with it how far mass transfer brings each species to
the ligand before the fluid moves on. Binding at equilibrium does not depend on resi-
dence time. Virus particles and DNA are large and diffuse slowly, so their binding is
limited by mass transfer and is the most likely to fall at a short residence time.
The step carries a modular viral clearance claim for an enveloped and a non-enveloped
model virus (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a). Particles of the retro-
virus model, XMuLV, carry a net negative charge at the load pH and bind the ligand




                                           13
by the same electrostatic interaction as the acidic impurities. The parvovirus model,
MVM, has no envelope and is small, and its capsid also carries a net negative charge
at the load pH, so it binds by the same mechanism. For both viruses, clearance is ex-
pected to fall as the load pH drops toward the point at which the particle surface loses
its negative charge, and as the conductivity rises (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
The XMuLV claim of this step is orthogonal to that of low-pH inactivation, which acts
on the viral envelope and not on the surface charge. MVM is not inactivated by low
pH, so this step and the virus filter carry the whole claim for it.
Residual DNA is a polyanion at every pH in the range studied and binds the ligand
strongly. Its clearance is expected to be robust to the operating parameters, and it
is monitored across the step rather than modelled (Section 5.4). Leached Protein
A carries a net negative charge at the load pH and binds while the antibody passes
through, so the step is expected to clear the ligand fragments that the capture step
releases. Ligand fragments held in complex by the antibody Fc are removed only
where the complex dissociates, and no clearance is claimed for that fraction. PCMP-
001 Version 2.0 sets a fragment criterion for the pool of this step and no aggregate
criterion (Table 7.1 of PCMP-001). Fragments are therefore measured and judged at
this step (Section 5.3), and aggregate is not a response of the designs.
The platform anion exchange step has been used to manufacture three previously
licensed humanized IgG1 antibodies, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and through A-Mab
clinical supply (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). That experience established the
mode of operation, the buffer system and the clearance the step delivers, including
the platform worst cases of this step on which the criteria of the upstream steps rely
(Section 7) and the platform claims for both viruses (Section 8).



4.2 Quality attributes in scope

The step contributes to the cumulative clearance of both model viruses. It claims 5.5
log10 for XMuLV, of a cumulative requirement of 16.7 log10, and 4 log10 for MVM, of
a cumulative requirement of 8.6 log10 (Table 8.1). It clears three impurities that are
formed upstream and cleared by more than one step, host cell protein, residual DNA
and leached Protein A. Fragments pass through the step unchanged on the platform
and are judged in its pool. Table 4.1 lists these attributes with their drug substance
criteria and their criticality.
Criticality was assigned with Tool #1 of the A-Mab case study, from the impact of
each attribute on safety and efficacy and the uncertainty in that impact (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009; International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). The two viral
clearance attributes carry very high criticality by rule, because a viral contamination
would harm the patient directly, whatever their score (master plan PCMP-001 §3).

4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

Each parameter of the step was ranked in the risk assessment RA-001 on its potential
impact on a critical quality attribute and on its potential to interact with other param-
eters, and the study type was assigned from the ranking. Parameters ranked high
on both counts were assigned to the multivariate design. Parameters ranked high on
impact but low on interaction were assigned to a univariate assessment. Parameters
held to a platform specification with no credible path to a quality attribute were not
studied, and their ranges are carried from platform knowledge and from the modular
claims established on prior products.
The outcome for this step is 4 parameters in the multivariate design and 1 assessed
one at a time (Table 6.1). The highest ranks went to load pH, Equil/Wash-1 conductiv-
ity and load conductivity, because together they set how strongly an acidic host cell
protein binds. A low load pH and a high conductivity both weaken that binding, and
together they let host cell protein flow through with the antibody. The design also
includes protein load, because the impurity mass loaded onto the column scales with
it, and the ligand has a finite capacity for the species it binds. For operating flow rate

a univariate assessment was chosen, because binding of the small, highly charged
impurities is fast relative to the residence time at every flow rate in the range.
The material loaded onto the step is the cation exchange pool, whose attributes are
set by the preceding step, characterized under the cation exchange plan PCP-007.
This study varies the parameters of the anion exchange step and does not vary the
quality of its load.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The study will be executed on a scale-down model of the commercial anion exchange
column, qualified under SOP-1001. The model keeps the bed height, the linear flow
velocity, the protein load in grams of antibody per litre of resin, and the equilibra-
tion, load, wash and chase volumes expressed in column volumes. Scaling on these
variables holds the residence time and the impurity mass per unit of ligand equal
to the commercial step. The residence time sets how far mass transfer brings each
species to the ligand before the fluid moves on, and the impurity mass per unit of
ligand sets how close the bed comes to its capacity. The resin, the buffers and the
packing method are the same as at commercial scale.
Qualification will compare the model against commercial-equivalent runs on step
yield, pool host cell protein, residual DNA, leached Protein A, the pressure across
the bed and the shape of the flow-through peak. PCMP-001 Version 2.0 replaces the
acceptance rule of Version 1.0 by the offset rule of its §7.1. The offset of an output is
the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean of the manufacturing-
scale batches, reported with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval, each end of which
is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound. The offset of each output with a criterion at
this step is estimated in this way. The offsets of the other compared outputs are
recorded where both scales measure them, and no conclusion depends on them. Of
the outputs compared, pool host cell protein is the only one with a criterion at this
step (Section 8), so the qualification decision concerns it alone.
The qualification compares 3 scale-down runs at set-point with 5 batches made at man-

qualification record is held with the study data and is cited in PCR-008.
The qualification does not compare fragments or viral clearance at the two scales.

and the impurity results describe the same step.
The centre point of each design will be executed in replicate. The replicates estimate
pure error, and pure error is the reference against which lack of fit is tested (Sec-
tion 5.5). A replicate is a full independent execution of the step and not a repeat
measurement of one pool, because a repeat injection estimates assay variability and
lack of fit must be judged against the variability of the process.

5.5 Statistical methods

Each parameter will be coded so that the set-point is 0 and the edges of the char-
acterization range are ±1. Coding places the parameters on one scale, so that an
effect estimated in coded units is comparable across parameters measured in differ-
ent physical units. Effects will be converted back to natural units when a range is
reported.
The screening data will be analysed by ordinary least squares with every main effect
and every two-factor interaction in the model. An effect will be reported as twice the
coded coefficient, which is the change in the response across the full characterization
range of the parameter. Terms significant at p < 0.05 will be identified as active. The
screening model identifies which parameters act on which response, and it is not the
model from which an operating region is derived.
The response surface data will be analysed with a full quadratic model in the 4 mul-
tivariate parameters, fitted on the coded factors. This is the predictive model of the
study. The operating region and the proven acceptable ranges of the multivariate
parameters will be derived from it, and PCR-008 will report the coefficients, the anal-
ysis of variance and the diagnostics for each response. A quality attribute is judged
on a single batch, because its criterion applies to each batch. The model predicts
the mean response, and at the edge of a region drawn on mean predictions a single
batch meets its criterion only about half the time if variability is symmetrical. The
judgement of a quality attribute therefore uses the 95 % prediction interval of a single
batch, and viral clearance is judged on the mean prediction at the worst case within
the NOR (Section 8).
Each response surface model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R², com-
puted from the prediction error sum of squares, with the model F-test, and with the
lack of fit test against pure error from the centre point replicates. PCMP-001 Version
2.0 (§7.2) sets four conditions for a model to be adequate for prediction, and they
replace the adequacy rule of Version 1.0:

  1. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  2. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. This is the limit on overfitting,
     because a model that fits its own runs much better than it predicts runs left out
     of the fit has more terms than the data support.
  4. The residuals show no pattern against the predicted values or the run order, and
     no single run dominates the fit.

PCMP-001 sets the levels and the limit of the first three conditions. The fourth carries
no limit in PCMP-001, and this plan tests it by the two tests that every plan of the
campaign applies. The absolute residuals are regressed on the predicted values, and



                                           20
the residuals are regressed on the run order, each at α = 0.05. A significant slope
shows a spread that grows with the level of the response in the first case and a drift
over the execution in the second. A run whose Cook’s distance exceeds 4/n, where
n is the number of runs in the design, is taken to dominate the fit. The factor of 4 is
the usual convention, and for the 28 runs of the response surface design the cut-off
is 0.14.
A parameter with no active term in either model will be described as having no de-
tectable effect on that response over the range studied. The range studied bounds
that statement, and it will be stated with the range attached.
Capability at commercial scale is estimated from the integrated process model of the
train (PCMP-001 §6.5). The model runs 2,000 simulated batches through every step
in sequence, with each process parameter drawn from the same distribution as in
the second proven acceptable range analysis (Section 8). Capability is reported as
Cpk against the drug substance criterion, one-sided for each attribute of this step,
since each of their criteria has one limit. It is judged in the master report PCMR-001
against two values (PCMP-001 §7.9). A Cpk of at least 1.33 is expected in routine
manufacture, and a Cpk below 1 needs an action in the control strategy. Between
the two values PCMR-001 names the side of the criterion that limits the Cpk and the
parameters that drive it. The same simulation gives, for each attribute, the proba-
bility that the drug substance is out of specification. The estimate carries no source
of commercial variability that the scale-down model does not reproduce, and confir-
mation at commercial scale is a Stage 2 activity (U.S. Food and Drug Administration
2011).

6.2 Screening design

The 4 multivariate parameters will be studied in a two-level full factorial design of
16 runs, augmented with 3 centre points, for 19 runs in total. A full factorial in
these parameters estimates every main effect and every two-factor interaction with-
out confounding, which the interaction between load pH and Equil/Wash-1 conductiv-
ity requires. The centre points estimate pure error and give a test for curvature, and
curvature at the centre indicates that a quadratic model is needed.
Runs will be executed in randomized order within one resin lot and one load lot. Ran-
domization protects the estimates against drift in the load material, in the resin or in
the assay over the execution period. Where the design cannot be completed within
one load lot, the lots should be treated as a block and the block term reported with
the effects. The planned matrix is given in Appendix A.



6.3 Response-surface design

The same parameters will be studied in a face-centred central composite design of 28
runs. The design adds 8 axial runs at the faces of the cube to 16 factorial runs and 4
centre points. Axial points placed at the faces keep every run inside the characteri-
zation range, which an axial distance beyond the faces would not.
The quadratic terms this design supports are needed because binding switches on
over a narrow band of pH, as the net charge of a bound species changes sign near its
isoelectric point, so clearance rises more sharply over the part of the load pH range
that lies nearest the isoelectric point of the bound species than over the rest of it. A
design with two levels in each parameter would fit that curve as a straight line and
would put the edge of the operating region at the wrong pH. The planned matrix is
given in Appendix B.
The response surface design will be executed on load material qualified under the cri-
teria in Section 7. Where it cannot be executed on the same load lot as the screening
design, the second lot will be compared with the first on charge variant profile, host
cell protein and protein concentration before the runs begin.

6.4 Univariate assessment

Operating flow rate will be assessed one at a time over the range in Table 6.1, with
every other parameter held at its set-point. The low, the centre and the high setting of
the range will be executed, and pool host cell protein and step yield will be measured
on each. A viral clearance run at the high setting will be included, because residence
time is the mechanism by which flow rate could act on clearance and the high setting
is the shortest residence time in the range.
A univariate assessment cannot resolve an interaction between flow rate and another
parameter, and the claim it supports is bounded by the assumption that the effect of
flow rate does not depend on where the other parameters sit. Binding of the small,
highly charged impurities is fast relative to the residence time at every flow rate in
the range, so residence time is not expected to limit their clearance anywhere in
the design. Virus particles and DNA are large and diffuse slowly, so their binding is
limited by mass transfer and is the most likely to fall at the shortest residence time.
Should the assessment show an effect of flow rate on any response, the parameter
will be carried into a multivariate design before its range is claimed.
The result will support the range for flow rate as a proven acceptable range, and it
will not enter the response surface model.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

PCR-008 will report the executed study against each rule of this section, and a crite-
rion that is not met will be dispositioned as a deviation rather than revised after the
fact. The rules are those of Version 1.0, except where PCMP-001 Version 2.0 sets a
rule in place of one, and each item below says which applies. Several rules of Version
1.0 set no numerical threshold, namely the load profile, the match of the spiked load,
the comparison of a second load lot, an effect of flow rate and an effect attributed
to the infectivity assay. This revision adds none, and PCR-008 states the approach it
applies to each.

  1. Scale-down model (PCMP-001 Version 2.0). The qualification runs are made, and
     the offset of each compared output is estimated, before the first characterization
     run. The model is qualified for pool host cell protein when the conclusion over
     the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its 90 % confidence interval,
     and the decision is made at analysis (Section 5.1). A model not qualified for an
     output is not used for that output. This replaces the acceptance rule of Version
     1.0 (Section 1.1, change 3).
  2. Load material (Version 1.0). Load material will be accepted as representative
     where its charge variant profile by icIEF, its host cell protein content, its protein
     concentration, its pH and its conductivity fall within the release profile of the

     inition of the operating region and re-executed in full on a qualified load. Data
     from an invalidated execution will be retained and may be used to investigate
     and confirm the cause, and they will not be used to set a range, a classification
     or a clearance claim.
  3. Model adequacy (PCMP-001 Version 2.0). A response surface model is used for
     prediction only if it passes the four conditions of Section 5.5. A model that fails
     the lack of fit test or the limit on overfitting is either refitted with a justified
     change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a range (PCMP-001
     §7.2). Where no adequate model results, PCR-008 reports the response descrip-
     tively, against the criterion, over the runs of the design. This replaces the ade-
     quacy rule of Version 1.0 (Section 1.1, change 5).
  4. Outputs against their criteria (Version 1.0, with the criteria of PCMP-001 Version

5. Operating region (Version 1.0, with the statistic of PCMP-001 Version 2.0). The

A proven acceptable range is the range of one parameter over which the attribute
it governs stays within its criterion, with the other parameters held or varied as the




                                                28
analysis states. Two analyses will be run for every pair of a parameter and an attribute
it governs, and both will be reported.
The first holds the other parameters at their set-points and evaluates the fitted re-
sponse surface model across the parameter’s characterization range. The second
varies the other parameters within their normal operating ranges by Monte-Carlo
simulation of the fitted model, including its residual variance, and reads the range
over which the predictive interval of the response stays within the criterion. The in-
terval used is two sided and covers the central 95 % of the simulated distribution. The
normal operating ranges in the control strategy will be set from the second analysis,
and the first will be reported beside it.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 (§6.4) defines the second analysis, which it calls the NOR-
propagated analysis, and what it compares with the criterion. Each other parameter
is drawn from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a standard devia-
tion equal to the width of its NOR divided by 6, and clipped to the characterization
range. The draw treats each NOR as ±3 standard deviations of the routine variation
of its parameter. For pool host cell protein and fragments the analysis compares the
95 % prediction interval of a single batch, from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile of
the simulated batches, which includes the model residual and the uncertainty of the
fitted coefficients. For an upper limit the 97.5th percentile must not exceed the limit.
For XMuLV and MVM it compares the worst case within the NOR. At each setting the
other parameters are set to every combination of their NOR low, set-point and NOR
high, 27 combinations in all, and the lowest mean predicted log reduction factor must
meet the claim, because viral clearance is validated under worst-case conditions. For
viral clearance this replaces the predictive interval of Version 1.0 (Section 1.1, change
2).
The analysis will be evaluated on a grid of 81 settings across the characterization
range, with 2,000 Monte-Carlo draws at each setting, under a fixed random seed
recorded with the analysis (Section 9). A range will be reported only where the ac-
ceptable settings form one contiguous interval containing the set-point. Where they
do not, the discontinuity will be reported as it stands and no range will be claimed
for that pair.
Each range will be reported in the natural units of the parameter together with the
criterion it was measured against (Section 7). For each governed attribute the report
will also carry a plot of the response against its governing parameter, with the accept-
able region shaded green and the set-point and the normal operating range marked.
The governing parameter is the multivariate parameter with the largest main effect
on that attribute in the response surface model.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 (§7.6) adds three rules. A range is proven acceptable for an
attribute when the NOR-propagated analysis meets the criterion across it, and for a
parameter that affects several attributes the range carried into the control strategy
is the intersection of its ranges. Where an output does not meet its criterion even
at the set-point, PCR-008 reports that no proven acceptable range exists for that
attribute and states the consequence for the NOR. A range that reaches the edge of
the characterization range is reported as reaching it, and no claim is made beyond
it.

control, and the script, its inputs and its outputs will be retained with the study record.
The Monte-Carlo analyses use a fixed random seed of 20240724, so that a reported
range can be reproduced exactly from the same data. Any change to the analysis
after the first execution will be recorded with its reason.
Data will be reviewed by a second qualified person against the raw records before
the analysis is run, and the review will be documented. Deviations from this plan will
be recorded and dispositioned in PCR-008 under the site deviation procedure, with
an impact assessment for each.

11 Deliverables and schedule

The deliverable of this plan is PCR-008, the process characterization report for the
anion exchange step. It will report the executed designs, the fitted models and their
adequacy, the operating region, the proven acceptable ranges, the classification of
each parameter, the clearance of both model viruses against the claims of this step,
the verification of the platform worst cases and the contribution this step makes to
the control strategy. PCR-008 rolls up into the master report PCMR-001, which con-
solidates the cumulative viral clearance claim across the contributing steps.
The study will run in four phases. The scale-down model qualification runs come first,
so that the offset of each compared output is estimated before the first characteriza-
tion run. The screening design is executed next, then the response surface design,
then the univariate assessment. The viral clearance runs may run in parallel with
the univariate assessment, because they use a separate laboratory and separate load
aliquots.

12 Risks and assumptions

The scale-down model may not represent the commercial column. The offset rule of
Section 5.1 addresses this risk for pool host cell protein. The offsets of fragments and
viral clearance are unknown, not zero, and their judgement rests on the spiked scale-
down model and on the expectation that the step does not change the fragment level
of its load. The operating region is not confirmed at scale at the edges of the ranges,
and Stage 2 confirms performance at commercial scale inside the NORs only.
The load material may not be representative of the routine cation exchange pool. A
load with more acidic charge variants carries more antibody whose net charge at
the load pH is close to neutral, and part of it can bind, so the yield is at risk. Load
material will be qualified before execution against the criteria in Section 7, and a
design executed on non-representative material will be re-executed.
The virus spike may perturb the load. The spike volume will be bounded and the
spiked load compared with the unspiked load, as Section 5.1 describes.
Material supply may constrain the execution. The designs together require a large vol-
ume of cation exchange pool, and pooling several cation exchange batches to obtain
it would change the load attributes the study holds constant. Load lots will therefore
be scheduled against the runs before execution begins.
The study assumes that platform knowledge from X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab transfers
to A-Mab at this step, because A-Mab has an isoelectric point well above the load pH
range and passes through the bed as the platform antibodies do, and the platform
buffer system is unchanged. It assumes the resin performs within its qualified life
cycle over the execution period under SOP-2008. It assumes that parameters not
carried into the study are held to platform specification, and an excursion in any of
them will be recorded as a deviation and assessed for impact in PCR-008.
