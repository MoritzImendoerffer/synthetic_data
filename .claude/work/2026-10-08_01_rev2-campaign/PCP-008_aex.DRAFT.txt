1 Purpose and scope

This plan sets out the Stage 1 process characterization of the anion exchange chro-
matography step of the A-Mab drug substance process (U.S. Food and Drug Adminis-
tration 2011). The step is Step 8 of the train and the last chromatography step before
virus filtration. It is operated in flow-through mode. The characterization supports
the transfer of the process from the development site to the commercial drug sub-
stance site under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. It is run on a scale-down model
of the manufacturing column (Section 5.1).
The study covers the five process parameters that the risk assessment RA-001 carried
forward for this step. Of these, four are studied in a screening design and a response-
surface design, and operating flow rate is assessed one factor at a time. The outputs
measured on the pool are host cell protein, the log reduction factors of the two model
viruses, fragments and step yield. The commercial process runs at a bioreactor scale
of 15,000 L, and the anion exchange step processes the cation exchange pool of one
batch at that scale.
Three activities lie outside this plan. Resin packing, sanitization and life cycle are
qualified under the resin procedure SOP-2008, and none of them is varied here. Con-
firmation of the ranges at commercial scale belongs to Stage 2 and is made during
process performance qualification (PPQ). The small-virus retentive filter is character-
ized under its own plan, PCP-009, and this study claims no clearance that belongs to
that filter.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

Anion exchange chromatography is the last chromatography step of the A-Mab pro-
cess and is operated in flow-through mode (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The
resin carries a quaternary amine ligand with a fixed positive charge. At the load pH
the antibody, whose isoelectric point is basic, carries a net positive charge and passes
through the bed. Species with a net negative charge at that pH bind to the ligand.
These are residual DNA, the acidic part of the host cell protein population, leached
Protein A and virus particles, whose surfaces are acidic. Yield in flow-through mode
is high, because the product is not retained. Product is lost only if its charge comes
close enough to neutral at the load pH for part of it to bind, and the range of load pH
is set to avoid that.
Whether an impurity binds depends on its net charge at the load pH and on how far
the ionic strength of the buffer shields that charge. At a higher load pH the pH lies
above the isoelectric point of more of the host cell proteins, so more of them carry
a net negative charge and bind. A higher Equil/Wash-1 conductivity shields the elec-
trostatic interaction and lets weakly bound host cell protein pass with the antibody.
The two parameters therefore act on the same binding equilibrium from opposite
sides, and an interaction between them is expected. The designs must estimate that
interaction free of confounding (Section 6.2).
The conductivity of the load acts by the same shielding, but during loading, when
the impurities compete with each other for the ligand. Its range is bounded by the
composition of the cation exchange pool that the previous step delivers. Protein load
acts through the impurity mass that comes with the antibody. Pool host cell protein is
expressed per milligram of antibody, so a higher load raises it only where the bound
impurity approaches the capacity of the ligand and starts to break through. Within
the range studied the impurity mass is expected to stay well below that capacity.
Flow rate sets the residence time in the bed. Binding of the small, highly charged
impurities is fast, so only a small effect of flow rate is expected within the range
studied.
The step carries a modular viral clearance claim for an enveloped and a non-enveloped
model virus (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a). Particles of the retro-
virus model, XMuLV, present an acidic surface at the load pH and bind the ligand
by the same electrostatic interaction as the acidic impurities. The parvovirus model,
MVM, has no envelope and is small. Its capsid is also acidic at the load pH, so it binds
by the same mechanism. For both viruses, clearance is expected to fall as the load pH
drops toward the point at which the particle surface loses its negative charge, and as




                                          13
the conductivity rises (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The XMuLV claim of this
step is orthogonal to that of low-pH inactivation, which acts on the viral envelope and
not on the surface charge. MVM is not inactivated by low pH, so this step and the
virus filter carry the whole claim for it.
Residual DNA is a polyanion at every pH in the range studied and binds the ligand
strongly. Its clearance is expected to be large and robust to the operating parameters,
and it is monitored rather than modelled (Section 5.4). Leached Protein A is acidic and
binds while the antibody passes, so the step is expected to clear the ligand fragments
that the capture step releases. Ligand fragments held in complex by the Fc region
of the antibody pass with it unless the complex dissociates. On the platform no step
after cation exchange changes the aggregate or the fragment level of the pool (master
plan PCMP-001 §7.3). Aggregate is therefore not a response of the designs at this step.
Fragments are measured, because the pool of this step carries a criterion for them.
The platform anion exchange step was used to manufacture three earlier licensed an-
tibodies, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and through A-Mab clinical supply (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009). That experience established the mode of operation, the buffer
system and the clearance to expect, including the platform worst cases on which the
criteria of the upstream steps rely (Section 7) and the platform claims for both viruses
(Section 8). The separation is by charge and does not depend on the fine structure of
the antibody. For this reason the study is expected to confirm and bound behaviour
that is already understood. The ranges studied are wider than the NOR (Section 6.1),
so that the edge of the operating region falls inside the data.



4.2 Quality attributes in scope

The step sets one critical quality attribute, the cumulative clearance of MVM, and
clears four others (Table 4.1). Fragments pass through the step unchanged on the
platform and are judged in its pool. Criticality was assigned with Tool #1 of the A-
Mab case study, as the product of impact and uncertainty, and attributes of high or
very high criticality are called critical (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009; Interna-
tional Council for Harmonisation 2023b). The two viral clearance attributes carry
very high criticality by rule, because a viral contamination would harm the patient
directly, whatever their score (master plan PCMP-001 §3).

Clearance of MVM is a viral safety attribute of very high criticality. The register
assigns it to this step, although its cumulative requirement is met by the claims of
this step and of virus filtration together (PCMP-001 §7.4). XMuLV clearance is shared
with low-pH inactivation and virus filtration, and each step adds its own increment

and carries no criterion at this step.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

Each parameter of the step was ranked in the risk assessment RA-001 on its potential
impact on a CQA and on its potential to interact with other parameters, and the study
type was assigned from the ranking. Parameters ranked high on both counts were
assigned to the multivariate designs. Parameters ranked high on impact but low on
interaction were assigned to univariate assessment. Parameters held to a platform
specification with no credible path to a quality attribute were not studied, and their
ranges come from platform knowledge and from the modular claims of the prior prod-
ucts (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
For this step four parameters were assigned to the designs and one to univariate
assessment (Table 6.1). The highest ranks went to load pH, Equil/Wash-1 conductivity
and load conductivity, because they act on the same binding equilibrium and their
interaction is the route by which impurity breaks through. The designs also include



                                                15
protein load, because the impurity mass loaded rises with it and the ligand has a finite
capacity for the bound species. For operating flow rate a univariate assessment was
chosen, because binding of the small, highly charged impurities is fast against the
residence time at every flow rate in the range.
The load of this step is the cation exchange pool, whose attributes are controlled
by the preceding step under the cation exchange plan PCP-007. This study varies
the parameters of the anion exchange step and not the quality of its load. The load
material is qualified against the release profile of the cation exchange pool before
execution (Section 7).

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The study is run on a scale-down model of the manufacturing anion exchange column,
whose qualification follows the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001. The model
keeps the bed height, the linear flow velocity, the protein load in grams of antibody per
litre of resin, and the equilibration, load, wash and chase volumes in column volumes.
Scaling on these quantities keeps the residence time and the impurity mass per unit
of ligand equal to those of the manufacturing column, and the binding equilibrium
depends on these two. The resin, the buffers and the packing method are those of
the manufacturing process.
Qualification compares the model, run at set-point, with batches made at manufactur-
ing scale. The outputs compared are step yield, pool host cell protein, residual DNA,
leached Protein A, the pressure across the bed and the shape of the flow-through
peak. The qualification study S-AEX-SDM has 3 scale-down runs at set-point and 5
manufacturing-scale batches. Its runs, and the offset estimate of each output, come
before the first characterization run of the step (Section 11).
The model is qualified for each output separately, by its offset from manufacturing
scale and not by a test of equivalence (master plan PCMP-001 §7.1). The offset of
an output is the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean of the
manufacturing-scale batches. It is reported with its two-sided 90 % confidence in-
terval, each end of which is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound. Where an output
is compared at both scales, every judgement of a scale-down result for that output
against a criterion first subtracts its offset, so that ranges and conclusions are stated
at manufacturing scale. The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion
against its criterion over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its inter-
val. That conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination of
NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the four multivariate parameters. It comes from
the response-surface model, so the decision is made at analysis and reported in the
paired report PCR-008. A model whose conclusion changes between the two ends of
the interval is not used for that output.
Of the outputs compared, only pool host cell protein has a criterion at this step (Sec-
tion 8). The qualification decision therefore concerns pool host cell protein alone.
The other outputs have their offsets recorded, and no conclusion depends on them.
The qualification does not compare fragments or viral clearance at the two scales, so
their offset is unknown, not zero. Both are judged as the model predicts them. For




                                           17
viral clearance the judgement rests on the spiking model described below, which fol-
lows the scaling of the characterization model. For fragments it rests on the assump-
tion that the fragment level of the pool does not depend on scale. That assumption
rests on the platform, where no step after cation exchange changes the fragment
level (PCMP-001 §7.3), so the pool is expected to carry the fragments of its load at
either scale.
The viral clearance runs are made on the same scale-down model in a containment
laboratory, as a small-scale spiking study (International Council for Harmonisation
2023a). The spiking model is qualified separately, because a virus spike changes the
load itself. That qualification confirms that the spiked load matches the unspiked load
in protein concentration, pH and conductivity, and that the spike volume stays below
the fraction at which these properties move. Spiked and unspiked runs are made on
matched load material, so that the clearance results and the impurity results describe
the same step.
The centre point of each design is run in replicate. The replicates estimate pure error,
against which lack of fit is tested (Section 5.5). A replicate is a full, independent run
of the step from its own load aliquot, not a repeat measurement of one pool. A repeat
injection would estimate the variability of the assay, and lack of fit must be judged
against the variability of the process.



5.2 Operation

The step will be operated as it is at manufacturing scale, under the operating proce-
dure SOP-2011. The column is equilibrated with the equilibration buffer, loaded with
the cation exchange pool adjusted to the target pH and conductivity, and chased with
the first wash buffer. The flow-through and the chase are collected as one pool, from
the start of the load to the end of the chase. The pool is filtered and held for virus
filtration under the hold conditions of the manufacturing process.
Resin, buffers and column packing are controlled to platform specification. Each
resin lot is released against its certificate of analysis and is packed and tested under
the resin procedure SOP-2008. A packed column is accepted on plate count and peak
asymmetry before use. Buffer lots are prepared and released under the site buffer
preparation procedure and are used within their assigned expiry. These inputs are
controlled for identity and quality, and they are not characterized in this study.
The chromatography system will be operated in the temperature-controlled chro-
matography chamber EQ-CHR-118, which is under calibration and change control
and whose next calibration is due on 2026-05-30. Its calibration status will be veri-
fied before the first run and confirmed at the end of the execution period.

5.3 Analytical methods

Each response is measured by a validated method (Table 3.2). Pool host cell protein is
measured by ELISA (AMV-3012) and reported in nanograms per milligram of antibody.
Residual DNA is measured by qPCR (AMV-3014) and leached Protein A by ELISA

5.4 Sampling plan

Samples are taken from the load and from the flow-through pool of every run. The load
sample supports the material qualification of Section 7 and gives the input against
which clearance is calculated. The pool sample carries the responses.

5.5 Statistical methods

Each multivariate parameter is coded so that the low and the high end of its range
studied are −1 and +1 and the centre of the range is 0. Coding puts the parameters on
one scale, so that effects estimated in coded units can be compared across parameters
with different units. For load pH, Equil/Wash-1 conductivity and load conductivity the
centre of the range is the set-point. For protein load it is not. Its set-point of 200 g/L
resin lies above the centre of its range, 175 g/L resin, and is coded +0.2. The centre
points of both designs therefore run at the centre of the protein load range. Effects
are converted back to natural units when a range is reported.
The screening data are fitted by ordinary least squares with every main effect and
every two-factor interaction. An effect is twice the coded coefficient, which is the
change in the response across the full range studied of the parameter. A term is
significant when its p-value is below α = 0.05 (master plan PCMP-001 §6.2). At that
level about one term in 20 with no real effect is called significant by chance. The
screening model identifies which parameters act on which response. It is not the
model from which an operating region or a range is derived.
The response-surface data are fitted with a full quadratic model in the four multi-
variate parameters, on the coded factors. This is the predictive model of the study.
The operating region, the proven acceptable ranges and the verification of the plat-
form worst cases are all derived from it, and the paired report PCR-008 reports its
coefficients, its analysis of variance and its diagnostics for each response. A qual-
ity attribute is judged on a single batch, because its criterion applies to each batch.
A response-surface model predicts the mean response, and at the edge of a region
drawn on mean predictions a single batch meets its criterion only about half the time
if variability is symmetrical. The judgement of a quality attribute therefore uses the
95 % prediction interval of a single batch and not the mean prediction. Viral clearance
is judged at the worst case within the NOR instead (Section 8).



                                           20
Each response-surface model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R², com-
puted from the prediction error sum of squares, with the model F-test, and with the
test of lack of fit against pure error from the centre points. A model is adequate for
prediction when it passes the four conditions of PCMP-001 §7.2:

  1. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  2. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. A model that fits its own
     runs much better than it predicts runs left out of the fit has more terms than the
     data support.
  4. The residuals show no pattern against the predicted values or the run order, and
     no single run dominates the fit.

Condition 4 is judged by two tests, which every plan of the campaign applies in the
same way. The absolute residuals are regressed on the predicted values, and the
residuals are regressed on the run order. A slope significant at α = 0.05 shows, in
the first case, a spread that changes with the level of the response and, in the second,
a drift over the execution. A run with a Cook’s distance above 4/n, where n is the
number of runs in the design, is taken to dominate the fit. This cut-off is a common
convention that falls as the design grows, and for the 28 runs of the response-surface
design it is 0.14.
A model that fails the lack of fit test or the limit on overfitting is either refitted with a
justified change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a range (PCMP-001
§7.2). A model that fails any condition is not used for prediction. Where no adequate
model results, PCR-008 reports the response against its criterion over the runs of the
design, and no range is set from it. A parameter with no significant term in either
model is described as having no detectable effect on that response over the range
studied, and the range is stated with that conclusion.
Capability at commercial scale is estimated from the integrated model of the train
(PCMP-001 §6.5). The model runs 2,000 simulated batches through every step in se-
quence. Each process parameter of each batch is drawn from a normal distribution
centred on its set-point, with a standard deviation equal to the width of its NOR di-
vided by 6, and clipped to the range studied. Capability is reported as Cpk against the
drug substance criterion. It is one-sided for every attribute of this step, since each
has one limit. It is judged in the master report PCMR-001 against the thresholds of
PCMP-001 §7.9. A Cpk of at least 1.33 is expected in routine manufacture, and a Cpk
below 1 needs an action in the control strategy. The same simulation gives, for each
attribute, the probability that the drug substance is out of specification. The estimate
carries no source of commercial variability that the scale-down models do not repro-
duce, and confirmation at commercial scale belongs to Stage 2 (U.S. Food and Drug
Administration 2011).

6.2 Screening design

The four multivariate parameters are studied in a two-level full factorial design of
16 runs with three centre points, 19 runs in total (Appendix A). A full factorial in
these parameters estimates every main effect and every two-factor interaction free
of confounding, which the expected interaction between load pH and Equil/Wash-1
conductivity requires (Section 4.1). The centre points estimate pure error and give a
test for curvature. Curvature at the centre shows that a quadratic model is needed.
Each run measures pool host cell protein, the log reduction factors of XMuLV and
MVM, fragments and step yield.
The runs are made in random order within one resin lot and one load lot. Randomiza-
tion protects the estimates against drift in the load material, the resin or the assays
over the execution period. Where a design cannot be completed within one load lot,
the lots should be treated as a block and the block term reported with the effects.



6.3 Response-surface design

The same parameters are studied in a face-centred central composite design of 28
runs (Appendix B). It adds eight axial runs at the faces of the cube to the 16 factorial
runs and four centre points, and it measures the same responses as the screening
design. With the axial runs at the faces every run stays inside the range studied,
which an axial distance beyond the faces would not allow.
The quadratic terms are needed because binding switches on over a narrow band
of pH, as the net charge of a bound species changes sign near its isoelectric point.
Clearance therefore rises more steeply over the part of the load pH range nearest
that point than over the rest of it. A design with two levels per parameter would fit
that curve as a straight line and would put the edge of the operating region at the
wrong pH.
The response-surface design is run on load material qualified under Section 7. Where
it cannot be run on the same load lot as the screening design, the second lot is com-
pared with the first on charge variant profile, host cell protein and protein concentra-
tion before the runs begin.



6.4 Univariate assessment

The operating flow rate is assessed one factor at a time over the range in Table 6.1,
with every other parameter at its set-point. The low, the centre and the high setting
of the range are run, and pool host cell protein and step yield are measured on each.
A viral clearance run at the high setting is included, because residence time is the
route by which flow rate could act on clearance and the high setting gives the shortest
residence time in the range.




                                          23
A univariate assessment cannot resolve an interaction between flow rate and another
parameter. The range it supports is bounded by the assumption that the effect of flow
rate does not depend on the settings of the other parameters. Binding of the small,
highly charged impurities is fast against the residence time at every flow rate in the
range, so residence time is not expected to limit clearance. Should the assessment
show an effect of flow rate on any response, the parameter will be carried into a
multivariate design before its range is claimed. Otherwise the result supports the
range of flow rate as a proven acceptable range, and it does not enter the response-
surface model.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

This section states the rules by which the study is judged. The paired report PCR-
008 reports the executed study against each of them. A criterion that is not met is
dispositioned as a deviation and is not revised after the fact.

  1. Scale-down model. The qualification runs are made, and the offset of each com-
     pared output is estimated, before the first characterization run. The model is
     qualified for pool host cell protein when the conclusion over the NOR is the
     same with the offset at either end of its 90 % confidence interval (Section 5.1).
     The decision is made at analysis, and a model not qualified for an output is not
     used for that output.
  2. Load material. Load material is representative where its charge variant profile
     by icIEF, its host cell protein content, its protein concentration, its pH and its
     conductivity fall within the release profile of the cation exchange pool. Load
     material outside that profile does not represent routine operation. A design run
     on it is invalidated for the definition of the operating region and run again in full
     on a qualified load. Data from an invalidated execution are retained and may be
     used to investigate and confirm the cause. They are not used to set a range, a
     classification or a clearance claim.
  3. Model adequacy. A response-surface model is used for prediction only if it passes
     the four conditions of Section 5.5. A model that fails is treated as Section 5.5
     states, and a response with no adequate model sets no range.
  4. Outputs against their criteria. Each output with a criterion at this step is judged
     against its intermediate acceptance criterion (Table 8.1), at manufacturing scale
     where the output has an offset. Pool host cell protein must meet ≤ 100 ng/mg
     and fragments ≤ 2 %, each judged on a single batch through the 95 % prediction
     interval. The log reduction factor of XMuLV must meet ≥ 5.5 log10 and that of
     MVM ≥ 4 log10, each judged on the mean prediction at the least favourable com-
     bination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the other parameters (Section 8).
     Step yield carries no criterion and is reported as a performance attribute.
  5. Operating region. The multivariate operating region is the part of the charac-
     terized region in which every output with a criterion meets it at the same time.
     The NOR of every parameter must fall inside that region. Where it does not, the
     NOR will be narrowed in the control strategy, and the change will be carried
     into the master report PCMR-001.
  6. Classification. Each parameter is classified under the parameter classification
     procedure SOP-4001 by the decision logic of the master plan PCMP-001 (§7.7),
     from the effect shown on a CQA and from how well the parameter is controlled. A
     parameter that acts on a CQA and has a low risk of leaving its acceptable range,
     because it is readily controlled and its proven acceptable range is wide against
     its NOR, is a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP). A parameter



                                           25
     with the same kind of effect and a high risk of leaving its acceptable range is a
     critical process parameter (CPP). A parameter that acts on process performance
     but on no quality attribute is a key process parameter (KPP). A parameter with
     no relevant effect on either is a general process parameter (GPP), and the range
     studied bounds that class. Neither PCMP-001 nor SOP-4001 sets a numerical
     threshold for a practically relevant effect or for a wide range. PCR-008 will
     therefore state, for each parameter, the size of each effect across its proven
     acceptable range and the margin between that range and the NOR, and will
     give the reason for each class from them.
  7. Verification of the platform worst cases. The criteria of the upstream steps as-
     sume that this step clears host cell protein at least 4-fold and leached Protein A
     at least 10-fold, the lowest clearance seen on the platform. The host cell protein
     criteria of the Protein A and cation exchange pools and the leached Protein A
     criterion of the Protein A pool are carried back through these values (PCMP-001
     §7.3). The host cell protein assumption is checked in PCR-008 on the response-
     surface model of this step, by the rule of PCMP-001 §7.5. The model is evaluated
     at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the multivariate pa-
     rameters, with the NOR and the set-point as characterized, so that a later change
     of a NOR does not move a criterion. Where the lowest clearance predicted within
     the NOR is below the platform value, the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a
     single batch at the least favourable combination replaces the platform value, and
     the upstream criteria become stricter. Where that bound is above the platform
     value and the model passes the adequacy conditions of Section 5.5, the bound
     replaces the platform value, and the upstream criteria become wider. Other-
     wise the platform value stands. Neither the point prediction nor a confidence
     bound of the mean ever replaces it. Leached Protein A is monitored and not
     fitted (Section 5.4), so its platform value is not checked on a model and stands.
  8. Completion. The study is complete when the scale-down model is qualified for
     pool host cell protein, every planned run is made or dispositioned, every re-
     sponse has an adequate model or a stated reason for the absence of one, the
     proven acceptable ranges contain the NORs, and both model viruses meet the
     claims of this step as item 4 judges them.

A change of a criterion by the verification of item 7 is argued by its risk in PCR-008.
The report states who bears the risk, the patient or the producer, and whether and
where an excursion would be detected. It also states what the downstream steps
must then clear, and the probability that the drug substance is out of specification
(PCMP-001 §7.5).

Two analyses are run for every pair of a multivariate parameter and an attribute with
a criterion at this step, and both are reported. The first holds the other parameters
at their set-points and evaluates the fitted response-surface model across the param-

contiguous range around the set-point over which the mean prediction meets the cri-

of its range studied. What it compares with the criterion depends on the kind of at-
tribute (PCMP-001 §6.4). For pool host cell protein and fragments it compares the 95
% prediction interval of a single batch, because each batch must meet the criterion.

each parameter. The interval includes the model residual and the uncertainty of the
fitted coefficients, and it runs from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile of the simulated
batches. For an upper limit the 97.5th percentile must not exceed the limit.
For XMuLV and MVM the NOR-propagated analysis compares the worst case within
the NOR. At each setting the other parameters are set to every combination of their
NOR low, set-point and NOR high, 27 combinations in all, and the lowest mean pre-
dicted log reduction factor must meet the claim. The claim must hold at the worst
case, because viral clearance is validated under worst-case conditions.
The NOR-propagated range is the one that supports a claim, and the NORs in the
control strategy are set from it. The set-point range is reported beside it. A range is
reported only where the acceptable settings form one contiguous interval that con-
tains the set-point. Where they do not, the discontinuity is reported as it stands and
no range is claimed for that pair. A range that reaches the edge of the range studied
is reported as reaching it, and no claim is made beyond it. Where an output does not
meet its criterion even at the set-point, the paired report PCR-008 reports that no
proven acceptable range exists for that attribute and states the consequence for the
NOR (PCMP-001 §7.6).
A parameter that acts on several attributes has a proven acceptable range for each,

reported in the natural units of the parameter, with the criterion it was judged against.
For each attribute the report also carries a plot of the response against its governing
parameter, with the acceptable region shaded green and the set-point and the NOR
marked. The governing parameter is the multivariate parameter with the largest
main effect on that attribute in the response-surface model.

12 Risks and assumptions

The scale-down model may not represent the manufacturing column. The offset rule
of Section 5.1 addresses this risk for pool host cell protein, and every claim made
from the model is bounded by that qualification. Fragments and viral clearance carry
no offset, and their judgement rests on the assumptions stated in Section 5.1. Design
spaces are not confirmed at scale at the edges of the ranges, and confirmation at
manufacturing scale is a Stage 2 activity.
The load material may not represent the routine cation exchange pool. An extended
hold of that pool after neutralization raises its acidic charge variants. That changes
the charge distribution of the species this step must bind, and so it can change what
the step clears. Load material is qualified before execution against the criteria of
Section 7, and a design run on material that is not representative is run again.
The virus spike may perturb the load. The spike volume is bounded, and the spiked
load is compared with the unspiked load (Section 5.1).
Material supply may limit execution. The designs need a large volume of cation ex-
change pool, and pooling several batches to obtain it would blur the load attributes
that the study holds constant. Load lots are therefore scheduled against the runs
before execution begins.
The study assumes that platform knowledge from X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab applies
to A-Mab at this step, because the separation is by charge and the platform buffer
system is unchanged. It assumes that the resin performs within its qualified life cycle
over the execution period, under the resin procedure SOP-2008. It also assumes that
the parameters not studied are held to platform specification. An excursion in any
of them is recorded as a deviation and assessed for impact in the paired report PCR-
008.
