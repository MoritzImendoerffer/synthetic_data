1 Purpose and scope

The A-Mab drug substance process is being transferred from the development site in
Cambridge, MA, to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI, under the Pro-
cess Transfer Plan PTP-001. Stage 1 process characterization supports that transfer
by establishing which process parameters affect product quality and by defining the
ranges within which the commercial process may be operated (U.S. Food and Drug
Administration 2011). This plan describes the characterization study for Step 9 of
the drug substance train, the small virus retentive filtration step. The commercial
process runs at a production bioreactor scale of 15,000 L.
The step is the dedicated small virus removal operation of the purification train and
the principal source of clearance of minute virus of mice (MVM). Its clearance is or-
thogonal to the two other viral safety steps, low pH inactivation (Step 6) and anion
exchange chromatography (Step 8), because it removes virus by size rather than by
inactivation or by binding (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a). The study
covers the 2 process parameters of the step. They are filtration volume (load), the
volume of load filtered per square metre of membrane, and filtration pressure. The re-
sponses of the designs are the log reduction factors (LRF) of MVM and of xenotropic
murine leukemia virus (XMuLV), fragments and step yield. The study will be exe-
cuted on a qualified scale-down model, on load material representative of the anion
exchange pool that feeds the step at commercial scale.
Three things are outside the scope of this plan. Qualification of the membrane itself
is held by the filter manufacturer and is not repeated here. The cumulative viral
clearance claim for the drug substance is consolidated across all three viral safety
steps in PCMR-001, and this study establishes only the contribution of Step 9. Process
validation at commercial scale is covered by the validation programme and not by this
study.
Version 2.0 of this plan is an amendment issued after the studies of this step had
run (Table 11.1). It restates the protocol of Version 1.0 with the changes listed in
Section 1.1, and its criteria govern the analysis that the report PCR-009 Version 2.0
presents.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

The step removes virus by size. The membrane carries a pore size distribution chosen
to retain the smallest viruses of concern, the parvoviruses, while the antibody passes
through. Retention is a sieving mechanism, so it depends on the integrity of the pore
structure and on how the effective pore size of the membrane changes as it fouls over
the course of a batch.
MVM sits close to the pore size cut-off, and its retention depends on the fraction
of pores at the large end of the distribution and on the flow through them. As the
membrane fouls with product and with residual impurity, flux redistributes toward
the least fouled and largest pores, and small virus can pass. MVM breakthrough
therefore rises with the volume filtered per unit area, and the highest load of a batch
is the point at which retention is weakest. A low transmembrane pressure or an
interruption of flow can also allow virus held within the pore structure to diffuse
through, so pressure is controlled as well as volume.
XMuLV is many times larger than the pores. It is retained completely while the mem-
brane is intact, and its clearance is insensitive to the operating parameters over any
range that can be run. Membrane integrity is confirmed after each batch by the post-
use integrity test under the operation procedure SOP-2012, and a membrane that
passes that test has no defect large enough for the test to detect.
The antibody is small enough to pass the pores freely, so step yield is limited only by
product held up in the fouling layer on the membrane and in the system. The fouling
layer, and the product held in it, grow with the volume filtered. Yield is therefore
expected to be high and to fall slowly as the volumetric load rises. Fragments, the
clipped species of the antibody, are smaller than the intact antibody and pass the
pores in the same way. The step is therefore not expected to change the fragment
level of its load.
The step is a platform operation. It has been used to manufacture three previously
licensed humanized IgG1 antibodies, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and to supply A-Mab
clinical material, with the same filter type and the same mode of operation (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). The mechanism of removal is size exclusion and does
not depend on the identity of the antibody, so the platform experience justifies the
ranges chosen in Section 6.1 and supports the expectation that MVM retention falls
with volumetric load while XMuLV retention does not. The material presented to the
filter is the anion exchange flow-through pool of the A-Mab process, and how that pool
fouls the membrane is specific to the product, so the maximum load and the pressure
range will be established on A-Mab material.

Both viral safety attributes are of very high criticality, and the risk they carry is pa-

Step yield is a process performance response. No drug substance attribute maps to
it, so it will be measured and reported but judged against no quality criterion.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The risk assessment RA-001 ranked the parameters of every unit operation twice,
once for the potential impact of the parameter on quality attributes and once for its
potential to interact with other parameters (International Council for Harmonisation
2023b). Parameters scoring high on either ranking were assigned to multivariate
study, parameters scoring lower were assigned to univariate study, and parameters
supported by platform knowledge alone were assigned to neither.




                                                 13
For this step the assessment placed 2 parameters in the multivariate group and 0
in the univariate group. The filtration volume (load) was ranked high because MVM
retention falls as the volume filtered per unit area rises, once fouling has carried the
flux onto the largest pores. The filtration pressure was ranked high because a loss of
pressure or a stop in flow lets MVM already held in the pore structure diffuse forward
into the filtrate, and because a rise in pressure drives more filtrate through the pores
that remain open. The filtration volume (load) decides how much of the membrane is
still open and pressure decides how fast the filtrate crosses what is left, so the fall in
MVM retention with load is expected to be steeper at the top of the pressure range
than at the bottom. The designs must be able to estimate that interaction.
No parameter of this step was left out of the study on the basis of prior knowledge
alone. The risk assessment recorded that the platform claim for this step is a viral
safety claim, and that a claim of that kind should be supported by product specific
data even where the mechanism is well understood.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The study will be run on a laboratory scale filtration system fitted with the same mem-
brane type and the same membrane chemistry as the commercial step. The model is
scaled on membrane area, so that the volumetric load in L/m2, the transmembrane
pressure and the flux per unit area are the same as at commercial scale. Load mate-
rial will be matched because the product and the residual impurity the pool carries
are what foul the membrane, and a cleaner pool fouls it more slowly. Filtration tem-
perature will be matched because a higher temperature lowers the viscosity of the
pool and raises the flux at a given pressure. The hold-up volume per unit area will
be matched because it fixes how much product is left wetting the membrane and the
tubing at the end of a run, which is what limits the step yield.
Qualification will follow the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001, in study S-VF-
SDM. Triplicate runs of the scale-down system will be operated at the set-points in
Table 6.1 on representative anion exchange pool, and the flux decay profile, the step
yield and the post-use integrity test result will be compared with the corresponding
manufacturing scale records. The manufacturing-scale data set is the records of 5
batches.
The model is qualified by the offset rule of the master plan PCMP-001 (§7.1). The offset
of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean of the
manufacturing-scale batches, reported with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval,
each end of which is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound. The model is qualified for
an output when the conclusion against its criterion over the NOR is the same with
the offset at either end of that interval. An output with no criterion at the step has
its offset recorded only. Step yield and flux decay have no criterion at this step, so
their offsets are recorded and no conclusion depends on them. The qualification runs
are made, and the offsets estimated, before the first screening run. The decision
whether the model is qualified for an output is made at analysis, because it needs the
response-surface model of the step.
The two LRF and fragments are not compared at both scales. Their offset is unknown,
not zero, and they are judged as the scale-down model predicts them (PCMP-001 §7.1).
Virus cannot be spiked into a manufacturing batch, so every clearance figure claimed
for this step comes from the scale-down model (International Council for Harmoni-
sation 2023a). Under PCMP-001 §7.1 the representativeness of the log reductions
rests on the spiking model described above, and that of the fragment result on the
mechanism of the step (Section 4.1).

5.2 Operation

The load is the flow-through pool of the anion exchange step. Each run will be op-
erated at constant transmembrane pressure to the volumetric load set by the design
point, and the filtrate will be collected in fractions across the load.
Before each run the filter will be wetted and its integrity tested, and after each run
the post-use integrity test will be performed, both under SOP-2012. A run whose
post-use integrity test fails will be excluded from the analysis and recorded as a devi-
ation, because a failed integrity test means the membrane was not intact and the log
reduction measured across it says nothing about the step.
Each design point will be executed once, with both model viruses spiked into the load
before filtration. Step yield will be determined from the same run by protein mass
balance, from the ultraviolet absorbance of the load and of the pooled filtrate. Runs
will be executed in randomized order within each design.
Filtration will be continuous. The claim made from this study applies to uninterrupted
operation, because an interruption of flow allows virus held in the pore structure to
diffuse through it. An interruption at commercial scale is a deviation and lies outside
the operating region this study will define.

5.4 Sampling plan

Samples will be taken from the load before spiking, from the spiked load, and from
the filtrate. The filtrate will be sampled as sequential fractions across the volumetric
load and as a pool at the end of the run. Fractionating the filtrate allows the log
reduction to be resolved against the volume filtered within a single run, which is the
variable that degrades MVM retention as the membrane fouls.
Each spiked load sample and each filtrate sample will be assayed for both model
viruses. The unspiked load and the unspiked filtrate pool will be assayed for protein
concentration for the yield calculation. Samples may be held frozen before assay
where the method validation supports it.
Every sample will be identified by run number and by the volumetric load at which it
was taken, and the identity will be recorded at the time of sampling.

5.5 Statistical methods

Both factors will be coded to the interval −1 to +1 across the characterization ranges
in Table 6.1, so that the estimated effects are comparable between the two factors
and between the two designs. Effects will be reported as the change in the response
over half the characterization range.
The screening design will be analysed with a model carrying both main effects and
their interaction. The response-surface design will be analysed with the full quadratic
model in both factors (Montgomery 2017). Coefficients will be reported with their
standard errors and p-values, and effects significant at α = 0.05 will be treated as
real.
Model adequacy will be judged from three things. The lack of fit will be tested against
the pure error estimated from the centre point replicates. The residuals will be plot-
ted against the predicted value and on a normal quantile plot. The coefficient of
determination and the residual standard error will be reported for each response.
The centre point replicates also give the reproducibility of each response, which will
be reported as a coefficient of variation and used as the pure error term.
Change 4 adds the statistics that the adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 need. These
are the adjusted and the predicted R², the model F-test, a regression of the absolute
residuals on the predicted value and of the residuals on run order, and Cook’s distance
for each run (Section 7).
Fragments are judged against their criterion on the prediction interval of a single
batch, because the criterion applies to each batch. The response-surface model pre-
dicts the mean response, so an interval is needed to judge a single batch. The two
LRF are judged on the mean predicted LRF at the least favourable combination of
NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the other parameter, because viral clearance is
validated at worst-case conditions (PCMP-001 §6.4).
Every judgement against a criterion is stated at manufacturing scale (PCMP-001 §7.1).
At this step no output with a criterion has a measured offset. Its offset is unknown, not
zero, and the scale-down prediction is compared with the criterion on the assumptions
stated in Section 5.1.
Analyses will be performed under the design of experiments procedure SOP-1002.
The analysis will be run from a script under version control with a fixed random seed,
so that every number in PCR-009 can be regenerated from the raw data.

Both parameters will be studied multivariately. The characterization range for the
filtration volume (load) runs from the lower NOR edge to 140 L/m2, which lies above

MVM retention falls as the volumetric load rises and holds steady below the set-point.

and a range extended downward would consume runs where nothing changes.
The pressure range runs from 8 to 30 psi and is widened on both sides of the NOR of 10
to 20 psi. Low pressures are included because a low transmembrane pressure allows
retained virus to diffuse through the pore structure. High pressures are included
because they raise the flux and compress the fouling layer, so more filtrate passes
through the largest open pores and MVM retention is expected to fall. Both edges
are reachable by the commercial equipment, so a range that covers them supports
the deviation assessment as well as the operating limits.

6.2 Screening design

The screening study S-VF-DOE will be a two-level factorial in both factors with centre
point replicates, 7 runs in total. Of these, 4 runs are the factorial points at the corners
of the characterization ranges and 3 are replicates of the centre point.
With 2 factors the full factorial is executed, so both main effects and the interaction
are estimated free of aliasing. The centre points detect curvature but cannot attribute
it to a factor, and curvature is resolved at the response-surface stage. The interaction
model also leaves 1 degree of freedom for lack of fit, so its fit statistics overstate how
well it fits (PCMP-001 §6.2). The screening runs establish the size and direction of
the two effects, and the reproducibility of the infectivity assays from the centre point
replicates, before the response-surface runs are committed.
The planned coded settings are given in Appendix A.



                                            20
6.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study S-VF-RSM will be a face-centred central composite design
in both factors, 12 runs in total. It is made up of 4 factorial points, 4 axial points and
4 centre point replicates.
The axial points sit on the edges of the characterization ranges rather than beyond
them. A rotatable design would place them outside those edges, and settings outside
the ranges in Table 6.1 are not settings the step may be claimed for, so the face-
centred form is used. The design supports the full quadratic model, which allows the
response to curve against the volumetric load. Curvature is expected there, because
the flux redistribution that lets small virus through accelerates as the membrane fouls,
and a model that could only fit a straight line would over-predict retention at the top
of the range.
The 4 replicates of the centre point give the pure error term for the lack of fit test in
Section 5.5 and an estimate of run to run reproducibility that does not depend on the
model.
The planned coded and natural unit settings are given in Appendix B.

experience.
Where the study finds an effect that the two designs cannot separate, a univariate
confirmation run may be added. Any such run will be justified against the data that
prompted it and reported in PCR-009 with the same acceptance criteria as the de-

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

The scale-down model criterion is stated in Section 5.1.
The following rule of Version 1.0 for the fitted models is kept. A fitted response-
surface model will be accepted as predictive where the lack of fit test is not signifi-
cant at α = 0.05 against the pure error from the centre point replicates, where the
residuals show no trend against the predicted value and no material departure from
normality, and where the residual standard error is small against the distance be-
tween the predicted response and its criterion. Version 1.0 set no threshold for the
departure from normality on the normal quantile plot or for the residual standard
error against the distance to the criterion, and PCR-009 states how it judges them.
Change 4 adds the four adequacy criteria of the master plan PCMP-001 (§7.2), which
a model must also meet.

  1. A significant model. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05, the level set for
     model terms.
  2. No significant lack of fit. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α =
     0.05.
  3. The limit on overfitting. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. A
     model that fits its own runs much better than it predicts left-out runs has more
     terms than the data support.
  4. Residuals and influence. The residuals show no pattern against predicted values
     or run order, and no single run dominates the fit. The absolute residuals are
     regressed on the predicted value, and the residuals on run order, each at α =
     0.05, and a significant slope counts as a pattern. A run whose Cook’s distance
     exceeds 4/n, a conventional cut-off where n is the number of runs, dominates the
     fit.

A model that fails the lack-of-fit test or the limit on overfitting is either refitted with a
justified change of terms or transformation, or it is not used to set a range. Where no
adequate model results, PCR-009 should report the response descriptively, against
the criterion, over the runs of the design (PCMP-001 §7.2).

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A proven acceptable range is the range of a parameter over which the response meets
its criterion, given a stated treatment of the other parameters (International Council
for Harmonisation 2009). Two analyses will be run for each parameter and each of the
two viral clearance responses, and both will be reported. Change 1 adds fragments
to them.
The first holds the other parameter at its set-point and scans the parameter of interest
across its characterization range on a grid of 201 points, using the fitted response-
surface model. It answers what the parameter may do while the rest of the step is
operated at target. Its range is the contiguous interval around the set-point over
which the mean prediction meets the criterion (PCMP-001 §6.4).
The second, the NOR-propagated analysis, scans the parameter of interest across its
characterization range on a grid of 81 points, as PCMP-001 §6.4 defines it. What it
compares with the criterion depends on the output.

  • For fragments, the analysis draws 2,000 values of the other parameter at each
    grid point from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a standard
    deviation of the NOR width divided by 6, clipped to the characterization range.
    Each draw carries the model residual and the uncertainty of the fitted coeffi-
    cients. The 95 % prediction interval of a single batch runs from the 2.5th to the
    97.5th percentile of the simulated batches, and its upper end must not exceed
    the criterion.
  • For the two LRF, the other parameter is set at each grid point to its NOR low, its
    set-point and its NOR high, and the lowest of the three mean predicted LRF must
    meet the claim of the step. Viral clearance is validated at worst-case conditions,
    so a claim that held only on average over the NOR would not support a validated
    log reduction.

Where the two analyses disagree, the range from the second is the one carried into the
control strategy, because the commercial process holds each parameter somewhere
within its normal operating range and not at its set-point.
The reported range in each case is the contiguous interval that contains the set-point.
Where the predicted response leaves acceptance inside the characterization range,
the proven acceptable range is narrower than the range studied, and PCR-009 will
state the value at which the response crosses its criterion. A proven acceptable range
that reaches the edge of the characterization range is reported as reaching it, and
no claim is made beyond it. Where an output does not meet its criterion even at the




                                          25
set-point, PCR-009 reports that no proven acceptable range exists for it and states
the consequence for the NOR (PCMP-001 §7.6).
Each proven acceptable range will be reported with a plot of the response against
the parameter, with the acceptance limit drawn, the acceptable region shaded green,
and the set-point and the NOR marked. The two analyses will be shown as the two
panels of one figure. The ranges themselves will be reported in PCR-009.

12 Risks and assumptions

The clearance claim for this step rests entirely on a scale-down model, because virus
cannot be spiked at manufacturing scale. The mitigation is to re-scale the model
on membrane area and on hold-up volume and to repeat the qualification, and any
change made to the model will be recorded in PCR-009.
Spiking adds material to the load, and retention depends on how the membrane fouls.
A spike that fouls the membrane differently from unspiked process material would
bias the measured log reduction, and the bias would be largest at the top of the
volumetric range, which is the part of the range the claim depends on. The virus
stock will therefore be clarified before addition and its volume kept small against the
load volume, and the flux decay of the spiked runs will be compared with that of the
unspiked qualification runs.
The log reduction that can be measured is bounded by the titre of the spiked load
and by the limit of detection of the infectivity assay. Where no virus is detected in the
filtrate, the result is a lower bound, and a lower bound that falls short of the criterion
in Section 7 cannot be resolved by repeating the assay. The load will therefore be
spiked to a titre that allows the criterion to be demonstrated with margin, and the
achieved titre will be reported with each run.
The study assumes that the anion exchange pool used in the runs represents commer-
cial material. Anion exchange is the step immediately upstream and its own charac-
terization is reported in the anion exchange report PCR-008. Pool from a run that
was itself out of trend will not be used, and the load material used in each filtration
run will be identified in PCR-009.
The study assumes uninterrupted filtration, as stated in Section 5.2. The ranges it
produces do not cover an interruption of flow, and an interruption at commercial
scale will be assessed as a deviation against the mechanism in Section 4.1 rather
than against these ranges.
Material supply is the principal risk to the schedule. Each design point consumes
anion exchange pool, and the runs will be scheduled against pool availability rather
than against a fixed calendar.
