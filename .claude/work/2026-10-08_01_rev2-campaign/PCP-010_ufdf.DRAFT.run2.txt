1 Purpose and scope

This plan defines the process characterization studies for the ultrafiltration and di-
afiltration (UF/DF) step of the A-Mab drug substance process. UF/DF is Step 10 and
the last step of the drug substance train. It concentrates the antibody, exchanges the
process buffer for the formulation buffer, and delivers the drug substance at its tar-
get concentration of 75 g/L. The commercial process runs at a production bioreactor
working volume of 15,000 L. The process is being transferred from the development
site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI. The
studies of this plan form part of the Stage 1 process design record that supports the
transfer and the Biologics License Application (BLA) (U.S. Food and Drug Administra-
tion 2011; International Council for Harmonisation 2008).
The plan covers the three process parameters of the step (Section 6.1), the scale-
down model on which they are studied, the analytical methods and the sampling,
the acceptance and decision criteria, and the analysis by which a proven acceptable
range (PAR) is assigned to each parameter. It applies to the drug substance process
only. Drug product manufacture and the development of the formulation belong to
the drug product program and are out of scope. That program fixes the composition
of the formulation buffer, and the studies treat it as a constant.
The step does not change the glycan variants of A-Mab, and no clearance of host
cell protein, DNA or leached Protein A is credited to it (Section 4.2). It can add
to aggregate and to the acidic charge variants (Section 4.1). No viral clearance is
claimed for this step.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

The UF/DF step concentrates the antibody and exchanges the process buffer for the
formulation buffer. Both operations run across a tangential flow membrane whose
pores pass buffer salts and retain the antibody. Ultrafiltration removes permeate
and raises the antibody concentration in the retentate. Diafiltration then adds for-
mulation buffer at the rate at which permeate leaves, so the retentate volume stays
constant while the permeable species are washed out. Each diavolume removes a
fixed fraction of the permeable species that remain. The residual process buffer com-
ponents therefore fall exponentially with the number of diavolumes, and the number
is set so that the retentate reaches the composition of the formulation buffer (Sec-
tion 7.2). The step ends with a second concentration to the DS target of 75 g/L and a
buffer flush that recovers the retentate.
The step can add to aggregate and to the acidic charge variants. The first route is
mechanical. The membrane retains the antibody, so the antibody accumulates at the
membrane wall in a polarization layer, where its concentration is higher than in the
bulk retentate. The whole retentate also passes many times through the recircula-
tion pump. Aggregate can form in both places, because there the antibody is most
concentrated and most sheared.
The A-Mab case study leaves the UF/DF step out (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009,
86), so it gives no expectation for this step. The expectations in the rest of this section
come from general knowledge of the tangential flow filtration of antibodies. On that
basis the risk of aggregate formation is expected to rise with the final concentration,
with the TMP and with the number of passes through the pump. The TMP drives
the permeate flux. Beyond the point at which the polarization layer limits the flux,
a higher TMP compresses that layer without raising throughput, and it raises the
antibody concentration at the wall. The shear at the wall is set by the crossflow and
by the geometry of the feed channel. The final concentration sets the viscosity of the
retentate and the concentration in the polarization layer. A higher viscosity raises
the pressure drop along the feed channel and the shear stress near the end of the
concentration. Within the normal operating ranges (NORs) the antibody spends little
time at the highest wall concentration and passes through the pump a limited number
of times. The effect on aggregate is therefore expected to be small.
The second route is chemical. The step keeps the antibody in solution for the length
of the operation, and deamidation of asparagine residues continues during that time.
Deamidation raises the acidic charge variants. The increase grows with the time in
solution, at a rate that rises with pH and temperature. The number of diavolumes




                                            11
and the TMP set how long the operation lasts, the first through the volume of buffer
exchanged and the second through the flux. For this reason the final retentate of
every run is assayed for charge variants as well as for aggregate.
The step is part of the platform train that was used for X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab
(PCMP-001 §2). Where and why aggregate forms in the step does not depend on
the antibody, so that experience applies to the qualitative behaviour of the step. How
readily an antibody aggregates does depend on the antibody, and so does the viscosity
of its retentate, which rises steeply as the concentration approaches the DS target.
For this reason the final DS concentration is studied for A-Mab and is not carried over
from the platform.

Aggregate is the attribute most at risk at this step, from pump shear and from the
high antibody concentration at the membrane wall. The acidic charge variants are
measured because deamidation continues while the antibody is in solution on the
skid. Both attributes arrive with the load. They arise in the production bioreactor,
and aggregate is reduced at the cation exchange step. The studies measure what
this step adds to each, against the load from the virus filtration step.
The other attributes of the register are not measured across the step. Afucosylation,
galactosylation and high mannose arise in the production bioreactor, and no down-
stream step changes them (PCMP-001 §3). The membrane retains every glycoform of
the antibody, so the step cannot change their distribution. Host cell protein, residual
DNA and leached Protein A are cleared by the chromatography steps before this one.
PCMP-001 Table 7.1 credits this step with no clearance of host cell protein or leached
Protein A, and residual DNA is judged on the drug substance only (PCMP-001 §7.3).
Fragments are not measured either. They form by proteolytic clipping of the antibody,
in culture and during the harvest hold (PCMP-001 §3), and by non-enzymatic hydroly-
sis of the hinge region, which proceeds slowly with time in solution. The step adds no



                                                   12
protease, and pump shear and the concentration at the wall aggregate the antibody
without cleaving it. Hinge hydrolysis continues on the skid, but over the duration of
the operation it is expected to add little. Version 1.0 left fragments out of the studies
of this step, and this amendment keeps that scope. PCMP-001 assumes that this step
does not change fragments (PCMP-001 Table 7.1), and the drug substance is tested
for fragments at release.
Viral clearance is credited to the low-pH viral inactivation, anion exchange and virus
filtration steps, and the cumulative claim is consolidated in PCMR-001 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2023a). It is not measured at this step.



4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

RA-001 ranked every process parameter of the drug substance process for its poten-
tial impact on the CQAs and on process performance, and for its potential to interact
with other parameters (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Parameters
ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to multivariate study. Pa-
rameters ranked lower, whose range could be supported one factor at a time, were
assigned to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). All three parameters of this step were
assigned to univariate study.
The three parameters act on the product by different routes. More diavolumes wash
more of the permeable process buffer components out of the retentate, and they add
time on the skid and passes through the pump. A higher TMP raises the permeate
flux until the polarization layer limits it. Beyond that point it raises the antibody
concentration at the membrane wall without raising throughput. A higher final DS
concentration raises the bulk concentration of the retentate, and with it the viscosity,
the pressure drop along the feed channel and the concentration at the membrane
wall.
The TMP and the final DS concentration both raise the antibody concentration at the
membrane wall. The TMP does so by driving more permeate through the polarization
layer, and the final DS concentration by raising the bulk concentration that feeds the
layer. An interaction between them is therefore possible. A study that varies one
factor at a time cannot resolve such an interaction. What the design does bound is the
effect of each parameter at the edge of its range studied, with the other parameters
at the values the commercial step will hold. Version 1.0 set a rule for this case, and
this plan keeps it. Where a measured attribute approaches its acceptance criterion
at an edge, the study should be extended to the combination of edges before a range
is claimed, and the extension should be agreed with Statistics and QA before it is
executed. Version 1.0 set no threshold for an attribute that approaches its criterion,
and PCR-010 states how the rule was applied.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies are run on the bench-scale skid EQ-TFF-142, registered as the ultrafiltra-
tion / diafiltration TFF skid and held under calibration and change control. Section 11
gives its calibration status for each study. The model holds the crossflow and the mem-
brane loading at their commercial values, both scaled by membrane area (PCMP-001
§5). The crossflow per unit membrane area sets the shear at the membrane wall, and
it sweeps retained antibody back into the bulk, so it also sets the antibody concen-
tration in the polarization layer. The membrane loading, in grams of antibody per
square metre, sets the volume that each square metre of membrane processes. At
the same TMP and crossflow the flux is the same, so a run of the model lasts as long
as the commercial step at the same settings. The membrane chemistry, the nominal
molecular weight cut-off and the feed channel geometry are those of the commercial
skid. The TMP, the number of diavolumes and the final concentration do not depend
on scale and are set at the values under study.
One quantity cannot be matched. The ratio of pump displacement to system hold-up
volume is larger at bench scale, so the antibody passes through the pump more often
per gram processed than at commercial scale. The bench model is therefore expected
to be conservative for aggregate formation by shear.
The qualification runs are made under SOP-1001, in study S-UFDF-SDM, where three
runs at the set-point condition are compared with the pilot and commercial record of
the step on the step yield, the permeate flux at the set-point TMP, the final concentra-
tion achieved and the change in aggregate content across the step. PCMP-001 §5 lists
the same outputs for this step. Version 1.0 did not state which batches of that record
form the reference, and PCR-010 states which do. Under PCMP-001 §7.1 the offset
of each output is the mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean of the
manufacturing-scale batches, reported with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval.
Each end of that interval is a one-sided 95 % confidence bound. PCMP-001 requires
the qualification runs, and the estimate of each offset, before the first characteriza-
tion run of a step.
Table 5.1 shows how each output of the qualification is used. Of the outputs that the
qualification compares, only aggregate carries a criterion at this step, the drug sub-
stance criterion for aggregate in the final retentate (Section 7.2). Every judgement

offset, as PCMP-001 §7.1 requires for an output with a criterion, so the conclusion is
stated at manufacturing scale. PCR-010 states how the offset of the change across the
step applies to the level in the final retentate. The step yield, the permeate flux and

The acidic charge variants and the buffer exchange also carry criteria at this step,
but the qualification does not compare them at both scales. Their offsets are there-

of the commercial step (SOP-2013) in the commercial formulation buffer, and a run
lasts as long as the commercial step at the same settings. For the buffer exchange it
rests on the passage of the buffer species through the membrane, which depends on
the number of diavolumes and on the membrane and not on scale.



5.2 Operation

The step receives the filtrate pool of the small virus retentive filtration step. The pool
is concentrated by ultrafiltration, diafiltered at constant volume against formulation
buffer for the planned number of diavolumes, concentrated a second time to the final
target, and recovered with a buffer flush. Operation follows SOP-2013. At the set-
point condition the step runs 8 DV at a TMP of 12 psi to a final concentration of 75
g/L.
All runs of the study are loaded from a single pool of the virus filtration step, so
that the variability of the load does not confound the comparison between the levels
of a parameter. Section 12 gives the bridging rule that applies if one pool is not
sufficient.

5.3 Analytical methods

The validated methods are listed in Table 3.2. Aggregate is measured by size exclu-
sion chromatography (SEC) under AMV-3011 and reported as the percentage of high
molecular weight species (HMW). Protein concentration is measured by ultraviolet
absorbance (A280) under AMV-3019. The mass balance and the final concentration
rest on that measurement. Charge variants are measured by imaged capillary iso-
electric focusing (icIEF) under AMV-3013 and reported as the percentage of acidic
species. The completeness of the buffer exchange is followed by the conductivity
and the pH of the retentate. These are in-process measurements read from the skid
instrumentation and are not validated assays, so they are used to characterize the
exchange curve and not to release a run.
This step has no designed experiment. Its univariate studies measure aggregate by
SEC, which PCMP-001 §7.8 keeps as the method for aggregate, and they do not mea-
sure fragments, which Version 1.0 left out of their scope (Section 4.2).
Aggregate in the load of this step is expected to be low. Aggregate binds the cation
exchange resin more strongly than the monomer, so it elutes late and is largely ex-
cluded from the pool at the cation exchange step. A change across this step may
therefore be of the same size as the repeatability of the SEC method. Version 1.0
set the following rule for this case, and it is unchanged. Analytical Development will
report the method variability that applies over the range in which the samples fall. A
measured change is judged against the pure error estimate described in Section 5.5
rather than against the nominal precision of the method.

5.5 Statistical methods

For each parameter and each response, the measured values will be regressed on the
level of the parameter over the range studied, and the slope will be tested against
zero at α = 0.05. The set-point condition is shared by the three series and will be run
three times. Those replicates give the pure error estimate of the combined process
and assay variability, against which a change across a range is read. PCMP-001 §6.2
sets the same level for the significance of a model term, and under it an attribute is
called affected by a parameter when the slope is significant at that level.
Statistical significance and practical significance will be reported separately. A slope
that is significant at α = 0.05 but predicts a change across the range that is small
against the acceptance criterion will be described in those terms, and the criterion
rather than the p-value decides whether the range is acceptable. No response-surface
model will be fitted for this step, because the design carries no factorial structure and
no interaction term can be estimated from it. The three levels of each series, with
the set-point replicated, do allow a curvature to be detected for that parameter on
its own. The model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 apply to response-surface
models and therefore do not apply here.
The buffer exchange will be analysed separately. The conductivity of the retentate
will be plotted against the number of diavolumes, and the exponential decay expected
from Section 4.1 will be fitted to the series. The analysis reports the number of diavol-
umes at which the conductivity of the retentate has reached the plateau set by the
formulation buffer and no further change is measured over the following diavolume.

6.1 Factors, ranges and study type

The parameters, their set-points, the ranges to be studied and the NORs are given

them high and judged that their ranges could be supported one factor at a time (Sec-

  Table 6.1: Parameters, ranges studied and normal operating ranges for the step.

          Parameter                  Unit Set-point Range studied NOR
          Number of diavolumes       DV           8 7–10           7–9
          Transmembrane pressure     psi         12 10–15          10–14
          Final DS concentration     g/L         75 65–85          70–80


Each range studied extends beyond the NOR at one end or at both. The number
of diavolumes is studied from 7 to 10 DV against a NOR of 7 to 9 DV. The range
extends above the NOR because more diavolumes mean more time on the skid and
more passes through the pump. Its lower edge is the lower edge of the NOR, which
is the lowest count the commercial step will run, and the buffer exchange curve char-
acterizes the step at that value. Below that edge the buffer exchange would be less
complete, and the study does not go there. The TMP is studied from 10 to 15 psi
against a NOR of 10 to 14 psi. The range extends above the NOR because a higher
TMP raises the antibody concentration at the membrane wall. Its lower edge is also
the lower edge of the NOR. A lower TMP would lengthen the operation, and with it
the passes through the pump and the time for deamidation, and the study does not
characterize that side beyond the NOR.
The final DS concentration is studied from 65 to 85 g/L against a NOR of 70 to 80
g/L, and the range extends on both sides. An excursion above the target raises the
viscosity of the retentate and the antibody concentration at the wall, and with them
the risk of aggregation. An excursion below the target leaves the drug substance
below the concentration that the drug product program expects.
The step will not be characterized outside these ranges, and no range wider than the
range studied will be proposed in PCR-010.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

PCMP-001 §7 sets the framework of acceptance criteria that every step plan applies.
This section states the criteria of that framework for the outputs of this step, and the
rules of Version 1.0 by which the studies are decided.



7.1 Scale-down model

Version 1.0 accepted the model under a rule in three parts. The mean step yield,
the permeate flux and the change in aggregate content across the step were not to
differ from the pilot and commercial record at α = 0.05. The final concentration
achieved was to lie within the reported variability of the A280 method. A difference
that was statistically significant was to be assessed for its size against the acceptance
criteria of Section 7.2 before the model was rejected, and that judgement was to be
documented in the qualification record and approved by Statistics and QA.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 replaces such tests with the offset for every scale-down model
(PCMP-001 §7.1). The offset is subtracted from each aggregate result and recorded
for the other outputs that the qualification compares (Section 5.1). Under PCMP-001
a model is qualified for an output when the conclusion over the NOR is the same with
the offset at either end of its confidence interval, and that conclusion is drawn from
the response-surface model of the step. This step has no response-surface model, so
the decision cannot be made as PCMP-001 states it, and this plan has no rule for it.
PCR-010 states the approach it takes.



7.2 Product quality

The final retentate is judged against the drug substance criteria of Table 4.1. Aggre-
gate must not exceed 5 % HMW, and the acidic charge variants must lie between
18 and 40 %. PCMP-001 §7.3 carries each drug substance criterion back through the
platform worst cases of the downstream steps to give the IACs of the steps before this
one. No step follows this one, so there is nothing to carry a criterion back through,
and PCMP-001 Table 7.1 defines no IAC for this step. The drug substance criteria
therefore apply directly (PCMP-001 §7.6). They come from the drug substance CQA
register (PCMP-001 §3), and no result of the studies of this plan enters them.
Each run is judged on its own measured result, which is a single batch, as PCMP-
001 §6.2 requires for a quality attribute. The offset of the acidic charge variants is




                                           20
unknown, because the qualification does not compare them, and each result is judged
as measured (PCMP-001 §7.1).
The derivation in PCMP-001 assumes that no step after cation exchange changes
aggregate, and that no step after low-pH viral inactivation changes the acidic charge
variants (PCMP-001 Table 7.1). This step can add to both, and the studies of this plan
measure what it adds. The verification of platform worst cases in PCMP-001 §7.5 uses
the response-surface model of a step, and no verification is planned at this step.
Version 1.0 set the criterion for the buffer exchange, and it is unchanged. The buffer
exchange is accepted when the conductivity and the pH of the retentate have reached
the plateau of the formulation buffer, by the analysis of Section 5.5, at or below the
lower edge of the diavolume NOR of 7 DV. Version 1.0 set no numerical tolerance for
the plateau, and PCR-010 states the one it uses.



7.3 Decision rules

Version 1.0 set the following decision rules, and they are unchanged.

  1. A level of a parameter is acceptable when every attribute measured on the final
     retentate of that run meets its criterion.
  2. A parameter passes over its range studied when every level in the range is accept-
     able and the change measured across the range is small against the criterion.
  3. Where a level fails, the range is truncated at the last acceptable level, and the
     failing level is reported as an edge of failure.
  4. Where a failure occurs at a level inside the NOR, execution stops, and the op-
     erating range is reviewed with the receiving site and with QA before the study
     continues.
  5. Where a run fails for a reason attributable to the equipment or to the method,
     and not to the parameter under study, the run may be repeated once. Both the
     original and the repeat are reported in PCR-010.

Version 1.0 set no threshold for a change that is small against the criterion, and PCMP-
001 sets none. PCR-010 states how it judges such a change.



7.4 Classification

Each parameter will be classified under SOP-4001 (International Council for Harmon-
isation 2009, 2012). Version 1.0 set the following rule, and this plan keeps it. A
parameter whose variation over the characterized range affects no quality attribute,
but whose control is needed for the step to perform consistently, is classified as a
key process parameter. A parameter whose variation affects a quality attribute is
classified as critical, or as well controlled critical where the risk of falling outside the
design space is low. The classification is an outcome of the studies and is reported in
PCR-010.




                                            21
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 adds the following to that rule (PCMP-001 §6.2, §7.7). Whether
a parameter affects an attribute is decided by the slope test of Section 5.5. A signifi-
cant effect that is too small to move the attribute appreciably within the PAR does not
by itself make a parameter critical. A parameter is well-controlled critical when its
risk of falling outside its acceptable range is low, because it is readily controlled and
its PAR is wide against its NOR. A parameter studied one factor at a time is classified
by the same rules as a parameter studied in a design (PCMP-001 §4). PCMP-001 and
SOP-4001 set no numerical threshold for a small effect or a wide PAR. PCR-010 will
therefore state the size of each effect across the PAR and the margin of the PAR to
the NOR, and classify on them.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A PAR will be assigned to each parameter from its univariate series. The acceptance
basis is the drug substance criteria of Table 4.1, because PCMP-001 defines no IAC for
this step. The PAR of a parameter is the contiguous span of levels over which every
attribute measured on the final retentate met its criterion, with the other parameters
at their set-points. Each PAR will be reported in PCR-010 together with the measured
series and the criterion against which it was read.
Three bounds apply to a PAR determined this way, and PCR-010 will state them where
it states the ranges.

  1. A univariate PAR holds with the other parameters at their set-points. It does not
     describe the step when two parameters sit at their range edges at the same time.
  2. PCMP-001 §6.4 defines a second analysis, the NOR-propagated analysis. It pre-
     dicts the response from a fitted multivariate model while each other parameter
     is drawn at random from a normal distribution around its set-point, with a stan-
     dard deviation of the width of its NOR divided by 6, clipped to the range studied.
     This step has no such model, so the NOR-propagated analysis is not performed.
     PCMP-001 §7.6 defines a PAR through that analysis, and for a parameter studied
     one factor at a time the range rests on its own data instead (PCMP-001 §4).
  3. A PAR cannot be wider than the range studied. Where every level in a range is
     acceptable, the claimed PAR is the range studied of Table 6.1 and nothing beyond
     it, and it is reported as reaching the edge of that range (PCMP-001 §7.6).

The ranges reported under this analysis become the basis of the operating ranges that
the receiving site runs to, together with the classification of Section 7.4. Where a PAR
is wider than the NOR, the margin between them is what a deviation is dispositioned
against. Where a PAR is truncated at an edge of failure, the NOR should be reviewed
against the truncated range before the step is transferred.

9 Data management and integrity

Data generated under this plan are recorded so that they are attributable, legible,
contemporaneous, original and accurate, and so that they remain complete, consis-
tent, enduring and available (ALCOA+). Run records are captured in the electronic
notebook under the study number. Analytical results are captured in the laboratory
information management system and reported against the sample identifiers of Ta-
ble 5.2. The study record also gives the execution window of each study and the cali-
bration status of the equipment it used. INV-001 preventive action PA3 requires the
execution dates and the calibration status of the equipment in every study record.
The statistical analysis of Section 5.5 is scripted and held under version control, so
that it can be re-executed on the retained raw data and return the same result. Every
calculation is reviewed by a second qualified person. A change to a record after it
has been made carries an audit trail with the reason for the change. Values quoted in
PCR-010 are read from the retained data set and are not re-entered by hand. Records
are retained under the record retention procedure of the sponsor for the life of the
product.

12 Risks and assumptions

The scale-down model gives more passes through the pump per gram of antibody than
the commercial skid (Section 5.1). The model is therefore expected to be conserva-
tive for aggregate formation, and a result that meets the criterion at bench scale is
expected to hold at commercial scale. Version 1.0 set the following rule for a failure
at bench scale, and it is unchanged. If aggregate fails its criterion at bench scale, the
condition will be repeated at pilot scale before an operating range is narrowed on the
strength of the bench result.
The study depends on a single pool of the virus filtration step for its load. If one pool
is not sufficient for the whole study, a second pool will be bridged by running the set-
point condition on both pools and comparing the responses against the pure error
estimate of Section 5.5. A difference larger than the pure error will be investigated
before the second pool is used, and the runs executed on each pool will be identified
in PCR-010.
The change in aggregate across the step may be small against the variability of the
SEC method. Version 1.0 set the following rule for that case, and it is unchanged.
Noise near the low end of the method range will be attributed to the assay rather
than to the parameter under study, unless the change measured across the range is
larger than the pure error estimate of Section 5.5. Version 1.0 did not say how this
rule relates to the slope test of Section 5.5, and PCR-010 states how it applies the two
together.
The design has one run at each edge of a range. The slope test of each series therefore
rests on few runs, and an effect that is small against the variability between runs may
not reach significance.
The plan rests on the following assumptions.

  1. The composition of the formulation buffer is fixed by the drug product program.
     A change to the formulation would change the target of the buffer exchange and
     would invalidate the diavolume study.
  2. The membrane chemistry and the membrane supplier are as qualified, and a
     change to either requires a new risk assessment before these data are applied.
  3. The material entering the step arrives within the criteria that PCMP-001 sets for
     the output of the virus filtration step (PCMP-001 Table 7.1). The study character-
     izes the step under representative load, and it does not challenge the step with
     material that is already outside those criteria.
