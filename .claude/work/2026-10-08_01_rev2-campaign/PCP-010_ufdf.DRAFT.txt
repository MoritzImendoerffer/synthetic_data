1 Purpose and scope

This plan defines the process characterization studies for the ultrafiltration and di-
afiltration (UF/DF) step of the A-Mab drug substance process. UF/DF is Step 10 and
the last step of the drug substance train. It concentrates the antibody, exchanges the
process buffer for the formulation buffer, and delivers the drug substance at its tar-
get concentration of 75 g/L. The commercial process runs at a production bioreactor
working volume of 15,000 L. The process is being transferred from the development
site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI. The
studies of this plan form part of the Stage 1 process design record that supports the
transfer and the Biologics License Application (BLA) (U.S. Food and Drug Administra-
tion 2011; International Council for Harmonisation 2008).
The plan covers the three process parameters of the step (Section 6.1), the scale-
down model on which they are studied, the analytical methods and the sampling,
the acceptance and decision criteria, and the analysis by which a proven acceptable
range (PAR) is assigned to each parameter. It applies to the drug substance process
only. Drug product manufacture and the development of the formulation belong to
the drug product program and are out of scope. That program fixes the composition
of the formulation buffer, and the studies treat it as a constant.
The step forms no quality attribute of A-Mab, and no clearance of any attribute is
credited to it (Section 4.2). What the step can add to aggregate and to the acidic
charge variants is described in Section 4.1, and the studies measure that increment
against the material that Step 9 delivers. No viral clearance is claimed for this step.
Clearance is credited to the low-pH viral inactivation, anion exchange and virus fil-
tration steps, and the cumulative claim is consolidated in PCMR-001 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2023a).

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

The UF/DF step concentrates the antibody and exchanges the process buffer for the
formulation buffer. Both operations run across a tangential flow membrane whose
pores pass buffer salts and retain the antibody. Ultrafiltration removes permeate
and raises the antibody concentration in the retentate. Diafiltration then adds for-
mulation buffer at the rate at which permeate leaves, so the retentate volume stays
constant while the permeable species are washed out. Each diavolume removes a
fixed fraction of the permeable species that remain. The residual process buffer com-
ponents therefore fall exponentially with the number of diavolumes, and the number
is set to bring them below what the formulation tolerates. The step ends with a sec-
ond concentration to the DS target of 75 g/L and a buffer flush that recovers the
retentate.
The step can add to aggregate and to the acidic charge variants. The first route is
mechanical. The membrane retains the antibody, so the antibody accumulates at the
membrane wall in a polarization layer, where its concentration is higher than in the
bulk retentate. The whole retentate also passes many times through the recircula-
tion pump. Aggregate can form in both places, because there the antibody is most
concentrated and most sheared.
The A-Mab case study leaves the UF/DF step out (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009,
86), so it gives no expectation for this step. The expectations in the rest of this sec-
tion come from general knowledge of the tangential flow filtration of antibodies. On
that basis the risk of aggregate formation is expected to rise with the final concen-
tration, with the TMP and with the number of passes through the pump. The TMP
drives the permeate flux. Beyond the point at which the polarization layer limits the
flux, a higher TMP compresses that layer without raising throughput, and it raises the
concentration and the shear that the antibody meets at the wall. The final concen-
tration sets the viscosity of the retentate and the concentration in the polarization
layer. A higher viscosity raises the pressure drop along the feed channel and the
shear near the end of the concentration. Within the normal operating ranges (NORs)
the exposure is brief, and the effect on aggregate is expected to be small.
The second route is chemical. The step keeps the antibody in solution for the length
of the operation, and deamidation of asparagine residues continues during that time.
Deamidation raises the acidic charge variants. The increase grows with the time in
solution, at a rate that rises with pH and temperature. The number of diavolumes
sets how long the operation lasts. For this reason the final retentate of every run is
assayed for charge variants as well as for aggregate.

The step is part of the platform train that was used for X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab
(PCMP-001 §2). Where and why aggregate forms in the step does not depend on
the antibody, so that experience applies to the qualitative behaviour of the step. How
readily an antibody aggregates does depend on the antibody, and so does the viscosity
of its retentate, which rises steeply as the concentration approaches the DS target.
For this reason the final DS concentration is studied for A-Mab and is not carried over
from the platform.

Aggregate is the attribute most at risk at this step, by the mechanical route of Sec-
tion 4.1. It carries a criticality of H, and its criterion is ≤ 5 % HMW (SEC). The acidic
charge variants carry a criticality of VL and a criterion of 18–40 %. They are mea-

and aggregate is reduced at the cation exchange step. The studies measure what this
step adds to each, against what Step 9 delivers.
The other attributes of the register are not measured across the step. Afucosylation,
galactosylation and high mannose are formed in the production bioreactor, and no

and the glycan variants of the antibody are the same size, so the step cannot change
their distribution. Host cell protein, residual DNA and leached Protein A are cleared
by the chromatography steps before this one. PCMP-001 credits this step with no
clearance of host cell protein or leached Protein A (Table 7.1), and residual DNA is
judged on the drug substance only (PCMP-001 §7.3).
Fragments are not measured either. They are formed by proteolytic clipping of the
antibody, in culture and during the harvest hold (PCMP-001 §3). This step adds no
protease, and its mechanical route acts on aggregate. The derivation of PCMP-001

4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

RA-001 ranked every process parameter of the drug substance process for its poten-
tial impact on the CQAs and on process performance, and for its potential to interact
with other parameters (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Parameters
ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to multivariate study. Pa-
rameters ranked lower, whose range could be supported one factor at a time, were
assigned to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). All three parameters of this step were as-
signed to univariate study. The step sets no CQA of its own, and its three parameters
act on the product by the different routes described below.
More diavolumes wash more of the permeable process buffer components out of the
retentate. They also add time on the skid and passes through the pump. A higher
TMP raises the permeate flux until the polarization layer limits it. Beyond that point
it raises the antibody concentration at the membrane wall without raising throughput.
A higher final DS concentration raises the bulk concentration of the retentate, and
with it the viscosity, the pressure drop along the feed channel and the concentration
at the membrane wall.
The TMP and the final DS concentration both raise the antibody concentration at the
membrane wall. The TMP does so by driving more permeate through the polarization
layer, and the final DS concentration by raising the bulk concentration that feeds the
layer. An interaction between them is therefore possible. A study that varies one
factor at a time cannot resolve such an interaction. What the design does bound is the
effect of each parameter at the edges of its range studied, with the other parameters
at the set-points the commercial step holds. Where a measured attribute approaches
its acceptance criterion at an edge, the study should be extended to the combination
of edges before a range is claimed. The extension should be agreed with Statistics
and QA before it is executed.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies are run on the bench-scale skid EQ-TFF-142, registered as the ultrafiltra-
tion / diafiltration TFF skid and held under calibration and change control. Section 11
gives its calibration status for each study. The model holds the membrane loading
and the crossflow at their commercial values, because together they decide what the
antibody meets in the step. The membrane loading, in grams of antibody per square
metre, fixes the bulk concentration that the step reaches and the time that the an-
tibody spends on the skid. The crossflow per unit membrane area sweeps retained
antibody from the membrane wall back into the bulk, so a higher crossflow lowers the
concentration in the polarization layer. Both are scaled by membrane area (PCMP-
001 §5). The membrane chemistry, the nominal molecular weight cut-off and the
feed channel geometry are those of the commercial skid. The TMP and the number
of diavolumes do not depend on scale and are set at the values under study.
One quantity cannot be matched. The ratio of pump displacement to system hold-up
volume is larger at bench scale, so the antibody passes through the pump more often
per gram processed than at commercial scale. The bench model is therefore expected
to be conservative for aggregate formation by shear.
The model is qualified under SOP-1001, in study S-UFDF-SDM. The three runs at the
set-point condition are compared with the pilot and commercial record of the step on
the step yield, the permeate flux at the set-point TMP, the final concentration achieved
and the change in aggregate across the step. Version 1.0 does not state how many
batches that record holds. Under PCMP-001 §7.1 the offset of each output is the
mean of the scale-down runs at set-point minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale
batches, so the commercial batches of that record are the reference. Each offset is
reported with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval, and each end of that interval is
a one-sided 95 % confidence bound. The qualification runs are made, and the offsets
estimated, before the first characterization run of the step.
Table 5.1 shows how each output of the qualification is used. Only the change in
aggregate bears on a criterion of this step, because aggregate in the final retentate
is judged against the drug substance criterion (Section 7.2). Every judgement of a
scale-down aggregate result against that criterion first subtracts the offset, so the
conclusion is stated at manufacturing scale. The step yield, the permeate flux and
the final concentration have no criterion at the step, and their offsets are recorded
only.

The qualification does not compare the acidic charge variants or the buffer exchange
at both scales. Their offsets are therefore unknown, not zero (PCMP-001 §7.1). Each
is judged as the scale-down model measures it, and its representativeness rests on the
mechanism. Deamidation depends on the pH and the temperature of the solution and
on the time spent in it. The model runs under the operation procedure of the com-
mercial step (SOP-2013), in the commercial formulation buffer, and the membrane
loading holds the time on the skid at its commercial value. The buffer exchange de-
pends on the number of diavolumes and on the passage of the buffer species through
the membrane, and neither depends on scale.



5.2 Operation

The step receives the filtrate pool of the small virus retentive filtration step. The pool
is concentrated by ultrafiltration, diafiltered at constant volume against formulation
buffer for the planned number of diavolumes, concentrated a second time to the final
target, and recovered with a buffer flush. Operation follows SOP-2013. At the set-
point condition the step runs 8 DV at a TMP of 12 psi to a final concentration of 75
g/L.
All runs of the study are loaded from a single Step 9 pool, so that the variability of the
load does not confound the comparison between the levels of a parameter. Section 12
gives the bridging rule that applies if one pool is not enough.

5.5 Statistical methods

For each parameter and each response, the measured values are regressed on the
level of the parameter over its range studied, and the slope is tested against zero at
α = 0.05. This is the campaign level for the significance of a model term (PCMP-001
§6.2). An attribute is called affected by a parameter when that slope is significant
at this level. The set-point condition is common to the three series, and each series
contributes one run at it. The three set-point runs are therefore replicates of one
condition. They give the pure error estimate of the combined process and assay vari-
ability, which is the band against which a change across a range is read.
Statistical significance and practical significance are reported separately. A slope
that is significant at α = 0.05, but predicts a change across the range that is small
against the acceptance criterion, is described in those terms. The criterion, and not
the p-value, decides whether the range is acceptable. No response-surface model is
fitted for this step, because the design has no factorial structure and no interaction or
curvature term can be estimated from it. The model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-001
§7.2 apply to response-surface models and therefore do not apply here.
A scale-down aggregate result is brought to manufacturing scale before it is compared
with its criterion. The offset of the change in aggregate across the step (Section 5.1)
is subtracted from it.
The buffer exchange is analysed separately. The conductivity of the retentate is plot-
ted against the number of diavolumes, and the exponential decay expected from Sec-
tion 4.1 is fitted to the series. The analysis reports the number of diavolumes at which

change measured over the following diavolume.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

PCMP-001 §7 sets the framework of acceptance criteria that every step plan applies.
This section states the criteria of that framework for the outputs of this step, and the
rules of Version 1.0 by which the studies are decided.



7.1 Scale-down model

The offset of each output that the qualification compares at both scales is estimated
and used as Section 5.1 describes (PCMP-001 §7.1). Under PCMP-001 a model is
qualified for an output when the conclusion over the NOR is the same with the off-
set at either end of its confidence interval, and that conclusion is drawn from the
response-surface model of the step. This step has no response-surface model, so the
decision cannot be made as PCMP-001 states it. Version 1.0 of this plan decided the
qualification by a test of equivalence, and PCMP-001 Version 2.0 replaces such tests
with the offset for every scale-down model. This plan therefore has no rule for the
qualification decision at this step. PCR-010 states the approach it takes.



7.2 Product quality

The final retentate is judged against the drug substance criteria of Table 4.1. Aggre-
gate must not exceed 5 % HMW. The acidic charge variants must lie within 18–40
%, so both ends of that range apply. PCMP-001 §7.3 derives an IAC for a step out-
put by carrying the drug substance criterion back through the platform worst case
of each downstream step. No step follows this one, so there is nothing to carry a
criterion back through, and Table 7.1 of PCMP-001 defines no IAC for this step. The
drug substance criteria therefore apply directly (PCMP-001 §7.6). They come from
the drug substance CQA register (PCMP-001 §3), and no result of the studies of this
plan enters them.
A quality attribute is judged on a single batch, because the criterion applies to each
batch (PCMP-001 §6.2). At this step each run is judged on its own measured result.
An aggregate result is first brought to manufacturing scale by its offset (Section 5.5).
The acidic charge variants have no offset, and each result is judged as measured.
The derivation in PCMP-001 credits no step after cation exchange with a change in
aggregate, and no step after low-pH viral inactivation with a change in acidic variants.
The criteria of those step outputs are therefore the drug substance criteria themselves
(PCMP-001 Table 7.1). The studies of this plan measure the increment that this step




                                          20
adds to each attribute, and PCR-010 reports it. The verification of platform worst
cases in PCMP-001 §7.5 uses the response-surface model of a step, and no verification
is planned at this step.
The buffer exchange is accepted when the conductivity and the pH of the retentate
have reached the plateau of the formulation buffer, by the analysis of Section 5.5, at
or below the lower edge of the diavolume NOR, 7 DV.



7.3 Decision rules

The studies are decided by the following rules.

  1. A level of a parameter is acceptable when every attribute measured on the final
     retentate of that run meets its criterion.
  2. A parameter passes over its range studied when every level in the range is accept-
     able and the change measured across the range is small against the criterion.
  3. Where a level fails, the range is truncated at the last acceptable level, and the
     failing level is reported as an edge of failure.
  4. Where a failure occurs at a level inside the NOR, execution stops, and the op-
     erating range is reviewed with the receiving site and with QA before the study
     continues.
  5. Where a run fails for a reason attributable to the equipment or to the method,
     and not to the parameter under study, the run may be repeated once. Both the
     original and the repeat are reported in PCR-010.

Neither Version 1.0 nor PCMP-001 sets a numerical threshold for a change that is
small against the criterion. The change across each range is therefore reported be-
side its margin to the criterion.



7.4 Classification

Each parameter is classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001 §7.7,
from the outcome of these studies (International Council for Harmonisation 2009,
2012). A parameter studied one factor at a time is classified by the same rules as
a parameter studied in a design, and its range is supported by its own data (PCMP-
001 §4). A parameter whose variation over its range affects no quality attribute, but
whose control is needed for the step to perform consistently, is classified as a key
process parameter (KPP). A parameter whose variation affects a quality attribute is
classified as a critical process parameter (CPP), or as a well-controlled critical process
parameter (WC-CPP) where the risk of falling outside its acceptable range is low.
A significant effect that is too small to move an attribute appreciably within its PAR
does not by itself make a parameter critical (PCMP-001 §7.7). PCMP-001 and SOP-
4001 set no numerical threshold for that judgement. PCR-010 will therefore state the
size of each effect across the PAR and the margin of the PAR to the NOR, and classify
on them. The classification is an outcome of the studies and is reported in PCR-010.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A PAR is assigned to each parameter from its univariate series. The criteria are the
drug substance criteria of Section 7.2, because PCMP-001 defines no IAC for this
step. Each scale-down aggregate result is first brought to manufacturing scale (Sec-
tion 5.5). The PAR of a parameter is the contiguous span of levels over which every
attribute measured on the final retentate met its criterion, with the other parameters
at their set-points. Each PAR is reported in PCR-010 with the measured series and
the criterion against which it was read.
Three bounds apply to a PAR determined this way, and PCR-010 states them where it
states the ranges.

  1. A univariate PAR holds with the other parameters at their set-points. It does not
     describe the step when two parameters sit at their range edges at the same time.
  2. The NOR-propagated analysis of PCMP-001 §6.4 is not performed. It predicts
     the response at settings that the design did not run, which needs a fitted mul-
     tivariate model, and this step has none. PCMP-001 §7.6 defines a PAR through
     that analysis. For a parameter studied one factor at a time, the range rests on
     its own data instead (PCMP-001 §4).
  3. A PAR cannot be wider than the range studied. Where every level in a range is
     acceptable, the PAR is the range studied of Table 6.1. It is reported as reaching
     the edge of that range, and no claim is made beyond it (PCMP-001 §7.6).

The ranges reported under this analysis become the basis of the operating ranges that
the receiving site runs to, together with the classification of Section 7.4. Where a PAR
is wider than the NOR, the margin between them is what a deviation is dispositioned
against. Where a PAR is truncated at an edge of failure, the NOR should be reviewed
against the truncated range before the step is transferred.

9 Data management and integrity

Data generated under this plan are recorded so that they are attributable, legible,
contemporaneous, original and accurate, and so that they remain complete, consis-
tent, enduring and available (ALCOA+). Run records are captured in the electronic
notebook under the study number. Analytical results are captured in the laboratory
information management system and reported against the sample identifiers of Ta-
ble 5.3. The study record also gives the execution window of each study and the
calibration status of the equipment it used. INV-001 preventive action PA3 requires
both in every study record.
The statistical analysis of Section 5.5 is scripted and held under version control, so
that it can be re-executed on the retained raw data and return the same result. Every
calculation is reviewed by a second qualified person. A change to a record after it
has been made carries an audit trail with the reason for the change. Values quoted in
PCR-010 are read from the retained data set and are not re-entered by hand. Records
are retained under the record retention procedure of the sponsor for the life of the
product.

12 Risks and assumptions

The scale-down model gives more passes through the pump per gram of antibody than
the commercial skid (Section 5.1). It is therefore expected to be conservative for ag-
gregate formation, and a result that meets the criterion at bench scale is expected to
hold at commercial scale. If aggregate fails its criterion at bench scale, the condition
will be repeated at pilot scale before an operating range is narrowed on the strength
of the bench result.
The study depends on a single Step 9 pool for its load. If one pool is not enough for
the whole study, a second pool will be bridged by running the set-point condition on
both pools and comparing the responses against the pure error band of Section 5.5.
A difference larger than that band will be investigated before the second pool is used,
and PCR-010 will identify the runs made on each pool.
The change in aggregate across the step may be small against the variability of the
SEC method. Noise near the low end of the method range will be attributed to the
assay and not to the parameter under study, unless the change measured across the
range is larger than the pure error band of Section 5.5.
The plan rests on the following assumptions.

  1. The drug product program fixes the composition of the formulation buffer. A
     change to the formulation would change the target of the buffer exchange and
     would invalidate the diavolume study.
  2. The membrane chemistry and the membrane supplier are as qualified. A change
     to either requires a new risk assessment before these data are applied.
  3. The material entering the step meets the criteria that PCMP-001 sets for the out-
     put of Step 9 (Table 7.1). The studies characterize the step under representative
     load, and they do not challenge it with material that already lies outside those
     criteria.
