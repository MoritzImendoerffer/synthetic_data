1 Purpose and scope

This plan defines the process characterization studies for the ultrafiltration and di-
afiltration (UF/DF) step of the A-Mab drug substance process. UF/DF is Step 10, the
last step of the drug substance train, and it delivers the drug substance at its tar-
get concentration of 75 g/L. The commercial process runs at a production bioreactor
working volume of 15,000 L. The process is being transferred from the development
site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI. The
studies of this plan form part of the Stage 1 process design record that supports the
transfer and the Biologics License Application (BLA) (U.S. Food and Drug Administra-
tion 2011; International Council for Harmonisation 2008).
The plan covers the three process parameters of the step (Section 6.1), the scale-
down model on which they are studied, the analytical methods and the sampling,
the acceptance and decision criteria, and the analysis by which a proven acceptable
range (PAR) is assigned to each parameter. It applies to the drug substance process
only. Drug product manufacture and the development of the formulation belong to
the drug product program and are out of scope. That program fixes the composition
of the formulation buffer, and the studies treat it as a constant.
The step does not change the glycan variants of A-Mab, and no clearance of host
cell protein, DNA or leached Protein A is credited to it (Section 4.2). It can add
to aggregate and to the acidic charge variants (Section 4.1). No viral clearance is
claimed for this step.



1.1 Changes in this revision

Version 1.0 of this plan was approved on 2026-02-23, before the first study of the
step began on 2026-02-25. Version 2.0 is issued under the QA investigation INV-001,
which concerned the acceptance criteria of the PAR analysis. QA opened INV-001
on 2026-08-03, after its review of the master report PCMR-001 Version 1.0 for BLA
readiness, and closed it on 2026-09-18. The investigation found that the criteria used
to judge each step in the PAR analysis had been set after the data were seen, and
that no approved plan contained them (INV-001 finding 1). As corrective actions, the
master plan PCMP-001 was reissued as Version 2.0 with acceptance criteria derived
a priori (INV-001 corrective action CA1), and each step plan was amended with those
criteria and with the method for fragments (corrective action CA2). This document
is that amendment of PCP-010, issued after the studies it governs were run, and it
describes only the studies that Version 1.0 planned. The design, the ranges, the levels
and the sampling points of Version 1.0 are unchanged, and so are its decision rules
except where the list below says otherwise. The changes are the following.

  (i) To estimate the offsets of a bench-scale tangential flow filtration (TFF) model of

      A-Mab and not on platform practice alone.

      of Section 8.
 (v) To provide the evidence on which the three parameters will be classified under
      SOP-4001, and to carry that classification into the control strategy through the
      report PCR-010 and the master report PCMR-001.

4 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

4.1 Unit-operation description and prior knowledge

The UF/DF step concentrates the antibody and exchanges the process buffer for the
formulation buffer. Both operations run across a tangential flow membrane whose
pores pass buffer salts and retain the antibody. Ultrafiltration removes permeate and
raises the antibody concentration in the retentate. Diafiltration then adds formulation
buffer at the rate at which permeate leaves, so the retentate volume stays constant
while the permeable species are washed out. Each diavolume removes a fixed fraction
of the permeable species that remain, so the residual process buffer components
fall roughly exponentially with the number of diavolumes. The conductivity of the
retentate approaches the value of the formulation buffer from above or below. At
high antibody concentration, Donnan and volume-exclusion effects can leave the pH
and the excipient levels of the retentate off the values of the buffer. The step ends
with a second concentration to the DS target of 75 g/L and a buffer flush that recovers
the retentate.
The step can add to aggregate and to the acidic charge variants. The first route is
physical. The membrane retains the antibody, so the antibody accumulates at the
membrane wall in a polarization layer, where its concentration is higher than in the
bulk retentate. The whole retentate also passes many times through the recirculation
pump. Aggregate can form at the wall, where the antibody is most concentrated, and
in the pump, where cavitation, air–liquid interfaces and particles shed by the pump
can start it. Shear alone causes little aggregation of an antibody.
The A-Mab case study leaves the UF/DF step out (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009,
86), so it gives no expectation for this step. The expectations in the rest of this section
come from general knowledge of the tangential flow filtration of antibodies. On that
basis the risk of aggregate formation is expected to rise with the final concentration,
with the TMP and with the number of passes through the pump. Below the point
at which the polarization layer limits the flux, a higher TMP drives more permeate
through the membrane and raises the antibody concentration at the wall. Beyond
that point the flux no longer rises, the wall concentration is at its limit, and extra
pressure compacts the layer at the wall. A higher crossflow raises the shear at the wall
and lowers the antibody concentration there, because it sweeps retained antibody
back into the bulk. A higher final concentration raises the viscosity of the retentate
and, through the bulk concentration, lowers the flux. A higher viscosity raises the
pressure drop along the feed channel. Within the normal operating ranges (NORs)
the exposure is short and the effect is expected to be small.
The second route is chemical. The step keeps the antibody in solution for the length
of the operation, and deamidation of asparagine residues continues during that time.



                                            11
Deamidation raises the acidic charge variants. The increase grows with the time in
solution, at a rate that rises with pH and temperature. The operation lasts longer
with more diavolumes, with a higher final concentration, which lowers the flux, and
with a lower TMP below the flux limit. For this reason the final retentate of every run
is assayed for charge variants as well as for aggregate.
The step is part of the platform train that was used for X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab
(PCMP-001 §2). The places where aggregate can form in the step are the same for
every antibody, so that experience applies to the qualitative behaviour of the step.
How readily an antibody aggregates depends on its colloidal stability and its self-
association, which differ between antibodies, and the viscosity of its retentate, which
rises with concentration, differs too. For this reason the final DS concentration is
studied for A-Mab and is not carried over from the platform.

4.3 Risk-based prioritization of parameters

The pre-characterization risk assessment RA-001 ranked every process parameter of
the drug substance process for its potential impact on the CQAs and on process perfor-
mance, and for its potential to interact with other parameters (International Council
for Harmonisation 2023b). Parameters ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were
assigned to multivariate study. Parameters ranked lower, whose range could be sup-
ported one factor at a time, were assigned to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). All
three parameters of this step were assigned to univariate study.
Each parameter acts on the product by its own route, and two of the routes meet in
the polarization layer. More diavolumes wash more of the permeable process buffer
components out of the retentate, and they lengthen the operation and add passes
through the pump. A higher TMP raises the flux and the antibody concentration at
the wall up to the flux limit, and beyond it compacts the layer at the wall. A higher
final DS concentration raises the viscosity of the retentate and, through the bulk
concentration, lowers the flux.
The final DS concentration therefore sets the flux limit, and with it the TMP at which
the polarization layer begins to limit the flux. The effect of the TMP depends on
the final DS concentration, and an interaction between them is possible. A study
that varies one factor at a time cannot resolve such an interaction. What the design
does bound is the effect of each parameter at the edge of its range studied, with the
other parameters at their set-points (Table A.1). For an attribute that approaches
its criterion at an edge, the rule of Version 1.0 is kept. Where a measured attribute
approaches its acceptance criterion at an edge, the study should be extended to the
combination of edges before a range is claimed, and the extension should be agreed
with Statistics and QA before it is executed. The threshold for “approaches” is left to
the report PCR-010, because Version 1.0 set none.

5 Materials and methods

5.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies are run on the bench-scale skid EQ-TFF-142, registered as the ultrafiltra-
tion / diafiltration TFF skid and held under calibration and change control. Section 11
gives its calibration status for each study. The model holds the crossflow and the mem-
brane loading at their commercial values, both scaled by membrane area (PCMP-001
§5). A higher crossflow per unit membrane area raises the shear at the membrane
wall and, by sweeping retained antibody back into the bulk, lowers the antibody con-
centration in the polarization layer. The membrane loading, in grams of antibody per
square metre, sets the volume that each square metre of membrane processes. At
the same TMP and crossflow the flux is the same, so a run of the model lasts as long
as the commercial step at the same settings. The membrane chemistry, the nominal
molecular weight cut-off and the feed channel geometry are those of the commercial
skid. The TMP, the number of diavolumes and the final concentration do not depend
on scale and are set at the values under study.
One quantity cannot be matched, the share of the batch held up in the system. With
the crossflow and the membrane loading matched per unit area, the antibody passes
through the pump as often at bench scale as at commercial scale. The bench system,
however, holds a larger share of its batch in its recirculation loop and has a larger
ratio of surface to volume. More antibody is therefore lost to hold-up, and more of it
meets surfaces and air–liquid interfaces per gram processed. Version 1.0 expected
the bench model to be conservative for aggregate formation, because it assumed
more passes through the pump at bench scale, and it expected a result that meets the
criterion at bench scale to hold at commercial scale. The report PCR-010 compares
these expectations with the data.
The qualification runs are made under SOP-1001, in study S-UFDF-SDM, where three
runs at the set-point condition are compared with the pilot and commercial record of
the step on the step yield, the permeate flux at the set-point TMP, the final concen-
tration achieved and the change in aggregate content across the step. Under the
master plan PCMP-001 §7.1 the offset of each output is the mean of the scale-down
runs at set-point minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches, reported with
its two-sided 90 % confidence interval. Each end of that interval is a one-sided 95
% confidence bound. PCMP-001 §7.1 requires the scale-down runs at set-point to be
made, and the offset of each output to be estimated, before the first characterization
run of the step. The decision whether the model is qualified for an output is made at
analysis, because it needs the response-surface model of the step (Section 7.1). Ver-
sion 1.0 did not name the manufacturing-scale batches of the comparison. PCR-010
names them and states when the offsets were estimated.

The acidic charge variants and the buffer exchange also carry criteria at this step.

rests on the passage of the buffer species through the membrane, which depends on
the number of diavolumes and on the membrane and not on scale.



5.2 Operation

The step receives the filtrate pool of the small virus retentive filtration step. The pool
is concentrated by ultrafiltration, diafiltered at constant volume against formulation
buffer for the planned number of diavolumes, concentrated a second time to the final
target, and recovered with a buffer flush. Operation follows SOP-2013. At the set-
point condition the step runs 8 DV at a TMP of 12 psi to a final concentration of 75
g/L.
All runs of the study are loaded from a single pool of the virus filtration step, so
that the variability of the load does not confound the comparison between the levels

of a parameter. Section 12 gives the bridging rule that applies if one pool is not
sufficient.



5.3 Analytical methods

The validated methods are listed in Table 3.2. Aggregate is measured by size ex-
clusion chromatography (SEC) under AMV-3011 and reported as the percentage of
HMW species. SEC remains the method for aggregate under the master plan PCMP-
001 §7.8. Protein concentration is measured by ultraviolet absorbance (A280) under
AMV-3019. The mass balance and the final concentration rest on that measurement.
Charge variants are measured by imaged capillary isoelectric focusing (icIEF) under
AMV-3013 and reported as the percentage of acidic species.
The completeness of the buffer exchange is followed by the conductivity and the pH
of the retentate. These are in-process readings from the skid instrumentation, not
validated assays. They decide the buffer-exchange criterion of Section 7.2, and so
whether the lower edge of the diavolume NOR rests on data for A-Mab.
Aggregate binds the cation exchange resin more strongly than the monomer, so it
elutes late and is largely excluded from the pool at the cation exchange step. The
aggregate content of A-Mab drug substance is low, and a change across this step
may be of the same size as the repeatability of the SEC method. The rules of Version
1.0 for such small changes are kept. Analytical Development will report the method
variability that applies over the range in which the samples fall. A measured change
is judged against the pure error estimate described in Section 5.5 rather than against
the nominal precision of the method. Noise near the low end of the method range will
be attributed to the assay rather than to the parameter under study, unless the change
measured across the range is larger than the pure error estimate of Section 5.5. How
this last rule and the slope test of Section 5.5 are applied together is stated in the
report PCR-010, because Version 1.0 did not relate them.

5.5 Statistical methods

For each parameter and each response, the measured values will be regressed on the
level of the parameter over the range studied, and the slope will be tested against
zero at α = 0.05. The set-point condition is shared by the three series and will be run
three times. Those replicates give the pure error estimate of the combined process
and assay variability, against which a change across a range is read. The master plan
PCMP-001 §6.2 sets the same level for the significance of a model term, and under
it an attribute is called affected by a parameter when the slope is significant at that
level.
Statistical significance and practical significance will be reported separately. A slope
that is significant at α = 0.05 but predicts a change across the range that is small
against the acceptance criterion will be described in those terms, and the criterion
rather than the p-value decides whether the range is acceptable. No response-surface
model will be fitted for this step, because the design carries no factorial structure and
no interaction term can be estimated from it. The model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-
001 §7.2 apply to response-surface models and therefore do not apply here.
The buffer exchange will be analysed separately. The conductivity of the retentate
will be plotted against the number of diavolumes, and the exponential decay expected

from Section 4.1 will be fitted to the series. The analysis reports the number of diavol-
umes at which the conductivity of the retentate has reached the plateau set by the
formulation buffer and no further change is measured over the following diavolume.

6.1 Factors, ranges and study type

The parameters, their set-points, the ranges to be studied and the NORs are given in
Table 6.1. All three are studied one at a time, because the risk assessment RA-001
assigned them to univariate study (Section 4.3).

  Table 6.1: Parameters, ranges studied and normal operating ranges for the step.

          Parameter                  Unit Set-point Range studied NOR
          Number of diavolumes       DV           8 7–10           7–9
          Transmembrane pressure     psi         12 10–15          10–14

Each range studied extends beyond the NOR at one end or at both. The number
of diavolumes is studied from 7 to 10 DV against a NOR of 7 to 9 DV. The range
extends above the NOR because more diavolumes mean more time on the skid and
more passes through the pump. Its lower edge is the lower edge of the NOR, which
is the lowest count the commercial step will run, and the buffer exchange curve char-
acterizes the step at that value. Below that edge the buffer exchange would be less
complete, and the study does not go there. The TMP is studied from 10 to 15 psi
against a NOR of 10 to 14 psi. The range extends above the NOR because pressure
above the flux limit compacts the polarization layer without raising throughput, so
more of the antibody sits at the highest concentration at the wall. Its lower edge is
also the lower edge of the NOR. A lower TMP would lengthen the operation, and with
it the passes through the pump and the time for deamidation, and the study does not
characterize that side beyond the NOR.
The final DS concentration is studied from 65 to 85 g/L against a NOR of 70 to 80
g/L, and the range extends on both sides. An excursion above the target raises the
viscosity and the bulk concentration of the retentate, lowers the flux and lengthens
the operation, and so raises the risk of aggregation. An excursion below the target
leaves the drug substance below the concentration that the drug product program
expects.
The step will not be characterized outside these ranges, and no range wider than the
range studied will be proposed in the report PCR-010.

7 Acceptance and decision criteria

7.1 Scale-down model

Version 1.0 accepted the model under a rule in three parts. The mean step yield,
the permeate flux and the change in aggregate content across the step were not to
differ from the pilot and commercial record at α = 0.05. The final concentration
achieved was to lie within the reported variability of the A280 method. A difference
that was statistically significant was to be assessed for its size against the acceptance
criteria of Section 7.2 before the model was rejected, and that judgement was to be
documented in the qualification record and approved by Statistics and QA.
The master plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0 replaces such tests with the offset of each
output from manufacturing scale (PCMP-001 §7.1, Section 5.1). Under PCMP-001 a
model is qualified for an output when the conclusion over the NOR is the same with
the offset at either end of its confidence interval, and that conclusion is drawn from
the response-surface model of the step. This step has no response-surface model, so
the qualification decision cannot be made as PCMP-001 states it, and this plan sets
no rule for it. The report PCR-010 states the approach it takes.



7.2 Product quality

The final retentate is judged against the drug substance criteria of Table 4.1. Aggre-
gate must not exceed 5 % HMW, and the acidic charge variants must lie between
18 and 40 %. The master plan PCMP-001 §7.3 carries each drug substance criterion
back through the platform worst cases of the downstream steps to give the IACs of
the steps before this one. No step follows this one, so PCMP-001 Table 7.1 defines no
IAC for it, and the drug substance criteria apply directly (PCMP-001 §7.6). They come
from the drug substance CQA register (PCMP-001 §3), and no result of the studies of
this plan enters them.
Under PCMP-001 §6.2 a quality attribute is judged on a single batch, not on a mean.
At this step each run is judged on its own measured result.
The derivation in PCMP-001 assumes that no step after cation exchange changes
aggregate, and that no step after low-pH viral inactivation changes the acidic charge
variants (PCMP-001 Table 7.1). This step can add to both, and the studies of this plan
measure what it adds. The verification of platform worst cases in PCMP-001 §7.5 uses
the response-surface model of a step, and no verification is planned at this step.




                                           21
The buffer-exchange criterion is that of Version 1.0. The buffer exchange is accepted
when the conductivity and the pH of the retentate have reached the plateau of the
formulation buffer, by the analysis of Section 5.5, at or below the lower edge of the
diavolume NOR of 7 DV. The analysis of Section 5.5 treats the conductivity only. Ver-
sion 1.0 set no analysis for the pH and no numerical tolerance for the plateau, so the
report PCR-010 states its approach to both.



7.3 Decision rules

Version 1.0 set the following decision rules, and they are unchanged.

  1. A level of a parameter is acceptable when every attribute measured on the final
     retentate of that run meets its criterion.
  2. A parameter passes over its range studied when every level in the range is accept-
     able and the change measured across the range is small against the criterion.
  3. Where a level fails, the range is truncated at the last acceptable level, and the
     failing level is reported as an edge of failure.
  4. Where a failure occurs at a level inside the NOR, execution stops, and the op-
     erating range is reviewed with the receiving site and with QA before the study
     continues.
  5. Where a run fails for a reason attributable to the equipment or to the method,
     and not to the parameter under study, the run may be repeated once. Both the
     original and the repeat are reported in PCR-010.

The threshold for a change that is small against the criterion is left to PCR-010, be-
cause neither Version 1.0 nor PCMP-001 sets one.



7.4 Classification

Each parameter will be classified under SOP-4001 (International Council for Harmon-
isation 2009, 2012). The classification rule of Version 1.0 is kept. A parameter whose
variation over the characterized range affects no quality attribute, but whose control
is needed for the step to perform consistently, is classified as a key process param-
eter. A parameter whose variation affects a quality attribute is classified as critical,
or as well controlled critical where the risk of falling outside the design space is low.
The classification is an outcome of the studies and is reported in PCR-010.
At a step with no response-surface model, the term that the master plan PCMP-001
§6.2 tests is the slope of Section 5.5, and the effect that PCMP-001 §7.7 weighs is the
change that this slope predicts across the PAR. A well controlled critical parameter
is one that is readily controlled and whose PAR is wide against its NOR (PCMP-001
§7.7), and a parameter studied one factor at a time is classified by the same rules as
one studied in a design (PCMP-001 §4). PCMP-001 and SOP-4001 set no numerical
threshold for a small effect or a wide PAR, so the report PCR-010 states the size of

each effect across the PAR and the margin of the PAR to the NOR, and classifies on
them.

8 Proven acceptable ranges (planned
  analysis)

A PAR will be assigned to each parameter from its univariate series. The acceptance
basis is the drug substance criteria for aggregate and the acidic charge variants (Ta-
ble 4.1). The PAR of a parameter is the contiguous span of levels over which every
attribute measured on the final retentate met its criterion, with the other parameters
at their set-points. Each PAR will be reported in the report PCR-010 together with
the measured series and the criterion against which it was read.
Three limitations apply to a PAR determined this way, and PCR-010 will state them
where it states the ranges.

  1. A univariate PAR holds with the other parameters at their set-points. It does not
     describe the step when two parameters sit at their range edges at the same time.
  2. The master plan PCMP-001 §6.4 defines a second analysis, the NOR-propagated
     analysis, which predicts the response from a fitted multivariate model while the
     other parameters are drawn at random around their set-points. This step has
     no such model, so that analysis is not performed. PCMP-001 §7.6 defines a PAR
     through it, and for a parameter studied one factor at a time the range rests on
     its own data instead (PCMP-001 §4).
  3. A PAR cannot be wider than the range studied. Where every level in a range is
     acceptable, the claimed PAR is the range studied of Table 6.1 and nothing beyond
     it, and it is reported as reaching the edge of that range (PCMP-001 §7.6).

The ranges reported under this analysis become the basis of the operating ranges that
the receiving site runs to, together with the classification of Section 7.4. Where a PAR
is wider than the NOR, the margin between them is what a deviation is dispositioned
against. Where a PAR is truncated at an edge of failure, the NOR should be reviewed
against the truncated range before the step is transferred.

9 Data management and integrity

Data generated under this plan are recorded so that they are attributable, legible,
contemporaneous, original and accurate, and so that they remain complete, consis-
tent, enduring and available (ALCOA+). Run records are captured in the electronic
notebook under the study number. Analytical results are captured in the laboratory
information management system and reported against the sample identifiers of each
run. As INV-001 preventive action PA3 requires, the study record also gives the exe-
cution dates of each study and the calibration status of the equipment it used.
The statistical analysis of Section 5.5 is scripted and held under version control, so
that it can be re-executed on the retained raw data and return the same result. Every
calculation is reviewed by a second qualified person. A change to a record after it
has been made carries an audit trail with the reason for the change. Values quoted
in the report PCR-010 are read from the retained data set and are not re-entered by
hand. Records are retained under the record retention procedure of the sponsor for
the life of the product.

12 Risks and assumptions

A failure of aggregate at bench scale is handled by a rule of Version 1.0, which is kept.
If aggregate fails its criterion at bench scale, the condition will be repeated at pilot
scale before an operating range is narrowed on the strength of the bench result.
If one pool is not sufficient for the whole study, a second pool will be bridged by
running the set-point condition on both pools and comparing the responses against
the pure error estimate of Section 5.5. A difference larger than the pure error will be
investigated before the second pool is used, and the runs executed on each pool will
be identified in the report PCR-010.
The design has one run at each edge of a range. The slope test of each series therefore
rests on few runs, and an effect that is small against the variability between runs may
not reach significance.
The plan rests on the following assumptions.

  1. The composition of the formulation buffer is fixed by the drug product program.
     A change to the formulation would change the target of the buffer exchange and
     would invalidate the diavolume study.
  2. The membrane chemistry and the membrane supplier are as qualified, and a
     change to either requires a new risk assessment before these data are applied.
  3. The material entering the step arrives within the criteria that the master plan
     PCMP-001 sets for the output of the virus filtration step (PCMP-001 Table 7.1).
     The study characterizes the step under representative load.
