Executive summary

This report gives the results of the Stage 1 characterization of the production bioreac-
tor, Step 3 of the A-Mab drug substance process. The production bioreactor is a fed-
batch culture with a working volume of 15,000 L at commercial scale, and it forms the
glycan, charge variant and aggregate quality attributes of A-Mab. The studies were
run in a qualified scale-down model under the Process Characterization Plan PCP-003
and the Process Characterization Master Plan PCMP-001, following ICH Q8(R2), ICH
Q11 and the A-Mab case study (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012;
CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). This is Version 2.0 of the report. It replaces
Version 1.0 after the QA investigations INV-001 and INV-002 (Section 1.4).
The scale-down model was compared with manufacturing scale in three runs at
the set-points against five manufacturing-scale batches. A screening design of 19
runs varied the five parameters assigned to multivariate study, and a face-centred
response-surface design of 28 runs then varied four of them. Under INV-002, 16 runs
were added to the response-surface design as a second block, after the results of the
planned runs were known. A univariate study varied the other four parameters one
at a time, and an osmolality study made during the Version 2.0 re-analysis measured
the effect of osmolality with the other parameters at the combination of NOR settings
least favourable for acidic variants.
The response-surface model of each of the five modelled attributes was selected on
all 44 runs by the procedure of INV-002 corrective action CA3. Every selected model
meets the model F-test, the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting of PCMP-001
§7.2. The models of galactosylation and acidic variants do not meet the criterion
on residuals and influential runs, because the spread of their residuals grows with
the predicted value, and they were used with that growth sized against their criteria
(PD-003-04). With each run flagged by its Cook’s distance deleted in turn, no conclu-
sion over the normal operating range (NOR) and no proven acceptable range (PAR)
changed.
Judged on the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch at manufacturing scale, afu-
cosylation, galactosylation, high mannose and aggregate meet their intermediate ac-
ceptance criteria at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high. Acidic
variants do not. At nine of the 81 combinations, all with pCO2 at its NOR high, the
interval reaches below the lower limit of 18.0 %. At the least favourable combina-
tion the predicted median is 18.4 % with the initial viable cell concentration at its
set-point, and about 17.7 % with that parameter at its NOR low (DEV-003-07). The
NOR was not narrowed (DEV-003-04). The risk was accepted on the grounds given in
Section 7.2, with monitoring of acidic variants at harvest and alarm limits inside the
NOR for culture pH, pCO2 and osmolality.

The five multivariate parameters were classified as well-controlled critical process pa-

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose main mechanism of action
is antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). It is produced by a recombinant
Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell line and purified by the platform train of Protein
A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small-virus
retentive filtration and ultrafiltration and diafiltration (UF/DF) (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009).
The production bioreactor is Step 3 of the drug substance process. It is a fed-batch
culture with a working volume of 15,000 L at commercial scale. The cells secrete
A-Mab into the medium over a culture of 17 days at the set-point, and the culture
fluid passes to harvest and clarification (Step 4) at the end of the run. The production
bioreactor is the only step of the A-Mab drug substance process at which product
quality attributes are formed. The glycan distribution is completed in the Golgi before
the antibody is secreted, and none of the purification steps separates or changes the
neutral glycoforms (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Charge variants are formed
in the cell and in the culture fluid, and aggregate in the culture fluid, so the culture
sets the levels at which both enter purification. The culture also sets the amount of
host cell protein and DNA that the purification steps have to clear.
This report gives the results of the studies planned in PCP-003. It applies the strategy,
the statistical methods and the acceptance criteria of PCMP-001 Version 2.0, and it
rolls up into PCMR-001. The risk assessment RA-001 set the scope of the studies.
The characterization supports the transfer of the process from the development site
in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI under the
Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. Table 1.1 lists these documents.

1.2 Objectives

PCP-003 §2 sets the following objectives, and Section 12 states the result against
each.

  1. To quantify the relationship between each studied parameter and each of the
     five modelled responses over the ranges studied, and to identify the interactions
     between parameters.
  2. To define the multivariate region of the response-surface parameters over which
     every modelled attribute is predicted to meet its intermediate acceptance crite-
     rion.
  3. To establish a PAR for each parameter against each attribute whose fitted model
     contains a significant term in that parameter, and to confirm that the NOR lies
     inside that range.
  4. To provide the demonstrated effects and the operating risk from which each
     parameter is classified under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001.
  5. To provide the process understanding and the capability estimate that justify the
     Stage 2 operating ranges and the contribution of this step to the control strategy.



1.3 Regulatory and scientific basis

The studies are Stage 1 process design studies in the lifecycle approach to process
validation (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). They follow the enhanced
approach of ICH Q8(R2) and ICH Q11. Prior knowledge and risk assessment iden-
tify the parameters that may affect an attribute, and designed studies then measure
and bound those effects (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012). ICH
Q8(R2) defines the design space and the proven acceptable range, and it treats the
criticality of an attribute as a continuum. The risk tools follow ICH Q9 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2023). The A-Mab case study supplies the process model,
the framework of quality attributes and the decision logic of parameter classification

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The platform on which A-Mab is produced was used for the humanized IgG1 antibod-
ies X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab and for the A-Mab clinical and engineering campaigns
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Cell line construction, basal medium, feed strat-
egy and the control of pH, temperature and dissolved oxygen are common to these
products. The directions of the effects seen on the platform are carried into this study
as expectations. Their sizes are not, and the fitted models of this study give them.
The glycan attributes are set by the enzymes of the Golgi. Core fucose is added by a
fucosyltransferase from the donor GDP-fucose. The fraction of antibody secreted with-
out core fucose therefore rises when the activity of that enzyme falls, when the donor
is depleted or when the glycan spends less time in the Golgi. Terminal galactose is
added by a galactosyltransferase from UDP-galactose. The donor and the manganese
cofactor of the enzyme are depleted late in a fed-batch culture, so galactosylation
is expected to fall as the culture ages. High mannose glycans remain when transit
through the Golgi is fast relative to the processing enzymes, or when those enzymes
are inhibited. The harvested pool contains everything secreted during the run, so a
longer culture weights it toward antibody made late, under depleted conditions. Cul-
ture pH, temperature, pCO2 and osmolality act on the same enzymes and donor pools
through the growth rate and the intracellular pH, so their effects on the glycans are
expected to interact.
Aggregate forms after secretion, while the antibody sits in the culture fluid at culture
temperature. A longer culture raises it through the longer time at temperature. A
culture pH closer to the isoelectric point raises it, because the net charge of the anti-
body falls and the repulsion between molecules weakens. Acidic variants are mainly
products of asparagine deamidation, together with sialylated glycans and glycated
lysines. A higher temperature and a higher pH speed deamidation, while pCO2 and
osmolality act on the intracellular pH and on sialylation. These routes do not respond
to a parameter in the same direction, so the net direction of each parameter on acidic
variants is not predictable from the chemistry and is read from the data. Host cell
protein and DNA are released when cells die and lyse, so their amount in the harvest
is expected to rise with the culture duration.
Dissolved carbon dioxide acidifies the cytosol, so a higher pCO2 is expected to act
on the Golgi enzymes in the same direction as a lower culture pH. Dissolved oxygen
supplies the oxidative metabolism of the cell, and the culture is not expected to be
limited by oxygen across the range studied. The initial viable cell concentration sets
where on the growth curve the culture starts. A different starting density does not




                                           13
change the cell line, the medium or the feed, so it is expected to change the inte-
gral of viable cells and the titre and not the quality attributes. The basal medium
concentration and the nutrient feed-1 volume set the supply of glucose, amino acids
and nucleotide sugar precursors, and across the ranges studied the feed replenishes
those components faster than the cells use them. None of these four parameters is
therefore expected to change a glycan attribute at a fixed culture age.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

The risk assessment RA-001 ranked every parameter of the step before characteriza-
tion began, on its potential impact on a quality attribute and its potential to interact
with other parameters (International Council for Harmonisation 2023). Parameters
ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to multivariate study, and
the others to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). For the production bioreactor five of
the nine parameters were assigned to multivariate study, namely culture pH, culture
temperature, CO2 partial pressure (pCO2), osmolality and culture duration. Each of
them changes the pH of the Golgi or the size of the nucleotide sugar pools. The effect
of one is therefore expected to depend on the levels of the others, and only a design
that varies them together can estimate that dependence. Dissolved oxygen, initial vi-
able cell concentration, basal medium concentration and nutrient feed-1 volume were
assigned to univariate study, for the reason given in Section 2.1.
The range studied of every parameter extends beyond its NOR at both ends, and it
is between 1.5 and 2.5 times as wide as the NOR. The wider range shows how each
attribute responds beyond the edges of routine operation, and it sets the knowledge
space within which a PAR can be claimed. No PAR is claimed beyond the range stud-
ied.

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies were run in bench-scale stirred-tank bioreactors of the same design class
as the commercial production vessel, with the same control scheme for pH, temper-
ature, dissolved oxygen and nutrient addition. The scale-down model was operated
under the bioreactor procedure SOP-2003. Parameters that do not depend on scale
were run at the commercial set-points. Parameters that depend on scale, such as the
agitation rate, the gas flow rates and the working volume, were set so that the small
scale matched the performance of the full scale (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
pCO2 is the exception. A large vessel strips carbon dioxide less efficiently than a
bench-scale vessel, so pCO2 was controlled to the level of each run and studied as
a factor, over a range that spans the levels the commercial vessel reaches (PCP-003
§5.1). The pCO2 of each run was measured by the probe of the scale-down bioreactor
system, EQ-BRX-205.
The qualification study S-BRX-SDM (2025-08-11 to 2025-09-05) followed the scale-
down model procedure SOP-1001. It compared three runs of the scale-down model
at the set-points with five manufacturing-scale batches of the A-Mab engineering and
clinical campaigns. A scale-down model need not reproduce the manufacturing mean,
provided the difference is known well enough that no conclusion depends on its exact
size (PCMP-001 §7.1). The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs
minus the mean of the manufacturing batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence
interval. For an output modelled on the log scale (Section 3.5) the offset is the ratio
of the geometric means, and a scale-down value is divided by it. Every result of the
scale-down studies that is judged against a criterion has the offset applied, so ranges
and conclusions are stated at manufacturing scale. The model is qualified for an
output when the conclusion over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of
its interval.

3.5 Statistical methods

The designs were generated, randomized and analysed under SOP-1002, with the
methods of PCMP-001 §6 (Montgomery 2017). Each factor was coded so that its set-
point is 0 and the ends of its range studied are −1 and +1. For this step the set-point
of every factor of the designs lies at the centre of its range studied, so the centre
points of the designs are at the set-points.
The screening data were fitted by least squares to the five main effects and the ten
two-factor interactions. An effect is twice the coded coefficient, the change in the
response across the full range studied. The model has 16 terms with the intercept in
19 runs. It leaves one degree of freedom for lack of fit and two for pure error. With
so few, R² overstates how well the model would predict new runs, so the screening
analysis was read only to identify active factors and interactions. Its lack-of-fit test




                                                 19
compares the mean of the centre points with the mean of the factorial runs, which is
the test for curvature.
The response-surface data were analysed by the model selection of INV-002 corrective
action CA3, adopted on 2026-09-15. PCP-003 prescribes the full quadratic model of
each output, with 14 terms besides the intercept, and the selection is recorded as the
discrepancy PD-003-03. It has three steps.

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic model gives the exponent
     λ that best makes the residuals normal with constant variance, with its 95 %
     interval (Box and Cox 1964). An output is modelled on the log scale where that
     interval contains 0, the log, and excludes 1, the raw scale. It is modelled on the
     raw scale otherwise.
  2. The model. Among all 1,337 submodels of the full quadratic that keep hierarchy,
     the model with the lowest AICc is taken. A submodel keeps hierarchy when it
     contains an interaction only with both its main effects, and a square only with
     its main effect.
  3. The check. The selected model is judged against the adequacy criteria of PCP-
     003 §7, listed below.

AICc estimates how well a model would predict new runs. It rewards a smaller resid-
ual and penalises every estimated term, and its small-sample correction raises the
penalty when the runs are few for the number of terms, as in these designs (Hurvich
and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002). A stepwise search changes one term
at a time and stops when no single change lowers AICc, so it can stop where only a
combination of changes would lower it further. On the data of this step a forward
search would have stopped at a model with a higher AICc for acidic variants, and a
backward search for aggregate. INV-002 also refitted each failing planned model by
hierarchical backward elimination, to simulate the augmentation. That refit is step-
wise, tests each term against zero instead of comparing models, and uses the raw
scale only.
The models were fitted to all 44 runs, the 28 planned runs and the 16 augmentation
runs, with a block term coded −1 for the planned runs and +1 for the augmentation.
A prediction is made with the block term at 0, the average of the two blocks.
Of the modelled attributes, high mannose, acidic variants and aggregate are modelled
on the log scale. On that scale the model reads ln(y) = b0 + b1·x1 + … + e. Trans-
formed back, y = eb0 · eb1·x1 · … · ee , so a main effect multiplies the output by e2b
from the low to the high end of its range when the other factors are at their set-points,
where the interaction terms vanish. The error between runs multiplies the output too,
so its size in percentage points grows with the level of the output. For high mannose
and acidic variants this is expected, because the precision of the glycan map and of
icIEF is a percentage of the level (Appendix D). For aggregate the profile supports
the log scale too, although the precision of SEC is an absolute amount. Least squares,
and the tests and prediction intervals built on it, assume residuals that are indepen-
dent and normally distributed with mean zero and the same variance over the whole
range studied. On the raw scale, the residual standard deviation of these outputs in
the upper half of the predicted values is 1.24 to 2.22 times that in the lower half. On




                                           20
the log scale the ratio is 0.81 to 1.88. For acidic variants the spread still grows on
the log scale, and Section 5.3.2 gives its size against the criterion.
On the log scale the criterion stays in its own unit, percent. The effect of a parameter
is a fold change, and the scale-down offset is a ratio. A prediction interval is calculated
on the log scale and transformed back. It keeps its 95 % coverage, because the
probability that ln y lies between two values is the probability that y lies between
their antilogs. It is asymmetric in percentage points, further above the predicted
value than below it. At the least favourable NOR combination for acidic variants,
for example, the interval runs 2.15 points below the predicted value and 2.43 points
above it. The predicted value of a log-scale model is the median of the output, its
geometric mean, and not its arithmetic mean. The prediction limits, which decide
the judgement against a criterion, are not affected by that.
A selected model is adequate for prediction under PCP-003 §7 when all of the following
hold. The first four restate the criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2.

  a. The model F-test. The F-test of the model is significant at α = 0.05.
  b. The lack-of-fit test. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  c. The limit on overfitting. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20.
     Predicted R² comes from the prediction error sum of squares (PRESS).
  d. The criterion on residuals and influential runs. A regression of the absolute
     residuals on the predicted values, and a regression of the residuals on the run
     order, each show no slope significant at α = 0.05. No run has a Cook’s distance
     above the median of the F distribution with p and n − p degrees of freedom,
     where p is the number of parameters of the model and n the number of runs. A
     run with a Cook’s distance above 4/n, which is 0.091 for the 44 runs of the models,
     is examined by deleting it and repeating the judgement. The augmentation runs
     continue the order of the planned runs.
  e. The normality criterion. The normal quantile plot of the residuals shows no de-
     parture from normality.

Each run above 4/n was deleted in turn and the model selection repeated. The output
was then judged over the NOR again, and its NOR-propagated PARs and the design
space were computed again. A model that does not meet the criterion on residuals
and influential runs was still used for the ranges and the design space, with the size
of each residual trend compared with the criterion of its output. That use is the
discrepancy PD-003-04.
Other models may lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one and fit the data almost
as well (Burnham and Anderson 2002). They were computed for every output. The
selected model decides, and the others are a sensitivity analysis. A feature that only
some of them carry is not reported as a finding.
Each output is judged against its intermediate acceptance criterion at manufacturing
scale. A quality attribute is judged on the exact 95 % prediction interval of a single
batch at each of the 81 combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the
four response-surface factors, with the offset applied. The interval comes from the
t distribution and includes the uncertainty of the coefficients and the residual. It is




                                            21
two-sided, so each of its ends is a one-sided 97.5 % limit. A two-sided criterion is
judged at both ends.
The PARs were computed as PCMP-001 §6.4 defines them. The set-point analysis
holds the other factors at their set-points, scans the factor across its range studied
at 201 points, and takes the contiguous range around the set-point over which the
mean prediction meets the criterion. The NOR-propagated analysis scans the factor
at 81 points. At each point it draws 2,000 settings of the other factors, each from
a normal distribution centred on its set-point with a standard deviation equal to the
width of its NOR divided by 6, clipped to the range studied. Each simulated batch
takes its own coefficients from their estimated distribution and its own residual. The
2.5th percentile of the simulated batches must not fall below a lower limit, and the
97.5th percentile must not exceed an upper limit. The NOR-propagated PAR is the
one that supports a claim.
The design space was evaluated on a grid of 11 evenly spaced points along each
response-surface factor, 14,641 points in all (Section 6). Capability at commercial
scale was estimated from the integrated model of the train, which runs 2,000
simulated batches through every step with each parameter drawn as in the NOR-
propagated analysis (PCMP-001 §6.5). Cpk is computed against the drug substance
criterion, as the lower of the two one-sided indices for a two-sided criterion. The
same simulation gives the probability that the drug substance falls outside its
criterion, as the count of simulated batches outside it and as the tail probability of a
normal distribution with the simulated mean and standard deviation.
Each univariate parameter was judged on the difference between the mean of its two
runs at the high end and the mean of its two runs at the low end of the range studied,
against the pooled standard deviation of the duplicate runs (8 degrees of freedom),
with a t-test at α = 0.05. This test is the report’s own approach. PCP-003 §6.4 states
what follows from a univariate effect but gives no test, and α = 0.05 is the level it
sets for model terms. The duplicate runs that the test needs are the discrepancy PD-
003-05. The results of the osmolality study were fitted by a straight line in osmolality,
coded −1, 0 and +1 at the NOR low, the set-point and the NOR high. Each level
was judged on the mean and on the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch at
manufacturing scale.

The screening study S-BRX-SCR varied the five multivariate parameters in a resolu-

actions of three factors or more. The runs were made in randomized order under
SOP-1002. The design carries no squared term, so it can detect curvature but cannot
attribute it to a factor. Appendix A gives the settings and the results.

forward (DEV-003-03). Appendix B gives the settings and the results.

5.3.4 What the augmentation changed

The 16 augmentation runs were added after the results of the planned runs were
known, and every selected model is fitted to them. Their pCO2 settings rest on a
probe whose calibration status the record does not give (DEV-003-08, equipment not

results.

fit test and the limit on overfitting for every attribute. Their NOR-propagated PARs

the design space covers 63.6 %, and high mannose exceeds the limit on overfitting.
That every PAR contains the NOR, and that the design space covers 73.7 %, therefore
rests on the augmentation runs and on the model selection together.

and the augmentation runs are an independent one. Table 5.9 shows how well the

5.6 Mechanistic interpretation

The fall of galactosylation with culture duration is the direction expected from the de-
pletion of UDP-galactose and of the manganese cofactor of the galactosyltransferase
late in the culture (Section 2.1). The fall is much larger at the low end of the pH
range than at the high end. Culture pH acts on the same enzyme through the pH of
the Golgi, so an interaction was expected, but the study does not show which prop-
erty of the cell sets its size. Culture temperature raises galactosylation at high pH




                                                  43
and has no effect at low pH, and the study does not show why the effect depends on
pH.
Afucosylation also falls with culture duration at low pH, and hardly at high pH. The
mechanism of Section 2.1 predicts the opposite direction. A pool weighted toward
antibody made late in the culture, when GDP-fucose and the processing capacity of
the Golgi have declined, should carry more antibody without core fucose, not less.
The study does not explain the direction.
A higher pCO2 lowers galactosylation, afucosylation and acidic variants. For all three
this is the direction of a higher culture pH, while Section 2.1 expected a higher pCO2
to act like a lower culture pH, because dissolved carbon dioxide acidifies the cytosol.
Osmolality was held constant while pCO2 varied in these designs, so a rise of osmo-
lality does not explain it. The mechanism does not explain the direction of the pCO2
effects.
Acidic variants rise with culture temperature, the direction in which a higher tem-
perature speeds deamidation. They fall as culture pH rises, the opposite of what
deamidation alone would give. Section 2.1 stated that the net direction of a parame-
ter on acidic variants is not predictable from the chemistry, because sialylation and
glycation respond differently, and the study does not identify which route dominates.
The effect of the initial viable cell concentration on acidic variants is not accounted
for by the mechanism either (Section 5.4).
Aggregate rises with culture pH and with culture duration, the directions expected
from the weaker repulsion between antibody molecules as the pH approaches the
isoelectric point and from the longer time at temperature (Section 2.1). On the log
scale the two effects multiply, so aggregate is highest after a long culture at high pH.
High mannose rises with culture pH and falls with culture temperature. A warmer
culture speeds the processing enzymes of the Golgi, which is consistent with less high
mannose, and the mechanism gives no direction for the pH effect.
For the operation of the step, the attribute nearest its limit is acidic variants at its
lower limit. A high culture pH, a high pCO2 and a low culture temperature bring
it there, and a high osmolality and a low initial viable cell concentration add to it.
Galactosylation approaches its upper limit at low pH, high temperature, short culture
and low pCO2, but at the least favourable NOR combination its prediction interval
stays 5.9 points below that limit. Routine operation is therefore limited by acidic
variants, and the control strategy acts on that corner (Section 10).

6 Design space

The design space of this step is the region of the four response-surface parameters
over which every modelled attribute meets its intermediate acceptance criterion. It
is judged on the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch, as PCMP-001 §6.2 requires.
A setting lies inside it when both ends of that interval lie within the criterion of every
attribute, at manufacturing scale. Osmolality and the four univariate parameters are
held at their set-points, so the design space is conditioned on them, and it is not
claimed beyond the ranges studied.
On the grid of 14,641 points, 73.7 % of the region studied lies inside the design space.
Acidic variants decide its boundary. They exclude 26.0 % of the grid points, galactosy-
lation 0.3 % and afucosylation 0.1 %, and high mannose and aggregate exclude none.
On the mean prediction in place of the single-batch interval, 91.7 % of the grid would
lie inside. A region drawn on mean predictions gives a single batch at its edge only
about an even chance of meeting the criterion (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009),
so it is shown here for comparison only. On the planned runs alone the design space
would cover 59.6 % (Section 5.3.4).
Figure 6.1 shows two planes of the design space in culture pH and pCO2, evaluated on
a grid of 41 by 41 settings within the NOR of the two parameters. In the first, culture
temperature and culture duration are at their set-points, and 3.1 % of that grid, at the

temperature is at its NOR low and culture duration at its NOR high, the settings least

7.1 Acceptance criteria

Each output is judged against its intermediate acceptance criterion (Table 7.1).
PCMP-001 §7.3 derives the criterion from the drug substance criterion by carrying
it backward through the worst case of every downstream step on the platform,
with no margin. Afucosylation, galactosylation and high mannose keep their drug
substance criteria, because no downstream step changes them. The upper limit of
acidic variants is carried back through the platform worst case of the increase across
low-pH viral inactivation, 1.00 points, to 39.0 %. In the low-pH response-surface
design every run at the same hold time gave the same acidic-variant result, so the full
quadratic model reproduces the results exactly, cannot be judged against PCMP-001
§7.2, and the platform value stands. The lower limit is carried back unchanged,
which assumes that no downstream step lowers acidic variants (PCP-003 §12).
The aggregate criterion is the drug substance limit multiplied by the aggregate clear-
ance factor of cation exchange, less the aggregate increase across low-pH viral inacti-
vation. From the platform values alone it is 9.30 %. Verification of the platform worst
cases changed both terms (PCMP-001 §7.5). The cation exchange model predicted
within its NOR a lowest clearance of 1.58-fold, worse than the platform worst case
of 2.00-fold. The one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch there, 1.40-fold,
replaced the platform value (PCR-007), which alone would set the criterion at 6.32
%. The low-pH viral inactivation model gave a bound of 0.56 points for the increase,
better than the platform value of 0.70 points, on a model that meets the first three
adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 (PCR-006). That bound replaced the platform
value too. The criterion in force is 6.46 %, stricter than the a priori value.
PCMP-001 §7.5 asks for the risk of the change. The net change is a tightening, so
more batches would fail at this step and fewer would reach the patient with too much
aggregate, and the risk moves toward the producer. The widening alone moves the
criterion 0.14 points back toward the patient. This report sets no in-process test
of aggregate at harvest, so an excursion beyond the criterion would be detected at
release, where aggregate is tested by SEC against the drug substance limit of 5.0
%. To meet that limit, cation exchange must clear at least 1.40-fold and low-pH viral
inactivation must add no more than 0.56 points. At the highest single-batch level that
the model of this step predicts within the NOR, 2.35 % at manufacturing scale, the
drug substance would then carry at most 2.07 %. None of the 2,000 batches of the
integrated model fell outside the aggregate criterion of the drug substance, which
puts the probability below 0.15 % at one-sided 95 % confidence (Section 8).
The widening rests on the bound of the low-pH viral inactivation model, from the 18
runs of its response-surface design. That model has predicted R² 0.004 below R² and



                                          47
a lack-of-fit p-value of 0.504, and it also meets the criterion on residuals and influen-
tial runs. PCR-006 gives the bound and states whether it depends on a single run, as
PCMP-001 §7.5 requires. The platform worst case is an increase over a hold of 180
min, while the NOR of the A-Mab hold as characterized ends at 120 min. At the edges
of the range studied, which include the longer hold, A-Mab added up to 0.84 points,
more than the platform value. The replacement therefore reflects the shorter hold
of A-Mab and not a more stable product. It holds for routine manufacture, which is
controlled within the hold-time NOR, and the NOR of hold time and temperature in
force ends at the same longest hold and highest temperature as the NOR as charac-
terized.
Without the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3, the verification would
have used the full quadratic models. The aggregate criterion would then be 6.19 %
and the upper limit of acidic variants 39.6 %. Judged against those criteria with the
models of this report, no conclusion over the NOR, no PAR and no qualification of the
scale-down model changes.

7.3 PARs

A PAR is a range of a parameter within which the attribute is shown by supportive
data to remain acceptable. ICH Q8(R2) defines it with the other parameters kept con-
stant (International Council for Harmonisation 2009), which is the set-point analysis.
The NOR-propagated analysis lets the other parameters vary across their NORs and
judges a single batch, so it is stricter, and it is the PAR that supports a claim (PCMP-
001 §6.4). Table 7.3 gives both for each of the four response-surface parameters and
each of the five attributes, from the selected model of the attribute with the offset
applied. Objective 3 requires a PAR where the selected model has a significant term
in the parameter, 16 of the 20 combinations. The other four, culture duration for high
mannose, pCO2 for high mannose, culture temperature for aggregate and pCO2 for
aggregate, have no term in the parameter and are given for information.
Every NOR-propagated PAR contains the NOR, and 18 span the whole range studied.
The exceptions are the PARs of culture pH and pCO2 for acidic variants. The PAR
of culture pH ends 0.075 pH units above the NOR high, and the PAR of pCO2 ends
1.5 mmHg above it. Measured in standard deviations of the routine variation of each
parameter, the width of its NOR divided by 6 (PCMP-001 §6.4), the margin is 2.25 for
culture pH and 0.15 for pCO2. On the planned runs alone these two PARs and that of
culture temperature do not contain the NOR (Section 5.3.4). In the set-point analysis
every PAR spans the range studied.
The range carried into the control strategy for each parameter is the intersection of
its required PARs (PCP-003 §7, PCMP-001 §7.6). It is pH 6.6–7.02 for culture pH, 34–
36 °C for culture temperature, 15–19 days for culture duration and 40–131 mmHg for
pCO2.

9 Parameter classification

The parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001
§7.7 (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). A parameter whose variation affects a
critical quality attribute within the range studied is a CPP when it has a high risk of
falling outside its acceptable range in operation. It is a WC-CPP when that risk is
low, because the parameter is readily controlled and its PAR is wide against its NOR.
A parameter that affects process performance and no critical quality attribute is a
KPP. A parameter with no relevant effect on either is a GPP. Neither PCMP-001 nor
SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold for a relevant effect or a wide PAR, so each class
below is argued from the effect sizes of Table 5.6 and from the margin between each
PAR and its NOR.

  • Culture pH is a WC-CPP. It changes all four critical attributes, and its effect on
    high mannose is large. Its NOR-propagated PARs for the critical attributes span
    the range studied, and its PAR for acidic variants ends 2.25 routine standard
    deviations above the NOR high. It is readily controlled by carbon dioxide and
    base addition.
  • Culture temperature is a WC-CPP. Its effects on afucosylation, galactosylation
    and high mannose are moderate, and its NOR-propagated PARs span the range
    studied for every attribute. It is readily controlled by the vessel jacket.
  • pCO2 is a WC-CPP. Its effect on galactosylation is moderate and on afucosylation
    small, and its NOR-propagated PARs for these attributes span the range studied.
    Its PAR for acidic variants ends 0.15 routine standard deviations above the NOR
    high. It is controlled by the sparge.
  • Osmolality is a WC-CPP. The screening showed it lowering galactosylation by
    2.50 points across its range, and the osmolality study showed it lowering afuco-
    sylation by 0.95 points across its NOR. It has no PAR from a model, so the wide
    PAR that PCMP-001 §7.7 names for a WC-CPP cannot be shown for it, and at its
    NOR high acidic variants fall below their limit at the least favourable combina-
    tion of the other parameters (Section 5.5). It is controlled through the base and
    feed additions.
  • Culture duration is a WC-CPP. Its effects on galactosylation and afucosylation
    are large and on aggregate moderate, and its NOR-propagated PARs span the
    range studied for every attribute. It is set by the day of harvest.
  • Dissolved oxygen is a KPP. No effect was detected on any attribute measured,
    at the precision of Table 5.11. The record holds no growth or titre result (DEV-
    003-05). Section 2.1 expects the culture not to be limited by oxygen across the
    range studied, which points to no effect on process performance either. The
    expectation and the class conflict, and the data of this study cannot resolve it.
  • The initial viable cell concentration is a KPP. It raises acidic variants, an attribute
    of very low criticality, by 3.51 points across its range, and no effect on a criti-



                                           57
    cal attribute was detected. The class rests on its role in growth and titre (Sec-
    tion 2.1), which the record does not hold. Its effect on acidic variants calls for
    the multivariate study recorded as DEV-003-07.
  • Basal medium concentration is a GPP. No effect was detected on any attribute
    measured, and the record holds no growth or titre result. PCP-003 §4.1 expects
    a higher basal medium concentration to raise growth and titre, which under
    PCMP-001 §7.7 points to a KPP. The expectation and the class conflict, and the
    data of this study cannot resolve it.
  • Nutrient feed-1 volume is a KPP. No effect was detected on any attribute mea-
    sured. The class rests on its role in the supply of glucose, amino acids and pre-
    cursors for growth and titre (Section 2.1), which the record does not hold. The
    feed under-delivery of DEV-003-02 left the feed within its NOR (Section 13.2).

10 Contribution to the control strategy

This step contributes the following elements to the control strategy of A-Mab.

  1. The five WC-CPPs are held within their NORs by the bioreactor control system.
     Culture pH is controlled by carbon dioxide and base addition, culture tempera-
     ture by the vessel jacket, pCO2 by the sparge, osmolality through the base and
     feed additions, and culture duration by the day of harvest. The ranges carried
     into the control strategy are those of Section 7.3.
  2. Alarm limits inside the NOR are set on the side that lowers acidic variants, the
     upper side for culture pH, for pCO2 and for osmolality. Their values are not in
     the record of this step.
  3. Acidic variants are measured at harvest. The measurement detects a low result
     before purification, but it cannot change the batch, and the record of this step
     gives no action limit for it.
  4. The KPPs are held within their NORs for process consistency. The delivered feed
     is checked by the mass reconciliation after the run, which detected DEV-003-02.
     The basal medium is prepared at its set-point under SOP-2004.
  5. The drug substance criteria of the attributes this step sets apply at release
     (PCMP-001 §3). The glycans, acidic variants and aggregate are tested there
     by the methods of Section 3.3.
  6. The host cell protein that this step releases into the harvest is cleared by Protein
     A, cation exchange and anion exchange, and is judged at each of those steps and
     at the drug substance. Residual DNA is cleared by the same steps and judged at
     the drug substance only (PCR-005, PCR-007 and PCR-008).
  7. Process performance qualification (PPQ) at the receiving site under PTP-001
     runs the process at its set-points. It confirms the process at scale near the centre
     of the NOR and does not test the edges of the ranges.

PCMR-001 consolidates these elements with those of the other steps into the control
strategy of the whole process.

11 Discussion

The conclusions of this report rest on the scale-down model, whose offsets are known
well enough that no conclusion over the NOR depends on their exact size. Four facts
limit how far that holds. The offset was estimated at the set-points only, so its use
toward the edges of the range studied is an assumption. The carbon dioxide stripping
of the commercial vessel was not matched, and osmolality was set independently of
pCO2 (Section 3.2). The growth and titre of the runs are not in the record, so the
model cannot be compared with manufacturing scale on process performance (DEV-
003-05). The difference in aggregate between the scales has no known cause (DEV-
003-09).
The following limitations apply to the conclusions of this report.

  1. The containment of the NOR by every PAR, and the size of the design space, rest
     on the 16 augmentation runs, added after the planned results were known, and
     on the model selection. On the planned runs alone three PARs for acidic variants
     do not contain the NOR.
  2. The models of galactosylation and acidic variants do not meet the criterion on
     residuals and influential runs, because their residual spread grows with the pre-
     dicted value. With that growth allowed for, the judgement over the NOR is the
     same.
  3. For acidic variants, 10 other models lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one
     and only culture pH, culture temperature, pCO2 and culture pH × culture tem-
     perature are common to all of them. The conclusion over the NOR is the same for
     each, but the other terms are not findings. At the set-point the model of acidic
     variants predicts above the mean of the centre points (Section 5.3.1).
  4. Osmolality has no PAR from a model, and its interactions with the other param-
     eters are known only from the screening design (DEV-003-03).
  5. The effect of the initial viable cell concentration on acidic variants comes from a
     univariate study, which cannot show its interactions, and the multivariate study
     that PCP-003 §6.4 requires has not been made (DEV-003-07). The capability does
     not include it.
  6. The pCO2 settings of the augmentation and osmolality studies rest on equipment
     whose calibration status is not recorded (DEV-003-08).
  7. The directions of the pCO2 effects and of the effect of culture duration on afuco-
     sylation are not the ones the mechanism of Section 2.1 gives, and the study does
     not explain them.
  8. The design space is not confirmed at commercial scale at its edges, and the
     capability comes from the integrated model, not from the models of this report.

12 Conclusions

1. The relationship of each parameter with each attribute was quantified (objective
   1). The selected response-surface models describe the five attributes over the
   ranges studied, and culture pH interacts with most of the other parameters.
2. The multivariate region was defined on the 95 % prediction interval of a single
   batch (objective 2). It covers 73.7 % of the region studied, 59.6 % on the planned
   runs alone, and acidic variants decide its boundary. The NOR does not lie entirely
   inside it (DEV-003-04).
3. A PAR was established for each response-surface parameter and each attribute
   whose selected model has a significant term in it, 16 in all (objective 3). On all
   runs every PAR contains the NOR, a result that rests on the augmentation runs
   and the model selection. Osmolality has no PAR from a model.
4. Each parameter was classified (objective 4). The step has five WC-CPPs, three
   KPPs and one GPP. The classes of dissolved oxygen, basal medium concentration
   and osmolality are not fully supported by the evidence of this study (Section 9).
5. The capability supports the control strategy of Section 10 (objective 5). The
   lowest Cpk of the drug substance is that of acidic variants, 1.05, between the
   minimum and the target of PCMP-001, and every other attribute that the step
   sets has a Cpk above the target.
6. The scale-down model is qualified for every output with a criterion.
7. Against the completion criteria of PCP-003 §7, the study is complete except for
   the operating region, which does not contain the NOR for acidic variants (DEV-
   003-04), for osmolality, which has no PAR from a model (DEV-003-03), and for
   the multivariate study of the initial viable cell concentration (DEV-003-07).
8. The results roll up into PCMR-001.

13.1 DEV-003-01, pCO2 probe drift during screening run 4

During screening run 4 the pCO2 probe of the scale-down system, EQ-BRX-205, read
6.5 mmHg higher than the offline reference, and the comparison of the two in in-
process monitoring detected the drift. The controller held the probe reading at the
design level of 160 mmHg, so the run ran at about 153.5 mmHg. The record gives
the cause as drift of the probe between two calibrations, and the equipment record
shows the probe in calibration at the time of execution.
The run is analysed at its recorded setting, a shift of 0.108 in coded units, in which 1
is the half-range of pCO2. With the run at its design setting instead, the set of signif-
icant effects is the same for every attribute. The residual of run 4 on the screening
model is at most 0.16 times the residual standard deviation for any attribute. With
run 4 excluded the screening model keeps two residual degrees of freedom. Fewer
effects then reach significance for galactosylation, high mannose, acidic variants and
aggregate, and for afucosylation the same number, but not the same set. The run
was retained. The record names screening run 4 only. The response-surface design
ran later on the same probe, and the record shows no recalibration between the two
designs, so it does not show whether the probe read high in those runs too.

A centre-point batch of the screening design received 4.2 % less nutrient feed-1 than

13.3 DEV-003-03, osmolality not carried into the
     response-surface design

The screening showed osmolality active on acidic variants and on galactosylation (Sec-
tion 5.2). PCP-003 §6.3 requires the factors carried into the response-surface design
to be those that the screening identifies as active, with the design amended before ex-
ecution if they differ from the planned subset. Osmolality was not carried forward and
the design was not amended. The departure was found in the Version 2.0 re-analysis
of the screening results. Osmolality had been left out because its range studied is
the narrowest relative to its NOR, against the rule of the plan. Every prediction, PAR
and the design space of this report are therefore conditioned on osmolality at its set-
point. The osmolality study measured the effect of osmolality at the combination of
NOR settings least favourable for acidic variants (PD-003-01, Section 5.5), and its
interactions with the other parameters are known from the screening design only.



13.4 DEV-003-04, NOR not narrowed for acidic variants

Acidic variants do not meet their criterion over the NOR on the prediction interval of
a single batch (Section 7.2), and the NOR was not narrowed as PCP-003 §7 requires.
The departure was found in the Version 2.0 re-analysis against the criteria of PCMP-
001 Version 2.0. The narrowing rule was set aside by a risk decision, whose grounds
and mitigation Section 7.2 gives.

13.5 DEV-003-05, process outputs not in the study record

The viable cell density, the viability and the titre of the qualification and characteri-
zation runs are not in the study record. PCP-003 §5.4 requires the culture to be moni-
tored for viable cell density and viability, and §5.1 lists both, with the titre, among the
outputs of the qualification. The review of the study record against PCP-003 found
the gap. The monitoring records were kept in the scale-down system and were not
transferred to the study record, and they cannot be recovered. No criterion of this
report depends on these outputs. Their absence means that the classes of the KPPs
and the GPP rest on platform knowledge for process performance (Section 9), and
that the scale-down model was not compared with manufacturing scale on growth
and titre.



13.6 DEV-003-06, univariate study started before the
     response-surface study

The univariate study started on 2025-10-06, before the response-surface study started
on 2025-10-20. PCP-003 §11 places the univariate assessments after the response-
surface design. The departure was found in the review of the execution dates (INV-
001 finding 5), and its cause was the scheduling of the bioreactors. The sequence
exists so that the factors of the response-surface design are chosen from the screening
result. The screening study had ended on 2025-10-03, before either study started, and
the univariate parameters were assigned by RA-001 and were not candidates for the
response-surface design. The order of the two studies therefore affects no analysis,
and the data were retained.



13.7 DEV-003-07, initial viable cell concentration not carried
     into a multivariate study

The univariate study showed acidic variants 3.51 points higher at the high end of
the initial viable cell concentration than at the low end, at manufacturing scale (Sec-
tion 5.4). The mechanism of PCP-003 §4.3 does not account for the effect, so PCP-003
§6.4 carries the parameter into a multivariate study and amends the plan. That was
not done, because the univariate results were not analysed for this rule in Version
1.0. The departure was found in the Version 2.0 re-analysis of the univariate results.
With the parameter at its NOR low and the other parameters at their least favourable
NOR combination, the effect would take the predicted median of acidic variants to
about 17.69 %, below the lower limit, and the lower prediction limit to 15.55 % (Sec-
tion 5.4). The judgement over the NOR and the capability of this report do not include
the effect.

its offset, a ratio of 0.917 with a 90 % confidence interval of 0.873 to 0.963, and the
model is qualified for it (Table 3.1). The cause of the difference is not known.

13.10 PD-003-01, osmolality study

The osmolality study (Section 4.5) showed that osmolality at its NOR high takes the
mean of acidic variants at the least favourable NOR combination below the lower limit,
which added an osmolality alarm to the mitigation of Section 7.2. It also showed that
a higher osmolality lowers afucosylation (Section 5.5).



13.11 PD-003-02, augmentation of the response-surface
      design

PCP-003 planned the 28 runs of the face-centred design. The 16 runs added under
INV-002 changed the selected models of four attributes, and the models selected on
the planned runs predict them poorly for high mannose and aggregate. With the
selected models on the planned runs alone, the PARs of culture pH, culture tempera-
ture and pCO2 for acidic variants would not contain the NOR, and the design space
would cover 59.6 % instead of 73.7 % (Section 5.3.4). The full quadratic model of
high mannose still exceeds the limit on overfitting on all runs.



13.12 PD-003-03, model selection

With the plan’s full quadratic model in place of the selected models, on all 44 runs,
high mannose would have no adequate model, the PAR of pCO2 for acidic variants
would end at 122 mmHg, inside the NOR, and the design space would cover 63.6
%. The selection also changes the criteria of this step through the verification of the
downstream steps (Section 7.1).




                                          70
13.13 PD-003-04, models that do not meet the criterion on
      residuals and influential runs

The selected models of galactosylation and acidic variants were used although the
spread of their residuals grows with the predicted value. No run of any model has
a Cook’s distance above the median of F(p, n − p). With the standard deviation of
the half of the predicted values in which each prediction lies, the judgement over the
NOR is the same for both attributes (Section 5.3.2).



13.14 PD-003-05, duplicate univariate runs

The duplicate runs give the pooled standard deviation of the study, with 8 degrees of
freedom, against which each univariate difference was tested, including the effect of
the initial viable cell concentration on acidic variants (Section 5.4).



13.15 PD-003-06, setting of osmolality

Because osmolality was set independently of pCO2, the screening design and the
osmolality study estimate the two effects separately, and the PAR of pCO2 does not
include the rise of osmolality that comes with pCO2 at scale (Section 7.3).
