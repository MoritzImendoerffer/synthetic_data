Executive summary

This report gives the results of the Stage 1 characterization of the production bioreac-
tor, Step 3 of the A-Mab drug substance process. The production bioreactor is a fed-
batch culture with a working volume of 15,000 L at commercial scale, and it forms the
glycan, charge variant and aggregate quality attributes of A-Mab. The studies were
run in a qualified scale-down model under the Process Characterization Plan PCP-003
and the Process Characterization Master Plan PCMP-001, following ICH Q8(R2), ICH
Q11 and the A-Mab case study (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012;
CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). This is Version 2.0 of the report. It replaces
Version 1.0 after the QA investigations INV-001 and INV-002 (Section 1.4).
The scale-down model was compared with manufacturing scale in three runs at the
set-points against five manufacturing-scale batches. A screening design of 19 runs
varied the five parameters assigned to multivariate study. A face-centred response-
surface design of 28 runs then varied four of them, and INV-002 added 16 runs to
it as a second block. A univariate study varied the other four parameters one at
a time. During the Version 2.0 re-analysis an osmolality study measured the effect
of osmolality with the other parameters at the combination of NOR settings least
favourable for acidic variants.
The response-surface model of each of the five modelled attributes was selected on all
44 runs, as corrective action CA3 of INV-002 requires. Every selected model meets
the model F-test, the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting of PCMP-001 §7.2.
None meets the criterion on residual patterns and influential runs. An influence anal-
ysis showed that no conclusion over the normal operating range (NOR) depends on a
single flagged run.
The intermediate acceptance criteria are met for afucosylation, galactosylation, high
mannose and aggregate at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high,
judged on the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch at manufacturing scale. They
are not met for acidic variants. At nine of the 81 combinations, all with pCO2 at
its NOR high, the prediction interval reaches below the lower limit of 18.0 %. At
the least favourable combination the predicted median is 18.4 % and the interval
reaches down to 16.2 %. With osmolality at its NOR high as well, the mean of the
osmolality study at that combination is 17.7 %. The NOR was not narrowed as PCP-
003 requires (DEV-003-04). The risk was accepted as the producer’s risk. Acidic
variants are monitored at harvest, and alarm limits inside the NOR are set for culture
pH, pCO2 and osmolality.
Each of the 20 proven acceptable ranges (PARs) computed with the other parameters
varying across their NORs contains the NOR, and 18 of them span the whole range
studied. The exceptions are the PARs of culture pH and pCO2 for acidic variants,
which end 0.075 pH units and 1.5 mmHg beyond the NOR high. On the single-batch

limit 73.7 % of the region studied meets every criterion, and acidic variants decide
the boundary.
The five multivariate parameters were classified as well-controlled critical process pa-
rameters (WC-CPPs). Dissolved oxygen, initial viable cell concentration and nutrient
feed-1 volume were classified as key process parameters (KPPs), and basal medium
concentration as a general process parameter (GPP). In the integrated model of the

The record of the step carries six deviations and six discrepancies from the plan (Sec-

§6.4 then carries the parameter into a multivariate study, and that study has not
been made (Section 5.4). The results roll up into the Process Characterization Mas-

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose main mechanism of action
is antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). It is produced by a recombinant
Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell line and purified by the platform train of Protein
A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small-virus
retentive filtration and ultrafiltration and diafiltration (UF/DF) (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009).
The production bioreactor is Step 3 of the drug substance process. It is a fed-batch
culture with a working volume of 15,000 L at commercial scale. The cells secrete
A-Mab into the medium over a culture of 17 days at the set-point, and the culture
fluid passes to harvest and clarification (Step 4) at the end of the run. The production
bioreactor is the only step of the A-Mab drug substance process at which product
quality attributes are formed. The glycan distribution is completed in the Golgi before
the antibody is secreted, and none of the purification steps separates or changes the
neutral glycoforms. The charge variant distribution is established inside the cell and
in the culture fluid, and the purification steps do not separate charge variants when
they are operated under platform conditions (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
The culture also sets the amount of host cell protein and DNA that the purification
steps have to clear.
This report gives the results of the studies planned in PCP-003. It applies the strategy,
the statistical methods and the acceptance criteria of PCMP-001 Version 2.0, and it
rolls up into PCMR-001. The risk assessment RA-001 set the scope of the studies.
The characterization supports the transfer of the process from the development site
in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI under the
Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. Table 1.1 lists these documents.

each.

  1. To quantify the relationship between each studied parameter and each of the
     five modelled responses over the ranges studied, and to identify the interactions
     between parameters.
  2. To define the multivariate region of the response-surface parameters over which

  3. To establish a PAR for each parameter against each attribute whose fitted model

1.3 Regulatory and scientific basis

The studies are Stage 1 process design studies in the lifecycle approach to process
validation (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). They follow the enhanced
approach of ICH Q8(R2) and ICH Q11. Prior knowledge and risk assessment iden-
tify the parameters that may affect an attribute, and designed studies then measure
and bound those effects (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012). ICH
Q8(R2) defines the design space and the proven acceptable range, and it treats the
criticality of an attribute as a continuum. The risk tools follow ICH Q9 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2023). The A-Mab case study supplies the process model,
the framework of quality attributes and the decision logic of parameter classification
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The designs and their analysis follow standard
response-surface methods (Montgomery 2017). The model selection of INV-002 uses
the Box-Cox transformation and Akaike’s information criterion with the small-sample

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The platform on which A-Mab is produced was used for the humanized IgG1 antibod-
ies X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab and for the A-Mab clinical and engineering campaigns
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Cell line construction, basal medium, feed strat-
egy and the control of pH, temperature and dissolved oxygen are common to these
products. The directions of the effects seen on the platform are carried into this study
as expectations. Their sizes are not, and the fitted models of this study give them.
The glycan attributes are set by the enzymes of the Golgi. Core fucose is added by
a fucosyltransferase from the donor GDP-fucose. The fraction of antibody secreted
without core fucose therefore depends on the activity of that enzyme, on the supply
of the donor and on the time the glycan spends in the Golgi. Terminal galactose is
added by a galactosyltransferase from UDP-galactose. The donor and the manganese
cofactor of the enzyme are depleted late in a fed-batch culture, so galactosylation
is expected to fall as the culture ages. High mannose glycans remain when transit
through the Golgi is fast relative to the processing enzymes, or when those enzymes
are inhibited. The harvested pool contains everything secreted during the run, so
a longer culture weights it toward antibody made late, under depleted conditions.
Culture pH, temperature, pCO2 and osmolality act on the same enzymes and donor
pools through the growth rate and the intracellular pH. Their effects on the glycans
are therefore expected to interact.
Aggregate forms after secretion, while the antibody sits in the culture fluid at culture
temperature. A longer culture raises it through the time at temperature. A culture
pH closer to the isoelectric point raises it because the net charge of the antibody
falls and the repulsion between molecules weakens. Acidic variants are mainly prod-
ucts of asparagine deamidation, together with sialylated glycans and glycated lysines.
Temperature and pH set the rate of deamidation, and pCO2 and osmolality act on the
intracellular pH and on sialylation. These routes do not respond to a parameter in
the same direction, so the net direction of each parameter on acidic variants is not
predictable from the chemistry and is read from the data. Host cell protein and DNA
are released when cells die and lyse, so their amount in the harvest is expected to
rise with the culture duration.
Among the parameters, culture duration is expected to set the age-dependent gly-
coforms and the impurity load most directly. Dissolved carbon dioxide acidifies the
cytosol, so pCO2 is expected to act on the Golgi enzymes in the same direction as a
lower culture pH. Dissolved oxygen supplies the oxidative metabolism of the cell, and




                                          13
the culture is not expected to be limited by oxygen across the range studied. The ini-
tial viable cell concentration sets where on the growth curve the culture starts. It is
expected to change the integral of viable cells and the titre more than any quality at-
tribute, because the cells themselves are the same. The basal medium concentration
and the nutrient feed-1 volume set the supply of glucose, amino acids and nucleotide
sugar precursors, and across the ranges studied the feed replenishes those compo-
nents faster than the cells use them. None of these four parameters is therefore
expected to change a glycan attribute at a fixed culture age.



2.2 Quality attributes in scope

Table 2.1 lists the seven quality attributes that the drug substance CQA register as-
signs to the production bioreactor. Criticality was assigned in RA-001 with Tool #1
of the A-Mab case study, whose score is the product of the impact of the attribute
and the uncertainty in that impact (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The levels
run from very low (VL) to very high (VH), and the attributes of high (H) and very
high criticality are called critical. For this step the critical attributes are afucosyla-
tion, galactosylation, high mannose and aggregate. Host cell protein has moderate

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

The risk assessment RA-001 ranked every parameter of the step before characteriza-
tion began, on its potential impact on a quality attribute and its potential to interact
with other parameters (International Council for Harmonisation 2023). Parameters
ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to multivariate study, and
the others to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). For the production bioreactor five of
the nine parameters were assigned to multivariate study, namely culture pH, culture
temperature, CO2 partial pressure (pCO2), osmolality and culture duration. Each of
them changes the pH of the Golgi or the size of the nucleotide sugar pools. The effect
of one is therefore expected to depend on the levels of the others, and only a design
that varies them together can estimate that dependence. Dissolved oxygen, initial vi-
able cell concentration, basal medium concentration and nutrient feed-1 volume were
assigned to univariate study, for the reason given in Section 2.1.
The range studied of every parameter extends beyond its NOR at both ends, and it
is between 1.5 and 2.5 times as wide as the NOR. The wider range shows how each
attribute responds beyond the edges of routine operation, and it sets the knowledge
space within which a PAR can be claimed. No PAR is claimed beyond the range stud-
ied.

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies were run in bench-scale stirred-tank bioreactors of the same design class
as the commercial production vessel, with the same control scheme for pH, temper-
ature, dissolved oxygen and nutrient addition. The scale-down model was operated
under the bioreactor procedure SOP-2003. Parameters that do not depend on scale
were run at the commercial set-points. Parameters that depend on scale, such as the
agitation rate, the gas flow rates and the working volume, were set so that the small
scale matched the performance of the full scale (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
pCO2 is the exception. A large vessel strips carbon dioxide less efficiently than a
bench-scale vessel, so pCO2 was controlled to the level of each run and studied as a
factor, over a range that spans the levels the commercial vessel reaches. The carbon
dioxide stripping of the commercial vessel was therefore not matched, a departure
from PCMP-001 §5 that PCP-003 §5.1 states. The pCO2 of each run was measured by
the probe of the scale-down bioreactor system, EQ-BRX-205.
The qualification study S-BRX-SDM (2025-08-11 to 2025-09-05) followed the scale-
down model procedure SOP-1001. It compared three runs of the scale-down model
at the set-points with five manufacturing-scale batches of the A-Mab engineering and
clinical campaigns. A scale-down model need not reproduce the manufacturing mean,
provided the difference is known well enough that no conclusion depends on its exact
size (PCMP-001 §7.1). The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs
minus the mean of the manufacturing batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence
interval. For an output modelled on the log scale (Section 3.5) the offset is the ratio
of the geometric means, and a scale-down value is divided by it. Every result in
this report that is judged against a criterion has the offset applied, so ranges and
conclusions are stated at manufacturing scale. The model is qualified for an output
when the conclusion over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its
interval.
Table 3.1 gives the offsets. The scale-down model is qualified for every output with a
criterion. For acidic variants the conclusion over the NOR is that the criterion is not
met, with the offset at its estimate and at either end of its interval, so that conclusion
does not depend on the offset either. Only for aggregate does the interval exclude a
ratio of 1. The scale-down model gives about 8 % less aggregate than manufacturing
scale, and the study did not investigate the cause.

2025-09-08. PCP-003 Version 1.0 qualified the model by a test of no difference be-
tween the scales. The offsets of Table 3.1 follow the rule of PCMP-001 Version 2.0

3.2 Cell line, media and culture operation

3.5 Statistical methods

The designs were generated, randomized and analysed under SOP-1002, with the
methods of PCMP-001 §6 (Montgomery 2017). Each factor was coded so that its set-
point is 0 and the ends of its range studied are −1 and +1. For this step the set-point
of every factor of the designs lies at the centre of its range studied, so the centre
points of the designs are at the set-points.
The screening data were fitted by least squares to the five main effects and the ten
two-factor interactions. An effect is twice the coded coefficient, the change in the
response across the full range studied. The model has 16 terms with the intercept in
19 runs. It leaves one degree of freedom for lack of fit and two for pure error. With
so few, R² overstates how well the model would predict new runs, so the screening
analysis was read only to identify active factors and interactions. Its lack-of-fit test




                                                 19
compares the mean of the centre points with the mean of the factorial runs, which is
the test for curvature.
The response-surface data were analysed by the model selection of INV-002 corrective
action CA3, adopted on 2026-09-15. PCP-003 prescribes the full quadratic model of
each output, with 14 terms besides the intercept, and the selection is recorded as the
discrepancy PD-003-03. It has three steps.

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic model gives the exponent
     λ that best makes the residuals normal with constant variance, with its 95 %
     interval (Box and Cox 1964). An output is modelled on the log scale where that
     interval contains 0, the log, and excludes 1, the raw scale. It is modelled on the
     raw scale otherwise.
  2. The model. Among all 1,337 submodels of the full quadratic that keep hierarchy,
     the model with the lowest AICc is taken. A submodel keeps hierarchy when it
     contains an interaction only with both its main effects, and a square only with
     its main effect.
  3. The check. The selected model is judged against the criteria of PCP-003 §7, listed
     below.

AICc estimates how well a model would predict new runs. It rewards a smaller resid-
ual and penalises every estimated term, and its small-sample correction raises the
penalty when the runs are few for the number of terms, as in these designs (Hurvich
and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002). The residual variance is counted as one
of the estimated parameters. Every candidate was fitted and the lowest AICc taken.
A stepwise search changes one term at a time and stops when no single change low-
ers AICc, so it can stop where only a combination of changes would lower it further.
On the data of this step a stepwise search by AICc, backward or forward, would have
stopped at a model with a higher AICc for acidic variants and aggregate. INV-002
also refitted each failing planned model by hierarchical backward elimination, to sim-
ulate the augmentation. That refit is stepwise, tests each term against zero instead
of comparing models, and uses the raw scale only. It is not the model used in this
report.
The models were fitted to all 44 runs, the 28 planned runs and the 16 augmentation
runs, with a block term coded −1 for the planned runs and +1 for the augmentation.
A prediction is made with the block term at 0, the average of the two blocks.
Of the modelled attributes, high mannose, acidic variants and aggregate are mod-
elled on the log scale. On that scale the model reads ln(y) = b0 + b1·x1 + … + e.
Transformed back, y = eb0 · eb1·x1 · … · ee , so each parameter multiplies the output
by a fixed factor, e2b from the low to the high end of its range. The error between
runs multiplies the output too, so its size in percentage points grows with the level
of the output. For high mannose and acidic variants this is expected, because the
precision of the glycan map and of icIEF is a percentage of the level (Appendix D).
For aggregate the profile supports the log scale too, although the precision of SEC
is an absolute amount. Least squares, and the tests and prediction intervals built on
it, assume residuals that are independent and normally distributed with mean zero
and the same variance over the whole range studied. On the raw scale, the residual
standard deviation of these outputs in the upper half of the predicted values is 1.24



                                         20
to 2.22 times that in the lower half. On the log scale the ratio is 0.81 to 1.88. For
acidic variants the spread still grows on the log scale, and Section 5.3.2 gives its size
against the criterion.
On the log scale the criterion stays in its own unit, percent. The effect of a parameter
is a fold change, and the scale-down offset is a ratio. A prediction interval is calculated
on the log scale and transformed back. It keeps its 95 % coverage, because the
probability that ln y lies between two values is the probability that y lies between
their antilogs. It is asymmetric in percentage points, further above the predicted
value than below it. At the least favourable NOR combination for acidic variants,
for example, the interval runs 2.15 points below the predicted value and 2.43 points
above it. The predicted value of a log-scale model is the median of the output, its
geometric mean, and not its arithmetic mean. The prediction limits, which decide
the judgement against a criterion, are not affected by that.
A selected model is adequate for prediction under PCP-003 §7 when all of the following
hold. The first four restate the criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2.

  a. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  b. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  c. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. Predicted R² comes from the
     prediction error sum of squares (PRESS).
  d. The residuals show no pattern against the predicted values or the run order, and
     no single run dominates the fit. A regression of the absolute residuals on the
     predicted values, and a regression of the residuals on the run order, each show
     no slope significant at α = 0.05. No run has a Cook’s distance above 4/n, which is
     0.091 for the 44 runs of the models. The augmentation runs continue the order
     of the planned runs.
  e. The normal quantile plot of the residuals shows no departure from normality.

Where a selected model does not meet criterion (d), the conclusions rest on an in-
fluence analysis, recorded as the discrepancy PD-003-04. Each run with a Cook’s
distance above the limit was deleted in turn, the model selection was repeated on the
remaining runs, and the output was judged over the NOR again. The size of each sig-
nificant residual trend was compared with the criterion of its output. The limit of 4/n
is a screening rule. It flags a run or two in most correctly specified models of a design
this size, so whether a conclusion depends on a flagged run is tested by deleting the
run. Cook’s distance already combines the residual of a run with its leverage.
Other models may lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one and fit the data almost
as well (Burnham and Anderson 2002). They were computed for every output. The
selected model decides, and the others are a sensitivity analysis. A feature that only
some of them carry is not reported as a finding.
Each output is judged against its intermediate acceptance criterion at manufactur-
ing scale. A quality attribute is judged on the exact 95 % prediction interval of a
single run at each of the 81 combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the
four response-surface factors, with the offset applied. The interval comes from the
t distribution and includes the uncertainty of the coefficients and the residual. It is




                                            21
two-sided, so each of its ends is a one-sided 97.5 % limit. A two-sided criterion is
judged at both ends.
The PARs were computed as PCMP-001 §6.4 defines them. The set-point analysis
holds the other factors at their set-points, scans the factor across its range studied
at 201 points, and takes the contiguous range around the set-point over which the
mean prediction meets the criterion. The NOR-propagated analysis scans the factor
at 81 points. At each point it draws 2,000 settings of the other factors, each from
a normal distribution centred on its set-point with a standard deviation equal to the
width of its NOR divided by 6, clipped to the range studied. Each simulated batch
takes its own coefficients from their estimated distribution and its own residual. The
2.5th percentile of the simulated batches must not fall below a lower limit, and the
97.5th percentile must not exceed an upper limit. The NOR-propagated PAR is the
one that supports a claim.
The design space was evaluated on a grid of 11 evenly spaced points along each
response-surface factor, 14,641 points in all (Section 6). Capability at commercial
scale was estimated from the integrated model of the train, which runs 2,000
simulated batches through every step with each parameter drawn as in the NOR-
propagated analysis (PCMP-001 §6.5). Cpk is computed against the drug substance
criterion, as the lower of the two one-sided indices for a two-sided criterion. The
same simulation gives the probability that the drug substance falls outside its
criterion, as the count of simulated batches outside it and as the tail probability of a
normal distribution with the simulated mean and standard deviation.
Each univariate parameter was judged on the difference between the mean of its two
runs at the high end and the mean of its two runs at the low end of the range studied.
The difference was tested against the pooled standard deviation of the duplicate runs,
with 8 degrees of freedom, at α = 0.05, the level PCP-003 sets for model terms. The
results of the osmolality study were fitted by a straight line in osmolality, coded −1, 0
and +1 at the NOR low, the set-point and the NOR high. Each level was judged on the
mean and on the 95 % prediction interval of a single batch at manufacturing scale.

The screening study S-BRX-SCR varied the five multivariate parameters in a resolu-

actions of three factors or more. The runs were made in randomized order under
SOP-1002. The design carries no squared term, so it can detect curvature but cannot
attribute it to a factor. Appendix A gives the settings and the results.



4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study S-BRX-RSM varied culture pH, culture temperature, cul-

levels. Osmolality was held at its set-point in every run. PCP-003 §6.3 planned this

the screening identifies as active, with the design amended before execution if the
subset differs. The screening identified osmolality as active (Section 5.2), and osmo-

results.

of at least 0.90 that the full quadratic models of high mannose and acidic variants

to augmentation was decided on 2026-09-02, before any further run was made, and

5.3.3 Effects of the parameters

The coefficients of the selected models are in coded units, so each measures the
change from the set-point to the end of the range of its factor. For a raw-scale model
the change across the full range of a factor is twice its coefficient. For a log-scale
model the output is multiplied by e2b across the range. Appendix C gives the co-
efficient tables of afucosylation, galactosylation, high mannose and aggregate, and
Table 5.6 and Table 5.7 those of acidic variants, the attribute nearest its limit.
Afucosylation falls as culture duration, culture pH, culture temperature and pCO2
rise. The interaction of culture pH with culture duration is as large as the main effect
of culture duration and of opposite sign. At the low end of the pH range a longer
culture lowers afucosylation by 5.43 points across the range of culture duration, and
at the high end of the pH range it changes it by 0.03 points. The squared term of
culture pH is negative, so with the other factors at their set-points afucosylation is
highest inside the pH range and falls toward both ends.
Galactosylation falls with culture duration, pCO2 and culture pH, and rises with cul-
ture temperature. Culture pH modifies each of these effects. Across the range of
culture duration galactosylation falls by 12.7 points at the low end of the pH range
and by 2.3 points at the high end. Across the range of pCO2 it falls by 8.7 and 0.6
points. Across the range of culture temperature it rises by 0.1 points at the low end
of the pH range and by 7.6 points at the high end.
High mannose rises with culture pH, by a factor of 1.42 across its range, and falls
with culture temperature, by a factor of 0.88. The small interaction of the two is
significant. Neither culture duration nor pCO2 is in the model.
Acidic variants fall with pCO2, by a factor of 0.68 across its range, and with culture
pH, by a factor of 0.81. They rise with culture temperature, by a factor of 1.18. The
main effect of culture duration is a factor of 0.99 and is not significant (p = 0.583). It
stays in the model because the interaction of culture pH with culture duration does.
The interaction of culture pH with culture temperature is significant. Of the terms
of this model, only culture pH, culture temperature, pCO2 and culture pH × culture
temperature are carried by every model within 2 AICc units of it (Section 5.3.5), so
the other terms are not reported as findings.
Aggregate rises with culture pH, by a factor of 1.79 across its range, and with culture
duration, by a factor of 1.59. The interaction of the two is negative on the log scale,
so the culture duration multiplies aggregate by less at high pH than at low pH. The
squared term of culture pH is negative, so the rise with pH flattens toward the top of
the range. Neither culture temperature nor pCO2 is in the model.

5.4 Univariate assessment

Table 5.9 gives every result of the univariate study for the five attributes at manufac-
turing scale, and Table 5.10 gives the difference between the two ends of each range
as measured, for host cell protein and DNA in the clarified harvest as well. Every
result of the five attributes meets its criterion at manufacturing scale. The half-width
of the 95 % confidence interval in Table 5.10 is the precision of each comparison. For
galactosylation, for example, a difference smaller than 3.1 points between the two
ends of a range would not have been detected.
The difference is significant at α = 0.05 for a single parameter and attribute. Acidic
variants are 3.42 points higher at the high end of the initial viable cell concentration
than at the low end, with a 95 % confidence interval of ± 2.30 points (p = 0.009).
No effect of dissolved oxygen, basal medium concentration or nutrient feed-1 volume
was detected on any attribute, at the precision given in Table 5.10, and no effect of
the initial viable cell concentration was detected on any other attribute.
PCP-003 §6.4 carries a univariate parameter into a multivariate study when it shows
an effect on a quality attribute that the mechanism of Section 2.1 does not account
for. That mechanism has the initial viable cell concentration change the integral of
viable cells and the titre, and no quality attribute, because the cells are the same. One
route to acidic variants would be the time the antibody spends in the culture fluid at
culture temperature, since a culture that starts at a higher cell concentration makes
its antibody earlier. That route does not account for the effect. Culture duration sets
the time in the culture fluid most directly, and it has no detectable main effect on
acidic variants, in the screening design (p = 0.964) or in the response-surface model
(p = 0.583). The effect is therefore not accounted for by the mechanism. Under PCP-
003 §6.4 the initial viable cell concentration is to be carried into a multivariate study
and the plan amended. That study has not been made.
The direction of the effect matters for the lower limit of acidic variants. A straight
line between the two ends of the range puts acidic variants 0.68 points lower at the
NOR low of the initial viable cell concentration than at its set-point, with the other
parameters at their set-points. The response-surface and osmolality studies ran at
the set-point of the initial viable cell concentration, so the judgement at the least
favourable NOR combination in Section 7.2 does not include this effect.

5.6 Mechanistic interpretation

The fall of galactosylation with culture duration is the direction expected from the de-
pletion of UDP-galactose and of the manganese cofactor of the galactosyltransferase
late in the culture (Section 2.1). The fall is much larger at the low end of the pH
range than at the high end. Culture pH acts on the same enzyme through the pH of
the Golgi, so an interaction was expected, but the study does not show which property
of the cell sets its size. Afucosylation shows the same pattern. It falls with culture
duration at low pH and hardly at high pH, which is consistent with the harvested pool




                                                   41
being weighted toward antibody made late in the culture, when the donor supply and
the Golgi function have declined. Culture temperature raises galactosylation at high
pH and has no effect at low pH, and the study does not show why the effect depends
on pH.
pCO2 lowers galactosylation, afucosylation and acidic variants. For all three this is
the direction of a higher culture pH. Dissolved carbon dioxide acidifies the cytosol,
and Section 2.1 therefore expected pCO2 to act like a lower culture pH. The data do
not show that, and in these designs osmolality was held constant while pCO2 varied.
The mechanism of Section 2.1 does not explain the direction of the pCO2 effects, and
they are reported as observed.
Acidic variants rise with culture temperature, the direction in which temperature
speeds deamidation. They fall as culture pH rises, which is the opposite of what
deamidation alone would give. Section 2.1 stated that the net direction of a parame-
ter on acidic variants is not predictable from the chemistry, because sialylation and
glycation respond differently, and the study does not identify which route dominates.
The effect of the initial viable cell concentration on acidic variants is not accounted
for by the mechanism either (Section 5.4).
Aggregate rises with culture pH and with culture duration, the directions expected
from weaker repulsion between antibody molecules as the pH approaches the isoelec-
tric point and from the longer time at temperature (Section 2.1). On the log scale the
two effects multiply, so aggregate is highest after a long culture at high pH. High
mannose rises with culture pH and falls with culture temperature. A warmer cul-
ture speeds the processing enzymes of the Golgi, which is consistent with less high
mannose, and the direction of the pH effect is reported as observed.
For the operation of the step, the attribute nearest its limit is acidic variants at its
lower limit, approached at high culture pH, high pCO2 and low culture temperature,
with high osmolality and a low initial viable cell concentration adding to it. Galactosy-
lation approaches its upper limit at low pH, high temperature, short culture and low
pCO2, but at the least favourable NOR combination its prediction interval stays 5.9
points below that limit. Routine operation is therefore limited by acidic variants, and
the control strategy acts on that corner (Section 10).

6 Design space

The design space of this step is the region of the four response-surface parameters
over which every modelled attribute meets its intermediate acceptance criterion. It
is defined on the single-batch limit, as PCMP-001 §6.2 requires. A setting lies inside
it when both ends of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run lie within the cri-
terion of every attribute, at manufacturing scale. Osmolality and the four univariate
parameters are held at their set-points, so the design space is conditioned on them,
and it is not claimed beyond the ranges studied.
On the grid of 14,641 points, 73.7 % of the region studied lies inside the design space.
Acidic variants decide its boundary. They exclude 26.0 % of the grid points, galactosy-
lation 0.3 % and afucosylation 0.1 %, and high mannose and aggregate exclude none.
On the mean prediction in place of the single-batch limit, 91.7 % of the grid would
lie inside. A region drawn on mean predictions gives a single batch at its edge only
about an even chance of meeting the criterion (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009),
which is why it is used here for comparison only.
Figure 6.1 shows two planes of the design space in culture pH and pCO2. In the
first, culture temperature and culture duration are at their set-points, and the corner
of high pH and high pCO2 of the NOR lies outside the design space, 3.1 % of the
NOR of the two parameters. In the second, culture temperature is at its NOR low
and culture duration at its NOR high, the settings least favourable for acidic variants,
and 18.8 % of the NOR of culture pH and pCO2 lies outside. The NOR therefore does
not lie entirely inside the design space. PCP-003 §7 requires the NOR to be narrowed
until it fits inside the declared region, and the NOR was not narrowed (DEV-003-04,
Section 7.2).

7.1 Acceptance criteria

Each output is judged against its intermediate acceptance criterion (Table 7.1).
PCMP-001 §7.3 derives the criterion from the drug substance criterion by carrying
it backward through the worst case of every downstream step on the platform,
with no margin. Afucosylation, galactosylation and high mannose keep their drug
substance criteria, because no downstream step changes them. The upper limit of
acidic variants is carried back through the platform worst case of the increase across
low-pH viral inactivation, 1.00 points, to 39.0 %. The low-pH model of A-Mab gives
a smaller increase. Its results have no scatter and the model fits them exactly, so it
cannot meet PCMP-001 §7.2 and the platform value stands. The lower limit is carried
back unchanged, which assumes that no downstream step lowers acidic variants
(PCP-003 §12).
The aggregate criterion is the drug substance limit multiplied by the aggregate clear-
ance factor of cation exchange, less the aggregate increase across low-pH viral inacti-
vation. From the platform values alone it is 9.30 %. Verification of the platform worst
cases changed both terms (PCMP-001 §7.5). The cation exchange model predicted
within its NOR a lowest clearance of 1.58-fold, worse than the platform worst case
of 2.00-fold. The one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch there, 1.40-fold,
replaced the platform value (PCR-007), which alone would set the criterion at 6.32
%. The low-pH viral inactivation model gave a bound of 0.56 points for the increase,
better than the platform value of 0.70 points, on a model that meets the first three
adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 (PCR-006). That bound replaced the platform
value too. The criterion in force is 6.46 %, stricter than the a priori value.
The change is argued by its risk, as PCMP-001 §7.5 requires. The net change is a
tightening, which moves risk from the patient to the producer. The widening part
alone moves the criterion 0.14 points back toward the patient. This report sets no
in-process test of aggregate at harvest, so an excursion beyond the criterion would
be detected at release, where aggregate is tested by SEC against the drug substance
limit of 5.0 %. The downstream steps must then clear what the criterion assumes.
Cation exchange must clear at least 1.40-fold, and low-pH viral inactivation must add
no more than 0.56 points. At the highest single-batch level that the model of this
step predicts within the NOR, 2.35 % at manufacturing scale, the drug substance
would then carry at most 2.07 %. None of the 2,000 batches of the integrated model
fell outside the aggregate criterion of the drug substance, which puts the probability
below 0.15 % at one-sided 95 % confidence (Section 8).
The widening rests on the bound of the low-pH viral inactivation model, from the 18
runs of its response-surface design. That model has predicted R² 0.004 below R² and



                                          45
a lack-of-fit p-value of 0.504. PCMP-001 §7.5 leaves the fourth adequacy criterion, on
residuals and influential runs, to PCR-006, which gives the bound and states whether
it depends on a single run. The bound has a limit that the reader needs. The platform
worst case is an increase over a hold of 180 min, while the NOR of the A-Mab hold as
characterized ends at 120 min. At the edges of the range studied, which include the
longer hold, A-Mab added up to 0.84 points, more than the platform value. The re-
placement therefore reflects the shorter hold of A-Mab and not a more stable product.
The criterion remains valid because routine manufacture never holds longer than the
NOR, and the NOR of hold time and temperature in force ends at the same longest
hold and highest temperature as the NOR as characterized.
Without the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3, the verification would
have used the full quadratic models. The aggregate criterion would then be 6.19 %
and the upper limit of acidic variants 39.6 %. No conclusion over the NOR, no PAR
and no qualification of the scale-down model of this step changes between the two
sets of criteria.

so its Cpk of 15.7 mainly reflects that distance. Aggregate is judged instead by the
single-batch limits over the NOR of this step (Section 7.2) and of the steps after it.

9 Parameter classification

The parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001
§7.7 (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). A parameter whose variation affects a
critical quality attribute within the range studied is a CPP when it has a high risk of
falling outside its acceptable range in operation. It is a WC-CPP when that risk is
low, because the parameter is readily controlled and its PAR is wide against its NOR.
A parameter that affects process performance and no critical quality attribute is a
KPP. A parameter with no relevant effect on either is a GPP. Neither PCMP-001 nor
SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold for a relevant effect or a wide PAR, so each class
below is argued from the effect sizes and from the margin between each PAR and its
NOR. The effects on the critical attributes come from the response-surface models
(Section 5.3.3) and, for the univariate parameters, from Table 5.10.

  • Culture pH is a WC-CPP. It changes all four critical attributes, multiplying aggre-
    gate by 1.79 and high mannose by 1.42 across its range and lowering galactosy-
    lation by 4.1 points at the other factors’ set-points. Its NOR-propagated PARs for
    the critical attributes span the range studied. Its PAR for acidic variants ends
    0.075 pH units above the NOR high. Culture pH is readily controlled by carbon
    dioxide and base addition, and an alarm limit inside the NOR guards its upper
    side.
  • Culture temperature is a WC-CPP. It changes afucosylation, galactosylation and
    high mannose, and its NOR-propagated PARs span the range studied for every
    attribute. It is readily controlled by the vessel jacket.
  • pCO2 is a WC-CPP. It lowers galactosylation by 4.6 points and afucosylation
    by 0.9 points across its range at the other factors’ set-points, and its NOR-
    propagated PARs for these attributes span the range studied. Its PAR for acidic
    variants ends 1.5 mmHg above the NOR high. pCO2 is controlled by the sparge,
    and an alarm limit inside the NOR guards its upper side.
  • Osmolality is a WC-CPP. The screening showed it lowering galactosylation by
    2.45 points across its range, and the osmolality study showed it lowering afuco-
    sylation by 0.95 points across its NOR. It has no PAR from a model. The osmolal-
    ity study supports its NOR for the critical attributes at the combination tested.
    Osmolality is controlled through the base and feed additions, and an alarm limit
    inside the NOR guards its upper side.
  • Culture duration is a WC-CPP. It has the largest effect on galactosylation, 7.5
    points across its range at the other factors’ set-points, it lowers afucosylation,
    and it multiplies aggregate by 1.59. Its NOR-propagated PARs span the range
    studied for every attribute. It is set by the day of harvest.
  • Dissolved oxygen is a KPP. No effect was detected on any attribute measured,
    at the precision of Table 5.10. The study record holds no growth or titre result




                                          55
    (DEV-003-05), so the class rests on the role of dissolved oxygen in the oxidative
    metabolism of the cell (Section 2.1). It is controlled by sparging and agitation.
  • The initial viable cell concentration is a KPP. It raises acidic variants, an attribute
    of very low criticality, by 3.42 points across its range, and no effect on a criti-
    cal attribute was detected. The class rests on its role in growth and titre (Sec-
    tion 2.1), which the record does not hold. Its effect on acidic variants calls for

  • Basal medium concentration is a GPP. No effect was detected on any attribute
    measured. The record holds no growth or titre result, so the class rests on the

    cursors for growth and titre (Section 2.1), which the record does not hold. The
    feed under-delivery of DEV-003-02 left the feed within its NOR (Section 13.2).

No parameter was classified as a CPP in the strict sense. The five WC-CPPs are critical
process parameters, kept within their NORs by the control of the bioreactor, and the

10 Contribution to the control strategy

This step contributes the following elements to the control strategy of A-Mab.

  1. The five WC-CPPs are held within their NORs by the bioreactor control system.
     Culture pH is controlled by carbon dioxide and base addition, culture tempera-
     ture by the vessel jacket, pCO2 by the sparge, osmolality through the base and
     feed additions, and culture duration by the day of harvest.
  2. Alarm limits inside the NOR are set on the side that lowers acidic variants, the
     upper side for culture pH, for pCO2 and for osmolality, from the risk decision of
     Section 7.2.
  3. Acidic variants are measured at harvest. The measurement detects a low result
     early, but it is a weak control, because it cannot change the batch.
  4. The KPPs are held within their NORs for process consistency. The delivered feed
     is checked by the mass reconciliation after the run, which detected DEV-003-02.
     The basal medium is prepared at its set-point under SOP-2004.
  5. The drug substance criteria of the attributes this step sets apply at release
     (PCMP-001 §3). The glycans, acidic variants and aggregate are tested there
     by the methods of Section 3.3.
  6. The host cell protein and DNA that this step releases into the harvest are cleared
     by Protein A, cation exchange and anion exchange, and are judged at those steps
     and at the drug substance (PCR-005, PCR-007 and PCR-008).
  7. The ranges are confirmed at commercial scale during process performance qual-
     ification (PPQ) at the receiving site under PTP-001.

PCMR-001 consolidates these elements with those of the other steps into the control
strategy of the whole process.

11 Discussion

The studies show how the four response-surface parameters set the glycans, the
acidic variants and the aggregate of the harvest. Culture duration sets galactosy-
lation and afucosylation most strongly, culture pH changes the size of almost every
other effect, and pCO2 and culture pH lower acidic variants. The selected models
meet the model F-test, the lack-of-fit test and the limit on overfitting for every at-
tribute, and every attribute except acidic variants meets its criterion across the NOR
on the prediction interval of a single batch. Acidic variants fail where pCO2 is at its
NOR high together with a high culture pH, a low culture temperature or both, and
the risk of that failure was accepted.
Confidence in the scale-down model rests on its offsets. The offset of every attribute
is small and known well enough that no conclusion over the NOR depends on its exact
size, and the model is qualified for every output with a criterion. Only for aggregate
does the scale-down model differ clearly from manufacturing scale, giving about 8 %
less, and the conclusions apply that offset. Three limits remain. The offset was esti-
mated at the set-points only. The carbon dioxide stripping of the commercial vessel
was not matched, and osmolality was set independently of pCO2, so the rise of osmo-
lality with pCO2 at scale is not represented. The growth and titre of the runs are not
in the record, so the scale-down model cannot be compared with manufacturing scale
on process performance.
The deviations are resolved as follows. DEV-003-01 and DEV-003-02 concern sin-
gle runs and change no conclusion. DEV-003-03 left osmolality out of the response-
surface design, and the osmolality study measured its effect at one combination of
the other parameters. DEV-003-04 records the NOR that was not narrowed, under
the accepted risk. DEV-003-05 records the process outputs that are missing from the
record. DEV-003-06 records a change of sequence that affected no analysis.
The following limitations apply to the conclusions of this report.

  1. No selected model meets the criterion on residual patterns and influential runs.
     The conclusions rest on the influence analysis, which showed that none of them
     depends on a single flagged run.
  2. For acidic variants, 10 other models lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one,
     and only culture pH, culture temperature, pCO2 and culture pH × culture tem-
     perature are common to all of them. The conclusion over the NOR is the same
     for each, but the other terms are not findings.
  3. The osmolality study and the augmentation were decided after the results they
     answer were known. A conclusion that rests on them carries that limitation.
  4. Osmolality has no PAR from a model, and its interactions with the other param-
     eters are known only from the screening design.




                                          58
5. The effect of the initial viable cell concentration on acidic variants comes from
   a univariate study, which cannot show its interactions. The multivariate study
   that PCP-003 §6.4 requires has not been made, and until it is made the range of
   the parameter is supported only with the other parameters at their set-points.
6. The direction of the pCO2 effects is not the one that the mechanism of Section 2.1
   suggests, and the study does not explain it.
7. The design space is not confirmed at commercial scale at its edges, and the ca-
   pability does not include the variation that the commercial vessel adds between
   batches. Both are confirmed in Stage 2.

12 Conclusions

1. The relationship of each parameter with each attribute was quantified (objective
   1). The selected response-surface models describe the five attributes over the
   ranges studied, and culture pH interacts with most of the other parameters.
2. The multivariate region was defined on the single-batch limit (objective 2). It
   covers 73.7 % of the region studied, and acidic variants decide its boundary.
   The NOR does not lie entirely inside it (DEV-003-04).
3. A PAR was established for each response-surface parameter and each attribute
   (objective 3). All 20 NOR-propagated PARs contain the NOR, and the PARs of cul-
   ture pH and pCO2 for acidic variants end just beyond the NOR high. Osmolality
   has no PAR from a model.
4. Each parameter was classified (objective 4). The step has five WC-CPPs, three
   KPPs and one GPP.
5. The capability supports the Stage 2 ranges and the control strategy of Section 10
   (objective 5). The lowest Cpk of the drug substance is that of acidic variants,
   1.05, between the minimum and the target of PCMP-001. Every other attribute
   that the step sets has a Cpk above the target.
6. The scale-down model is qualified for every output with a criterion.
7. Against the completion criteria of PCP-003 §7, the study is complete except for
   the operating region, which does not contain the NOR for acidic variants (DEV-
   003-04), and for osmolality, which has no PAR from a model (DEV-003-03). The
   multivariate study of the initial viable cell concentration that PCP-003 §6.4 re-
   quires has not been made.
8. The results roll up into PCMR-001.

13.1 DEV-003-01, pCO2 probe drift during screening run 4

During screening run 4 the pCO2 probe of the scale-down system, EQ-BRX-205, read
6.5 mmHg away from the offline reference. The comparison of the probe with the
offline reference in in-process monitoring detected the drift. The controller holds the
probe reading at the design level, so the true pCO2 of the run differed from its design
level of 160 mmHg by that amount. The record gives the size of the drift but not its
sign, so the setting actually run is not known. The root cause was drift of the probe
between calibrations. The probe was in calibration at the time of execution.
The drift is 0.108 in coded units, in which 1 is the half-range of pCO2. The screening
model was refitted with run 4 at each of the two possible settings, 153.5 mmHg and
166.5 mmHg, and for every attribute the set of significant effects was the same as at
the design setting. The residual of run 4 on the screening model is at most 0.16 times
the residual standard deviation for any attribute. With run 4 excluded the screening
model keeps two residual degrees of freedom, and fewer effects reach significance.
The run was retained and analysed at its design setting. The response-surface design,
which carries the predictive models, was made later and is not affected.



13.2 DEV-003-02, nutrient feed-1 under-delivery on a
     centre-point batch

A centre-point batch of the screening design received 4.2 % less nutrient feed-1 than
its target, from lot LOT-FED-3120. The mass reconciliation after the run detected it,
and the root cause was a calibration offset of the feed pump. The batch received a feed
volume of about 11.5 % of working volume, inside the NOR of 10–14 % of working
volume and the range studied of 9–15 % of working volume. The univariate study
detected no effect of the nutrient feed-1 volume on any attribute across that range
(Section 5.4). The glycan results of the batch were verified by three determinations
under AMV-3010. With the precision of the method, three determinations give a 95
% confidence interval of ± 6.21 % of the result. The record does not say which of
the three screening centre points was affected, and Table 5.1 gives their spread. The
batch was retained.



13.3 DEV-003-03, osmolality not carried into the
     response-surface design

The screening showed osmolality active on acidic variants, and also on galactosylation
(Section 5.2). PCP-003 §6.3 requires the factors carried into the response-surface
design to be those that the screening identifies as active, with the design amended
before execution if they differ from the planned subset. Osmolality was not carried
forward and the design was not amended. The departure was found in the Version
2.0 re-analysis of the screening results. Osmolality had been left out because its




                                          62
range studied is the narrowest relative to its NOR, against the rule of the plan. As
a result every prediction, PAR and the design space of this report are conditioned
on osmolality at its set-point. The osmolality study measured the effect of osmolality
at the combination of NOR settings least favourable for acidic variants (PD-003-01,
Section 5.5). Its interactions with the other parameters are known from the screening
design only.



13.4 DEV-003-04, NOR not narrowed for acidic variants

Acidic variants fall below their lower limit at nine of the 81 combinations of NOR
low, set-point and NOR high, on the prediction interval of a single batch (Section 7.2).
PCP-003 §7 requires the NOR to be narrowed until it fits inside the declared operating
region. The NOR was not narrowed. The departure was found in the Version 2.0
re-analysis against the criteria of PCMP-001 Version 2.0. The narrowing rule was
set aside by a risk decision. The risk was accepted as the producer’s risk, with the
monitoring of acidic variants at harvest and alarm limits inside the NOR for culture
pH, pCO2 and osmolality, as Section 7.2 argues.



13.5 DEV-003-05, process outputs not in the study record

The viable cell density, the viability and the titre of the characterization runs are not
in the study record. PCP-003 §5.4 requires the culture to be monitored for viable
cell density and viability, and §5.1 lists both, with the titre, among the outputs of the
qualification. The review of the study record against PCP-003 in the Version 2.0 re-
analysis found the gap. The monitoring records were kept in the scale-down system
and were not transferred to the study record, and they cannot be recovered. The
qualification runs lack the same results. No criterion of this report depends on these
outputs. Their absence means that the classes of the KPPs and the GPP rest on plat-
form knowledge for process performance (Section 9), and that the scale-down model
was not compared with manufacturing scale on growth and titre.

13.7 PD-003-01, osmolality study

PCP-003 kept osmolality in the screening design only. The osmolality study ran os-
molality at three levels at the combination of NOR settings least favourable for acidic
variants, with four runs at each level (Section 4.5). It was made because the screen-
ing showed osmolality lowering acidic variants, the attribute nearest its lower limit,
and narrowing the osmolality NOR to its set-point was not accepted. It showed that
osmolality at its NOR high takes the mean of acidic variants at that combination below
the lower limit, and that osmolality lowers afucosylation (Section 5.5).



13.8 PD-003-02, augmentation of the response-surface
     design

PCP-003 planned the 28 runs of the face-centred design. INV-002 added 16 runs
as a second block, because the full quadratic models of high mannose and acidic
variants did not meet the limit on overfitting, and it calculated the number of runs.
The augmentation changed the selected models of four attributes and showed that the
models selected on the planned runs predict the new runs poorly for high mannose
and aggregate (Section 5.3.4). The full quadratic model of high mannose still exceeds
the limit on overfitting on all runs.



13.9 PD-003-03, model selection

PCP-003 prescribes the full quadratic model of each output. Every output was anal-
ysed by the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3, a block term kept and
the offset taken on the scale of the model (Section 3.5). The full quadratic carries
terms that the data do not support, and selecting the model is standard practice. On
all runs the selection gives high mannose an adequate model that the full quadratic
would not, and it puts three attributes on the log scale. Without it the criteria of this
step would differ as Section 7.1 states, and no conclusion would change.




                                           67
13.10 PD-003-04, influence analysis

PCP-003 requires models that meet every adequacy criterion. No selected model of
this step meets the criterion on residual patterns and influential runs, so the conclu-
sions rest on an influence analysis (Section 3.5). The analysis changed no conclusion
(Section 5.3.2).



13.11 PD-003-05, duplicate univariate runs

PCP-003 §6.4 plans one run at each end of each univariate range. Two runs were made
at each end, to measure the variation between runs against which a univariate effect
is judged. Without the duplicates the effect of the initial viable cell concentration on
acidic variants could not have been tested.



13.12 PD-003-06, setting of osmolality

PCP-003 does not state how osmolality is brought to its design level when pCO2 also
varies. Osmolality was set with sodium chloride added with each feed, with pCO2
held at its level by the sparge, in the screening design and in the osmolality study.
The designs therefore estimate the effects of osmolality and pCO2 separately, and
the PAR of pCO2 does not include the rise of osmolality that comes with pCO2 at
scale (Section 7.3).
