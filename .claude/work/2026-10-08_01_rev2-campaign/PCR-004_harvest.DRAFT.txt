Executive summary

This report presents the process characterization of harvest and clarification, Step 4
of the A-Mab drug substance process, at Version 2.0. The study was planned in PCP-
004. At commercial scale the step clarifies the contents of a 15,000 L production
bioreactor by continuous disk-stack centrifugation, depth filtration and sterilizing-
grade filtration, and it delivers the clarified harvest to Protein A capture. The A-Mab
case study assumes, for simplicity, that harvest has no impact on product quality
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The drug substance CQA register nevertheless
assigns fragments to this step, because proteases from lysed cells continue to clip
the antibody while the clarified harvest is held. The report supports the transfer of
the process from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug
substance site in Grafton, WI.
Version 1.0 of this report described a univariate study of each parameter, S-HAR-
UNI (2025-11-03 to 2025-11-21), and narrated its outcome without a result for any
parameter effect. That study has no data in the record. During the Version 2.0 re-
analysis, after the QA investigation INV-001 closed, two further studies were made.
In the univariate study S-HAR-UNI2 (2026-09-21 to 2026-09-29) each parameter was
run at 5 levels in duplicate, with the other parameters at their set-points. In the hold
study S-HAR-HLD (2026-09-19 to 2026-09-28) one pool of clarified harvest was held
at two temperatures for 7 days. PCP-004 foresaw neither study, and each is recorded
as a discrepancy from the plan (PD-004-01 and PD-004-02). The scale-down model of
the harvest train was not qualified, because no laboratory-scale disk-stack centrifuge
exists (PD-004-03). Every result is therefore stated at the scale of the study, and
its offset from manufacturing scale is unknown, not zero. Five further discrepancies
record what the study did not do as the plan set out. The culture and the centrate were
not sampled, the filter pressure and the Protein A load challenge were not measured,
the depth filter load series was run only on centrate from the set-point field, records
of execution were not kept, and this report states the rules the plan leaves open (PD-
004-04 to PD-004-08).
Turbidity responded to the parameters, and no effect on a quality attribute was de-
tected. Centrate turbidity fell from a mean of 122.6 NTU at 6,000 g to 43.9 NTU at
12,000 g. Post-clarification turbidity stayed at or below 4.73 NTU across the whole
range of centrifugation (rcf) and across depth filter load up to 140 L/m2. Both runs
at 160 L/m2 exceeded the clarification limit of 10 NTU. Across the NOR of centrifu-
gation (rcf) the change in post-clarification turbidity, −0.22 NTU, is smaller than the
repeatability limit of the turbidity method, the smallest difference between two sin-
gle results that the method tells apart. No slope against the setting was detected for
host cell protein, residual DNA, aggregate, fragments or step yield in any of the three
series, at α = 0.05. The A-Mab assumption is therefore kept. The study supports it

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody developed on the platform of Nova-
cyte Biologics (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Its drug substance is produced
in a fed-batch production bioreactor and purified by the platform train of Protein A
capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, virus filtration
and UF/DF.
Harvest and clarification, Step 4, lies between the production bioreactor and Protein
A capture. At commercial scale it is run once per batch on the contents of a 15,000 L
production bioreactor. The culture passes a continuous disk-stack centrifuge, a train
of depth filters and a sterilizing-grade filter, in that order (SOP-2005 and SOP-2006).
The clarified harvest is then held until capture.
The step removes cells and cell debris. It is not credited with clearing any impurity,
and no viral clearance is claimed for it. Its settings change the feed to Protein A
capture in two ways. A clarification that removes fewer cells and less debris passes
more fine material to capture. An operation that breaks more cells releases more
host cell protein and DNA into the clarified harvest.
The drug substance CQA register assigns fragments to this step (PCMP-001 Table
3.1). Proteases released by dead and lysing cells clip the antibody in the reactor, and
clipping continues in the clarified harvest, because clarification removes the cells but
not the proteases already in solution. PCMP-001 sets no criterion for fragments at
harvest, and fragments are judged at the downstream steps (PCMP-001 Table 7.1).



1.2 Objectives

PCP-004 §2 set five objectives for the study, given here in its numbering:

  1. Qualify a scale-down model of the commercial harvest train and show that it
     represents the centrifugation and depth filtration performance of that train.
  2. Establish the effect of the centrifugal field, over 6,000–12,000 g, on the clarity
     of the centrate and on the host cell protein and DNA released by cell breakage.
  3. Establish the effect of depth filter load, over 80–160 L/m2, on the particulate
     content of the clarified harvest and on the pressure the filter train develops.
  4. Establish whether the step alters the size-variant profile of the antibody, and
     establish the effect of post-clarification turbidity, over 1–20 NTU, on the material
     presented to capture.



                                           9
  5. Define a proven acceptable range for each parameter, determine whether the
     normal operating range lies inside the proven acceptable range, and provide
     the evidence on which each parameter is classified under the parameter classi-
     fication procedure SOP-4001.

Objective 1, the qualification of the scale-down model, was not met, because no qual-
ification was made (Section 3.1). Objective 2, the effect of the field, was met in part.
The effect of the field on the clarity of the centrate was established, and no effect on
host cell protein or DNA in the clarified harvest was detected (Section 5.2). The re-
lease by cell breakage itself was not established, because the culture before harvest
was not sampled (PD-004-04). Objective 3, the effect of depth filter load, was met for
the particulate content of the clarified harvest and not for the filter pressure, which
was not measured (PD-004-05). Objective 4, whether the step alters the size variants
and how turbidity affects the material presented to capture, was met in part. The
study shows whether a parameter changed aggregate or fragments in the clarified
harvest, but it did not measure the antibody before the step, and the Protein A load
challenge that was to show the effect of turbidity on capture was not run (Section 4.2).
Objective 5, the proven acceptable ranges and the evidence for the classes, was met
within the limits stated in Section 7.



1.3 Regulatory and scientific basis

The study is part of the Stage 1 process design record of the commercial process (U.S.
Food and Drug Administration 2011). It follows the enhanced approach of ICH Q8(R2)
and ICH Q11 (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012), with quality risk
management under ICH Q9 (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Criti-
cality is treated as a continuum, as ICH Q8(R2) describes (International Council for
Harmonisation 2009). A proven acceptable range is the range of a parameter over
which the step has been shown to meet its criteria, with the other parameters held at
their set-points (International Council for Harmonisation 2009).
The A-Mab case study supplies the CQA framework, the risk methodology and the
assumption that harvest has no impact on product quality (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). The case study notes that the assumption might not hold in a real
process. No viral clearance is claimed for this step, and no spiking study was made
(International Council for Harmonisation 2023a).

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

Harvest by continuous centrifugation and depth filtration is the platform recovery
operation at Novacyte Biologics. It was used for three earlier antibodies, X-Mab, Y-
Mab and Z-Mab, and the equipment, the grade of the filter media and the control
scheme are unchanged for A-Mab (PCP-004 §4.1). The mechanisms below are there-
fore expected to hold for A-Mab. They give the direction in which each parameter is
expected to act, and the size of each effect for A-Mab is what this study measured.
In the disk-stack centrifuge, cells and debris settle under the centrifugal field at a
velocity that rises with the field and with the difference between their density and
that of the medium. A particle settles onto a disc, is captured there and slides out-
ward to the bowl wall, from which the solids are discharged at intervals. A higher
field therefore clarifies more completely. It can also break more cells. The culture is
accelerated to the speed of the bowl in the feed zone, and the shear there rises with
that speed, while the flow between the discs is laminar and low in shear. Cells broken
in the feed zone release host cell protein and DNA into the centrate, and they leave
fine debris that is harder to capture.
The depth filter train removes the fine debris and the colloidal material that the cen-
trifuge passes. Its media are positively charged, so they adsorb part of the DNA and
fine debris as well as retaining particles by size. A depth filter has a finite capacity.
As retained solids fill the void space of the matrix and occupy its charged sites, the
pressure drop rises and more fine material passes. At the extreme, cells and debris
are forced through the pores. Depth filter load is the volume of centrate per unit
of media area, and the solids that this volume carries decide how far the media are
saturated.
Post-clarification turbidity measures the fine particulate and colloidal material that
the filters passed. It is not set but produced by the other two parameters. A higher
centrifugal field lowers it, because fewer cells and less debris reach the filters, and it
can also raise it, because more broken cells leave fine debris. It rises as the filter load
approaches the capacity of the media. It matters because this material reaches the
Protein A column, where it fouls the bed and shortens the working life of the resin.
Host cell protein and DNA in the clarified harvest are the burden of the culture plus
whatever cells release when they break during the operation. Harvest does not clear
host cell protein. A gentle harvest keeps the burden close to what left the reactor,
and a harsh one adds intracellular protein to it. Aggregate could rise at harvest only
where the antibody meets an air-liquid interface strong enough to unfold it, in the




                                            12
feed zone or in a foaming transfer. Within normal operation that exposure is brief, so
aggregate is not expected to change.
Fragments are clipped antibody, mostly cleaved in or near the hinge into Fab and
Fab+Fc species. Clipping starts in the reactor, where proteases released by dead
and lysing cells act on the secreted antibody. It continues in the clarified harvest,
and its rate rises with temperature, so a harvest held cold clips more slowly than one
held at room temperature. Fragments can also form by disulfide reduction. Lysed
cells release reducing enzymes, thioredoxin reductase among them, which can break
the interchain disulfide bonds of the antibody in the harvest. That source rises with
cell lysis and shear, and so it could rise with the field. A longer culture ends at lower
viability and carries more protease into harvest. Viability at the end of culture is a
property of the culture and is characterized in PCP-003 and PCR-003, not here.
The A-Mab case study assumes, for simplicity, that harvest has no impact on product
quality, and it notes that this might not be true in a real process (CMC Biotech Work-
ing Group 2009, 85). PCP-004 keeps the assumption, and the criteria of PCMP-001
Version 2.0 rest on it (Section 2.2). Three expectations follow for the study:

  1. Centrate turbidity falls as the field rises, and host cell protein and DNA in the
     clarified harvest rise with the field if the shear in the feed zone breaks more
     cells.
  2. Post-clarification turbidity stays low while the depth filter has capacity left, and
     it rises as the load approaches that capacity.
  3. No parameter of the step changes aggregate. Fragments change with the time
     and temperature of the hold, and with the field only if the field breaks enough
     cells to reduce the antibody.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

The parameters of the study and the study type of each follow the pre-characterization
risk assessment RA-001. RA-001 ranked each parameter on its potential to affect
a quality attribute and on its potential to interact with another parameter, and it
assigned a study type from the combined ranking. It brought 3 parameters of this
step into scope and assigned each of them to univariate study. No parameter was
ranked high enough to require a multivariate design, so no screening or response-
surface design was run for this step (PCMP-001 §2).
Protein A capture and the two ion exchange steps clear the host cell protein and DNA
that cell breakage releases (PCR-005, PCR-007 and PCR-008). A poorly chosen set-
ting at harvest was therefore expected to load capture more heavily without changing
the drug substance.
The range studied extends beyond the NOR at both ends for centrifugation (rcf) and
for depth filter load. For post-clarification turbidity the lower edge of the range stud-
ied is the lower limit of the NOR, and only the upper edge extends beyond it. A range
that stops at the NOR limits cannot show where the operation begins to fail, and an
excursion beyond a NOR limit would then have no data behind it.

3 Materials and methods

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

PCP-004 §5.1 planned a scale-down model of the harvest train in three parts, each
matched on the quantity that drives its own mechanism. Cell damage in the feed
zone was to be reproduced in a bench shear device at the energy dissipation rate
calculated for the commercial feed zone. Sedimentation was to be reproduced in a
laboratory centrifuge at the relative centrifugal field of the commercial bowl and at
a matched ratio of flow to sigma factor. Depth filtration was to be reproduced with
disks of the commercial filter media, at the same flux and to the same load per unit
area. The plan required the model to be qualified against the commercial train under
SOP-1001 before the first characterization run.
No qualification was made (PD-004-03). No laboratory-scale disk-stack centrifuge
exists, only a pilot-scale one. The results of the univariate study S-HAR-UNI2 and the
hold study S-HAR-HLD are therefore reported at the scale of the study, with no offset
to manufacturing scale. The offset of every output is unknown, not zero. PCMP-001
§5 asks a report to state the consequence where the model is not qualified for an
output. The consequences for this step are the following:

  1. Post-clarification turbidity. The clarification limit of 10 NTU is judged on results
     at the scale of the study. The proven acceptable range of depth filter load, which
     that limit bounds (Section 7), is therefore a range at the scale of the study, and
     the capacity of the commercial filter train per unit area may differ. PCP-004 had
     left this output out of the planned comparison, so its offset would have been
     unknown even with a qualified model.
  2. Centrate turbidity and step yield. No criterion depends on them, and their offsets
     would only have been recorded (PCMP-001 §7.1).
  3. Host cell protein, residual DNA, aggregate and fragments. The conclusions on
     these attributes are that no parameter changed them within the range studied.
     An offset that is the same at every level of a series, as a difference or as a ra-
     tio, leaves a slope of zero at zero, and the conclusions rest on that. A scale
     effect that differs between levels would not be seen. PCP-004 §5.1 expected the
     bench model to under-represent cell lysis, because the acceleration of the feed
     in the commercial feed zone and the periodic discharge of solids are not fully
     reproduced. A rise of host cell protein or DNA with the field could therefore be
     steeper at commercial scale than at the scale of the study.
  4. The hold. The changes over the hold are differences within one pool. The level
     that enters the hold comes from the production bioreactor model at manufactur-
     ing scale for aggregate and acidic variants (PCR-003), and from the results of
     S-HAR-UNI2, at the scale of the study, for fragments (Section 3.5).



                                          16
The study therefore uses its results to bound ranges and to establish the direction and
the approximate size of each effect, as PCP-004 §5.1 intended. It does not predict the
concentration of an impurity in a commercial batch.



3.2 Operation

PCP-004 §5.2 describes the operation of a characterization run. Each run is a single
pass of the whole harvest through the model train, in the order of the commercial
operation. The culture is taken from the production bioreactor scale-down model
at the end of culture, at the conditions characterized in PCP-003. It is fed to the
centrifuge at the field of the run and at the matched ratio of flow to sigma factor. The
centrate is collected, sampled and passed over the depth filter train at constant flux
to the load of the run, and then over a sterilizing-grade filter. Temperature is held at
the commercial set-point throughout, and the time between the end of culture and
the end of clarification is held constant. Every parameter not varied in a run is held
at its set-point (Table 4.1).
The record of the univariate study S-HAR-UNI2 gives, for each run, the parameter
varied, its level and setting, the replicate and the results. It holds none of the records
of execution that PCP-004 required (PD-004-07). These are the viability and the cell
density of each culture, the order of the runs, the time from the end of culture to
clarification and its temperature, the constant flux and the matched ratio of flow to
sigma factor, one assay campaign per series with common reagent lots, the calibra-
tion status of the equipment for each run, and the agreement of the analysis with
Statistics before the first run. The record also does not name the culture of each run,
so the analysis by culture that PCP-004 §12 provides for a series run on more than
one culture could not be made.

3.5 Statistical methods

Each response of the univariate study S-HAR-UNI2 was analysed against the setting
of the parameter varied in its series, by linear regression on the 10 runs of the series.
PCP-004 §5.4 and §5.5 name the replicates at the set-point condition as the estimate of
the variation between runs against which every effect is tested. The 4 runs with every
parameter at set-point give that estimate on 3 degrees of freedom, and the slopes are
tested, and their intervals calculated, against it. The slope was tested at α = 0.05,
the significance level of the campaign (PCMP-001 §6.2). A response is affected by a
parameter when its slope is significant at that level. Each regression is reported with
its R² and its residual standard deviation, as PCP-004 §5.5 requires. No threshold is
set for either, and no decision rests on them.
With the residual error of the regression, on 8 degrees of freedom, in place of the set-
point replicates, the same slopes are significant and no other. The intervals change
with the error term, and Appendix B gives the residual version as a sensitivity.
Each change is reported over the whole range studied, as the slope times the width
of the range, with its 95 % confidence interval. Where no slope was detected, the
interval gives the precision of the comparison, which is the range of changes across
the range studied that the data do not exclude. For a significant slope PCP-004 §5.5
asks for the change across the NOR, compared with the precision of the method, and
for an effect smaller than the method can resolve to be reported as detected and not
material. PCP-004 sets no size for the smallest difference a method tells apart (PD-
004-08). This report takes it as the repeatability limit, the difference that two single
results exceed by chance with a probability of 5 %. That is 2.77 times the standard
deviation of a single result, at the mean of the set-point runs. At α = 0.05, about one
test in twenty of a response with no real effect gives a significant slope by chance
(PCMP-001 §6.2). The study made 19 such tests, apart from the check of the turbidity
challenge (Section 5.4).
PCP-004 §5.5 allows a quadratic term where the relationship is not linear, and it names
no test for curvature. None was fitted. Where the individual results show a response
that is not linear, as for post-clarification turbidity against depth filter load, the report
describes them, and the decision rests on the individual results against the limit. The
variation between the set-point replicates does not include such a departure from a
straight line, so for a response that is not linear the interval of the slope understates
its uncertainty. Every result of turbidity, host cell protein and DNA lies above the
limit of quantitation of its method, so no result was excluded.
The impurity release criterion of PCP-004 §7 compares host cell protein and DNA in
the clarified harvest with the same measurement on the unclarified culture of the
run, and the product quality criterion compares aggregate before and after the step.
The samples those comparisons need were not taken. This report therefore judges
both criteria on the slope against the setting, the analysis PCP-004 §5.5 sets for every



                                             20
response. A parameter passes them over its range where no slope is detected for
host cell protein, residual DNA or aggregate. PCP-004 lists fragments in scope but
names them neither as a response of its study nor in its criteria, and they are judged
the same way.
No response-surface model is fitted for this step, and no Monte Carlo propagation of
a fitted model is made (PCP-004 §5.5). A range from this study holds with the other
parameters at their set-points. The capability of the drug substance comes from a
Monte Carlo simulation of the whole train through the step models (PCMP-001 §6.5).
It simulates 2,000 batches, each parameter drawn from a normal distribution centred
on its set-point, with a standard deviation of one sixth of the width of its NOR, and
clipped to the range studied.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §5 judges a held pool against the criteria it must meet on
entering the next step. For aggregate and acidic variants the criteria for the load of
Protein A capture are those of the production bioreactor output, which pass through
harvest unchanged (Section 2.2). PCMP-001 sets no fragments criterion for that load.
This report compares fragments with the drug substance criterion of ≤ 2 %, and no
fragments criterion of the train is stricter (PCMP-001 Table 7.1). Two rules of the
judgement are this report’s own, chosen after the results of the hold study S-HAR-
HLD were known. They set the level that enters the hold and the change over it.

  1. The level that enters the hold is the highest level expected in routine manufac-
     ture, and the lowest for the lower limit of acidic variants. For aggregate and
     acidic variants it is the end of the two-sided 95 % prediction interval of a single
     batch over the NOR of the production bioreactor, at manufacturing scale (PCR-
     003). Each end is a one-sided 97.5 % limit. For fragments, which the bioreactor
     designs did not measure, it is the highest result of S-HAR-UNI2. PCP-004 pro-
     vides culture from the set-point of the production bioreactor for those runs (§5.2
     and §12), and their fragments match the process model at the end of a culture
     at set-point (Section 5.5). A longer culture within its NOR ends at lower viabil-
     ity and carries more fragments into harvest (Section 2.1), and no result of the
     campaign measures that increase. The fragments level that enters the hold is
     therefore not the highest level expected in routine manufacture.
  2. The change over the hold is the largest change from day 0 at each temperature.
     That is the largest increase, and for the lower limit of acidic variants the largest
     decrease. The expected level after the hold is the level that enters plus that
     change, and it meets the criterion or it does not. A change smaller than the
     difference between the two results of the pool on day 0 is not taken as resolved.

5.7 Mechanistic interpretation

Centrate turbidity followed the sedimentation mechanism of Section 2.1. A higher
field raises the settling velocity of cells and debris, so more of them reach a disc and
are captured before the fluid leaves the stack. The fall levelled off above the set-point.
That is consistent with a remaining turbidity carried by particles too small to settle
in the time available at any field studied. The study did not measure particle size.
No rise in host cell protein or DNA was detected at the higher fields. The shear in
the feed zone rises with the speed of the bowl, and cells broken there would add both
to the centrate. At the scale of the study the data show no such rise up to 12,000




                                                33
g. The model was not qualified, and PCP-004 §5.1 expected a bench model to under-
represent lysis, so this result does not show that a commercial feed zone breaks no
cells at the highest field.
Post-clarification turbidity fell a little as the field rose, and the centrate results show
why. A higher field passed less turbidity to the depth filter, which then had less fine
material to pass. The second effect the mechanism allows, finer debris from broken
cells, was not seen, which agrees with the absence of a detected rise in host cell
protein and DNA.
Post-clarification turbidity against depth filter load showed the capacity of the media.
Up to 120 L/m2 the filter retained the fine material of the centrate as well as at the
lowest load. At 140 L/m2 both results were a little higher, and at 160 L/m2 the mean
turbidity rose 2.3-fold and crossed the limit. This is the shape the mechanism predicts
for media that approach saturation, in which retained solids fill the void space and
occupy the charged sites until fine material passes. The study places the onset of
the rise between 140 L/m2 and 160 L/m2 for centrate made at the set-point field.
Centrate from a lower field carries more turbidity (Section 5.2) and is expected to
saturate the media at a lower load. The bracket that PCP-004 planned in order to
measure this was not run.
No effect of a parameter on fragments in the clarified harvest was detected. Across
the range of centrifugation (rcf) fragments changed by −0.03 percentage points (95 %
confidence interval −0.23 to 0.16, p = 0.615), so the data show no dependence on the
field at that precision. Disulfide reduction after cell lysis would make fragments rise
with the field (Section 2.1), and the data did not show it within the range studied. The
hold study showed that fragments depend on temperature, as clipping by proteases
predicts. Acidic variants followed the same pattern, because deamidation also runs
faster at a higher temperature. For the operation of the step this means that the
field and the filter load change how much fine material reaches capture. While the
clarified harvest is held, proteases keep clipping the antibody and deamidation keeps
forming acidic variants, and both run faster when it is warmer. The time and the
temperature of the hold therefore change how far fragments and acidic variants rise
before capture.

No design space is claimed for this step. A design space is a multivariate region, and

7 Proven acceptable ranges

PCP-004 §7 sets four decision criteria. They are those of Version 1.0, in its words,
and no result of the studies was used to set or change them. Their application to the
univariate study S-HAR-UNI2 is the following:

  1. Impurity release. Host cell protein and residual DNA in the clarified harvest are
     compared with the same measurements on the unclarified culture that produced
     it. A level passes when no increase is detected at α = 0.05, or when a detected in-
     crease is smaller than the method can resolve. With no result for the unclarified
     culture, a parameter passes over its range where no slope against the setting is
     detected for either impurity (Section 3.5).
  2. Clarification. Post-clarification turbidity is compared with the upper limit of its
     NOR, 10 NTU. The criterion asks whether a harvest setting delivers clarified
     harvest within the NOR. A setting that delivers more has not clarified to the
     NOR, whatever the impurity results show, and that is why the proven accept-
     able range of depth filter load stops at 140 L/m2. The basis of 10 NTU is the
     NOR itself, which is set from production experience. It is not derived from a
     measured effect on Protein A capture. The criterion was applied to each result
     of the centrifugation (rcf) and depth filter load series. In the turbidity series the
     turbidity is the challenge level itself, so the criterion does not apply there.
  3. Product quality. Aggregate is compared before and after the step, and a level
     passes when no increase is detected at α = 0.05. With no result before the step,
     a parameter passes over its range where no slope against the setting is detected
     for aggregate. Fragments were judged the same way.
  4. Step yield is reported and not gated. It is given for every level in Appendix A
     and compared with the set-point runs. Every result lay between 92.4 and 97.7
     %, against a mean of 95.7 % for the set-point runs, and no slope against the
     setting was detected for it in any series.

A parameter meets the criteria over a range when every level in that range passes
the first three. The proven acceptable range is then that range, bounded at the levels
tested. Where a level fails, the range is bounded at the last level that passed (PCP-004
§7 and §8).

residual standard deviation of a regression, because no decision rests on them. For
post-clarification turbidity, whose series is a challenge, the range is decided by the
impurity release and product quality criteria alone. A rule to combine two depth filter
load series, on centrate from 6,000 g and from 12,000 g, is not needed, because only

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the rule of PCMP-001 §7.7. A param-
eter that affects a CQA is a CPP or a WC-CPP, and the two differ in the risk that the
parameter falls outside its acceptable range in operation. A parameter that affects
process performance and no CQA is a KPP, and one that affects neither is a GPP. An at-
tribute is affected by a parameter when the effect is significant at α = 0.05 (PCMP-001
§6.2). Neither PCMP-001 nor SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold for a practically
relevant effect, so each class below states the size of each effect across the proven
acceptable range and the margin of that range to the NOR. The size of an effect is
the difference between the level means at the two ends of the range.

  • Centrifugation (rcf) was classified as a KPP. It changed the clarity of the feed to
    capture. Across its proven acceptable range of 6,000–12,000 g centrate tur-
    bidity changed by −78.7 NTU and post-clarification turbidity by −0.69 NTU,
    with every result below the clarification limit. Across the NOR the change in
    post-clarification turbidity is smaller than the repeatability limit of the turbid-
    ity method (Section 5.2). No effect on a quality attribute or on step yield was
    detected, and the confidence intervals of Table 5.3 bound what the study could
    have missed. The margin of the range to the NOR is 2,000 g at each end. The
    class rests on host cell protein, residual DNA, aggregate and fragments in the
    clarified harvest, the attributes the study measured.
  • Depth filter load was classified as a KPP. It changed post-clarification turbidity by
    0.51 NTU across its proven acceptable range of 80–140 L/m2, where every result
    lay between 3.76 and 4.73 NTU, and the turbidity exceeded the clarification limit
    at 160 L/m2. No effect on a quality attribute or on step yield was detected. The
    margin of the range to the NOR is 20 L/m2 below and none above. The upper
    limit of its NOR lies on the edge of that range at the scale of an unqualified model,
    and the turbidity of the clarified harvest, tested on every batch, is the control
    that detects a filter that approaches its capacity (Section 10).
  • Post-clarification turbidity was classified as a GPP. It is measured, not set. This
    class is a judgement. Its basis is that over 1–20 NTU no effect on a quality
    attribute of the clarified harvest or on step yield was detected. The margin of its
    proven acceptable range to the NOR is none at the lower end, where both meet
    the edge of the range studied, and 10 NTU at the upper end. By the rule above a
    GPP also has no effect on process performance, and the data do not show that for
    turbidity. The mechanism expects turbidity to affect Protein A capture through
    fouling of the bed, and that effect was not measured, because the Protein A load
    challenge was not run (PD-004-05).

No effect of a parameter of this step on a CQA was detected within the range studied,
so the step has no CPP and no WC-CPP. This agrees with the A-Mab assumption that
harvest has no impact on product quality.

10 Contribution to the control strategy

The control strategy of the drug substance has two purposes. Product quality and
safety are assured by controlling the parameters linked to a CQA within the limits
of the design space, and process consistency by controlling the KPPs within estab-
lished limits and monitoring the process attributes (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). Harvest contributes to the second. Its contributions are the following:

  1. Centrifugation (rcf) and depth filter load are controlled within their NORs, 8,000–
     10,000 g and 100–140 L/m2, as KPPs.
  2. The turbidity of the clarified harvest is measured on every batch by nephelometry
     (AMV-3015) as an in-process control (PCP-004 §4.1 and §8), against its NOR of
     1–10 NTU. The upper limit of that NOR is the clarification limit. The control
     detects a centrifuge that clarifies poorly or a depth filter near its capacity. PCP-
     004 §5.3 makes it the measurement by which the clarified harvest is released to
     capture. The upper NOR limit of depth filter load lies on the edge of its proven
     acceptable range, so this test is the control that detects an excursion of the filter
     load.
  3. No quality attribute is controlled at harvest. Host cell protein and DNA are con-
     trolled by their clearance at Protein A capture, cation exchange and anion ex-
     change (PCR-005, PCR-007 and PCR-008), and residual DNA by that clearance,
     not by a release test (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Fragments have no cri-
     terion at harvest. They are judged at the downstream steps against the criteria
     of PCMP-001 Table 7.1 and tested at release of the drug substance by nrCE-SDS.
  4. The hold of the clarified harvest is not a parameter of PCP-004, and this report
     sets no limit for it. On one pool, the hold study supports a hold of up to 7 days
     at 2-8 °C or at 21 °C for aggregate, fragments and the upper limit of acidic
     variants, the attributes it measured. The fragment level taken to enter the hold
     is not the highest expected in routine manufacture (Section 3.5). Bioburden and
     endotoxin were not part of the hold study, so a hold time in manufacturing needs
     that support as well. A cold hold slows the rise of fragments and acidic variants.

PCR-003 sets the monitoring of acidic variants at harvest of every production batch,
with an action limit of 18.72 %, as part of the mitigation of the acidic-variant risk it
accepted. The hold study shows that acidic variants rise in a clarified harvest held
at 21 °C, by 1.39 percentage points in 7 days, so the time and the temperature of a
sample before it is tested bear on that monitoring.
These contributions roll up into PCMR-001, which consolidates the control strategy of
the train. The ranges and classes are those against which the 5 process performance
qualification batches planned in PTP-001 will be run at the receiving site.

11 Discussion

The harvest train of A-Mab behaves as the platform mechanisms predict. Settling
velocity rises with the centrifugal field, so less turbidity reaches the depth filter at a
higher field, and most of that fall lies below the set-point. The depth filter keeps its
clarity until its load approaches the capacity of the media, which for centrate from
the set-point field lies between 140 L/m2 and 160 L/m2 at the scale of the study. No
effect of either parameter, or of the turbidity of the clarified harvest within 1–20 NTU,
on a quality attribute was detected. Fragments and acidic variants change with the
time and the temperature of the hold, not with the operation of the step.
Confidence in the scale-down model is the main limitation. The model was not quali-
fied, so no output has a known offset from manufacturing scale, and the results give
directions and approximate sizes, not commercial levels. Two consequences matter
most. The capacity of the commercial depth filter train per unit area may differ from
that of the study. With the upper NOR limit of depth filter load on the edge of its
proven acceptable range, a lower capacity at commercial scale would place that limit
beyond the edge, and the turbidity test on every batch is the control that would detect
it. Cell breakage at the highest field may also be greater in the commercial feed zone
than in the study, so the absence of a rise in host cell protein and DNA holds at the
scale of the study. Protein A capture and the two ion exchange steps clear what such
breakage would add.
The results rest on two studies that PCP-004 did not plan. Every parameter result
comes from the univariate study made during the re-analysis (PD-004-01), not from
the planned study. The hold judgement comes from the 7-day hold study (PD-004-02),
by rules for the entering level and the change that this report chose after the results
were known (Section 3.5). The univariate study also departed from the design of PCP-
004 (PD-004-04 to PD-004-07). The culture before harvest, the filter pressure and the
Protein A load challenge were not measured, the depth filter load series was not run
on centrate from both ends of the range of centrifugation (rcf), three NOR limits were
not run as levels, and the records of execution were not kept. Where the plan left a
rule open, such as the smallest difference a method must tell apart, this report states
the rule it applied (PD-004-08).
In routine manufacture the turbidity test on every batch detects poor clarification,
and the purification steps clear host cell protein and DNA. The process performance
qualification batches at the receiving site will run the step at commercial scale within
its NORs (PTP-001).

12 Conclusions

1. Turbidity responded to the parameters of harvest, and no effect on a quality at-
   tribute was detected. No slope was detected for host cell protein, residual DNA,
   aggregate, fragments or step yield in any series, at α = 0.05. The A-Mab as-
   sumption that harvest has no impact on product quality is kept, for the attributes
   measured in the clarified harvest.
2. The proven acceptable ranges are 6,000–12,000 g for centrifugation (rcf), 80–
   140 L/m2 for depth filter load and 1–20 NTU for post-clarification turbidity, at
   the scale of the study and with the other parameters at their set-points. Each
   NOR lies inside its range. The upper NOR limit of depth filter load lies on the
   edge of its range.
3. Centrifugation (rcf) and depth filter load are KPPs, and post-clarification turbid-
   ity is a GPP. The step has no CPP and no WC-CPP.
4. Held for 7 days at 2-8 °C or 21 °C, the clarified harvest meets the criteria for the
   load of Protein A capture for aggregate and fragments and the upper limit for
   acidic variants. It was judged by rules chosen after the results were known (Sec-
   tion 3.5), on one pool and for the attributes measured. The lower limit for acidic
   variants is not met at the level that enters the hold, which PCR-003 reports.
5. In the simulation of the train, the drug substance Cpk for fragments, the attribute
   the register assigns to harvest, is 10.62, above the target of 1.33.
6. The scale-down model was not qualified, and every result is stated at the scale
   of the study.
7. The two study events of Version 1.0, a depth filter flush turbidity above its alert
   limit and a post-clarification turbidity above the NOR on one run, have no bearing
   on these conclusions.

PD-004-01. PCP-004 planned a univariate study of each harvest parameter, S-HAR-
UNI, which Version 1.0 described and narrated without a result for any parameter
effect. S-HAR-UNI2 was made during the Version 2.0 re-analysis, from 2026-09-21
to 2026-09-29, to give the harvest parameters the recorded results that Version 1.0
lacked. Each parameter was run at 5 levels in duplicate with the others at set-point,
and post-clarification turbidity as a challenge. Its levels leave out three NOR limits,
8,000 g, 10,000 g and 10 NTU, and it measured fragments, which PCP-004 does not
name as a response of its study (Section 4.2).
PD-004-02. No plan foresaw a hold study. S-HAR-HLD held the clarified harvest at
two temperatures for 7 days, from 2026-09-19 to 2026-09-28, with aggregate by SEC,
acidic variants by icIEF and fragments by nrCE-SDS at every time point. It was made
because Version 2.0 judges a held intermediate against its intermediate acceptance
criteria and Version 1.0 had no hold result to judge. The rules for the level that
enters the hold and for the change over it were chosen after the results were known
(Section 3.5), and the judgement of Section 5.6 carries that limitation.
PD-004-03. PCP-004 required the scale-down model to be qualified against the com-
mercial train before the first characterization run. No qualification was made, be-
cause no laboratory-scale disk-stack centrifuge exists, only a pilot-scale one. Every
result is therefore reported at the scale of the study, with no offset to manufacturing
scale, and Section 3.1 states the consequence for each output.
PD-004-04. PCP-004 planned samples of the unclarified culture, the centrate and the
depth filtrate for host cell protein and DNA, so that the impurity release and product
quality criteria compare each run with its own culture (PCP-004 §5.4 and Appendix
B). Only the clarified harvest was sampled, because the samples before and after the
step were not scheduled in the study record. The comparison within a run could not
be made, and the report judges both criteria on the slope of each result against its
parameter (Section 3.5). The study therefore shows whether a parameter changed
an attribute of the clarified harvest, not whether the step changed it.
