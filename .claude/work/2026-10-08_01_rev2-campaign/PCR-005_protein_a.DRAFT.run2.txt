Executive summary

This report presents the process characterization of the Protein A chromatography
step (Step 5) of the A-Mab drug substance process. Protein A chromatography is
the capture step of the purification train. It removes most of the host cell protein and
DNA of the clarified harvest, and it adds one impurity of its own, Protein A ligand that
leaches from the resin. The characterization studies were run under the approved
plan PCP-005 in a scale-down model of the commercial column, between 2025-11-
05 and 2026-04-17. Supplementary set-point runs of the scale-down model followed
from 2026-09-07 to 2026-09-11. The report follows the lifecycle approach to process
validation (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011), the enhanced approach of ICH
Q8 and ICH Q11 (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012) and the A-Mab
case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). This is Version 2.0 of the report. It
re-analyses the experiments of Version 1.0 against the criteria of PCMP-001 Version
2.0, issued under the investigation INV-001 into acceptance criteria set after the data
were seen, and it adds the fragment results by nrCE-SDS.
The step has 6 process parameters. Of these, 4 were studied in a 19-run screening
design followed by a 28-run response-surface design, and 2 were studied one factor
at a time. Protein load and elution buffer pH were classified as WC-CPPs, load flow
rate and end of pool collect as KPPs, and operating temperature and bed height as
GPPs. No parameter of the step was classified as a CPP.

  • F1. Over all 9 scale-down set-point runs, the offset of the scale-down model from
    manufacturing scale is -251 ng/mg for pool host cell protein and -1.18 ppm for
    leached Protein A. Leached Protein A is lower in the scale-down model, for an
    expected cause given in Section 3.1. The model is qualified for leached Protein
    A over the NOR as characterized.
  • F2. At the protein load NOR as characterized, 20–40 g/L resin, the scale-down
    model is not qualified for pool host cell protein. With the offset at the low end of
    its 90 % confidence interval, the upper 95 % prediction limit of a single run over
    the NOR reaches 16,550 ng/mg at manufacturing scale, above the criterion of ≤
    16,379 ng/mg. This report therefore narrows the protein load NOR to 20–38 g/L
    resin. At the narrowed NOR the same limit is 16,062 ng/mg, and the model is
    qualified for pool host cell protein at both ends of the interval (Section 3.1). On
    the 3 set-point runs that PCP-005 planned, the model would not be qualified for
    pool host cell protein even at the narrowed NOR (Section 13).
  • F3. Pool host cell protein rises with protein load and as elution buffer pH falls,
    and the two parameters interact. It falls as the end of pool collect is extended.
    The response-surface model for pool host cell protein is significant, shows no
    lack of fit and predicts left-out runs within the limit of PCP-005 §7 (criteria 2 to
    4). It does not meet the limit on influential runs, a Cook’s distance below 4/n
    (criterion 5, finding F8, Section 5.3).

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose primary mechanism of action
is antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. It is developed by Novacyte Bio-
logics and is being transferred from development at the sending site to commercial
drug substance manufacture at 15,000 L scale at the receiving site, under the Pro-
cess Transfer Plan PTP-001. The drug substance is purified by the platform train of
Protein A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small
virus retentive filtration and UF/DF.
Protein A chromatography is the first chromatographic step of the purification train.
It is operated in bind and elute mode. The resin binds the antibody from the clarified
harvest at neutral pH, while host cell protein, DNA and most other components of the
feed are removed in the load flow-through and the wash. A low-pH buffer elutes the
antibody into a small volume, and the eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation (Step
6) without neutralization. The step is the principal point of clearance for host cell
protein and DNA, and it partly clears fragments. It introduces one attribute of the
CQA register, leached Protein A. Although Protein A chromatography removes some
virus, no viral clearance claim is made for this step (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). The viral clearance claim of the process rests on low-pH inactivation, anion
exchange and virus filtration, and it is consolidated in PCMR-001.



1.2 Objectives

The objectives of the characterization were the following, as PCP-005 §2 states
them.

  1. To estimate the offset between the scale-down model and manufacturing scale
     for each output of the step, and to qualify the model by that offset for each output
     with a criterion.
  2. To identify which of the 4 parameters of the multivariate design, and which of
     their two-factor interactions, affect pool host cell protein, step yield and leached
     Protein A.
  3. To fit response-surface models for pool host cell protein, step yield, leached Pro-
     tein A and fragments by nrCE-SDS, and to judge their adequacy for prediction.
  4. To determine the PAR of each multivariate parameter for each attribute with a
     criterion at the step.

  6. To establish how long, and at which temperature, the Protein A eluate may be

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The same capture step has been used to purify the platform antibodies X-Mab, Y-Mab
and Z-Mab, and A-Mab through clinical manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). Staphylococcal Protein A, immobilized on the resin, binds the Fc region of
the antibody at the interface between the second and third constant domains of the
heavy chain. When the pH is lowered, histidine residues at this interface become
protonated, the interaction weakens, and the antibody is released.
Host cell protein reaches the eluate by two routes. Some binds to the resin back-
bone or to the ligand. Some associates with the antibody through electrostatic and
hydrophobic interactions, which the wash does not disrupt while the antibody stays
bound to the ligand. A lower elution pH protonates more histidine and acidic residues,
which disrupts more of both kinds of association and releases more host cell protein
with the product. Pool host cell protein was therefore expected to rise as elution
buffer pH falls. In the A-Mab case study pool host cell protein also rose with protein
load (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009), and the same direction was expected here.
Pool host cell protein is reported per mass of antibody, and host cell protein that trav-
els with the antibody rises in proportion to it. That route alone does not explain a
rise with load, and no mechanism for the rise with load was assumed. Both parame-
ters act on the host cell protein that is eluted, so an interaction between them was
possible, and the designs can estimate it.
Step yield is lost when antibody breaks through during the load or remains on the
column at the end of collection. Binding is limited by diffusion into the pores of the
bead. For this reason breakthrough rises as the load approaches the dynamic bind-
ing capacity and as the residence time falls. A higher load flow rate shortens the
residence time and lowers the dynamic binding capacity, and this costs yield only
when the bed is close to capacity. Yield was therefore expected to fall with flow rate
mainly at high load, and to rise as the end of pool collect is extended into the tail of
the elution peak.
Leached Protein A comes from hydrolysis of the coupling chemistry and from cleavage
of exposed ligand by proteases that the clarified harvest carries into the column. The
leached fragments elute with the antibody because they still bind Fc. Leaching is
highest on new resin, in its first cycles. Neither the life of the resin nor the protease
activity of the load is a setting of the run, so within the ranges of one run leached
Protein A was expected to be flat.
A fragment is captured only if it still carries Fc. Fab fragments flow through with the
load, and the pool carries less fragment than the feed. A lower elution pH may add



                                           14
a little acid-catalysed hinge hydrolysis during elution. The pool also still carries host
cell protein, part of which elutes in the low molecular weight window of SEC and
is read as fragment. SEC therefore overstates fragments in this matrix, and nrCE-
SDS does not (INV-001 finding 6). DNA is polyanionic and does not bind Protein A.
Its clearance is large and was not expected to depend on the operating parameters
within their ranges.
Protein A, when operated under platform conditions, does not separate glycan vari-
ants or charge variants, because it binds the Fc region independently of both (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). Nor does it separate aggregate from monomer, since
both carry Fc. In the Protein A characterization of the case study no process parame-
ter had a significant effect on aggregate (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Aggre-
gate formation at low pH over the inactivation hold is characterized in PCR-006. For
these reasons glycan variants, charge variants and aggregate were not responses of
the designs.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

RA-001 assessed every parameter of the step before characterization began. Each pa-
rameter was ranked on its potential impact on the CQAs and on process performance,
and on its potential to interact with other parameters. RA-001 gives the scores and
the rationale.
Protein load, elution buffer pH, load flow rate and end of pool collect were assigned
to multivariate study. Each carries an effect expected from the mechanism, and the
interactions of protein load with elution buffer pH and with load flow rate can only be
estimated in a design that varies the parameters together. Operating temperature
and bed height were assigned to univariate study. A higher temperature speeds the
diffusion of the antibody into the pores, which raises the dynamic binding capacity,
and it strengthens the hydrophobic contacts at the Fc–ligand interface, which makes
elution slightly less complete. Over the range studied both effects were expected to
be small against those of load and pH, and the resin manufacturer specifies binding
capacity across that range. Bed height is held by the packing specification in manu-
facture, and at constant linear velocity it sets the residence time.
The ranges studied are between 1.9 and 2.3 times the width of the NOR. They show
how the step behaves near and beyond the edges of routine operation, and they bound
the knowledge space within which a PAR can be claimed.

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model is a laboratory column packed with the commercial Protein A
resin and operated under SOP-2007 on the scaling principles of PCMP-001 §5. The
bed height and the residence time of the commercial column were kept. Load, wash
and elution volumes were normalized to column volumes, and buffers were made to
the commercial recipes. Column efficiency was checked by plate count and peak
asymmetry after packing. PCP-005 sets no criterion for either, and both are reported
for information.
The model was qualified under SOP-1001 by the offset rule of PCMP-001 §7.1, as PCP-
005 §5.1 applies it. Set-point runs of the scale-down model were compared with 5
manufacturing-scale batches of A-Mab run at set-point. The outputs compared were
pool host cell protein, leached Protein A, step yield, and column efficiency as plates
per metre and peak asymmetry. The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down
runs minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches. It is reported with its two-
sided 90 % confidence interval, from a Student t interval with pooled variance. Every
judgement of a scale-down result against a criterion in this report first subtracts the
offset of that output. Every range and every conclusion against a criterion is therefore
stated at manufacturing scale.
The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion over
the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its confidence interval. That
conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination of NOR
low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface factors. Step yield and column
efficiency have no criterion at the step. Their offsets are recorded, and no conclusion
depends on them.
PCP-005 planned 3 set-point runs of the scale-down model in the qualification study
S-PA-SDM (2025-11-05 to 2025-11-14), before the first run of the screening design.

SDM2 (2026-09-07 to 2026-09-11) during the Version 2.0 re-analysis, to narrow the
confidence interval of the offset for pool host cell protein. The plan did not foresee

3.2 Operation

Each run followed SOP-2007 at laboratory scale. The column was equilibrated, loaded
with clarified harvest to the target protein load, washed, and eluted with the elution
buffer at the pH set by the design. The eluate was collected from the rise of the ab-
sorbance at 280 nm until the end of pool collect set by the design, in column volumes.
The column was then stripped, cleaned and stored under SOP-2008. The pool was
not neutralized, because the commercial eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation at
elution pH.
Each design was loaded from one pool of clarified harvest produced at set-point and
held frozen in aliquots. A single feed keeps feed variation out of the estimated effects.
The effect of feed quality on the step was therefore not estimated in these studies. The
case study reports that Protein A capture is robust over a wide range of feed inputs
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009), and the feed is characterized in PCR-003 and
PCR-004. Runs were executed in random order within each design. The resin was
used within the life-cycle limits of SOP-2008, and the life cycle of the resin is outside
the scope of this report.

3.5 Statistical methods

The designs were built and analysed under SOP-1002 with the common approach of
PCMP-001 §6. Factors were coded from −1 to +1 over their range studied, with 0
at the centre. For each of the 4 design factors the centre of the range studied is the
set-point, so the centre-point runs of both designs are set-point runs.
The screening data were fitted with a model of main effects and two-factor interac-
tions. Effects are reported as the change in the response over the coded range of
the factor, which is twice the coefficient. Significance was judged at α = 0.05. The
screening model identifies effects. A two-level design cannot resolve curvature, so
the screening model was not used to predict between its corners.
The response-surface data were fitted with a full quadratic model. This is the pre-
dictive model of the step, and every PAR and every prediction of a quality attribute
in this report comes from it. Step yield is the exception, because its full quadratic
model failed the limit on predictive power and was replaced by a reduced model
(Section 5.3). Coefficients are reported in coded units, so that each measures the
change in the response over half the range studied. Effects quoted from them are
twice the coefficient. Each model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R²
(from PRESS), the model F-test, and the test of lack of fit against pure error from the
centre points. A prediction of pool host cell protein or leached Protein A is translated
to manufacturing scale by subtracting the offset of that output before it is compared
with a criterion (Section 3.1).




                                          21
The adequacy criteria of PCP-005 §7 were applied as follows. The numbers are those
of PCP-005 §7, where criterion 1 is the qualification of the scale-down model (Sec-
tion 3.1).

  2. Model significance. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Lack of fit. The lack of fit test is not significant at α = 0.05.
  4. Predictive power. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20.
  5. Residuals. Regressions of the absolute residuals on the predicted values, and of
     the residuals on run order, show no slope significant at α = 0.05, and no run has
     a Cook’s distance above 4/n (0.14 for 28 runs).

A model that fails criterion 3 or 4 was refitted with a justified change of terms, or
it was not used to set a range. Where no adequate model resulted, the response is
reported descriptively against its criterion over the runs of the design.
The PAR analysis follows PCMP-001 §6.4 and is described in Section 7. Capability at
commercial scale was estimated from the integrated model of the train, which runs
2,000 simulated batches with each parameter drawn within its NOR. Capability is
reported as a one-sided Cpk against the drug substance criterion for each attribute
of this step. It is judged against the target of 1.33 and the minimum of 1.00 that
PCMP-001 §7.9 sets. The same simulation gives the probability that a drug substance
batch falls outside its specification.

4.2 Screening design

The screening design (study S-PA-SCR, 2025-11-17 to 2025-12-05) was a full two-level
factorial design in the 4 factors, with 16 factorial runs and 3 centre points, 19 runs
in all. Because the design is a full factorial, no effect is aliased with another, and
every main effect and every two-factor interaction can be estimated. The responses
were pool host cell protein, step yield and leached Protein A, with fragments by SEC
recorded for information. All 4 factors were carried into the response-surface design
whatever the screening result, as PCP-005 §6.2 planned. The screening design there-
fore served to identify effects and interactions and to check the ranges. Appendix A
gives the matrix and the results.



4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface design (study S-PA-RSM, 2026-01-12 to 2026-02-06) was a face-
centred central composite design in the same 4 factors. It has 16 factorial runs, 8
axial runs at the faces of the cube and 4 centre points, 28 runs in all. A face-centred
design keeps every run inside the range studied. The full quadratic model leaves 13
residual degrees of freedom, with 3 for pure error and 10 for lack of fit. The responses
were pool host cell protein, step yield, leached Protein A and fragments by nrCE-SDS
on the retained pools. Appendix B gives the matrix and the results.

4.5 Hold study of the Protein A eluate

The Protein A eluate is held at elution pH before viral inactivation. A pool produced at
set-point was divided and held at 2-8 °C and 21 °C for up to 7 days in study S-PA-HLD
(2026-02-02 to 2026-02-16). Samples were taken on days 0, 1, 3, 5 and 7 at each
temperature. Aggregate and fragments were measured by SEC, and acidic variants
by icIEF, at every sample point. The samples of days 0 and 7 were retained and tested
by nrCE-SDS in study S-PA-RET. Protein A capture does not separate aggregate or
charge variants. The held eluate therefore carries the criteria that PCMP-001 sets
for the output of the production bioreactor, ≤ 6.64 % HMW and 18–39.6 % acidic
variants. Fragments are judged against the intermediate acceptance criterion of the
pool, ≤ 2.45 % by nrCE-SDS, on the days with an nrCE-SDS result.
The hold rule of PCP-005 §7 (criterion 7) limits the increase during the hold, not the
level measured in the one held pool. The increase at a sample point is the result at
that time minus the result on day 0 at the same temperature. A hold time is supported
at a temperature when the largest increase up to that time, added to the highest level
expected in the Protein A pool in manufacture, stays within the criterion. For aggre-
gate and acidic variants the highest expected level is the upper 95 % prediction limit
of the production bioreactor model within its NOR (PCR-003), because capture does
not change them. No adequate model results for fragments at this step (Section 5.3).
For fragments the highest expected level is therefore the highest result measured on
the retained pools of the response-surface design, which span the NOR and the range
studied beyond it. The qualification of the scale-down model does not compare these
attributes, so the hold results carry no offset.

5.8 Mechanistic interpretation

Pool host cell protein rises as elution buffer pH falls. A lower pH protonates more
histidine and acidic residues, which disrupts the electrostatic association of host cell
protein with the antibody and with the resin, so more host cell protein is released with
the product. The positive quadratic term in pH shows that this release accelerates
at the low end of the pH range. Pool host cell protein also rises with protein load,
as in the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Host cell protein
that travels with the antibody rises in proportion to the antibody and cannot raise the
value per mg. This study does not establish the mechanism of the load effect. The two
parameters interact, and the fall of pool host cell protein with rising pH is steeper at
high load.




                                                37
End of pool collect lowers pool host cell protein per mass of antibody in the response-
surface design. Collecting further into the tail adds antibody and little host cell pro-
tein, so the ratio falls. The same extension raises yield, which is the largest single
effect on yield in both designs. The mechanism does not explain why the effect of end
of pool collect on pool host cell protein had the opposite sign in the screening design
(finding F11).
A higher load flow rate shortens the residence time and lowers the dynamic binding
capacity. This costs yield only when the bed is close to capacity, which is why the cost
appears at high load and nearly vanishes at low load. Load flow rate has no significant
effect on pool host cell protein.
Leached Protein A could not be modelled, and the mechanism gives no reason for it to
follow the settings of a single run. The expected cause of the offset of leached Protein
A between the two scales, the protease activity of the held load, is not a setting of
a run (Section 3.1). Fragments by nrCE-SDS fall slightly as elution pH rises, which
is the direction expected from acid-catalysed hinge hydrolysis during elution. The
effect is small because the antibody spends only the duration of the elution at low pH
in the column.
For operation, elution buffer pH and protein load have the largest effects on pool host
cell protein, and the risk lies in the corner of low pH and high load. End of pool collect
is also associated with pool host cell protein. Within its NOR it moves the boundary
of that corner little (Section 6). Load flow rate is associated with lower yield at high
load.

6 Design space

The design space of this step is the region in protein load and elution buffer pH, the
two WC-CPPs, over which pool host cell protein meets its intermediate acceptance
criterion at manufacturing scale. Leached Protein A and fragments met their criteria
in every run of the designs, and step yield has no criterion, so they do not constrain it.
On the mean predictions of the response-surface model, with load flow rate and end of
pool collect at their set-points, 96.3 % of the region of protein load and elution buffer
pH studied meets the criterion. The excluded region is the corner of high protein load
and low elution buffer pH. It lies wholly below the elution buffer pH NOR of 3.4–3.7,
at pH 3.32 and below, and it reaches down to a protein load of 37 g/L resin, inside the
protein load NOR of 20–38 g/L resin. At that corner the model predicts 20,082 ng/mg
at manufacturing scale, against ≤ 16,379 ng/mg. At the corner of the two NORs it
predicts 11,891 ng/mg. With load flow rate and end of pool collect at the corners of
their NORs, the fraction of that region lies between 92.6 and 98.3 %. With load flow
rate at its set-point and end of pool collect at the low and the high edge of its NOR,
the fraction is 94.2 % and 97.9 %. Over the whole range studied of all 4 factors, 93.5
% of the design region meets the criterion.
A region defined on mean predictions is not a claim for a single batch. At the boundary
of such a region a single batch meets the criterion only about half the time (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). The claim is therefore made through the PARs of
Section 7, which use the 95 % prediction interval of a single run.
Over the NOR the model predicts a mean pool host cell protein between 3,802 to
13,320 ng/mg at manufacturing scale (Table 7.2). The upper limit of the 95 % pre-
diction interval of a single run reaches at most 15,384 ng/mg over the NOR, below
the criterion. Movement outside the NOR and within a PAR would be assessed under
change control.

For pool host cell protein the drug substance limit of ≤ 100 ng/mg is carried back
through cation exchange and anion exchange. With the platform worst cases of 40-
fold and 4-fold clearance, the a priori criterion was ≤ 16,000 ng/mg. The criterion in
force is ≤ 16,379 ng/mg, with the basis “back-calculated, widened on verification”. It
was widened by the rule of PCMP-001 §7.5. Under that rule the A-Mab value replaces
a platform worst case only where two conditions hold. The one-sided 95 % confidence
bound of the A-Mab worst case within the NOR must be better than the platform value.
The response-surface model of the downstream step must also pass the adequacy
tests of PCMP-001 §7.2, on lack of fit and on how far predicted R² falls short of R².
The point prediction is never used. PCMP-001 stated this rule before any Version 2.0
report applied it.
The widened value rests on cation exchange alone. The cation exchange model, from
28 runs with 4 centre points, predicts a lowest host cell protein clearance within its
NOR of 43.62-fold. This value is the lowest over every combination of NOR low, set-
point and NOR high of the response-surface factors of cation exchange. Its one-sided
95 % confidence bound, 40.95-fold, carries the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients
and is better than the platform value of 40-fold. The model passes both adequacy
tests. Predicted R² falls short of R² by 0.14, within the limit of 0.20, and the lack of
fit test gives p = 0.68. The bound therefore replaced the platform value.
The anion exchange model, from 28 runs with 4 centre points, gives a bound of 4.84-
fold, also better than its platform value of 4-fold. It is not credited, because its pre-
dicted R² falls short of R² by 0.27, more than the limit of 0.20. A model that predicts
left-out runs this much worse than it fits its own runs cannot carry a criterion up-
stream. The platform value stands for anion exchange. The cation exchange bound
and the anion exchange platform value give a clearance of 164-fold and a criterion of
≤ 16,379 ng/mg.
The widened criterion holds only while cation exchange runs within its NOR. At the
edges of its range studied, the lowest host cell protein clearance measured in a cation
exchange run was 30.34-fold, below the platform value. The models of the two steps
are reported in PCR-007 and PCR-008.
For fragments the drug substance limit of ≤ 2.00 % is carried back through cation
exchange and then through the largest increase of low-pH viral inactivation. The
criterion in force is the a priori value, ≤ 2.45 %, with the basis “back-calculated”.
Both downstream bounds are better than their platform values. They are 1.34-fold
for the fragment clearance of cation exchange, against 1.30-fold, and 0.13 percentage
points for the fragment increase of low-pH viral inactivation, against 0.15. Neither is
credited, because both fragment models fail the predictive power test. Predicted R²
falls short of R² by 1.16 for cation exchange and by 0.73 for low-pH viral inactivation.
The platform values of 1.30-fold and 0.15 percentage points therefore stand.

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001 §7.7.
The classification rule of PCP-005 §7 (criterion 8) adds that an effect is practically
relevant for an attribute when the NOR-propagated PAR of the parameter is narrower
than its range studied. It also states that a significant effect whose PAR spans the
whole range studied does not by itself make the parameter critical. At this step that
one-factor test identifies elution buffer pH, whose PAR for pool host cell protein is
narrower than its range studied. The PAR of protein load spans its range studied, but
its effect is relevant all the same. Together with elution buffer pH it takes pool host
cell protein above its criterion within the range studied, in the corner of high load and
low pH (Section 6). Its NOR is also narrowed by this report, because the qualification
of the scale-down model for pool host cell protein depends on it (Section 3.1). The
classes and the reason for each are the following.

  • Protein load was classified as a WC-CPP. It raises pool host cell protein (3,872
    ng/mg per half range) and interacts with elution buffer pH, and with it bounds the
    design space. It is set by the load volume and the titre, and it is readily controlled
    within its NOR. The NOR is narrowed in this revision from 20–40 to 20–38 g/L
    resin, so that the scale-down model is qualified for pool host cell protein over it
    (Section 3.1). Its PAR spans the range studied of 10–50 g/L resin.
  • Elution buffer pH was classified as a WC-CPP. It has the largest effect on pool
    host cell protein (-5,245 ng/mg per half range), and with protein load it bounds
    the design space. Buffer pH is controlled at preparation and verified at release.
    Its PAR for pool host cell protein, 3.25–3.9, is narrower than its range studied
    of 3.2–3.9 and wide against its NOR of 3.4–3.7. The deviation DEV-005-01, an
    elution buffer prepared below its target pH, shows that a buffer below the NOR
    can pass release when the pH meter is incorrectly calibrated.
  • Load flow rate was classified as a KPP. It lowers step yield at high load. A faster
    load shortens the residence time, which lowers the dynamic binding capacity,
    and near saturation of the bed this lets antibody break through. It has no signif-
    icant effect on pool host cell protein, leached Protein A or fragments.
  • End of pool collect was classified as a KPP. It has the largest effect on step yield.
    It also has a significant effect on pool host cell protein, -2,111 ng/mg across the
    range studied, and the sign of that effect differs between the designs (finding
    F11). Its NOR-propagated PAR for pool host cell protein spans the whole range
    studied. Across its NOR, the share of the region of protein load and elution
    buffer pH studied that meets the criterion moves from 94.2 % at the low edge to
    97.9 % at the high edge (Section 6). The class would be revisited if a later study
    confirmed the screening sign.
  • Operating temperature was classified as a GPP. No difference from the set-point
    runs was detected at either end of its range. The intervals allow a rise in pool



                                           46
    host cell protein of up to 4,291 ng/mg. Such a rise would still leave pool host cell
    protein within its criterion at manufacturing scale, and every individual result
    met its criterion (Section 5.6).
  • Bed height was classified as a GPP. No difference from the set-point runs was
    detected at either end of its range. The intervals allow a rise in pool host cell
    protein of up to 2,815 ng/mg, and every individual result met its criterion (Sec-
    tion 5.6). Bed height is fixed by the packing specification of the commercial
    column and does not vary in operation. A short bed at the upper end of the load
    range was not characterized, and no claim is made for it.

In summary the step has 2 WC-CPPs, 2 KPPs and 2 GPPs, and no CPP. The classes
of protein load and elution buffer pH rest on the size of their effects, on the PAR of
elution buffer pH, and on the corner where the two parameters take pool host cell
protein above its criterion together. Both effects agree in direction with the A-Mab
case study. The KPP classes rest on the reduced model for step yield, on the direction
of the yield effects in both designs, and on the mechanism of breakthrough and of
peak collection.

10 Contribution to the control strategy

The Protein A step contributes to the control strategy in the following ways.

  1. Process parameters. Protein load and elution buffer pH are controlled within
     their NORs as WC-CPPs. The NOR of protein load is 20–38 g/L resin, narrowed
     from 20–40 g/L resin in this revision (Section 3.1). Load flow rate and end of
     pool collect are controlled within their NORs as KPPs. Movement outside the
     NOR and within the PARs of Section 7 is assessed under change control.
  2. Material controls. Elution buffer is released against its pH specification. The elu-
     tion buffer prepared below its target pH (DEV-005-01) showed that this release
     test depends on a correctly calibrated pH meter.
  3. Resin and load. Leached Protein A depends on the life of the resin and on the pro-
     tease activity of the load. Resin use, cleaning and sanitization are controlled by
     SOP-2008. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture, the expected cause
     of the offset of leached Protein A between the scales (Section 3.1), is character-
     ized in PCR-004.
  4. Hold. The Protein A eluate may be held at elution pH for up to 7 days at 2-8 °C
     or 21 °C before viral inactivation.
  5. Downstream clearance and release. Host cell protein, leached Protein A and
     fragments are further cleared by cation exchange (PCR-007) and anion exchange
     (PCR-008), and they are tested at release of the drug substance. The criterion
     for pool host cell protein relies on cation exchange running within its NOR (Sec-
     tion 7). Viral clearance is claimed at low-pH viral inactivation (PCR-006), anion
     exchange and virus filtration (PCR-009), and not at this step.

The control strategy of the whole train is consolidated in PCMR-001.

11 Discussion

The characterization confirms the main effects expected for this step. Elution buffer
pH and protein load have the largest effects on pool host cell protein, and they in-
teract. The mechanism of the load effect is not established, because pool host cell
protein is reported per mg of antibody. End of pool collect, protein load and load
flow rate have the largest effects on step yield. One result does not follow the ex-
pectations. End of pool collect lowers pool host cell protein in the response-surface
design and raised it, not significantly, in the screening design, for a reason that was
not identified.
The scale-down model is qualified by its offset for both outputs with a criterion, but
for pool host cell protein only after two changes. At the protein load NOR as char-
acterized, the conclusion for pool host cell protein changed across the confidence
interval of the offset, so the NOR was narrowed to 20–38 g/L resin. On the 3 planned
runs the interval was too wide for a qualification even at the narrowed NOR, and 6
set-point runs were added (PD-005-01). Both changes were made after the Version
2.0 re-analysis had shown the result on the planned runs. The rule and the criterion
were not changed. The narrowing costs routine operation the upper 2 g/L resin of
the load NOR. Leached Protein A is lower in the laboratory column, for the expected
cause given in Section 3.1. Subtracting its offset raises each result by 1.18 ppm, and
the highest result of the designs stays far below its criterion at 5.79 ppm. The supple-
mentary runs narrowed the confidence interval of the offset for pool host cell protein
by 46 % (Section 13). The offset was estimated at set-point only. The report there-
fore assumes that it is the same across the ranges studied, and this assumption is not
tested.
The criterion for pool host cell protein now depends on the cation exchange bound of
40.95-fold. Within its NOR cation exchange clears host cell protein better than the
platform worst case, at the one-sided 95 % confidence bound, and its model passes
the adequacy tests. At the edges of its range studied it cleared less than the platform
value. The criterion of this step therefore holds only while cation exchange runs
within its NOR. Anion exchange is credited with its platform value only, because its
model fails the predictive power test.
The response-surface model for pool host cell protein is significant, shows no lack of
fit and meets the limit on predictive power, with a lack of fit test close to its limit.
It does not meet the limit on influential runs, a Cook’s distance below 4/n, and no
model does. The conclusions on pool host cell protein rest instead on the deletion of
single runs, which showed that no run decides them. The Cook’s distance limit of 4/n
is better suited to a design with low leverage than to a face-centred design with 15
terms in 28 runs. A limit based on the leverage of each run would serve the purpose




                                          49
of the criterion better. The residuals show no trend with the run order of execution
(Table 5.9).
Leached Protein A and fragments gave no adequate model. For both, any effect of
the parameters is small against the variation of the runs and the assay.
The 2 deviations of the study did not change its conclusions (Section 13). The elution
buffer prepared below its target pH (DEV-005-01) was below the NOR but inside the
range studied. The out-of-trend leached Protein A result (DEV-005-02) was replaced
by a valid re-assay.
The other limitations are those the plan named. Each design used one feed, so the
effect of feed quality on the step was not estimated. The limits of the univariate study
are stated in Section 4.4. The laboratory column does not reproduce the hold of the
manufacturing load before capture, and the offset of leached Protein A is expected to
carry that difference.

12 Conclusions

1. The step was characterized under PCP-005 in a screening design, a response-
   surface design, a univariate study and a hold study.
2. The scale-down model is qualified by its offset for leached Protein A, and for pool
   host cell protein at the narrowed protein load NOR of 20–38 g/L resin, on all 9

3. Elution buffer pH and protein load have the largest effects on pool host cell
   protein, and it falls as the end of pool collect is extended. Its model is significant,
   shows no lack of fit and meets the limit on predictive power. Because no model

   End of pool collect, protein load and load flow rate have the largest effects on
   step yield. Leached Protein A and fragments met their criteria in every run, and
   no adequate model results for either.

7. The eluate may be held at elution pH for 7 days at 2-8 °C or 21 °C.
8. At commercial scale the lowest Cpk among the attributes of this step is 6.19, for

9. The 2 deviations were investigated and the data retained without changing the

13.1 DEV-005-01. Elution buffer pH below target on a
     response-surface run

The elution buffer of lot LOT-BUF-5290 was prepared below its target pH. It was used
on run 23 of the response-surface design, whose design setting for elution buffer pH
was 3.55. The buffer had passed release on an incorrectly calibrated pH meter. The
error was found at a review of the buffer preparation record after the run, and the
pH of the lot was 3.38. This is below the NOR of 3.4–3.7 and inside the range studied
of 3.2–3.9. The investigation found an error at the titration end point during buffer
preparation.
The run gave a pool host cell protein of 8,418 ng/mg at manufacturing scale, below
the criterion of ≤ 16,379 ng/mg. At the recorded pH of the buffer lot the model
predicts 12,212 ng/mg, and the result is well below that prediction. At the design
setting the model predicts 8,853 ng/mg, and the result is closer to it. The result does
not show the rise in pool host cell protein that a buffer at the recorded pH would be
expected to cause. The data do not establish why. The run was analysed at its design
setting. There its residual is -434 ng/mg, within one residual standard deviation of
the model (895 ng/mg). Its Cook’s distance for pool host cell protein is 0.03, below
the limit of 4/n. The run does not influence the fit more than the other runs of the
design (Section 5.5). The run was retained. The release test is the control on which
the WC-CPP class of elution buffer pH relies, and here it did not detect the error
because the meter was incorrectly calibrated. Run 23 carries a footnote in Appendix
B.

analysis. The first result was kept in the record with its status marked. The re-assay
result lies inside the range of the other runs of the design. At manufacturing scale it
is 3.30 ppm, far below the criterion of ≤ 158 ppm.



13.3 PD-005-01. Supplementary set-point runs in the
     scale-down model qualification

PCP-005 planned 3 set-point runs of the scale-down model in the qualification study
S-PA-SDM, compared with 5 manufacturing-scale batches. During the Version 2.0
re-analysis, while INV-001 was open, a further 6 set-point runs were made in study
S-PA-SDM2 (2026-09-07 to 2026-09-11). They were made to narrow the confidence
interval of the offset for pool host cell protein, which is the output that sets the ranges
of the step. The plan did not foresee these runs. They were made under the same
procedures as the planned runs.

13.4 PD-005-02. Pool host cell protein conclusion resting on
     an influence analysis

PCP-005 planned to set the ranges and the design space from a response-surface
model that meets every model adequacy criterion of PCP-005 §7. No model of the
step met the limit on influential runs (criterion 5), a Cook’s distance below 4/n for
every run (Section 5.5). The model for pool host cell protein is significant, shows no
lack of fit and meets the limit on predictive power. Its conclusions rest on an influence
analysis that the plan does not describe, in which each run above the limit was deleted
in turn and the model refitted. With any one of these runs deleted, the upper 95 %
