Executive summary

This report presents the process characterization of the Protein A chromatography
step (Step 5), the capture step of the A-Mab drug substance purification train. It
removes most of the host cell protein and DNA of the clarified harvest, and it adds one
impurity of its own, Protein A ligand that leaches from the resin. The characterization
studies were run under the approved plan PCP-005 in a scale-down model of the
commercial column, between 2025-11-05 and 2026-04-17.
This Version 2.0 re-analyses the experiments of Version 1.0 against the criteria of
the master plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0, issued under the investigation INV-001. It
adds the fragment results by nrCE-SDS and analyses each response-surface output by
model selection, as the investigation INV-002 requires. The normal operating range
(NOR) of protein load in force is narrowed from 20–40 to 20–39 g/L resin.
The step has 6 process parameters. Of these, 4 were studied in a 19-run screening
design followed by a 28-run response-surface design, and 2 were studied one factor
at a time. Protein load and elution buffer pH were classified as well-controlled critical
process parameters (WC-CPPs). Load flow rate and end of pool collect were classified
as KPPs, and operating temperature and bed height as GPPs. No parameter of the
step is in the class CPP.

  • F1. The scale-down model is qualified for leached Protein A and, over the NOR in
    force, for pool host cell protein, which in the scale-down model is 0.973 times its
    value at manufacturing scale (90 % confidence interval 0.891 to 1.063). Over the
    protein load NOR as characterized, 20–40 g/L resin, the model is not qualified
    for pool host cell protein, because with the ratio at the low end of its interval the
    upper prediction limit of a single run, 17,143 ng/mg, exceeds the criterion. The
    NOR of protein load in force was therefore narrowed to 20–39 g/L resin. The qual-
    ification also rests on the widened criterion, on the 6 set-point runs added during
    the re-analysis (PD-005-01) and on the choice of the selected model. Leached
    Protein A is 1.18 ppm lower in the scale-down model, as expected from the longer
    hold of the load at manufacturing scale. Fragments were not compared at the
    two scales (Section 3.1).
  • F2. Each output of the response-surface design was analysed by model selection
    (PD-005-04), and each selected model meets the adequacy criteria of PCP-005
    §7 on lack of fit and predictive power. Pool host cell protein is modelled on the
    log scale (Section 3.5, Section 5.3).
  • F3. Pool host cell protein rises with protein load and as elution buffer pH falls,
    by factors of 2.43 and 3.30 across the ranges studied. The two effects multiply.
    Whether a further interaction of the two exists on the log scale the data do not
    decide. Pool host cell protein falls by 15 % as the end of pool collect is extended
    across its range (Section 5.3.1).

  • F4. Step yield rises with end of pool collect and levels off towards the upper end
    of its range. It falls with protein load, and load flow rate lowers it only at high
    load (Section 5.3.2).
  • F5. Leached Protein A does not depend on the 4 parameters of the multivariate
    design by more than the design resolves. This rests on the re-assay of run 11
    (DEV-005-02). Every run met the criterion of ≤ 158 ppm at manufacturing scale,
    the highest at 5.79 ppm (Section 5.3.3).

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose primary mechanism of action
is antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. It is developed by Novacyte Bio-
logics and is being transferred from development at the sending site to commercial
drug substance manufacture at 15,000 L scale at the receiving site, under the Pro-
cess Transfer Plan PTP-001. The drug substance is purified by the platform train of
Protein A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small
virus retentive filtration and UF/DF.
Protein A chromatography is the first chromatographic step of the purification train.
It is operated in bind and elute mode. The resin binds the antibody from the clarified
harvest at neutral pH, while host cell protein, DNA and most other components of the
feed are removed in the load flow-through and the wash. A low-pH buffer elutes the
antibody into a small volume, and the eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation (Step
6) without neutralization. The step is the principal point of clearance for host cell
protein and DNA, and it partly clears fragments. It introduces one attribute of the
CQA register, leached Protein A. Although Protein A chromatography removes some
virus, no viral clearance claim is made for this step (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). The viral clearance claim of the process rests on low-pH inactivation, anion
exchange and virus filtration, and it is consolidated in PCMR-001.



1.2 Objectives

The objectives of the characterization were the following, as PCP-005 §2 states
them.

  1. To estimate the offset between the scale-down model and manufacturing scale
     for each output that the qualification compares, and to qualify the model by that
     offset for each of these outputs with a criterion.
  2. To identify which of the 4 parameters of the multivariate design, and which of
     their two-factor interactions, affect pool host cell protein, step yield and leached
     Protein A.
  3. To fit response-surface models for pool host cell protein, step yield, leached Pro-
     tein A and fragments by nrCE-SDS, and to judge their adequacy for prediction.
  4. To determine the PAR of each parameter of the multivariate design for each
     attribute of the Protein A pool with a criterion.

  5. To establish the effect of operating temperature and bed height over their ranges,
     one factor at a time.
  6. To provide the data for the classification of every parameter of the step under
     SOP-4001, and to support the Stage 2 ranges and the control strategy.

The hold study of the eluate was not an objective of PCP-005. It was made dur-

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The same capture step has been used to purify the platform antibodies X-Mab, Y-Mab
and Z-Mab, and A-Mab through clinical manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). Staphylococcal Protein A, immobilized on the resin, binds the Fc region of
the antibody at the interface between the second and third constant domains of the
heavy chain. When the pH is lowered, histidine residues at this interface become
protonated, the interaction weakens, and the antibody is released.
Host cell protein reaches the eluate by two routes. Some binds weakly to the resin
backbone or to the ligand. Some associates with the antibody through electrostatic
and hydrophobic interactions, which the wash does not disrupt while the antibody
stays bound to the ligand. This product-associated host cell protein leaves the column
with the antibody at any elution pH. A lower elution pH desorbs more of the host cell
protein held on the resin, and that protein leaves the column with the product. Pool
host cell protein was therefore expected to rise as elution buffer pH falls. In the A-Mab
case study pool host cell protein also rose with protein load (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009), and the same direction was expected here. Product-associated host cell
protein rises in proportion to the antibody, which leaves the value per mg unchanged.
PCP-005 therefore assumed no mechanism for the rise with load (PCP-005 §4.1). The
plan expected that the two effects could interact, and both designs can estimate an
interaction.
Step yield is lost when antibody breaks through during the load or remains on the
column at the end of collection. Breakthrough begins earlier as the load approaches
the dynamic binding capacity of the bed. The dynamic binding capacity itself falls
as the residence time shortens, because binding is limited by the diffusion of the
antibody into the pores of the bead. A higher load flow rate shortens the residence
time, which costs yield only when the load is close to the capacity. Yield was therefore
expected to fall with flow rate mainly at high load, and to rise as the end of pool collect
is extended into the tail of the elution peak.
Leached Protein A comes from hydrolysis of the coupling chemistry and from cleavage
of exposed ligand by proteases that the clarified harvest carries into the column. The
leached fragments elute with the antibody because they still bind Fc. Leaching is
highest on new resin, in its first cycles. A higher protein load or a lower load flow
rate lengthens the loading time, and with it the contact of the ligand with the harvest
proteases. Leached Protein A is reported per mass of antibody, and if a similar amount
of ligand leaches in each cycle the value in ppm falls as protein load rises. PCP-005




                                            13
expected leached Protein A to depend little on the parameters within their ranges
(PCP-005 §4.1).
An antibody fragment that still carries Fc is captured and elutes with the intact anti-
body. A Fab fragment lacks Fc and is expected to flow through with the load, so the
pool is expected to carry less fragment than the feed. Native Protein A also binds the
variable domain of heavy chains of the VH3 family, and a Fab fragment of such an
antibody can be captured as well. Whether the Fab of A-Mab binds the ligand of this
resin was not tested here. A lower elution pH may add a little acid-catalysed hinge
hydrolysis during elution. SEC overstates fragments in this matrix, because it reads
part of the host cell protein as fragment (INV-001 finding 6). DNA is polyanionic and
does not bind Protein A. Its clearance is large and was not expected to depend on the
operating parameters within their ranges.
Protein A, when operated under platform conditions, does not separate glycan vari-
ants or charge variants, because it binds the Fc region independently of both (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). Nor does it separate aggregate from monomer, since
both carry Fc. In the Protein A characterization of the case study no process param-
eter had a significant effect on aggregate (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). For
these reasons glycan variants, charge variants and aggregate were not responses of
the designs.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

The pre-characterization risk assessment RA-001 assessed every parameter of the
step before characterization began. Each parameter was ranked on its potential im-
pact on the CQAs and on process performance, and on its potential to interact with
other parameters.
Protein load, elution buffer pH, load flow rate and end of pool collect were assigned
to multivariate study. Each carries an effect expected from the mechanism, and the
interactions of protein load with elution buffer pH and with load flow rate can only be
estimated in a design that varies the parameters together. Operating temperature
and bed height were assigned to univariate study. A higher temperature speeds the
diffusion of the antibody into the pores, which raises the dynamic binding capacity,
and it strengthens the hydrophobic contacts at the Fc–ligand interface, which makes
elution slightly less complete. Over the range studied both effects were expected to
be small against those of load and pH, and the resin manufacturer specifies binding
capacity across that range. Bed height is held by the packing specification in manu-
facture, and at constant linear velocity it sets the residence time.
The ranges studied are between 1.9 and 2.3 times the width of the NOR. They bound
the knowledge space within which a PAR can be claimed. Load flow rate is set as a
linear velocity, so at the set-point bed height it fixes the residence time. The residence
time is 6 min at the set-point flow rate, and over the range studied it runs from 4 min
at the highest flow to 12 min at the lowest. The upper end of the flow range therefore
tests the shortest residence time, the condition most likely to cause breakthrough at
high load, and the two ends of the elution pH range test the strongest and the weakest
elution.

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model is a laboratory column packed with the commercial Protein A
resin and operated under SOP-2007 on the scaling principles of PCMP-001 §5. The
bed height and the residence time of the commercial column were kept. Load, wash
and elution volumes were normalized to column volumes, and buffers were made to
the commercial recipes. Column efficiency was checked by plate count and peak
asymmetry after packing. PCP-005 sets no criterion for either, and both are reported
for information.
The model was qualified under SOP-1001 by its offset from manufacturing scale, the
rule of PCMP-001 §7.1 that PCP-005 §5.1 applies to this step. Set-point runs of the
scale-down model were compared with 5 manufacturing-scale batches of A-Mab run
at set-point. The outputs compared were pool host cell protein, leached Protein A,
step yield, and column efficiency as plates per metre and peak asymmetry. The offset
of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of the manufacturing-
scale batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval from a Student t interval
with pooled variance. It is taken on the scale on which the output is modelled (Sec-
tion 3.5). Pool host cell protein is modelled on the log scale. Its offset is therefore
the difference of the mean logarithms, reported as the ratio of the geometric means
of the two scales, a multiplicative offset. A scale-down result of pool host cell protein
is divided by this ratio before it is judged against a criterion, and a result of leached
Protein A has its offset subtracted. Every range and every conclusion for these two
outputs is therefore at manufacturing scale.
The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion over
the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its confidence interval. That
conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination of NOR
low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface factors, judged on the upper
prediction limit of a single run (Section 3.5). Step yield and column efficiency have
no criterion at the step. Their offsets are recorded, and no conclusion depends on
them.

3.2 Operation

Each run followed SOP-2007 at laboratory scale. The column was equilibrated, loaded
with clarified harvest to the target protein load, washed, and eluted with the elution
buffer at the pH set by the design. The protein load of each run was set from the load
volume and the titre of the clarified harvest by Protein A affinity HPLC (AMV-3021,
under SOP-3016). The eluate was collected from the rise of the absorbance at 280
nm until the end of pool collect set by the design, in column volumes. The column



                                           18
was then stripped, cleaned and stored under SOP-2008. The pool was not neutralized,
because the commercial eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation at elution pH.
Each design was loaded from one pool of clarified harvest produced at set-point and
held frozen in aliquots. A single feed keeps feed variation out of the estimated effects.
The effect of feed quality on the step was therefore not estimated in these studies. The
case study reports that Protein A capture is robust over a wide range of feed inputs
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009), and the feed is characterized in PCR-003 and
PCR-004. Runs were executed in random order within each design. The resin was
used within the life-cycle limits of SOP-2008, and the life cycle of the resin is outside
the scope of this report.

3.5 Statistical methods

The designs were built and analysed under SOP-1002 with the common approach of
PCMP-001 §6. Factors were coded from −1 to +1 over their range studied, with 0
at the centre. For each of the 4 design factors the centre of the range studied is the
set-point, so the centre-point runs of both designs are set-point runs. In this report
a term of a model is a main effect, an interaction or a square, and the intercept is
counted apart.
The screening data were fitted with a model of main effects and two-factor interac-
tions. Effects are reported as the change in the response over the coded range of
the factor, which is twice the coefficient. Significance was judged at α = 0.05. The
screening model identifies effects. A two-level design cannot resolve curvature, so
the screening model was not used to predict between its corners.
PCP-005 §5.5 planned to fit the full quadratic model, an intercept and 14 terms, to
each output of the response-surface design. Each output was analysed instead by
model selection, which INV-002 introduced as its corrective action CA3 on 2026-09-
15 for every report with a response-surface design. PCMP-001 Version 2.0 still names
the full quadratic model, so the selection is a discrepancy from the plan, recorded as
PD-005-04 (Section 13). The selection first chooses the scale of each output and then
the terms of its model. Pool host cell protein is the one output put on the log scale,
and both choices are explained below with its data.


3.5.1 The scale of the model

On the raw scale a model describes pool host cell protein in ng/mg as a sum, 𝑦 =
𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + ⋯ + 𝜀, where 𝑥1 is a coded parameter, 𝑏1 its coefficient and 𝜀 the run-to-
run error. Each parameter then adds or removes a fixed amount, and the error is a
fixed amount too. On the log scale the model is ln 𝑦 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + ⋯ + 𝜀. Transformed
back, 𝑦 = 𝑒𝑏0 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏1 𝑥1 ⋯ 𝑒𝜀 , so each parameter multiplies pool host cell protein by a
fixed factor. The factor is 𝑒𝑏 per coded unit and 𝑒2𝑏 from the low to the high end of
the range studied. The run-to-run error multiplies the output as well, so its size in
ng/mg grows with the level. In the selected model pool host cell protein rises by a
factor of 2.43 from the low to the high end of protein load, falls by a factor of 3.30
from the low to the high end of elution buffer pH, and falls to 0.85 times its value over
the range of end of pool collect.
Two properties of this output make a factor the natural description. A purification
step removes a share of what is present, so a parameter that changes the performance
of the step changes the share of host cell protein that reaches the pool, not a fixed
amount. The precision of the host cell protein ELISA is a percentage of the level, 9.5
% (AMV-3012), so the scatter of the results grows with their level.
Least squares, and the t-tests, F-tests and prediction intervals built on it, assume that
the residuals are independent and normally distributed, with mean zero and the same
variance over the whole range studied. On the raw scale the residuals of pool host cell
protein do not have the same variance. In the full quadratic model on the raw scale,



                                           21
their root mean square is 679 ng/mg in the lower half of the predicted values and 957
ng/mg in the upper half, 41 % larger. In the full quadratic model on the log scale the
two halves give 0.067 and 0.074 in natural-log units, 10 % apart, so the variance is
about constant. The Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic model (Box and Cox 1964)
points the same way. Its estimate of the exponent λ is 0.06, and its 95 % interval,
−0.32 to 0.42, contains 0, the log scale, and excludes 1, the raw scale. The rule of
the selection puts an output on the log scale exactly when this interval contains 0
and excludes 1. For step yield, leached Protein A and fragments the interval contains
1, and these outputs stay on the raw scale. The residual plot of the selected model
shows no funnel (Figure 5.2).
The log scale changes how results are read, not what is judged. The criterion stays
in ng/mg. Effects are fold changes. The scale-down offset of pool host cell protein is
a ratio of geometric means, and a scale-down prediction is divided by it (Section 3.1).
A prediction interval is calculated on the log scale and transformed back. It keeps
its 95 % coverage, because the probability that ln 𝑦 lies between two values is the
probability that 𝑦 lies between their antilogarithms. It is therefore suitable, and on
the log scale the assumption of least squares holds, which on the raw scale it does
not. The interval is asymmetric, further above the predicted value than below it. At
the set-point the predicted value is 7,150 ng/mg at manufacturing scale, and the 95 %
prediction interval of a single run runs from 5,687 to 8,989 ng/mg, 1,463 below and
1,839 above. The predicted value is the median of pool host cell protein, its geometric
mean, not its arithmetic mean. The upper prediction limit, which decides against an
upper criterion, does not depend on that distinction. Near an upper criterion, where
the level is high, a model on the raw scale understates the scatter, because its residual
standard deviation is pooled over all levels. The interval from the log scale is wider
there, as the data require.


3.5.2 The terms of the model

The terms were then chosen by a search with one goal, the model with the lowest
AICc among all hierarchical submodels of the full quadratic model. A hierarchical
model contains an interaction only with both of its main effects, and a square only
with its main effect. AICc, Akaike’s information criterion with the correction for small
samples (Hurvich and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002), estimates how well
a model would predict new runs. It rewards the fit and penalises every estimated
parameter, and it counts the residual variance as one of them. Its correction raises
the penalty when the runs are few for the number of parameters, as in a design of
28 runs, so that the criterion does not favour too many terms. Every candidate was
fitted, 1,337 hierarchical models for 4 factors, and the one with the lowest AICc was
taken.
A stepwise search, which adds or removes one term at a time, stops when no sin-
gle change lowers AICc. It can therefore stop at a model that only a combination of
changes would improve. On the data of this campaign a stepwise search by AICc
would have stopped at a model with a higher AICc for 12 of the 21 outputs that
have a criterion and an AICc. At this step a search forward from the mean would
have stopped at 4 terms for pool host cell protein. A search backward from the full



                                          22
quadratic model would have stopped at 4 terms for leached Protein A and 5 for frag-
ments, each with a higher AICc than the selected model. The backward elimination
of INV-002 corrective action CA2 differed in three ways. It was stepwise, it removed
terms by p-value, a test of each term against zero and not a comparison of models,
and it used the raw scale only. For fragments it kept elution buffer pH alone, where
the search keeps protein load and elution buffer pH.
The selected model was then judged against the model adequacy criteria of PCP-005
§7 listed below. A model with no term has no F-test, and criterion 2 does not apply
to it. Such a model shows that, within the range studied, the output does not depend
on the parameters by more than the variation of the runs can resolve. Finally, the
scale-down offset of each output was taken on the scale of its model.


3.5.3 Adequacy, prediction and ranges

Each model is reported with R², adjusted R², predicted R² (from PRESS) (Montgomery
2017), the model F-test, and the test of lack of fit against pure error from the centre
points. Coefficients are reported in coded units, so that each measures the change
in the response over half the range studied, in natural-log units for a log-scale model.
The adequacy criteria of PCP-005 §7 were applied as follows. The numbers are those
of PCP-005 §7, where criterion 1 is the qualification of the scale-down model (Sec-
tion 3.1).

  2. Model significance. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Lack of fit. The lack of fit test is not significant at α = 0.05.
  4. Predictive power. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. PCMP-001
     §7.2 compares predicted R² with R², not with adjusted R².
  5. Residuals. Regressions of the absolute residuals on the predicted values, and of
     the residuals on run order, show no slope significant at α = 0.05, and no run has
     a Cook’s distance above 4/n (0.14 for 28 runs).

PCMP-001 §7.2 provides that a model that fails the lack of fit test, or whose predicted
R² falls short of R² by more than 0.20, is refitted with a justified change of terms or
transformation, or is not used to set a range.
Each output with a criterion is judged over the NOR at every combination of NOR low,
set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface factors. The value compared with
the criterion is the upper end of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run, which
includes the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients and the residual. The criteria of this
step are upper limits, so only that end is compared, which makes it a one-sided 97.5
% prediction limit (PCP-005 §7). This report calls it the upper prediction limit.
The PAR analysis follows PCMP-001 §6.4. The univariate PAR, which PCP-005 §8.2
calls the set-point analysis, holds the other parameters at their set-points. It is judged
on the prediction of the model, the median for pool host cell protein and the mean for
the other outputs. The NOR-propagated PAR is a multivariate PAR. It is the range of a
parameter over which a single batch meets the criterion while the other parameters
vary within their NORs. It is computed by Monte Carlo at 81 grid points across the
range studied, with 2,000 operating points at each. Each other parameter is drawn



                                           23
from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with a standard deviation of one
sixth of its NOR, clipped to its range studied, and the coefficients of the model are
drawn from their estimated covariance together with the residual. At each grid point
the 97.5th percentile of the simulated batches must meet an upper criterion, so that
at least 97.5 % of them meet it. The NOR-propagated PAR is the one that supports a
PAR carried into the control strategy. The design space is computed on the median
predictions of pool host cell protein, on a grid of 41 points per factor over the range
studied (Section 6).
Because no selected model meets criterion 5, each output with a criterion was also
examined by an influence analysis, in which each run with a Cook’s distance above
4/n is deleted in turn, the model selection repeated and the output judged over the
NOR again, and by the size of each residual trend against the criterion of its output
(Section 5.5, discrepancy PD-005-02).
Capability at commercial scale was estimated from the integrated process model of
the train, which runs 2,000 simulated batches with each parameter drawn within its
NOR. Capability is reported as Cpk against the drug substance criterion, one-sided
for a criterion with one limit and the lower of the two one-sided indices for a two-
sided criterion. It is judged against the target of 1.33 and the minimum of 1.00 of
PCMP-001 §7.9. The same simulation counts the batches outside each drug substance
criterion. Where none falls outside, the probability of an out-of-specification batch in
the simulation is bounded by the one-sided 95 % upper confidence limit of a proportion
with no event in 2,000 trials.

4.5 Hold study of the Protein A eluate

The Protein A eluate is held at elution pH before viral inactivation. The hold study
S-PA-HLD (2026-09-19 to 2026-09-28) was made during the Version 2.0 re-analysis,
after INV-001 closed, and is recorded as the discrepancy PD-005-05 (Section 13.3.2).
One eluate was produced at set-point, with elution buffer pH 3.55, divided and held
at 2-8 °C and 21 °C for up to 7 days. Samples were taken on days 0, 1, 3, 5 and 7 at
each temperature, and aggregate by SEC, acidic variants by icIEF and fragments by
nrCE-SDS were measured at every sample point.
The plan set no hold criterion, because it foresaw no hold study. The held eluate is the
load of low-pH viral inactivation, so it is judged against the intermediate acceptance
criteria of that load, which the rule of PCMP-001 §7.3 gives. For aggregate and acidic
variants they equal the criteria of the production bioreactor output, because capture
is expected to leave both unchanged (Section 2), and for fragments that of the Protein
A pool. This report does not test the expectation for aggregate and acidic variants,
because neither was measured in the load of this step. For aggregate the criterion
is ≤ 6.46 % HMW. It is the drug substance limit of 5 % multiplied by the aggregate
clearance of cation exchange, 1.404-fold, less the aggregate increase of low-pH viral
inactivation, 0.559 percentage points. Each value is the one-sided 95 % prediction
bound of a single batch of its step, which replaced a platform worst case (PCMP-001
§7.5). Cation exchange cleared less aggregate within its NOR than its platform worst
case of 2.00-fold, which made the criterion stricter. Low-pH viral inactivation added
less aggregate than its platform worst case of 0.70 points, which made it wider. The
net change, from ≤ 9.30 % a priori to ≤ 6.46 %, makes the criterion stricter. The
platform aggregate increase was measured over a hold of 180 min, the longest hold
studied at low pH, and the A-Mab bound within the hold-time NOR as characterized,
which ends at 120 min. The bound is smaller because the A-Mab hold is shorter, not
because the product is more stable. At the edges of the range studied A-Mab added
up to 0.84 points of aggregate, more than the platform value of 0.70, and within
the NOR at most 0.53 points. The aggregate criterion is still right, because routine
manufacture does not hold longer than the NOR.
For acidic variants the criterion is 18–39 %, its a priori value. Its upper limit is the
drug substance limit of 40 % less the platform worst case of the increase in low-pH
viral inactivation, 1.00 percentage points (Section 7), and its lower limit is that of the
drug substance. Fragments are judged against the intermediate acceptance criterion
of the pool, ≤ 2.45 % by nrCE-SDS.
Against these criteria the report judges what the hold adds to the eluate, by a hold
rule of its own that is part of the discrepancy PD-005-05. A hold time is supported
at a temperature when the largest increase from day 0 up to that time, added to the
highest level expected in the Protein A pool in manufacture, stays within its criterion.
For aggregate and acidic variants the highest level expected is the upper prediction
limit of the production bioreactor model over its NOR (PCR-003), and for fragments
that of the fragment model of this step, 1.09 % (Section 5.3.4). Acidic variants are
also judged against their lower limit (INV-001 finding 4).

5.8 Mechanistic interpretation

Pool host cell protein rises as elution buffer pH falls. A lower pH desorbs more of
the host cell protein held weakly on the resin backbone and on the ligand, and this
protein leaves the column with the product. On the log scale the pH effect is linear,
so each step down in pH multiplies pool host cell protein by the same factor.
Pool host cell protein also rises with protein load, as in the A-Mab case study (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). PCP-005 assumed no mechanism for this rise (PCP-
005 §4.1), and the designs do not test one. One explanation would give effects that
multiply. Suppose a higher load leaves more host cell protein held weakly on the resin
for each gram of antibody bound, and a lower elution pH releases a larger share of
what is held. The host cell protein in the pool per mg of antibody is then the product
of the two. The designs do not distinguish this explanation from others.
End of pool collect lowers pool host cell protein per mass of antibody. The weakly
held impurity desorbs early, at the elution front, so collecting further into the tail of
the peak adds antibody with little host cell protein. This does not explain why the
effect had the opposite sign in the screening design (finding F11).
Pool host cell protein is slightly higher at both ends of the flow range than at the
set-point. A short residence time at high flow lowers the dynamic binding capacity
and broadens the mass transfer zone, which could raise pool host cell protein, but
the same mechanism predicts no rise at low flow. The rise is at most 14 % across the
range studied and 4 % within the NOR, and it is not interpreted further.
Step yield behaves as Section 2 expected. Load flow rate costs yield only at high load,
where the shorter residence time lets antibody break through near the capacity of
the bed. The gain from extending the end of pool collect becomes smaller as the tail
of the elution peak is exhausted, which is the negative square of the model.
Leached Protein A shows no dependence on the parameters within the range studied.
Neither of the two opposite effects of protein load expected in Section 2, a longer
exposure of the ligand to the harvest proteases and a dilution of the leached ligand
over more antibody, is resolved against the variation of the runs. The offset of leached
Protein A between the scales is expected to come from the protease activity of the
held load, which is not a setting of a run (Section 3.1).
Fragments by nrCE-SDS fall slightly as elution pH rises, which is the direction ex-
pected from acid-catalysed hinge hydrolysis during elution. The effect is small be-
cause the antibody spends only the duration of the elution at low pH in the column.
For operation, the risk to pool host cell protein lies at high protein load combined
with low elution buffer pH, never with both inside their NORs (Section 6). Within
their NORs, end of pool collect moves pool host cell protein by 8 % and load flow rate
by at most 4 %.

7.1 Acceptance basis

Each output of the step is judged against the intermediate acceptance criterion that
PCMP-001 §7.3 derives and that PCP-005 §8.1 applies to this step. The derivation is
a priori, which here means that it uses the drug substance criteria and the platform
worst cases of X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and no data from this campaign. The drug
substance criterion is carried backward through the train with the worst case that
each downstream step showed on the platform, and no margin is applied. PCMP-001
Version 2.0, which states this rule and the one below, took effect on 2026-09-30, after
every study of this report had ended.
PCMP-001 §7.5 then checks each platform worst case against the A-Mab results of
its step. The check uses the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch, at
the least favourable combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the response-
surface factors of the downstream step, on its unfavourable side. These are the NORs
as characterized. Where the worst case that the model predicts within the NOR is
worse than the platform value, the bound replaces the platform value and the criteria
upstream become stricter. Where the bound is better than the platform value and
the model passes the adequacy tests of PCMP-001 §7.2 on lack of fit and predictive
power, the bound replaces the platform value and the criteria upstream become wider.
Otherwise the platform value stands. A point prediction never replaces it.
The bound is that of a single batch, not a confidence bound of the mean, for three
reasons that PCMP-001 §7.5 gives. A quality attribute is judged on a single batch,
and a single batch can fall beyond a bound of the mean. The platform worst case that
the bound may replace was itself the result of a single run. A bound from a tolerance
interval, which covers a stated share of all batches, would be statistically purer, but
too conservative to set against a platform worst case of one run. Table 7.1 gives the
downstream values on which the criteria of this step rely.

value. The 40-fold of cation exchange and the 4.27-fold of anion exchange give a
combined clearance of 171-fold and the criterion in force of ≤ 17,069 ng/mg, 7 %
wider than the a priori value. At the edges of their ranges studied, outside their
NORs, the lowest clearance measured in a run was 30.3-fold at cation exchange and
3.63-fold at anion exchange, both below their platform values. The criterion therefore
relies on both steps running within their NORs (PCR-007, PCR-008).
The widening of the criterion for pool host cell protein is argued by its risk, in the
four parts that PCMP-001 §7.5 asks for.

  1. The risk is the producer’s. A wider criterion admits a Protein A pool with more
     host cell protein, and a drug substance batch that then exceeded its criterion
     would be rejected at release.
  2. An excursion would be detected at release of the drug substance, where host cell
     protein is tested by ELISA (AMV-3012). The limit of quantitation of the ELISA, 1
     ng/mg, is far below the criterion of ≤ 100 ng/mg. No figure is available to this
     report for the coverage of the ELISA, the share of the host cell proteins that its
     antibodies recognise.
  3. A pool at the criterion meets the drug substance limit only if cation exchange
     clears at least 40-fold, to the cation exchange pool criterion of ≤ 427 ng/mg, and
     anion exchange at least 4.27-fold. Cation exchange is credited with its platform
     value, which lies above its own single-batch bound of 36.7-fold. If both steps
     cleared only their single-batch bounds, a pool at the criterion would give 109
     ng/mg in the drug substance, above the limit. Over the NOR the upper prediction
     limit of a single pool is 15,349 ng/mg, below the criterion.
  4. In the integrated process model no simulated batch of 2,000 exceeded the host
     cell protein criterion of the drug substance, and the normal tail probability is
     below one per million (Section 8).

The widening of the criterion for pool host cell protein rests on the model selection of

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001 §7.7.
A parameter whose effect on a CQA is significant and practically relevant is critical. A
critical parameter is a WC-CPP when it has a low risk of leaving its acceptable range
in operation, and a CPP otherwise. A KPP affects only process performance, and a
GPP has no relevant effect. The practical-relevance rule of PCP-005 §7, part of its
classification criterion (criterion 7), states that an effect on an attribute is practically
relevant when the NOR-propagated PAR of the parameter is narrower than its range
studied. It also states that a significant effect whose PAR spans the whole range stud-
ied does not by itself make the parameter critical. The rule identifies the effect of
elution buffer pH on pool host cell protein as practically relevant, because its NOR-
propagated PAR, 3.21–3.9, is narrower than its range studied (Section 7). Every other
NOR-propagated PAR spans its range studied, so the rule identifies no other param-
eter on its own. The class of each parameter therefore also rests on the size of its
effects, on whether an effect takes an attribute beyond its criterion within the range
studied, and on how readily the parameter is controlled. The classes and the reason
for each are the following.

   • Protein load was classified as a WC-CPP. It raises pool host cell protein by a
     factor of 2.43 across its range studied. Together with elution buffer pH it takes
     pool host cell protein above its criterion within the range studied, and 7 results
     of the designs at the corner of high load and low pH exceeded the criterion
     (Section 5.3.1). The load is set from the load volume and the titre of the clarified
     harvest by Protein A affinity HPLC (AMV-3021). It is readily controlled within
     its NOR of 20–39 g/L resin, inside a PAR that spans the range studied of 10–50
     g/L resin. The NOR in force was narrowed in this revision from 20–40 g/L resin,
     the NOR as characterized, because over that NOR the scale-down model is not
     qualified for pool host cell protein (Section 3.1).
   • Elution buffer pH was classified as a WC-CPP. It has the largest effect on pool
     host cell protein, a factor of 3.30 across its range studied, which the practical-
     relevance rule identifies as practically relevant, and fragments fall slightly as
     it rises. With protein load it bounds the design space. Buffer pH is controlled
     at preparation and verified at release. Its NOR-propagated PAR for pool host
     cell protein, 3.21–3.9, is wide against its NOR of 3.4–3.7, whose lower edge lies
     0.19 pH units inside the PAR. The deviation DEV-005-01 showed that a buffer
     below the NOR can pass release when the pH meter is incorrectly calibrated
     (Section 13.1).
   • Load flow rate was classified as a KPP. It lowers step yield at high load, because a
     faster load shortens the residence time and lowers the dynamic binding capacity.
     Its only effect on a CQA is the curvature of pool host cell protein, a rise of at
     most 4 % within its NOR and 14 % across its range studied. Within the NOR



                                            52
    that does not move pool host cell protein appreciably against its criterion. Its
    NOR-propagated PAR spans the range studied for every output, so the practical-
    relevance rule does not make it critical. No effect of load flow rate on leached
    Protein A or fragments was detected within the range studied.
  • End of pool collect was classified as a KPP. It has the largest effect on step yield.
    It also lowers pool host cell protein, by 15 % across its range studied and 8 %
    across its NOR, and the sign of that effect differs between the designs (finding
    F11). The effect does not take pool host cell protein beyond its criterion with the
    other parameters within their NORs, and its PAR spans the range studied. The
    class would be revisited if a later study confirmed the screening sign.
  • Operating temperature was classified as a GPP. No difference from the set-point
    runs was detected in pool host cell protein, step yield or leached Protein A at
    either end of its range. The intervals allow a rise in pool host cell protein of

    (Section 5.6). The class rests on the attributes measured. Fragments were not
    measured in the univariate study, and a higher temperature could speed hinge

  • Bed height was classified as a GPP. No difference from the set-point runs was

    ments were not measured in the univariate study, and a shorter bed shortens
    the residence time, which could change the capture of fragments.

In summary the step has 2 WC-CPPs, 2 KPPs and 2 GPPs. No parameter is in the class
CPP. The two WC-CPPs are critical parameters that are well controlled. The classes
of protein load and elution buffer pH rest on the size of their effects on pool host cell
protein, on the practical-relevance rule for elution buffer pH, and on the corner where
the two together take it above its criterion. Both effects agree in direction with the
A-Mab case study. The KPP classes rest on the model for step yield, on the direction
of the yield effects in both designs, and on the mechanism of breakthrough and of
peak collection.

10 Contribution to the control strategy

The Protein A step contributes to the control strategy in the following ways.

  1. Process parameters. Protein load and elution buffer pH are controlled within
     their NORs as WC-CPPs, and load flow rate and end of pool collect within their
     NORs as KPPs. The NORs are those of PCP-005, except that of protein load,
     which this revision narrowed to 20–39 g/L resin (Section 3.1). Movement outside
     the NOR and within the PARs of Section 7 is assessed under change control.
  2. Material controls. Elution buffer is released against its pH specification, on a
     calibrated pH meter (DEV-005-01).
  3. Resin and load. The protein load is set from the titre of the clarified harvest by
     Protein A affinity HPLC (AMV-3021). Leached Protein A is highest on new resin
     and is expected to rise with the protease activity of the load. Resin use, cleaning
     and sanitization are controlled by SOP-2008. The hold of the clarified harvest
     before capture, the expected cause of the offset of leached Protein A between
     the scales, is characterized in PCR-004.
  4. Hold. An eluate made at set-point may be held at elution pH for up to 7 days at
     2-8 °C or 21 °C before viral inactivation. An eluate made at other settings within
     the NOR was not held (Section 5.7).
  5. Downstream clearance. Host cell protein, leached Protein A and fragments are
     further cleared by cation exchange (PCR-007) and anion exchange (PCR-008).
     The criterion for pool host cell protein relies on cation exchange clearing host cell
     protein at least 40-fold, its platform worst case, and anion exchange at least 4.27-
     fold, its single-batch bound, while both run within their NORs (Section 7). Host
     cell protein is tested at release of the drug substance. Viral clearance is claimed
     at low-pH viral inactivation (PCR-006), anion exchange and virus filtration (PCR-
     009), and not at this step.

The control strategy of the whole train is consolidated in PCMR-001.

11 Discussion

The characterization confirms the main effects expected for this step. Elution buffer
pH and protein load have the largest effects on pool host cell protein, and on the log
scale their effects multiply. Whether a further interaction of the two exists the data do
not decide (Section 5.3.1). End of pool collect, protein load and load flow rate have
the largest effects on step yield. Leached Protein A shows no dependence on the
parameters within the range studied, and fragments depend only slightly on elution
buffer pH. End of pool collect lowers pool host cell protein in the response-surface
design but raised it, not significantly, in the screening design, for a reason that was
not identified.
Model selection gave leached Protein A and fragments models that meet the adequacy
criteria on lack of fit and predictive power, which their full quadratic models do not.
The choice of model is a discrepancy from the plan, so its effect on the conclusions
was examined. The conclusion over the NOR is the same with each of the 9 models
within 2 AICc units of the selected model of pool host cell protein, and with run 23
analysed in each of three ways.
The qualification of the scale-down model for pool host cell protein holds over the
NOR in force and not over the protein load NOR as characterized. It rests on the
widened criterion, on the 6 set-point runs added after the planned runs had been
analysed, and on the choice of the selected model (Section 3.1). The offsets were
estimated at set-point only. The report therefore assumes that the ratio for pool host
cell protein and the difference for leached Protein A are the same across the ranges
studied, and this assumption is not tested. Fragments have no measured offset. Their
conclusions assume no difference between the scales, on the mechanism of capture
and on the bound from the hold study of the clarified harvest (Section 3.1).
The criterion for pool host cell protein links this step to the two later steps. It credits
cation exchange with its platform worst case of 40-fold, above the single-batch bound
of 36.7-fold of its A-Mab model, and anion exchange with its single-batch bound of
4.27-fold. The control of this step therefore depends on the control of those steps
(PCR-007, PCR-008), and an excursion would be detected at release of the drug sub-
stance (Section 7).
No selected model meets criterion 5, and the conclusions rest on the influence anal-
ysis and on the size of the two residual trends (PD-005-02). The re-assay of run 11
decides whether leached Protein A depends on end of pool collect, but not its judge-
ment against the criterion (Section 13.2). Each design used one feed, so the effect
of feed quality was not estimated. The univariate study cannot detect interactions,
has 2 runs at each end, takes its reference from the qualification study and did not
measure fragments. The hold study held one eluate, made at set-point.

13.2 DEV-005-02. Leached Protein A result of run 11 initially
     out of trend

The leached Protein A result of run 11 of the response-surface design was out of
trend at analytical review. The first result was 3.82 ppm in the scale-down model,
5.00 ppm at manufacturing scale. The sample was re-assayed under AMV-3016, and
the re-assay gave 2.12 ppm, 3.30 ppm at manufacturing scale. The first result was 1.8
times the re-assay. The investigation suspected carryover in the ELISA, which would
add analyte from a preceding sample, and did not confirm it. The re-assay result is
the one reported in Appendix B and used in the analysis, because the investigation
attributed the first result to that suspected cause. The first result was kept in the




                                                   58
record with its status marked. Both results lie inside the range of the other runs of
the design, and both are far below the criterion of ≤ 158 ppm.
With the first result in place of the re-assay, the analysis changes. The model selection
then keeps end of pool collect, and that model is not significant (p = 0.052). It fails cri-
terion 2, so leached Protein A would be judged on its measured results. Every result
of both designs then meets the criterion, the highest at 5.79 ppm at manufacturing
scale. The trend of the residuals on run order is then not significant (p = 0.064). The
judgement of leached Protein A against its criterion is therefore the same with either
result. Whether leached Protein A depends on end of pool collect, and whether its
residuals drift with run order, depends on which result is used.

13.3.1 PD-005-01. Supplementary set-point runs in the scale-down

PCP-005 planned 3 set-point runs of the scale-down model in the qualification study

S-PA-SDM2 (2026-09-07 to 2026-09-11), under the same procedures as the planned
runs. Table 13.3 gives the qualification on the 3 planned runs alone.
On the planned runs alone the scale-down model is not qualified for pool host cell
protein, even over the NOR in force. The ratio is 0.935 (0.795 to 1.100) on the planned

runs narrowed the half-width of the interval on the log scale by 46 %, and this made
the qualification possible. For leached Protein A the model is qualified on the planned
runs as well, with the scale-down model 1.60 ppm lower (0.43 to 2.76 ppm lower).
