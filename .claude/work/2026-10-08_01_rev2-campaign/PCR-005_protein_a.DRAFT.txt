Executive summary

This report presents the process characterization of the Protein A chromatography
step (Step 5) of the A-Mab drug substance process. Protein A chromatography is
the capture step of the purification train. It removes most of the host cell protein and
DNA of the clarified harvest, and it adds one impurity of its own, Protein A ligand that
leaches from the resin. The characterization studies were run under the approved
plan PCP-005 in a scale-down model of the commercial column, between 2025-11-05
and 2026-04-17. Further set-point runs of the scale-down model followed from 2026-
09-07 to 2026-09-11. The report follows the lifecycle approach to process validation
(U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011), the enhanced approach of ICH Q8 and
ICH Q11 (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012) and the A-Mab case
study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
This is Version 2.0 of the report. It re-analyses the experiments of Version 1.0 against
the criteria of the process characterization master plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0, issued
under the investigation INV-001 into acceptance criteria set after the data were seen.
It adds the fragment results by nrCE-SDS. Each output of the response-surface design
is analysed by model selection, which the investigation INV-002 into the adequacy of
the response-surface models applies to every report.
The step has 6 process parameters. Of these, 4 were studied in a 19-run screening
design followed by a 28-run response-surface design, and 2 were studied one factor
at a time. Protein load and elution buffer pH were classified as WC-CPPs, load flow
rate and end of pool collect as KPPs, and operating temperature and bed height as
GPPs. No parameter of the step was classified as a CPP.

  • F1. The scale-down model is qualified for pool host cell protein and leached
    Protein A over the NOR, on all 9 set-point runs and also on the 3 runs that PCP-
    005 planned. Pool host cell protein in the scale-down model is 0.973 times its
    value at manufacturing scale, a ratio of geometric means with a 90 % confidence
    interval of 0.891 to 1.063. Leached Protein A is 1.18 ppm lower in the scale-down
    model. The expected cause is protease in the clarified harvest, which cleaves
    more ligand from the resin when the load is held longer before capture, as it is
    at manufacturing scale (Section 3.1).
  • F2. Each output of the response-surface design was analysed by model selection,
    a discrepancy from the plan (PD-005-04). The scale was chosen from the Box-
    Cox profile, and the terms by the lowest AICc among the hierarchical submodels
    of the full quadratic model. Each selected model meets the adequacy criteria
    of PCP-005 §7 on lack of fit and predictive power, and each model with a term
    is significant. Pool host cell protein is modelled on the log scale (Section 3.5,
    Section 5.3).

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose primary mechanism of action
is antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. It is developed by Novacyte Bio-
logics and is being transferred from development at the sending site to commercial
drug substance manufacture at 15,000 L scale at the receiving site, under the Pro-
cess Transfer Plan PTP-001. The drug substance is purified by the platform train of
Protein A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small
virus retentive filtration and UF/DF.
Protein A chromatography is the first chromatographic step of the purification train.
It is operated in bind and elute mode. The resin binds the antibody from the clarified
harvest at neutral pH, while host cell protein, DNA and most other components of the
feed are removed in the load flow-through and the wash. A low-pH buffer elutes the
antibody into a small volume, and the eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation (Step
6) without neutralization. The step is the principal point of clearance for host cell
protein and DNA, and it partly clears fragments. It introduces one attribute of the
CQA register, leached Protein A. Although Protein A chromatography removes some
virus, no viral clearance claim is made for this step (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). The viral clearance claim of the process rests on low-pH inactivation, anion
exchange and virus filtration, and it is consolidated in PCMR-001.



1.2 Objectives

The objectives of the characterization were the following, as PCP-005 §2 states
them.

  1. To estimate the offset between the scale-down model and manufacturing scale
     for each output that the qualification compares, and to qualify the model by that
     offset for each of these outputs with a criterion.
  2. To identify which of the 4 parameters of the multivariate design, and which of
     their two-factor interactions, affect pool host cell protein, step yield and leached
     Protein A.
  3. To fit response-surface models for pool host cell protein, step yield, leached Pro-
     tein A and fragments by nrCE-SDS, and to judge their adequacy for prediction.
  4. To determine the PAR of each parameter of the multivariate design for each
     attribute of the Protein A pool with a criterion.

  5. To establish the effect of operating temperature and bed height over their ranges,
     one factor at a time.
  6. To establish how long, and at which temperature, the Protein A eluate may be
     held at elution pH before viral inactivation.
  7. To provide the data for the classification of every parameter of the step under
     SOP-4001, and to support the Stage 2 ranges and the control strategy.

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The same capture step has been used to purify the platform antibodies X-Mab, Y-Mab
and Z-Mab, and A-Mab through clinical manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). Staphylococcal Protein A, immobilized on the resin, binds the Fc region of
the antibody at the interface between the second and third constant domains of the
heavy chain. When the pH is lowered, histidine residues at this interface become
protonated, the interaction weakens, and the antibody is released.
Host cell protein reaches the eluate by two routes. Some binds weakly to the resin
backbone or to the ligand. Some associates with the antibody through electrostatic
and hydrophobic interactions, which the wash does not disrupt while the antibody
stays bound to the ligand. Host cell protein carried on the antibody leaves the column
with it at any elution pH. A lower elution pH desorbs more of the host cell protein
held on the resin, and that protein leaves the column with the product. Pool host
cell protein was therefore expected to rise as elution buffer pH falls. In the A-Mab
case study pool host cell protein also rose with protein load (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009), and the same direction was expected here. Host cell protein carried
on the antibody rises in proportion to the antibody, which leaves the value per mg
unchanged. PCP-005 therefore assumed no mechanism for the rise with load (PCP-
005 §4.1). The plan expected that the two effects could interact, and both designs
can estimate an interaction.
Step yield is lost when antibody breaks through during the load or remains on the
column at the end of collection. Breakthrough begins earlier as the load approaches
the dynamic binding capacity of the bed. The dynamic binding capacity itself falls
as the residence time shortens, because binding is limited by the diffusion of the
antibody into the pores of the bead. A higher load flow rate shortens the residence
time. This costs yield only when the load is close to the capacity. Yield was therefore
expected to fall with flow rate mainly at high load, and to rise as the end of pool collect
is extended into the tail of the elution peak.
Leached Protein A comes from hydrolysis of the coupling chemistry and from cleavage
of exposed ligand by proteases that the clarified harvest carries into the column. The
leached fragments elute with the antibody because they still bind Fc. Leaching is
highest on new resin, in its first cycles. A higher protein load or a lower load flow
rate lengthens the loading time, and with it the contact of the ligand with the harvest
proteases. Leached Protein A is reported per mass of antibody, and if a similar amount
of ligand leaches in each cycle the value in ppm falls as protein load rises. PCP-005




                                            14
expected leached Protein A to depend little on the parameters within their ranges
(PCP-005 §4.1).
An antibody fragment that still carries Fc is captured and elutes with the intact anti-
body. A Fab fragment lacks Fc and is expected to flow through with the load, so the
pool is expected to carry less fragment than the feed. Native Protein A also binds
the variable domain of heavy chains of the VH3 family, and a Fab fragment of such
an antibody can be captured as well. Whether the Fab of A-Mab binds the ligand of
this resin was not tested here. A lower elution pH may add a little acid-catalysed
hinge hydrolysis during elution. The pool also still carries host cell protein, part of
which elutes in the low molecular weight window of SEC and is read as fragment.
SEC therefore overstates fragments in this matrix, and nrCE-SDS does not (INV-001
finding 6). DNA is polyanionic and does not bind Protein A. Its clearance is large and
was not expected to depend on the operating parameters within their ranges.
Protein A, when operated under platform conditions, does not separate glycan vari-
ants or charge variants, because it binds the Fc region independently of both (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). Nor does it separate aggregate from monomer, since
both carry Fc. In the Protein A characterization of the case study no process parame-
ter had a significant effect on aggregate (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Aggre-
gate formation at low pH over the inactivation hold is characterized in PCR-006. For
these reasons glycan variants, charge variants and aggregate were not responses of
the designs.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

The pre-characterization risk assessment RA-001 assessed every parameter of the
step before characterization began. Each parameter was ranked on its potential im-
pact on the CQAs and on process performance, and on its potential to interact with
other parameters.
Protein load, elution buffer pH, load flow rate and end of pool collect were assigned
to multivariate study. Each carries an effect expected from the mechanism, and the
interactions of protein load with elution buffer pH and with load flow rate can only be
estimated in a design that varies the parameters together. Operating temperature
and bed height were assigned to univariate study. A higher temperature speeds the
diffusion of the antibody into the pores, which raises the dynamic binding capacity,
and it strengthens the hydrophobic contacts at the Fc–ligand interface, which makes
elution slightly less complete. Over the range studied both effects were expected to
be small against those of load and pH, and the resin manufacturer specifies binding
capacity across that range. Bed height is held by the packing specification in manu-
facture, and at constant linear velocity it sets the residence time.
The ranges studied are between 1.9 and 2.3 times the width of the NOR. They show
how the step behaves near and beyond the edges of routine operation, and they bound
the knowledge space within which a PAR can be claimed.

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model is a laboratory column packed with the commercial Protein A
resin and operated under SOP-2007 on the scaling principles of PCMP-001 §5. The
bed height and the residence time of the commercial column were kept. Load, wash
and elution volumes were normalized to column volumes, and buffers were made to
the commercial recipes. Column efficiency was checked by plate count and peak
asymmetry after packing. PCP-005 sets no criterion for either, and both are reported
for information.
The model was qualified under SOP-1001 by its offset from manufacturing scale, the
rule of PCMP-001 §7.1 that PCP-005 §5.1 applies to this step. Set-point runs of the
scale-down model were compared with 5 manufacturing-scale batches of A-Mab run
at set-point. The outputs compared were pool host cell protein, leached Protein A,
step yield, and column efficiency as plates per metre and peak asymmetry. The offset
of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of the manufacturing-
scale batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval from a Student t interval
with pooled variance. It is taken on the scale on which the output is modelled (Sec-
tion 3.5). Pool host cell protein is modelled on the log scale. Its offset is therefore
the difference of the mean logarithms, reported as the ratio of the geometric means
of the two scales. A scale-down result of pool host cell protein is divided by this ratio
before it is judged against a criterion, and a result of any other judged output has its
offset subtracted. Every range and every conclusion against a criterion in this report
is therefore at manufacturing scale.
The model is qualified for an output when the conclusion against its criterion over
the NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its confidence interval. That
conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every combination of NOR
low, set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface factors, judged on the upper
prediction limit of a single run (Section 3.5). Step yield and column efficiency have
no criterion at the step. Their offsets are recorded, and no conclusion depends on
them.
PCP-005 planned 3 set-point runs of the scale-down model in the qualification study
S-PA-SDM (2025-11-05 to 2025-11-14), before the first run of the screening design. A
further 6 set-point runs were made in study S-PA-SDM2 (2026-09-07 to 2026-09-11)
during the Version 2.0 re-analysis, to narrow the confidence interval of the offset for
pool host cell protein. The plan did not foresee these runs, and they are recorded as
the discrepancy from the plan PD-005-01 (Section 13.3.1). Table 3.1 gives the offsets
over all 9 scale-down runs, with 12 degrees of freedom.

cleaves more ligand from the resin during loading. The cleaved ligand still binds
Fc and elutes with the antibody. This study does not test that cause. Under the
qualification rule each leached Protein A result of the designs is raised by 1.18 ppm

of the clarified harvest within the 7 days of that study. The hold study of the eluate
measured attributes that the qualification did not compare, so its results also carry
no offset.



3.2 Operation

Each run followed SOP-2007 at laboratory scale. The column was equilibrated, loaded
with clarified harvest to the target protein load, washed, and eluted with the elution
buffer at the pH set by the design. The eluate was collected from the rise of the ab-
sorbance at 280 nm until the end of pool collect set by the design, in column volumes.
The column was then stripped, cleaned and stored under SOP-2008. The pool was
not neutralized, because the commercial eluate passes to low-pH viral inactivation at
elution pH.
Each design was loaded from one pool of clarified harvest produced at set-point and
held frozen in aliquots. A single feed keeps feed variation out of the estimated effects.
The effect of feed quality on the step was therefore not estimated in these studies. The
case study reports that Protein A capture is robust over a wide range of feed inputs
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009), and the feed is characterized in PCR-003 and
PCR-004. Runs were executed in random order within each design. The resin was
used within the life-cycle limits of SOP-2008, and the life cycle of the resin is outside
the scope of this report.

3.5 Statistical methods

The designs were built and analysed under SOP-1002 with the common approach of
PCMP-001 §6. Factors were coded from −1 to +1 over their range studied, with 0
at the centre. For each of the 4 design factors the centre of the range studied is the
set-point, so the centre-point runs of both designs are set-point runs.
The screening data were fitted with a model of main effects and two-factor interac-
tions. Effects are reported as the change in the response over the coded range of
the factor, which is twice the coefficient. Significance was judged at α = 0.05. The
screening model identifies effects. A two-level design cannot resolve curvature, so
the screening model was not used to predict between its corners.
PCP-005 §5.5 planned to fit the full quadratic model of 15 terms to each output of the
response-surface design. Each output was analysed instead by model selection, the



                                               21
analysis that INV-002 introduced as its corrective action CA3 on 2026-09-15 for every
report with a response-surface design. The selection departs from PCP-005, and it is
recorded as the discrepancy from the plan PD-005-04 (Section 13.3.4). PCMP-001 is
not changed by it. The selection has four steps.

  1. Scale. The Box-Cox transformation (Box and Cox 1964) was applied to the full
     quadratic model, and the profile likelihood of its exponent λ gives a 95 % interval
     for λ. An output is modelled on the log scale (λ = 0) when that interval contains
     0 and excludes 1, and on the raw scale otherwise. A log scale requires a positive
     output.
  2. Terms. Every hierarchical submodel of the full quadratic model was fitted. In
     a hierarchical model an interaction appears only with both of its main effects,
     and a square only with its main effect. The model with the lowest AICc, Akaike’s
     information criterion with the correction for small samples (Hurvich and Tsai
     1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002), was selected. Every submodel was fitted,
     because a stepwise search can stop at a model with a higher AICc.
  3. Check. The selected model was judged against the model adequacy criteria of
     PCP-005 §7 listed below. A model with no term has no F-test. It shows that, within
     the range studied, the output does not depend on the parameters by more than
     the variation of the runs can resolve.
  4. Offset. The scale-down offset of each output was taken on the scale of its model
     (Section 3.1).

The full quadratic model keeps all 14 of its terms whatever the data support. A term
with no effect adds its estimation error to every prediction and widens every predic-
tion interval. The limit on how far predicted R² may fall short of R² guards against
such overfitting (Montgomery 2017). AICc weighs the fit of a model against the num-
ber of its terms, and its correction for small samples matters at 28 runs. On the log
scale an effect acts as a factor on the output. A back-transformed mean prediction is
therefore the median of the output, not its mean. A prediction interval is computed
on the log scale and back-transformed. Its coverage stays exact, and in the units of
the output it is wider above the median than below. Coefficients of a log-scale model
are in natural-log units per half range, and a coefficient b changes the output by a
factor of exp(2b) across the range studied.
Each model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R² (from PRESS), the model
F-test, and the test of lack of fit against pure error from the centre points. Coefficients
are reported in coded units, so that each measures the change in the response over
half the range studied. The adequacy criteria of PCP-005 §7 were applied as follows.
The numbers are those of PCP-005 §7, where criterion 1 is the qualification of the
scale-down model (Section 3.1).

  2. Model significance. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Lack of fit. The lack of fit test is not significant at α = 0.05.
  4. Predictive power. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20.
  5. Residuals. Regressions of the absolute residuals on the predicted values, and of
     the residuals on run order, show no slope significant at α = 0.05, and no run has
     a Cook’s distance above 4/n (0.14 for 28 runs).




                                           22
PCMP-001 §7.2 provides that a model that fails the lack of fit test or the limit on
predictive power is refitted with a justified change of terms or transformation, or is
not used to set a range.
Each output with a criterion is judged over the NOR at every combination of NOR low,
set-point and NOR high of the 4 response-surface factors. The value compared with
the criterion is the upper end of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run, which
includes the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients and the residual. The criteria of this
step are upper limits, so only that end is compared, which makes it a one-sided 97.5
% prediction limit (PCP-005 §7). This report calls it the upper prediction limit.
The PAR analysis follows PCMP-001 §6.4. The set-point PAR is a univariate PAR with
the other parameters at their set-points, judged on the mean prediction. The NOR-
propagated PAR is the range of a parameter over which a single batch meets the cri-
terion while the other parameters vary within their NORs. It is computed by Monte
Carlo at 81 grid points across the range studied, with 2,000 operating points at each.
Each other parameter is drawn from a normal distribution centred on its set-point,
with a standard deviation of one sixth of its NOR, and the coefficients of the model
are drawn from their estimated covariance together with the residual. The NOR-
propagated PAR is the one that supports a claim. The design space is computed
on mean predictions, on a grid of 41 points per factor over the range studied (Sec-
tion 6).
The influence analysis deletes each run with a Cook’s distance above 4/n in turn,
repeats the model selection, and judges the output over the NOR again (Section 5.5).
PCP-005 does not describe it, and it is recorded as the discrepancy from the plan
PD-005-02.
Capability at commercial scale was estimated from the integrated process model of
the train, which runs 2,000 simulated batches with each parameter drawn within its
NOR. Capability is reported as Cpk against the drug substance criterion, one-sided
for a criterion with one limit and the lower of the two one-sided indices for a two-sided
criterion. It is judged against the target of 1.33 and the minimum of 1.00 of PCMP-001
§7.9. The same simulation counts the batches outside each drug substance criterion.
Where none falls outside, the probability of an out-of-specification batch is bounded
by the one-sided 95 % upper confidence limit of a proportion with no event in 2,000
trials.

4.2 Screening design

The screening design (study S-PA-SCR, 2025-11-17 to 2025-12-05) was a full two-level
factorial design in the 4 factors, with 16 factorial runs and 3 centre points, 19 runs
in all. Because the design is a full factorial, no effect is aliased with another, and
every main effect and every two-factor interaction can be estimated. The responses
were pool host cell protein, step yield and leached Protein A, with fragments by SEC
recorded for information. All 4 factors were carried into the response-surface design
whatever the screening result, as PCP-005 §6.2 planned. The screening design there-
fore served to identify effects and interactions and to check the ranges. Appendix A
gives the matrix and the results.



4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface design (study S-PA-RSM, 2026-01-12 to 2026-02-06) was a face-
centred central composite design in the same 4 factors. It has 16 factorial runs, 8
axial runs at the faces of the cube and 4 centre points, 28 runs in all. A face-centred
design keeps every run inside the range studied. The full quadratic model would
leave 13 residual degrees of freedom, with 3 for pure error and 10 for lack of fit. The
responses were pool host cell protein, step yield, leached Protein A and fragments by
nrCE-SDS on the retained pools. Appendix B gives the matrix and the results.



4.4 Univariate assessment

Operating temperature and bed height were studied one factor at a time in study S-PA-
UNI (2025-12-08 to 2025-12-12), with the other parameters at their set-points. Each
was run 2 times at the low and the high end of its range studied. The 3 set-point runs
of the planned qualification study S-PA-SDM served as the reference, as PCP-005 §6.4

4.5 Hold study of the Protein A eluate

The Protein A eluate is held at elution pH before viral inactivation. A pool produced at
set-point was divided and held at 2-8 °C and 21 °C for up to 7 days in study S-PA-HLD
(2026-02-02 to 2026-02-16). Samples were taken on days 0, 1, 3, 5 and 7 at each
temperature. Aggregate and fragments were measured by SEC, and acidic variants
by icIEF, at every sample point. The samples of days 0 and 7 were retained and tested
by nrCE-SDS in study S-PA-RET.
Protein A binds the Fc region of monomer, aggregate and every charge variant alike,
so capture does not change the levels of aggregate and acidic variants. The held
eluate is therefore judged against the criteria in force for the output of the production
bioreactor (PCMP-001 §7.3). For aggregate the criterion is ≤ 7.04 % HMW. It is the
drug substance limit of 5 % multiplied by the aggregate clearance of cation exchange,
1.52-fold, less the aggregate increase of low-pH viral inactivation, 0.54 percentage
points. Both downstream values are 95 % confidence bounds that replaced platform
worst cases, and together they moved the criterion from ≤ 9.30 % a priori (Section 7).
For acidic variants the criterion is 18–39.6 %. Its upper limit is the drug substance
limit of 40 % less the largest increase of low-pH viral inactivation, 0.40 percentage
points at its 95 % confidence bound, and its lower limit is that of the drug substance.
Fragments are judged against the intermediate acceptance criterion of the pool, ≤
2.45 % by nrCE-SDS, on the days with an nrCE-SDS result.
The hold criterion of PCP-005 (criterion 7) limits the increase during the hold, not the
level measured in the one held pool. The increase at a sample point is the result at that
time minus the result on day 0 at the same temperature. A hold time is supported
at a temperature when the largest increase up to that time, added to the highest
level expected in the Protein A pool in manufacture, stays within the criterion. For
aggregate and acidic variants the highest level expected is the upper prediction limit
of the production bioreactor model over its NOR (PCR-003). For fragments it is the
upper prediction limit of the fragment model of this step over its NOR (Section 5.3.4).
Acidic variants have a two-sided criterion, and they are also judged against its lower
limit. There the largest fall over the hold is added to the lowest level expected, the
lower end of the 95 % prediction interval of the production bioreactor model over its
NOR.

5.8 Mechanistic interpretation

Pool host cell protein rises as elution buffer pH falls. A lower pH desorbs more of the
host cell protein held weakly on the resin backbone and the ligand, and this protein
leaves the column with the product. Host cell protein that rides on the antibody leaves




                                                   38
with it at every pH studied and does not explain the pH effect. On the log scale the
pH effect is linear, so each step down in pH multiplies pool host cell protein by the
same factor, whatever the protein load.
Pool host cell protein also rises with protein load, as in the A-Mab case study (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009). As the load approaches the dynamic binding capacity,
the mass transfer zone is expected to extend further down the bed, and host cell
protein that the wash would otherwise displace is then carried into the eluate. The
designs do not test this mechanism. It is consistent with effects that multiply, because
the load then sets how much host cell protein is held weakly on the column and the
elution pH sets the fraction of it that is released.
End of pool collect lowers pool host cell protein per mass of antibody. The weakly
held impurity desorbs early, at the elution front, so collecting further into the tail of
the peak adds antibody with little host cell protein. The mechanism does not explain
why the effect had the opposite sign in the screening design (finding F11).
Load flow rate acts on pool host cell protein only through a small curvature, with a
rise at both ends of its range. The broader mass transfer zone at a short residence
time would explain a rise at high flow, and the mechanism of Section 2 predicts no
rise at low flow. The effect is at most 14 % across the range studied and 4 % within
the NOR, and it is not interpreted further.
Step yield falls with load flow rate only at high load. A faster load shortens the res-
idence time, which lowers the dynamic binding capacity, and near the capacity of
the bed this lets antibody break through. At low load the bed has capacity to spare.
Extending the end of pool collect recovers antibody from the tail of the elution peak.
The gain becomes smaller as the tail is exhausted, which is the negative square of the
model.
Leached Protein A shows no dependence on the parameters within the range studied.
The two effects of protein load expected in Section 2 act in opposite directions. A
longer loading time exposes the ligand longer to the harvest proteases, and a larger
load spreads the leached ligand over more antibody. Neither is resolved against the
variation of the runs. The offset of leached Protein A between the scales is expected
to reflect the protease activity of the held load, which is not a setting of a run (Sec-
tion 3.1).
Fragments by nrCE-SDS fall slightly as elution pH rises, which is the direction ex-
pected from acid-catalysed hinge hydrolysis during elution. The effect is small be-
cause the antibody spends only the duration of the elution at low pH in the column.
For operation, the risk to pool host cell protein lies at high protein load combined
with low elution buffer pH, outside both NORs. Within their NORs, end of pool collect
moves pool host cell protein by 8 % and load flow rate by at most 4 %.

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001 §7.7.
The classification rule of PCP-005 §7 (criterion 8) adds that an effect on an attribute
is practically relevant when the NOR-propagated PAR of the parameter is narrower
than its range studied. It also states that a significant effect whose PAR spans the
whole range studied does not by itself make the parameter critical. At this step every
NOR-propagated PAR spans its range studied (Section 7), so criterion 8 identifies no
parameter on its own. The class of each parameter therefore rests on the size of its
effects, on whether an effect takes an attribute beyond its criterion within the range
studied, and on how readily the parameter is controlled. The classes and the reason
for each are the following.

  • Protein load was classified as a WC-CPP. It raises pool host cell protein by a
    factor of 2.43 across its range studied. Together with elution buffer pH it takes
    pool host cell protein above its criterion within the range studied, and 3 results
    of the designs at the corner of high load and low pH exceeded the criterion
    (Section 5.3.1). The load is set by the load volume and the titre, and it is readily
    controlled within its NOR of 20–40 g/L resin, inside a PAR that spans the range
    studied of 10–50 g/L resin.
  • Elution buffer pH was classified as a WC-CPP. It has the largest effect on pool
    host cell protein, a factor of 3.30 across its range studied, and fragments fall
    slightly as it rises. With protein load it bounds the design space. Buffer pH is
    controlled at preparation and verified at release, and its PAR spans the range
    studied of 3.2–3.9, wide against its NOR of 3.4–3.7. The deviation DEV-005-01
    showed that a buffer below the NOR can pass release when the pH meter is
    incorrectly calibrated (Section 13.1).
  • Load flow rate was classified as a KPP. It lowers step yield at high load, because a
    faster load shortens the residence time and lowers the dynamic binding capacity.
    Its only effect on a CQA is a curvature on pool host cell protein of at most 4 %
    within its NOR and 14 % across its range studied, too small to move pool host
    cell protein appreciably against its criterion. It has no effect on leached Protein
    A or fragments.
  • End of pool collect was classified as a KPP. It has the largest effect on step yield.
    It also lowers pool host cell protein, by 15 % across its range studied and 8 %
    across its NOR, and the sign of that effect differs between the designs (finding
    F11). The effect does not take pool host cell protein beyond its criterion with the
    other parameters within their NORs, and its PAR spans the range studied. The
    class would be revisited if a later study confirmed the screening sign.
  • Operating temperature was classified as a GPP. No difference from the set-point
    runs was detected at either end of its range. The intervals allow a rise in pool
    host cell protein of up to 4,291 ng/mg. Such a rise would still leave pool host cell



                                          48
    protein within its criterion at manufacturing scale, and every individual result
    met its criterion (Section 5.6).
  • Bed height was classified as a GPP. No difference from the set-point runs was
    detected at either end of its range. The intervals allow a rise in pool host cell
    protein of up to 2,815 ng/mg, and every individual result met its criterion (Sec-
    tion 5.6). Bed height is fixed by the packing specification of the commercial
    column and does not vary in operation. A short bed at the upper end of the load
    range was not characterized, and no claim is made for it.

In summary the step has 2 WC-CPPs, 2 KPPs and 2 GPPs, and no CPP. The classes of
protein load and elution buffer pH rest on the size of their effects on pool host cell
protein and on the corner where the two together take it above its criterion. Both
effects agree in direction with the A-Mab case study. The KPP classes rest on the
model for step yield, on the direction of the yield effects in both designs, and on the
mechanism of breakthrough and of peak collection.

10 Contribution to the control strategy

The Protein A step contributes to the control strategy in the following ways.

  1. Process parameters. Protein load and elution buffer pH are controlled within
     their NORs as WC-CPPs, and load flow rate and end of pool collect within their
     NORs as KPPs. The NORs are those of PCP-005. Movement outside the NOR
     and within the PARs of Section 7 is assessed under change control.
  2. Material controls. Elution buffer is released against its pH specification, on a
     calibrated pH meter (DEV-005-01).
  3. Resin and load. Leached Protein A is highest on new resin and is expected to
     rise with the protease activity of the load. Resin use, cleaning and sanitization
     are controlled by SOP-2008. The hold of the clarified harvest before capture, the
     expected cause of the offset of leached Protein A between the scales, is charac-
     terized in PCR-004.
  4. Hold. The Protein A eluate may be held at elution pH for up to 7 days at 2-8 °C
     or 21 °C before viral inactivation.
  5. Downstream clearance. Host cell protein, leached Protein A and fragments are
     further cleared by cation exchange (PCR-007) and anion exchange (PCR-008).
     The criterion for pool host cell protein relies on both steps running within their
     NORs (Section 7). Viral clearance is claimed at low-pH viral inactivation (PCR-
     006), anion exchange and virus filtration (PCR-009), and not at this step.

The control strategy of the whole train is consolidated in PCMR-001.

11 Discussion

The characterization confirms the main effects expected for this step. Elution buffer
pH and protein load have the largest effects on pool host cell protein, and the two
effects multiply. On the raw scale the model of Version 1.0 described this as an inter-
action, and on the log scale none remains. This study was not designed to establish
the mechanism of the load effect. End of pool collect, protein load and load flow rate
have the largest effects on step yield. Leached Protein A does not depend on the
parameters within the range studied, and fragments depend only slightly on elution
buffer pH. One result does not follow the expectations. End of pool collect lowers
pool host cell protein in the response-surface design and raised it, not significantly,
in the screening design, for a reason that was not identified.
Model selection changed what the models of this step can support. The full quadratic
models of leached Protein A and fragments predict left-out runs worse than their own
mean. Their selected models meet the adequacy criteria and support PARs over the
whole range studied. The choice of model is a discrepancy from the plan, so its effect
on the conclusions was examined. The log scale is chosen however run 23 is analysed,
and with the run at its design pH, or excluded, the selection adds the interaction of
protein load with elution buffer pH. The conclusion over the NOR is the same in every
case (Section 13.1).
The scale-down model is qualified by its offset for the 2 outputs it compares that have
a criterion. For pool host cell protein the conclusion is the same at both ends of the
offset interval, on the planned runs alone and on all runs. The further runs narrowed
the interval of the ratio by 46 % on the log scale. The offsets were estimated at set-
point only. The report therefore assumes that the ratio for pool host cell protein and
the difference for leached Protein A are the same across the ranges studied, and this
assumption is not tested. Fragments carry no offset, and the hold study of the clarified
harvest bounds what a longer hold of the load could add to them (Section 3.1).
The criterion for pool host cell protein now depends on two later steps. It holds while
cation exchange and anion exchange run within their NORs, where their models show
a clearance better than the platform worst case at the 95 % bound. The control of
this step is therefore linked to the control of those steps, which their own reports set
(PCR-007, PCR-008).
No selected model meets the limit on influential runs, a Cook’s distance below 4/n. The
conclusions rest instead on the deletion of single runs, which showed that no run
decides them. The limit of 4/n is better suited to a design with low leverage than to a
face-centred design, whose factorial and axial runs carry high leverage. A limit based
on the leverage of each run would serve the purpose of the criterion better. The two
residual trends found are small against the criteria, and neither is significant once
the number of tests is allowed for.

The 2 deviations of the study did not change its conclusions. Run 23 is analysed at
the elution buffer pH it was made at (DEV-005-01). The leached Protein A result of
run 11 that was out of trend was replaced by a re-assay on a suspected cause, and
the re-assay result lies inside the range of the other runs (DEV-005-02).
The other limitations are those the plan named. Each design used one feed. The
univariate study cannot detect interactions, has 2 runs at each end, and takes its
reference from the qualification study. The laboratory column does not reproduce
the hold of the manufacturing load before capture, and the offset of leached Protein
A is expected to carry that difference.

12 Conclusions

 1. The step was characterized under PCP-005 in a screening design, a response-
    surface design, a univariate study and a hold study.
 2. The scale-down model is qualified by its offset for pool host cell protein and
    leached Protein A over the NOR, on all 9 set-point runs and on the 3 planned runs
    alone. Pool host cell protein is judged with a ratio offset and leached Protein A
    with a difference.
 3. Each output of the response-surface design was analysed by model selection.
    Each selected model meets the adequacy criteria on lack of fit and predictive
    power, and each model with a term is significant. No model meets the limit on
    influential runs, and the conclusions rest on an influence analysis.
 4. Elution buffer pH and protein load have the largest effects on pool host cell
    protein, and the two effects multiply. Pool host cell protein falls as the end of
    pool collect is extended. End of pool collect, protein load and load flow rate
    have the largest effects on step yield. Leached Protein A does not depend on
    the parameters within the range studied, and fragments fall slightly as elution
    buffer pH rises.
 5. Every output met its intermediate acceptance criterion across the NOR at man-
    ufacturing scale, and the NOR-propagated PAR of every parameter of the multi-
    variate design spans its range studied for every output. On the mean predictions
    of its model, pool host cell protein exceeds its criterion only at high protein load
    with low elution buffer pH, outside both NORs.
 6. Protein load and elution buffer pH are WC-CPPs, load flow rate and end of pool
    collect are KPPs, and operating temperature and bed height are GPPs. The step
    has no CPP.
 7. No difference from the set-point runs was detected for operating temperature or
    bed height, and their ranges studied are supported with the other parameters
    at their set-points.
 8. The eluate may be held at elution pH for 7 days at 2-8 °C or 21 °C. The lowest
    level of acidic variants expected in the pool is below the lower limit of 18 % before
    any hold, at the least favourable corner of the production bioreactor NOR, and
    the hold does not lower acidic variants.
 9. At commercial scale the Cpk of host cell protein and of fragments exceeds the
    target of 1.33. Leached Protein A, whose Cpk is not meaningful, is judged on
    the pool and in the drug substance against its criteria.
10. The 2 deviations were investigated and the data retained without changing the
    conclusions. The 4 discrepancies from the plan are recorded in Section 13. The
    results roll up into PCMR-001.

The elution buffer of lot LOT-BUF-5290 was used on run 23 of the response-surface

median predicted at pH 3.38, 10,368 ng/mg, which is 2.0 residual standard deviations.
Its Cook’s distance, 0.16, is above the limit of 4/n. At the design pH of 3.55 the same
model predicts 7,761 ng/mg, closer to the result. The result does not show the full
