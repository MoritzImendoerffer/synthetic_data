Executive summary

Low-pH viral inactivation is Step 6 of the A-Mab drug substance process. The Protein
A eluate is held at acid pH for a set time and then neutralized. The hold inactivates
enveloped viruses, and the step contributes one of the three claims that together
meet the cumulative requirement for clearance of xenotropic murine leukemia virus
(XMuLV), a log reduction of 6.60 log10 . The same conditions can add aggregate, frag-
ments and acidic variants to the pool. This report presents the Stage 1 characteri-
zation of the step under its plan PCP-006 and the master plan PCMP-001 (U.S. Food
and Drug Administration 2011). The studies were run in a scale-down model of the
step, qualified for aggregate and acidic variants, and in a small-scale spiking model,
at the development site in Cambridge, MA.
Inactivation pH, hold time and temperature were studied in a screening design of 11
runs and a response-surface design of 18 runs. A-Mab concentration was studied one
factor at a time. These characterization studies ran from 2026-01-07 to 2026-04-17.
Five further studies were made from 2026-09-19 to 2026-09-29, during the Version 2.0
re-analysis and after the results of the designs were known. They are discrepancies
from the plan and are listed in Section 13. The findings are the following.
F1. At the normal operating ranges (NORs) against which the studies were planned,
the XMuLV claim was not met. The lowest mean predicted log reduction was 6.16
log10 , at pH 3.6, 60 min and 19 °C, against the claim of 6.60 log10 . The pH set-point
and NOR were therefore moved to pH 3.4 and pH 3.3–3.5, the hold-time NOR was
narrowed to 100–120 min with its set-point at 110 min, and the temperature NOR
was narrowed to 20–23 °C. Across the NOR in force the lowest mean predicted log
reduction is 6.77 log10 , so the claim is met there.
F2. Aggregate, fragments and acidic variants meet their intermediate acceptance
criteria across the NOR in force. The upper limit of the 95 % prediction interval of
a single batch of aggregate is at most 2.10 % HMW at manufacturing scale, against
7.02 %. Fragments and acidic variants have no adequate model and are judged on
their measured results, 0.87 to 1.13 % and 20.2 to 20.6 % respectively. The 33 acidic
variant results of the designs and the concentration study show no scatter at a given
hold time, so no adequacy test can be applied to that output.
F3. The selected models of aggregate and of the XMuLV log reduction meet all four
model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2. Five runs carry a Cook’s distance above
the cut-off at which PCP-006 §7.1 calls for an examination, and each was deleted
in turn and the judgement repeated. The NOR in force stays inside every proven
acceptable range (PAR), the claim is met over it, the decision to move the NOR holds
and every class holds. With run 5 deleted, the temperature term leaves the XMuLV
model and the XMuLV ranges widen.

F4. The PARs are pH 3.2–3.61, 84–180 min and 17.2–25 °C, each set by XMuLV clear-
ance. The edges of the NOR in force lie 0.11 pH units, 16 min and 2.8 °C inside
them.
F5. Inactivation pH was classified as a critical process parameter (CPP). Hold time
and temperature were classified as well-controlled critical process parameters (WC-
CPPs), and A-Mab concentration as a general process parameter (GPP). The GPP class
rests on the mechanism of the step for XMuLV clearance, because the concentration
study cannot exclude a loss of clearance larger than the margin of the claim.
F6. Within the NOR as characterized the step adds at most 0.56 percentage points
of aggregate at the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch, against the
platform value of 0.70. That bound moved the aggregate criterion of the production
bioreactor output from 6.32 to 6.46 % HMW. At a hold of 180 min, outside the NOR, the

at the upper ends of their NORs, product quality met every criterion of the step and

more than the required fold, but the lower confidence bound of the three runs fell
below it, so the rule fixed for the study was not met. This was accepted on its risk

F8. The neutralized pool met its criteria after 7 days at 21 °C and 25 °C.

the target of 1.33. Acidic variants have the lowest Cpk of the attributes this step
changes, 1.05, which lies between the minimum of 1.00 and the target of 1.33. The
lower end of the criterion limits it, and the low-pH hold only raises acidic variants.
F10. Four deviations were recorded. Two were retained with a bounded impact. The
third, a clearance measurement the concentration study did not make, was closed by
a confirmation study. The fourth, an input pool the designs did not test, is resolved
by taking the increase across the step against the nominal load of the step model.

tion Master Report PCMR-001.

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose drug substance is made by
Novacyte Biologics. It is purified by the Novacyte platform train of Protein A cap-
ture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, virus filtration and
ultrafiltration and diafiltration (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The process is
transferred under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-001 from the development site in
Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI, where it runs
at the 15,000 L scale.
Low-pH viral inactivation follows Protein A capture. The Protein A eluate leaves the
column at low pH. It is adjusted to the inactivation pH, held for the hold time at the set
temperature and then neutralized with base. The neutralized pool is the load of cation
exchange. The step is operated under its standard operating procedure, SOP-2009.
The step contributes one of the three claims that meet the cumulative requirement
for clearance of the enveloped model virus XMuLV. The other two claims are those
of anion exchange and virus filtration. The step can also add aggregate, fragments
and acidic variants, which are formed upstream, so each of them has an intermediate
acceptance criterion at the output of this step (Section 7.1). No claim is made for the
parvovirus minute virus of mice (MVM), which has no lipid envelope for low pH to
disrupt. No clearance of host cell protein is claimed for the step.
This report is paired with the plan PCP-006, under which the studies were run. Its
results roll up into PCMR-001 with the reports of the other steps. Table 1.1 lists the
related documents.

1.2 Objectives

PCP-006 §2 set six objectives for the studies, and this report answers each of them.

  1. To quantify the effects of inactivation pH, hold time and temperature, and of
     their interactions, on XMuLV clearance, aggregate, fragments and acidic vari-
     ants (Section 5).
  2. To fit response-surface models that predict these outputs over the range stud-
     ied, and to test each model against the adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 (Sec-
     tion 5.3).
  3. To determine, for each attribute that has a criterion at this step, the PAR of
     inactivation pH, hold time and temperature, and to establish whether the NOR
     of each lies within it (Section 7.4).
  4. To verify the platform worst cases of this step on which the criteria upstream of
     it rely (Section 7.7).
  5. To assess the effect of A-Mab concentration on aggregate, acidic variants and
     XMuLV clearance one factor at a time, and to support its range against the cri-
     teria for aggregate and acidic variants (Section 7.6).
  6. To provide the evidence for the classification of each parameter under SOP-4001,
     and for the contribution of the step to the control strategy in PCMR-001 (Sec-
     tion 9, Section 10).



1.3 Regulatory and scientific basis

The studies form the process design stage of the lifecycle approach to process val-
idation (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). They follow the enhanced ap-
proach of ICH Q8(R2) (International Council for Harmonisation 2009) and ICH Q11
(International Council for Harmonisation 2012), in which prior knowledge and risk as-
sessment identify the parameters that may affect each attribute and designed experi-
ments then measure and bound those effects. Criticality is treated as a continuum, as
ICH Q8(R2) describes. The parameters were ranked under ICH Q9 in the risk assess-
ment RA-001 before characterization began (International Council for Harmonisation
2023b).
Viral safety follows ICH Q5A(R2) (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a).
Each claimed step is studied in its own small-scale spiking model, a laboratory model

of the step into which a known titre of the model virus is added, and the cumulative
claim is built from the independent log reductions of low-pH inactivation, anion ex-
change and virus filtration. The process model, the attribute framework and the deci-
sion logic for parameter classes come from the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Work-
ing Group 2009). The designs and their analysis follow standard response-surface
methodology (Montgomery 2017), with the Box-Cox transformation (Box and Cox

and Tsai 1989).

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The low-pH hold belongs to the Novacyte downstream platform and was used to man-
ufacture three earlier humanized IgG1 antibodies, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab. The pro-
cess conditions of the step have remained essentially unchanged for those products
and through the development of A-Mab (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). That his-
tory establishes the mechanism of the step and the range of performance to expect
from it, so this characterization confirms and bounds known behaviour. The same
history supplies the platform claim for XMuLV clearance and the largest increases
in aggregate, fragments and acidic variants that the step has shown on the platform.
The intermediate acceptance criteria of Section 7.1 are built on those values, and
Section 7.7 checks them against the A-Mab data.
Low pH inactivates an enveloped virus by denaturing the glycoproteins of its envelope
and by disrupting the envelope itself. The virus needs those glycoproteins to bind to a
cell and to fuse with its membrane. Inactivation is commonly described by first-order
kinetics, in which the log reduction grows with the hold time at a rate that rises as the
pH falls and as the temperature rises. On that description a fall in pH was expected to
raise the log reduction most steeply near the operating range, because the envelope
glycoproteins denature much faster with each small fall in pH there. A longer hold
and a warmer hold were each expected to raise the log reduction, and the effect of the
hold time was expected to be larger at low pH. Section 5.4 compares that description
with the surface the data give.
The same conditions act on the antibody. At acid pH the second constant domain of
the heavy chain is partially unfolded, which exposes hydrophobic surface. Molecules
in that state associate, so aggregate forms for as long as the pool is held at low pH,
and faster in a warmer pool, because both the unfolding and the association speed
up with temperature. Aggregate formed in the hold is carried into cation exchange,
the step that removes it. Time at low pH also adds acidic variants, through direct
acid-catalysed hydrolysis of side-chain amides, and a small amount of acid-catalysed
cleavage in the hinge region, which adds fragments. Over a hold of the length studied,
both increases were expected to be small, the acidic variants against the level formed
in the production bioreactor and the fragments against the level the load already
carries.
The step was not expected to change the glycan attributes. Those are formed in the
production bioreactor, and no downstream step changes them (PCMP-001 §3). The
criteria also assume no change in host cell protein across the step.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

RA-001 assessed every parameter of the step before characterization began. Each
parameter was ranked on its potential impact on the CQAs and on its potential to
interact with other parameters, as ICH Q9 describes (International Council for Har-
monisation 2023b). RA-001 gives the scores and the rationale for each assignment.
Inactivation pH, hold time and temperature were assigned to multivariate study. A
lower pH, a longer hold and a warmer hold were each expected to raise the log re-
duction of XMuLV, and a longer or warmer hold to raise the aggregate that forms.
The three were also expected to interact, because on the first-order description the
log reduction is the product of a rate that depends on pH and temperature and the




                                                  15
time over which that rate acts. A design that varies the three together was therefore
needed to estimate the clearance and the aggregate at their combinations.
A-Mab concentration was assigned to univariate study. A higher antibody concen-
tration raises the rate at which partially unfolded molecules meet, so aggregate was
expected to form faster in a more concentrated pool. The mechanism of the step gives
no reason to expect an effect of the antibody concentration on the inactivation of the
virus, because the acid denatures the envelope of the virus whatever the concentra-
tion of antibody around it. The concentration is also a property of the Protein A pool
rather than a setting of the hold, so it was assessed one factor at a time.

Virus cannot be introduced into the manufacturing process, so XMuLV clearance was
measured in the spiking model of the step (International Council for Harmonisation
2023a). PCP-006 §5.1 sets out the spiking model. It is the scale-down model, run on
the same Protein A eluate and at the same inactivation pH, hold time and temperature.
The XMuLV spike is added after the pool has been acidified and before the hold begins,
at the volume ratio fixed in SOP-2009, which is small enough that the spike changes
neither the pH nor the antibody concentration of the pool. The input titre is taken
from the spiked pool at the start of the hold and the output titre at the end, and the
log reduction factor (LRF) is the difference between the two. Each run of a design
is made twice, once spiked for the titres and once unspiked for the quality attributes.
The inactivation rate depends on the pH, the temperature and the matrix of the pool,
and not on its volume. The spiking runs use Protein A eluate produced at the set-
points of the Protein A step, so the matrix is that of manufacture, and the runs of the
designs held the A-Mab concentration at its set-point. The small-scale result therefore
represents the manufacturing step at that concentration, provided the whole pool
reaches the inactivation pH.
The centre points of each design are replicate runs at the design centre. They give
the pure error against which lack of fit is tested (Section 5.1).



3.2 Operation

The runs followed the operation of PCP-006 §5.2. Each run starts from Protein A elu-
ate produced at the set-points of the Protein A step, which is characterized under
PCR-005. The eluate is adjusted to the inactivation pH of the run and brought to its
temperature, and the hold time is counted from the moment the inactivation pH is
reached. At the end of the hold the pool is neutralized with base and filtered. Neu-
tralization stops the inactivation and slows the reactions that the acid drives on the
antibody. In both designs the A-Mab concentration was held at its set-point of 20

g/L. In the two concentration studies it is set to each planned value before the pH is
adjusted.
The acid and base solutions and the other inputs of the step are prepared to their
platform specifications. They are controlled for identity and quality and were not
characterized.

3.5 Statistical methods

Each factor is coded −1 at the low end of its range studied, +1 at the high end and
0 at the midpoint (Montgomery 2017). An effect is the change in the response over
the coded range of the factor, which is twice its coefficient in coded units. A model
term is significant when its p-value is below α = 0.05, the level PCMP-001 §6.2 sets.
About one term in twenty with no real effect is called significant at that level. In this
report the terms of a model are counted beside the intercept.
The screening data were fitted by least squares with a model of main effects and
two-factor interactions, which has six terms for three factors. The screening model
identifies effects. Its centre points can detect curvature but cannot attribute it to
a factor, so no prediction in this report comes from it. The response-surface model
is the predictive model, and every prediction, every PAR, the design space and the
verification of the platform worst cases come from it.
PCP-006 §5.5 prescribed the full quadratic model of each output, which has nine
terms. This report selects the model of each output instead, as corrective action CA3
of INV-002, adopted on 2026-09-15, requires for every report of the campaign. The
change is discrepancy PD-006-04, the model selection (Section 13.2). The selection
has three steps.

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic estimates the exponent λ
     that would make the residuals of the model best behaved, with λ = 1 the raw
     scale and λ = 0 the log scale (Box and Cox 1964). The log scale is used when
     the 95 % interval of λ contains 0 and excludes 1, which means the data support
     the log scale and reject the raw one.
  2. The model. Among all 95 submodels of the full quadratic that keep strong hi-
     erarchy, an interaction only with both its main effects and a square only with
     its main effect, the one with the lowest AICc is taken. AICc estimates how well
     a model would predict new runs and penalises every term it estimates, and its
     small-sample correction raises that penalty when the runs are few for the num-
     ber of terms, as they are in a design of 18 runs (Hurvich and Tsai 1989). The
     residual variance counts as one of the estimated parameters. Every candidate
     is fitted, and the one with the lowest AICc is taken.
  3. The check. The selected model is judged against the four adequacy criteria of
     PCMP-001 §7.2. These are a significant model F-test at α = 0.05, no significant
     lack of fit against pure error at α = 0.05, a limit on overfitting, under which
     R² may exceed predicted R² by no more than 0.20, and residuals with no pat-
     tern against the predicted values or the run order and no run dominating the
     fit. PCP-006 §7.1 tests the fourth by a regression of the absolute residuals on
     the predicted values and a regression of the residuals on the order of execution,
     each at α = 0.05, and by the Cook’s distance of each run. A run whose Cook’s
     distance exceeds 4/n, which is 0.22 for 18 runs, is examined by deleting it and
     repeating the judgement. The model fails the criterion where a run exceeds the
     50 % quantile of the F distribution with the degrees of freedom of the model,
     which is a much larger distance. Predicted R² comes from the prediction error
     sum of squares, in which each run is predicted by the model fitted without it




                                          21
     (Montgomery 2017). An output with no adequate model sets no range and is
     judged on its measured results.

A model that reproduces its results exactly is never adequate. With no residual there
is no pure error for the lack-of-fit test and no residual mean square for the model F-
test, so the criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 cannot be applied, and the prediction interval
of such a model has zero width. The acidic variants of this step are such an output
(Section 5.3).
Aggregate, fragments and acidic variants must meet their criteria in every batch,
and a response-surface model predicts the mean response. Each of them is therefore
judged through the 95 % prediction interval of a single run, which stands for a single
batch, at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the three factors,
with the offset of the scale-down model applied. That interval is two-sided, so its upper
end is a one-sided 97.5 % limit. XMuLV clearance is judged on the mean predicted
LRF at the least favourable of those combinations, because viral clearance is validated
at worst-case conditions. The prediction interval of a single run of the log reduction
is reported beside it for information.
PCMP-001 §6.4 defines two PAR analyses per attribute and factor. The second, which
PCMP-001 calls the NOR-propagated PAR, is the range of the factor over which the
attribute meets its criterion while the other two factors vary within their NORs. For
a quality attribute it draws 2,000 operating points of the other two factors at each of
81 grid points, each factor from a normal distribution centred on its set-point whose
standard deviation is one sixth of the width of its NOR, clipped to the range stud-
ied, and compares the central 95 % of the simulated batches with the criterion. For
XMuLV clearance it draws nothing. At each grid point the other two factors take every
combination of their NOR low, set-point and NOR high, and the lowest mean predic-
tion is compared with the claim. The verification of the platform worst cases uses the
one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch (Section 7.7). Commercial-scale
capability comes from the integrated model of the train, which runs 2,000 simulated
batches through every step (Section 8).
The examination of a flagged run follows PCP-006 §7.1. Each run with a Cook’s dis-
tance above the cut-off was deleted in turn, the selection repeated on the remaining
17 runs and the output judged over the NOR again (Section 5.3.2). One analysis is not
in PCP-006, which sets no rule for it. Where other models lie within 2 AICc units of
the selected one, those models were fitted and their conclusions compared with those
of the selected model, which decides. That comparison is the sensitivity analysis of
the model selection and is recorded within discrepancy PD-006-04 (Section 5.3.1).

4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface design S-VI-RSM was a face-centred central composite design

factors were carried forward from the screening, because each of them was expected

each (Section 5.2). Every output of the neutralized pool was measured on every run.
The input pool was not tested, which is deviation DEV-006-04 (Section 13.1). The
design matrix and the results are in Appendix B.

5.4 Mechanistic interpretation

Figure 5.4 shows the predicted XMuLV log reduction over pH and hold time at the
two ends of the temperature NOR in force.




Figure 5.4: Predicted XMuLV log reduction over inactivation pH and hold time, at the
            two ends of the temperature NOR in force. The claim of the step is drawn
            as a white contour, the NOR in force as a grey box. Lighter shading is a
            larger log reduction.


The log reduction falls as the pH rises and grows as the hold lengthens, and the
contour of the claim runs from the bottom left of each panel to the top right. The
surface the data give is a plane in the three factors. It carries no interaction of pH
with hold time, so the hold time that restores the claim after a rise in pH is the same
at every pH within the range studied, and it carries no curvature, so the log reduction
grows at a constant rate with the hold time rather than at a rate that falls away as the
virus is depleted. A first-order description of the inactivation predicts both features,
and 18 runs do not resolve either. What the model does show is the trade between the
three factors. A rise of 0.10 pH units, the half-width of the pH NOR, lowers the log
reduction by 0.15 log10 , which a hold longer by 15 min or warmer by 2.7 °C offsets.
Because the pH range of routine operation is narrow and the pH of a large pool is
held by acid addition and mixing, that trade is what makes the inactivation pH the
parameter the claim is most exposed to (Section 9).
Aggregate grows with the hold time and with the temperature, as the association
of partially unfolded antibody leads one to expect. At acid pH the second constant
domain of the heavy chain is partially unfolded and exposes hydrophobic surface,




                                          36
molecules in that state associate, and the extent of the association depends on how
long they are held and how fast they meet. No interaction of hold time and tem-
perature was resolved, and one of the models within 2 AICc units carries one (Sec-
tion 5.3.1), so the data do not decide whether the two effects add or interact. Within
the NOR as characterized the predicted rise across the step is 0.53 percentage points,
with a single-batch bound of 0.56 points (Section 7.7). The mechanism also leads one
to expect faster aggregation at lower pH, because a more acid pool is further un-
folded. No such effect was resolved within the range studied, and the data bound
a linear effect within about 0.03 points over that range (Section 5.3), so within this
range the hold time and the temperature determine the aggregate the step adds.
The acidic variants grow with the hold time and with nothing else, by 0.20 percent-
age points per 60 min. Direct acid-catalysed hydrolysis of the side-chain amides of
asparagine and glutamine is the expected source of that increase at low pH. The rise
over the whole hold-time range studied, 0.4 points, is small against the level of about
21 % in the neutralized pool, which is formed upstream, and the attribute stays far
inside its criterion at every setting of the step (Section 7.2).
Fragments grow with the hold time as well. The stability study resolved an increase of
0.048 percentage points per 60 min at the two pH values it compared (Section 7.5.1),
which is what acid-catalysed cleavage in the hinge region leads one to expect. At that
rate a hold at the upper end of the NOR in force adds about 0.10 points. The increase
is small against the level the pool carries, and the attribute leaves 1.47 points to its
criterion (Section 7.2). No effect of temperature on fragments was resolved in any
study.

6 Design space

The design space of the step is the region of inactivation pH, hold time and temper-
ature over which every attribute with a criterion at this step meets it. PCP-006 §7.6
states how it is drawn. It was evaluated on a grid of 11 levels of each factor over
the ranges studied, 1,331 points in all, with each output judged on the statistic it is
judged on everywhere else in this report. Aggregate is inside when both ends of the
95 % prediction interval of a single run lie within its criterion at manufacturing scale,
and the XMuLV log reduction is inside when its mean prediction reaches the claim.
Of the region studied, 63.9 % meets the criteria of the two outputs that have an
adequate model. XMuLV clearance alone rejects 36.1 % of the grid, and aggregate
rejects none of it. The region is therefore bounded by the clearance claim and not by
product quality, and its boundary is the contour drawn in Figure 5.4. Judging both
outputs on their mean predictions gives the same region, because aggregate stays far
from its criterion at every point of the grid, so the choice of statistic does not move this
boundary. Acidic variants and fragments have no adequate model, so neither draws
a boundary. Every measured result of both attributes meets its criterion across the
designs, which span the whole region studied, and the region is not constrained by
either attribute on that evidence.
The NOR in force lies inside the design space, with the margins Section 7.5 gives,
and the region studied is the knowledge space the ranges studied bound. Movement
within the design space is not a change from a regulatory filing perspective, as ICH
Q8(R2) states, and is assessed under the quality system of ICH Q10 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2008). A move outside the NOR but inside the design
space needs a prospective risk assessment under that system. A move outside the
region studied has no evidence in this report and needs new characterization data.
Two bounds apply to the region. The models were fitted in the scale-down model and
the spiking model of the step, and the clearance boundary rests on the spiking model,
whose representativeness is argued in Section 3.1 rather than measured at scale. The
boundary is drawn on the mean predicted log reduction, so at the boundary itself a
single batch falls below the claim about half the time, and the step is operated at the
set-point with the margin Section 7.5 states.

9 Parameter classification

Each parameter was classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of PCMP-001
§7.7. A critical process parameter (CPP) has an effect on a CQA that is significant and
practically relevant within the characterized range, and a high risk of falling outside
its acceptable range in operation. A well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-
CPP) has the same kind of effect but a low risk of falling outside that range, because
it is readily controlled and its PAR is wide against its NOR. A key process parameter
(KPP) affects process performance and no CQA, and a general process parameter
(GPP) affects neither. Neither PCMP-001 nor SOP-4001 sets a numerical threshold
for an effect that is practically relevant or for a PAR that is wide against its NOR, so
neither phrase decides a class on its own. The size of each effect across the PAR and
the margin from the PAR to the NOR are therefore given with each class. The classes
are in Table 4.1.

  • Inactivation pH (CPP). A rise in pH across the range studied lowers the XMuLV
    log reduction by 1.22 log10 (p < 0.001), which is 19 % of the claim. Across the
    PAR of pH 3.2–3.61 the same model gives a change of 0.63 log10 , so the claim
    is lost at the upper edge of that range. The margin from the upper NOR edge
    to the PAR edge is 0.11 pH units, about the half-width of the NOR itself, 0.10
    pH units, so an excursion beyond the NOR of the size the NOR allows about its
    set-point would reach the edge of the PAR. The pH of a large acidified pool is
    held by acid addition and mixing, which is the least directly controlled of the
    three settings. The risk of operating outside the acceptable range is therefore
    high, and the parameter is a CPP.
  • Hold time (WC-CPP). A longer hold raises the XMuLV log reduction by 1.21 log10
    over the range studied (p < 0.001) and aggregate by 0.472 percentage points
    over the same range (p < 0.001). It also raises the acidic variants by 0.4 points
    over that range and the fragments by 0.10 points at the rate the stability study
    measured (Section 7.5.1). Across its PAR of 84–180 min the clearance effect is

11 Discussion

The step is understood well enough to be operated and transferred. The three pa-
rameters trade against each other along the contour of Figure 5.4, and no interaction
among them was resolved by 18 runs. Aggregate grows with the hold time and with
the temperature, the acidic variants and the fragments with the hold time, and all
three stay far inside their criteria across the region studied. What limits the step is
not product quality but the clearance claim, which is why the design space of Sec-
tion 6 is bounded by the clearance contour and why the ranges in force were set from
it.
The ranges in force were decided on these data, after the results were seen (Sec-
tion 7.5). The evidence that they are safe to operate in is of three kinds. The model
predicts the claim at the least favourable combination of the NOR with a margin of
0.17 log10 . The quality attributes were measured at the new pH set-point over hold
times from 0 to 180 min (Section 7.5.1) and at the low end of the pH PAR with hold
time and temperature at the upper ends of their NORs (Section 7.5.3), and every
result met its criterion. Cation exchange was run on material from both of those
conditions and cleared aggregate as the criteria assume.
Confidence in the scale-down model is highest for the two outputs the qualification
compared at both scales. The offsets of aggregate and acidic variants are small, their
intervals contain zero, and the conclusion over the NOR is the same at either end of
both intervals, so the model is qualified for both (Section 3.1). Six limitations bound
what this report claims.

  1. Fragments and viral clearance were not compared at both scales. Their offsets
     are unknown rather than zero. For fragments the conclusion rests on the assump-
     tion that cleavage in the hinge region depends on pH, time and temperature and
     not on the size of the vessel, and the margin of 1.47 percentage points to the
     criterion bounds what the assumption can cost. For viral clearance it rests on
     the spiking model, in which the matrix, the pH and the temperature are those of
     manufacture and only the volume differs.
  2. The log reduction carries the variability of a cell-based assay. Its coefficient
     of variation at the centre points is 5.7 %, the largest of any response, and the
     claim is therefore judged on the mean prediction. A single spiked run at the
     least favourable corner of the NOR can fall below the claim (Section 7.2). The
     screening and the response-surface design also gave different pH effects on the
     same corner settings (Section 5.2), which bounds how precisely one campaign
     of spiked runs fixes that effect.
  3. Two results depend on one run. Both selected models meet every adequacy crite-
     rion, and the runs the examination of PCP-006 §7.1 covers changed no conclusion

12 Conclusions

1. The step meets its claim of 6.60 log10 of XMuLV clearance across the NOR in
   force, which is pH 3.3–3.5, 100–120 min and 20–23 °C. The lowest mean pre-
   dicted log reduction in that NOR is 6.77 log10 .
2. The proven acceptable ranges are pH 3.2–3.61, 84–180 min and 17.2–25 °C, each
   set by the clearance claim and each containing the NOR in force. Of the region
   studied, 63.9 % meets the criteria of the two outputs that have an adequate
   model.
3. Aggregate, fragments and acidic variants meet their intermediate acceptance
   criteria across the NOR in force, with the margins of Table 7.2, and the neutral-
   ized pool still meets them after 7 days at 21 °C and 25 °C. On material from
   the confirmation runs cation exchange cleared aggregate as the criteria assume
   and did not meet the rule for fragments, which was accepted on its risk (Sec-
   tion 7.5.3).
4. The scale-down model is qualified for aggregate and for acidic variants. Frag-
   ments and viral clearance were not compared at both scales, and their conclu-
   sions rest on the arguments of Section 3.1.
5. Inactivation pH is a CPP, hold time and temperature are WC-CPPs and A-Mab
   concentration is a GPP.
6. Within the NOR as characterized the step adds less aggregate than the platform
   worst case at the bound of a single batch, which widened the aggregate criterion
   of the production bioreactor output from 6.32 to 6.46 % HMW. The platform
   values for fragments and acidic variants stand.
7. The tightest capability of the attributes this step changes is the acidic variant
   index of 1.05 against the lower end of its criterion, which the low-pH hold moves
   away from. Cumulative XMuLV clearance has an index of 1.70.
8. Four deviations were recorded and resolved, and six discrepancies from the plan
   are registered. These ranges, classes and controls roll up into PCMR-001.

DEV-006-01, hold-time record discrepancy between the control system
and the manual log

During batch-record reconciliation the hold time recorded by the distributed control
system (DCS) and the hold time in the manual log were found to differ by 4 min. The
root cause was an offset between the clocks of the two records, a clock synchronisa-
tion error. Whichever record is right, the impact is bounded by the effect of the hold
time on each output. A hold-time error of that size changes the predicted XMuLV log
reduction by 0.040 log10 , which is smaller than the residual standard deviation of its
model, 0.39 log10 , and smaller than the margin of 0.17 log10 at the least favourable




                                          65
NOR combination. It changes the predicted aggregate by 0.016 percentage points
against a margin of 4.92 points to the criterion. The runs were retained and analysed
at their design settings.


DEV-006-02, cytotoxicity of the XMuLV infectivity assay at low dilution

The assay review found that the sample matrix was cytotoxic to the indicator cells of
the XMuLV infectivity assay (AMV-3017) at low dilution, with the cytotoxicity recorded
at a dilution factor of 8. The root cause is the matrix of the sample. The record names
no affected run, so the deviation is assessed for the study as a whole. Wells that are
cytotoxic cannot be read for infection, so the titre of an affected sample is read from
higher dilutions, which raises the lowest titre the assay can report. A higher limit for
the output titre can only make a computed log reduction smaller, never larger. The
runs were retained.


DEV-006-03, XMuLV clearance not measured on the A-Mab concentration
runs

PCP-006 §6.4 required aggregate, acidic variants and the XMuLV log reduction on
every run of the concentration study S-VI-UNI. The study measured aggregate and
acidic variants only. The root cause was that spiking had been scheduled for the de-
signed studies only, so no titre was taken in the concentration study. The departure
was found during the Version 2.0 re-analysis, when the plan was compared with the
study record. Version 1.0 of this report did not notice it. The consequence was that
the clearance across the concentration range had no result, while the range 5–35 g/L
was supported for aggregate and acidic variants only. The deviation was closed by
the confirmation study S-VI-UNI2, which spiked XMuLV at both ends of the range in
duplicate (Section 7.6). That study is recorded as discrepancy PD-006-06, the clear-
ance at the two ends of the concentration range, because the plan did not foresee
it.


DEV-006-04, input pool of the design runs not tested

PCP-006 §5.3 and §5.4 require a sample of the input pool, taken before acidification,
on every run of both designs, tested for aggregate, acidic variants and A-Mab concen-
tration. No input pool result is in the record of either design. The root cause was that
the input samples were not scheduled in the study record. The departure was found
during the Version 2.0 review of the study record against PCP-006, after the designs
had been analysed. The consequence is that the increase in an attribute across the
step cannot be formed run by run. The verification of the platform worst cases needs
that increase, and it is therefore taken against the aggregate level of the load in the
nominal batch of the step model (Section 7.7). Every criterion of the step is on the
output of the step, so no judgement of an output against its criterion is affected. The
runs were retained.
