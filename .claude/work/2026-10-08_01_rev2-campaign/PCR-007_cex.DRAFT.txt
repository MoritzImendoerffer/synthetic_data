Executive summary

Cation exchange chromatography is Step 7 of the A-Mab drug substance process and
its principal aggregate polishing step. The antibody is bound to the resin and eluted
by a step change in pH, and the step clears host cell protein, residual DNA, leached
Protein A and part of the fragments. It sets no quality attribute of its own. This report
presents the Stage 1 characterization of the step under the plan PCP-007 (U.S. Food
and Drug Administration 2011). The characterization studies ran from 2026-02-04
to 2026-03-27 on a scale-down model of the commercial column, for a process that
runs at a production bioreactor scale of 15,000 L at the commercial site in Grafton,
WI. During the re-analysis, two studies of the low-pH viral inactivation report ran this
step on the same scale-down model, at its set-point and on material from the low-pH
step, to test how well it clears aggregate and fragments. They are S-CEX-VER, from
2026-09-24 to 2026-09-25, and S-VI-WC, from 2026-09-26 to 2026-09-29.
Protein load, load/wash conductivity, elution buffer pH and elution stop collect were
studied in a two-level full factorial screening design of 19 runs and in a face-centred
central composite design of 28 runs. Elution flow rate was assessed one factor at
a time. The scale-down model is qualified for aggregate and host cell protein by its
offsets from manufacturing scale, and every judgement of those two outputs is stated
at manufacturing scale. Each output is judged against an intermediate acceptance
criterion. The a priori criterion is the value derived before the studies, from the drug
substance criterion and the platform worst cases of the later steps. The criterion in
force is that value after this campaign’s data on the later steps tightened or widened
it. For host cell protein the qualification holds at the criterion in force of 418 ng/mg
and would not hold at the a priori criterion of 400 ng/mg.
Aggregate and host cell protein met their intermediate acceptance criteria at every
combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high. The single-run limit is the upper
end of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run, at manufacturing scale. Its high-
est value over the NOR was 1.40 % for aggregate against 4.90 %, and 289 ng/mg for
host cell protein against 418 ng/mg. The aggregate criterion is the drug substance
criterion of 5.00 % less the largest rise expected across UF/DF, 0.104 percentage
points. Fragments have no adequate model and were judged on their measured re-
sults, which lay between 0.47 % and 0.76 % against 2.00 %. On the single-run limit
and at the criteria in force, the design space covers the whole characterized region.
At the a priori host cell protein criterion it would cover 99.85 % of it. The proven
acceptable range of every parameter for aggregate and host cell protein is its range
studied.
The models of aggregate and host cell protein were selected on the log scale and meet
the first three adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2. The host cell protein model does
not meet the fourth, because its residuals drift with the order of execution. That drift

amounts to a factor of 1.13 over the study. The highest single-run limit over the NOR
stays below the criterion with the whole drift added to it. The highest limit over the
whole region studied would not, and nor would the limit over the NOR with the offset
at the unfavourable end of its interval, on which the qualification rests (Section 5.3.2).
Of the 5 parameters, 4 were classified as well-controlled critical process parameters.
Elution flow rate was classified as a general process parameter, by a judgement of
the practical relevance of its effect on residual DNA (Section 9).
Within its NOR the step cleared aggregate less than the platform worst case on which
the criteria upstream rely. At manufacturing scale the lowest clearance factor pre-
dicted was 1.58 against 2.00. The one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch,
1.40, replaced the platform value and tightened the aggregate criteria of the low-pH
pool and the bioreactor harvest. The platform worst case for host cell protein was
confirmed with the offset at its estimate. It would not be confirmed with the offset at
the unfavourable end of its interval, or under 3 of the 5 other models within 2 AICc
units of the selected model. The platform worst case for fragments was confirmed by
the rule of PCP-007 §7.6, which confirms a platform worst case when the lowest clear-
ance factor predicted within the NOR is not below it. The fragment model behind that
prediction does not meet PCMP-001 §7.2. Leached Protein A is not a response of the
designs.
The capability indices of the drug substance attributes that the step clears all exceed
the target of 1.33, and the lowest is 6.14 for host cell protein. No simulated batch of
2,000 fell outside its criterion. The capability of the step itself, which the plan asked
for, was not estimated.
Version 1.0 recorded 2 events of execution, which this report keeps as study events
under their Version 1.0 DEV numbers. Screening run 6 used a load/wash buffer from
an expired lot (DEV-007-01), and the column inlet temperature rose above its set-
point during response-surface run 14 (DEV-007-02). Both runs were retained in the
analysis. The register records 11 discrepancies from the plan. Four concern the anal-
ysis and the scale-down model. They are the model selection, conclusions that rest on
a model that does not meet the criterion on residuals, the flow-rate study without its
set-point run, and the residual DNA offset, which was recorded and not investigated.
Five concern records the plan required that are not in the study record. These are the

the fractionated runs of S-CEX-FRC, which no version of the plan foresaw, and the
approaches this report takes where the plan sets no threshold, with capability given
for the drug substance and not for the step.

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody developed on the Novacyte plat-
form (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Its drug substance is purified by Protein
A chromatography, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange chromatography,
anion exchange chromatography, small-virus retentive filtration and ultrafiltra-
tion/diafiltration. Cation exchange is Step 7 of this train. It receives the neutralized
pool of the low-pH viral inactivation step and passes its eluate pool to anion
exchange.
The step is operated in bind and elute mode on a resin with a sulfonate ligand. Below
its isoelectric point the antibody carries a net positive charge and binds the ligand.
After a wash at the conductivity of the load, the antibody is eluted by a step change
to the elution buffer, whose pH weakens the binding. The pool is collected between
a threshold on the ascending edge of the elution peak and the stop collect threshold
on the descending edge, both read as absorbance at 280 nm. Aggregate binds more
strongly than monomer and elutes later, so the step separates the two by where the
pool is cut. Host cell protein, residual DNA and leached Protein A that do not bind,
or bind only weakly, leave in the load flow-through and the wash.
No quality attribute is set at this step. Aggregate is formed in the production biore-
actor and added to during the low-pH hold. No later step removes aggregate, and
UF/DF adds a little within its NOR. The pool of this step is therefore judged against
the drug substance criterion less the largest rise expected across UF/DF (Section 7.1).
Host cell protein is cleared here and again at anion exchange. No viral clearance is
claimed for cation exchange. Enveloped virus is claimed at low-pH inactivation, anion
exchange and virus filtration, and small viruses at anion exchange and virus filtration
(International Council for Harmonisation 2023a).
The process is being transferred from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the
commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-
001. It operates there at a production bioreactor scale of 15,000 L. Table 1.1 lists the
documents this report depends on and the document it rolls up into.

1.4 Changes in this revision

Version 1.0 of this report was approved on 2026-07-24. Version 2.0, effective 2026-09-
30, is issued under two Quality Assurance (QA) investigations. INV-001 (Acceptance
criteria for proven-acceptable-range analysis set after data review) was opened on
2026-08-03 after a review of PCMR-001 Version 1.0 for the Biologics License Appli-
cation (BLA). It was closed on 2026-09-18 with the classification major. It found
that the criteria used to judge each step in the proven acceptable range analysis had




                                           12
been set after the data were seen and appeared in no approved plan (INV-001 finding
1). INV-002 (Response-surface models below the model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-
001 Version 2.0) judged the response-surface models of the outputs with a criterion
against the first three model adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2. Its corrective ac-
tion CA3, adopted on 2026-09-15, analyses every response-surface output by model
selection.
This revision changes the report in seven ways.

  1. The acceptance criteria. Version 1.0 judged the pool against an aggregate limit
     of 1.00 % and a host cell protein limit of 193 ng/mg. Each was the drug substance
     criterion carried back through the nominal clearance of the later steps in this
     campaign and then divided by a margin, 5.0 for aggregate and 3.2 for host cell
     protein (INV-001 finding 2). Version 2.0 judges the pool against the intermediate
     acceptance criteria of PCMP-001 §7.3, derived from the drug substance criteria
     and the platform worst cases of the later steps (Section 7.1). Table 1.3 compares
     the two versions. Two criteria in force differ from their a priori values. The host
     cell protein criterion is wider than its a priori value of 400 ng/mg, and Section 7.1
     argues that change by its risk. The aggregate criterion is tighter than the drug
     substance criterion of 5.00 % by the rise that UF/DF may add (Section 7.1).
  2. Fragments. Fragments are judged at this step for the first time, against the
     drug substance criterion, on results by non-reduced capillary electrophoresis
     with sodium dodecyl sulfate (nrCE-SDS) under the analytical method decision
     AMD-001 (Section 3.3).
  3. The models. Every output of the response-surface design is analysed by model
     selection instead of the full quadratic model of the plan (discrepancy PD-007-01,
     Section 3.5).
  4. The scale-down model. The model is qualified for each output by its offset from
     manufacturing scale, not by a test of equivalence, and every judgement of an
     output with a measured offset is made at manufacturing scale (Section 3.1).
  5. The judgement. Each quality attribute is judged on the 95 % prediction interval
     of a single run, and the design space is drawn on the same statistic (Section 3.5).
  6. The verification. The report verifies the platform worst cases of cation exchange
     on which the criteria upstream rely (Section 7.4).
  7. The record. The execution window of each study is recorded (Section 3.2). The
     events of execution that Version 1.0 recorded are reported as study events under
     their Version 1.0 numbers, and the departures from the plan as discrepancies
     (Section 13). Version 1.0 of this report stated that the load, the flow-through and
     the wash of each run were assayed and that the column was released against
     SOP-2008. No result of those assays or of that release is in the record, and
     Version 2.0 records each gap as a discrepancy.

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The cation exchange step has been operated on the Novacyte humanized IgG1 plat-
form for three earlier products, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and throughout A-Mab
clinical and engineering manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The resin
chemistry, the buffer system and the mode of operation are unchanged for A-Mab.
This experience establishes the mechanisms of the step and identified the parame-
ters worth studying. It also supplies the platform worst cases from which the criteria
are derived (Section 7.1 and Section 7.4).
Aggregate presents more positive charge to the ligand than monomer does, so it is
retained more strongly and elutes on the tail of the elution peak. The clearance of
aggregate depends on how well the two species are resolved and on where collection
stops. At the load pH, host cell proteins with an isoelectric point below that pH carry
a net negative charge and leave in the load flow-through. Those with an isoelectric
point above it bind with the antibody. The weakly bound among them are displaced
in the wash, and the rest are separated from the antibody in the elution. A part of
the host cell protein is held in association with the bound antibody and elutes with it.
DNA carries a strong negative charge and is repelled by the ligand, so its clearance is
large and is not expected to depend on the parameters studied. Free leached Protein
A does not bind under load conditions, but the leached Protein A in complex with
the antibody binds with it, which limits the clearance to a modest factor. Fragments
carry less positive charge than intact antibody and elute on the leading side of the
main peak. Yield is lost at both edges of the pool, before collection starts and after it
stops.
PCP-007 §4.1 draws four expectations from this experience, and the multivariate de-
signs were built to test them.

  1. A higher protein load occupies more of the ligand. The monomer and aggregate
     peaks broaden and overlap, and more host cell protein is held with the bound
     antibody, so aggregate and host cell protein in the pool are expected to rise and
     yield to fall as load rises.
  2. A higher load/wash conductivity is expected to strip more of the weakly bound
     host cell protein in the wash and to have little effect on the aggregate separation.
     The ionic strength of the load and the wash does not change the relative charge
     of aggregate and monomer at the elution pH, and that difference in charge is
     what separates them.
  3. A higher elution buffer pH weakens binding and brings monomer and aggregate
     off closer together, so it is expected to raise aggregate in the pool.




                                          15
  4. The stop collect point is an absorbance on the descending edge, so a lower set-
     ting stops collection later. Stopping later is expected to take in more monomer
     and the leading part of the aggregate peak, which raises both the yield and the
     aggregate of the pool, and to add eluate that carries few fragments.

The plan adds that a higher conductivity is expected to remove more host cell protein
at a high load than at a low one, and that elution flow rate, within its range, is expected
to change the sharpness of the peak too little to move the aggregate content of the
pool. Curvature was expected in the effects of elution stop collect and elution buffer
pH (PCP-007 §6.3). The stop collect moves the end of the pool along the falling edge
of the peak. The elution buffer pH changes the charge of the antibody, and so the
position and the width of the peak against the collection thresholds. Because the
aggregate peak has a maximum, equal steps in either setting add unequal amounts
of aggregate to the pool.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

All process parameters of the step were carried into the risk ranking and filtering
of RA-001 (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Each parameter was
ranked for its potential impact on a quality attribute and for its potential to interact
with other parameters, and a parameter with no data or rationale was ranked at the
highest level (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The assignment produced three
groups.

  1. Protein load, load/wash conductivity, elution buffer pH and elution stop collect
     were assigned to the multivariate designs. The first, third and fourth move the
     aggregate peak against the stop point, and load acts with conductivity on the
     host cell protein that survives the wash, so their effects cannot be assumed to
     add.
  2. Elution flow rate was assigned to a univariate assessment. It acts on the sepa-
     ration through the residence time of the antibody in the column and the rate of
     mass transfer into and out of the resin, and within its range these were expected
     to change the sharpness of the elution peak only a little.
  3. Parameters fixed by equipment design or held to platform specification, such as
     bed height, packing quality and buffer composition, were assigned no new study.
     Their ranges rest on the platform knowledge and on the procedures in Table 1.2.

The range studied of each parameter is wider than its NOR at both ends (Table 4.1).
The ranges were set from the control capability of the commercial equipment and
from the range over which the platform has been operated, so that the study bounds
the step beyond the region in which it runs (International Council for Harmonisation
2009). The elution buffer pH range stops short of incomplete elution at its low end
and of the co-elution of aggregate with monomer at its high end.

3.2 Operation

Each run followed the operation of SOP-2010. The neutralized low-pH pool was
brought to the load conductivity of the run and applied to the equilibrated column
up to the protein load of the run. The wash used the same conductivity, because the
platform holds load and wash conductivity equal, and the two were therefore varied
as one factor. The antibody was eluted by a step change to the elution buffer of the
run, and the pool was collected from a threshold on the rising edge of the peak to the
stop collect threshold on its falling edge, both read as OD280. Strip, sanitization and
re-equilibration followed SOP-2008. Buffer composition, packing and resin storage
were not varied.
PCP-007 §5.1 required plate count and asymmetry to be tested, and the column re-
leased against SOP-2008, before each campaign. Plate count and asymmetry are
recorded for the 3 scale-down runs of the qualification only, with no acceptance lim-
its. No packing result is recorded for the screening, response-surface and flow-rate
campaigns (discrepancy PD-007-08). PCP-007 §6.2 also required the feed lot of every
run to be recorded. The record does not name the feed lot of any run (discrepancy
PD-007-11).

3.5 Statistical methods

All factors were analysed in coded units. Each factor is coded so that its set-point is
0 and the ends of its range studied are −1 and +1. At this step the set-point of every
factor is the centre of its range, so the design centre and the set-point coincide. The
letter codes of Table 4.2 are used for the model terms.
The screening design was analysed by ordinary least squares with all main effects
and all two-factor interactions. An effect is twice the coded coefficient, the change in
the response across the whole range of the factor, and a term is significant at p < 0.05
(PCMP-001 §6.2). The screening model identifies effects. It is not the predictive model
of the step, because a model without quadratic terms cannot describe curvature.
The response-surface model is the predictive model. Every judgement over the NOR,
every proven acceptable range, the design space and the verification of the platform
worst cases come from it. The plan prescribed the full quadratic model of each output,
with 14 terms besides the intercept (PCP-007 §5.5). Each output was instead analysed
by the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3, in three steps (discrepancy
PD-007-01).

  1. The scale was chosen from the Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic (Box and Cox
     1964). The log scale is used where the 95 % interval of the Box-Cox exponent λ
     contains 0 and excludes 1, and the raw scale otherwise.




                                           24
  2. On that scale, every hierarchical submodel of the full quadratic was fitted, 1,337
     candidates for four factors, and the one with the lowest corrected Akaike infor-
     mation criterion (AICc) was taken. A hierarchical model keeps the main effects
     of any interaction or square it contains.
  3. The selected model was judged against the four adequacy criteria of PCMP-001
     §7.2. An output whose selected model fails one of the first three criteria has no
     adequate model. It is judged on its measured results and sets no range.

AICc is the small-sample form of Akaike’s criterion (Hurvich and Tsai 1989), and se-
lecting a model by such a criterion is standard practice (Burnham and Anderson 2002).
It estimates how well a model would predict new runs. It rewards a close fit and pe-
nalizes every estimated term, and the residual variance is counted as one of those
terms. Its correction for small samples raises the penalty when the runs are few for
the number of terms, as they are in a design of 28 runs, so that the criterion does not
favour too many terms. The search is exhaustive because a stepwise search changes
one term at a time and stops when no single change lowers AICc, which can leave it
short of a model that only a combination of changes reaches. On the data of this step
a stepwise search, backward or forward, would have reached the selected model for
aggregate. For host cell protein a backward search would have stopped at a model
of 8 terms and a forward search at one of 3, with AICc values of −27.4 and −27.9
against −31.2 for the selected model, and for fragments both would have stopped at
a model with a higher AICc. In this report the terms of a model are counted without
the intercept.
The Box-Cox profile chose the log scale for aggregate (λ = 0.18, 95 % interval −0.40
to 0.62) and for host cell protein (λ = −0.01, interval −0.88 to 0.87). It kept the
raw scale for fragments, whose interval contains 1. On the log scale the model is
ln 𝑦 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + ⋯ + 𝜀. Transformed back, 𝑦 = 𝑒𝑏0 𝑒𝑏1 𝑥1 ⋯ 𝑒𝜀 , so each parameter
multiplies the output by a fixed factor 𝑒𝑏 per coded unit, and by 𝑒2𝑏 from the low
to the high end of its range. The scatter between runs multiplies the output too, so
its size in the unit of the output grows with the level. This is the behaviour expected
of a purification step, which removes a share of what is present, so that a parameter
changes the share rather than a fixed amount. It is also expected of a method whose
precision is a percentage of the level, as the ELISA for host cell protein is.
Least squares, and the t-tests, F-tests and prediction intervals built on it, assume
residuals that are independent and normally distributed, with mean zero and the
same variance over the whole range studied. For these two outputs the evidence for
the log scale is the Box-Cox profile. The residuals themselves do not show the scatter
growing with the level on the raw scale. For the full quadratic on the raw scale, the
residual SD in the upper half of the predictions is only 5 % larger than in the lower
half for aggregate and 6 % larger for host cell protein. For the full quadratic on the
log scale it is smaller in the upper half (0.040 against 0.062 for aggregate). At 28 runs
neither difference is large enough to decide the scale. The regression of the absolute
residuals on the predicted values, one of the tests of the fourth criterion, finds no
trend in the selected log-scale model of either output (Table 5.5).
On the log scale the criteria stay in their own units, % and ng/mg. Effects are fold
changes, and Section 5.3 gives them. A prediction interval is calculated on the log




                                           25
scale and transformed back, which keeps its 95 % coverage, because the probability
that ln y lies between two values is the probability that y lies between their antilogs.
The interval is asymmetric. At the least favourable NOR combination for host cell
protein the median is 227 ng/mg and the interval runs from 178 to 289 ng/mg, further
above the median than below it. The predicted value is the median of the output, its
geometric mean, not its arithmetic mean. The upper prediction limit, which decides
against an upper criterion, is not affected by that.
Each model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R², the last from the predic-
tion error sum of squares. The four adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 are applied
as PCP-007 §7.2 states them as tests.

  1. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  2. Lack of fit against the pure error of the centre points is not significant at α =
     0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20. This is the limit on overfitting.
     A model that fits its own runs much better than it predicts runs left out of the fit
     has more terms than the data support.
  4. The absolute residuals regressed on the predicted values, and the residuals re-
     gressed on the order of execution, show no slope significant at α = 0.05. A run
     with a Cook’s distance above 4/n is examined by deleting it and repeating the
     judgement, and the model fails this criterion only where a run’s Cook’s distance
     exceeds the median of the F distribution with p and n − p degrees of freedom.

A model with no term has no F-test, so the first criterion does not apply to it. An output
with a selected model that fails the fourth criterion is still used for the ranges and
the design space, and that use is discrepancy PD-007-02. For each such model this
report states the size of the trend against the criterion of its output, and it deletes each
flagged run in turn, repeats the selection and judges the output over the NOR again.
Where other models lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one, they are reported as
a sensitivity, and the selected model decides.
A quality attribute is judged on a single batch. The judgement over the NOR asks
whether the exact 95 % prediction interval of a single run, at manufacturing scale,
lies within the criterion at every one of the 81 combinations of NOR low, set-point
and NOR high of the four factors (PCP-007 §7.4). The interval is two-sided, so its
upper end is a one-sided 97.5 % limit, and since every criterion at this step is an
upper limit, the upper end decides. The design space is the set of settings within the
ranges studied at which every output with a criterion meets it at both ends of the
same interval. It is evaluated on a grid of 11 levels per factor, 14,641 points in all.
Two proven acceptable ranges are computed for each parameter and each output with
a criterion and an adequate model (PCMP-001 §6.4). The set-point analysis scans the
parameter across its range with the other factors at their set-points and judges the
mean prediction, which is the median for a log-scale model, at manufacturing scale.
The NOR-propagated analysis scans the parameter at 81 points and draws, at each,
2,000 settings of the other factors, each from a normal distribution centred on its set-
point, with a standard deviation equal to the width of its NOR divided by 6, and clipped
to the range studied. Each draw takes its own fitted coefficients from their sampling
distribution and its own residual. A setting is accepted where the 97.5th percentile



                                            26
of the simulated batches, at manufacturing scale, does not exceed the criterion. The
range reported is the contiguous accepted interval that contains the set-point. The
simulation uses the fixed seed 20240724. The NOR-propagated range is the one that
supports a claim.
The verification of the platform worst cases follows PCP-007 §7.6. The clearance fac-
tor is the level that the nominal batch of the process model brings to the step divided
by the pool level that the response-surface model predicts. For aggregate and host
cell protein the prediction is first converted to manufacturing scale with the offset at
its estimate, as PCMP-001 §7.1 rule 2 requires of every judgement against a criterion.
Fragments have no measured offset and enter as the scale-down model gives them.
The model gives the lowest clearance factor over the 81 combinations of the NOR and
the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch at the least favourable combi-
nation. Capability is estimated for the drug substance from the integrated model of
the train, which simulates 2,000 batches with every parameter drawn within its NOR
(PCMP-001 §6.5), and is judged against the target capability index of 1.33 and the min-
imum of 1.0 (PCMP-001 §7.9). PCP-007 §5.5 also asked for the capability of the step,
from the fitted models over draws within the NOR. It was not estimated (discrepancy
PD-007-10).

4.2 Screening design

The screening study S-CEX-SCR ran from 2026-02-16 to 2026-02-27. It is a two-level
full factorial in the four factors, 16 runs, with 3 centre-point runs, 19 runs in all. A full

factor interaction independently. The runs were executed in a randomized order, with

the centre points at the positions given in Section 3.4. Appendix A gives the design

4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study S-CEX-RSM ran from 2026-03-02 to 2026-03-27. It is a
face-centred central composite design in the same four factors, with 16 factorial runs,
8 axial runs and 4 centre-point runs, 28 runs in all. The axial runs sit on the faces of
the cube, so no run lies outside the range studied of any factor. The design adds the
quadratic terms that the screening design cannot estimate. All four screening factors
were carried into it. The design therefore did not need the augmentation that PCP-
007 §6.3 requires when an active screening factor is left out of the response-surface
design. Appendix B gives the design and its results.



4.4 Univariate assessment

The flow-rate study S-CEX-UNI ran from 2026-03-16 to 2026-03-20, within the window
of the response-surface study. Elution flow rate was run at each end of its range, 100
cm/hr and 300 cm/hr, in duplicate, with every other parameter at its set-point. The
plan also required a run at the set-point, which was not made, and the qualification
runs at the set-point serve as the reference instead (discrepancy PD-007-03). Pool
aggregate, pool host cell protein, step yield, and residual DNA and leached Protein
A in the load and the pool were measured on each run. A study of one factor at a
time cannot show whether flow rate interacts with the other parameters. That limit
was accepted because flow rate sets the residence time in the resin, and with it the
sharpness of the peak, while it changes neither the charge of the antibody nor the
ionic strength that holds it to the ligand.

5.6 Mechanistic interpretation

Protein load and elution buffer pH acted on aggregate in the directions expected. A
higher load occupies more of the ligand and broadens the elution peaks, so the ag-
gregate front overlaps more of the monomer. A higher pH lowers the positive charge
of both species, which weakens their binding and brings them off closer together.
The positive load × pH interaction fits the same picture, since the separation that
load erodes is the separation that a high pH has already compressed. Within this
range conductivity has no demonstrated effect on aggregate, as expected. The ionic
strength of the load and the wash weakens the binding of every species during load-
ing and washing. It does not change the relative charge of aggregate and monomer
at the elution pH, which is what separates them.
The elution stop collect acted on aggregate in the opposite direction to the expectation
of PCP-007 §4.1. A higher setting stops collection earlier on the descending edge and
should leave more of the aggregate tail out of the pool. In both designs aggregate
instead rose as the setting rose, by 1.37-fold across the range in the response-surface
model, with its lowest value near 0.63 AU. The selected yield model has no stop collect
term, where a later stop was expected to recover more monomer (Section 5.3.6). The
mechanism of Section 2.1 does not explain these results, and this report gives no
cause for them.
Protein load and load/wash conductivity acted on host cell protein in the directions
expected. More load leaves more host cell protein held with the bound antibody, and
a higher ionic strength strips more of the weakly bound species in the wash. On the
log scale each acts as a constant factor, so in ng/mg conductivity removes more host
cell protein at a high load, where there is more to remove, as the plan expected. A
change with load of the share that conductivity removes was not found. The pH ×
conductivity interaction was not expected, and the mechanism of Section 2.1 does
not explain it.




                                           43
Residual DNA clearance did not depend on any of the four factors of the response-
surface design, as expected for a species repelled by the ligand. The lower clearance
at the highest flow rate is not explained by that mechanism either. Leached Protein
A was cleared by a modest factor, as expected for a species partly bound in complex
with the antibody. Yield fell with load, as expected when broader peaks lose more
monomer at their edges.

The design space of the step is the region of its four multivariate parameters, within
their ranges studied, over which every output with a criterion meets it at both ends
of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run (PCMP-001 §6.2, PCP-007 §7.5). Ag-

the region through their measured results in the scale-down model, all of which met

in force. The region drawn on the mean prediction, for comparison, is also the whole
region (100 %).
That result rests on the widening of the host cell protein criterion (Section 7.1). At

The 22 points outside lie at 40 g/L resin, 3 mS/cm and elution buffer pH 6.16 and

nearest to its criterion. Its highest single-run limit over the region is 408 ng/mg at

mS/cm, elution buffer pH 6.2 and elution stop collect 1.5 AU, against 4.90 %. The NOR
lies inside the ranges studied for every parameter, and inside the design space.
Four results bound this claim near the corner of highest load and lowest conductivity.

tion 5.2). Of the other host cell protein models within 2 AICc units of the selected

the run-order drift of host cell protein added, 1.13 % of the grid would exceed the
criterion (Section 5.3.2). The design space has not been confirmed at manufacturing
scale anywhere near the edges of the ranges studied. The claim also holds only at
the set-point of elution flow rate, which is not a factor of the designs, and only within
the qualified life of the resin.
Under ICH Q8(R2), working within an approved design space does not need a regula-

is still assessed under the change management of the quality system (International
Council for Harmonisation 2008). The NOR of this step was not changed, and the
plan’s rule that a range is not narrowed to make a margin the data do not require
applies (PCP-007 §7.5).

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of the A-Mab case
study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009), as PCMP-001 §7.7 sets it out. A critical
process parameter “has an effect on a CQA that is significant and practically relevant
within the characterized range, and a high risk of falling outside its acceptable range
in operation”. A well-controlled critical process parameter “has the same kind of
effect on a CQA but a low risk of falling outside its acceptable range, because it is
readily controlled and its PAR is wide against its NOR”. A key process parameter
affects process performance and has no effect on a CQA within its range. A general
process parameter “has no relevant effect on a CQA or on process performance within
its range”. PCMP-001 §7.7 adds that “a significant effect that is too small to move the
attribute appreciably within the PAR does not by itself make a parameter critical”. An
attribute is called affected by a parameter when a term that contains the parameter is
significant at α = 0.05 (PCMP-001 §6.2). No numerical threshold is set for practical
relevance or for a PAR that is wide against its NOR. Each class below is therefore
stated with the size of the effect across the proven acceptable range and the margin
of that range to the NOR.

  • Protein load was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects aggregate, through its main
    effect and its interactions with pH and stop collect, and host cell protein, through
    its main effect. With the other factors at their set-points, aggregate rises from
    0.56 % to 1.03 % and host cell protein from 99.7 to 211 ng/mg across the range
    of 10–40 g/L resin. Its proven acceptable range is the range studied, 8 g/L resin
    beyond each end of its NOR, and the load is set by the mass of antibody applied
    per litre of resin.
  • Load/wash conductivity was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects host cell protein,
    through its main effect and its interaction with pH, and has no demonstrated
    effect on aggregate. Host cell protein falls from 225 to 113 ng/mg across 3–7
    mS/cm. Its proven acceptable range is the range studied, 1 mS/cm beyond each
    end of its NOR.
  • Elution buffer pH was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects aggregate, through its
    main effect and its interactions with load and stop collect, and host cell protein
    through its interaction with conductivity. Aggregate rises from 0.64 % to 0.90
    % across pH 5.8–6.2. Its proven acceptable range is the range studied, 0.05 pH
    units beyond each end of its NOR, the narrowest margin of the four.
  • Elution stop collect was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects aggregate, through its
    main effect, its curvature and its interactions with load and pH, in the direction
    opposite to the expectation (Section 5.6). Aggregate is 0.72 %, 0.76 % and 0.99
    % at settings of 0.5 AU, 1 AU and 1.5 AU. Its proven acceptable range is the
    range studied, 0.2 AU beyond each end of its NOR.




                                          57
  • Elution flow rate was classified as a GPP. The class rests on a judgement of prac-
    tical relevance, for which no plan sets a threshold. Its range studied of 100–300
    cm/hr extends 50 cm/hr beyond each end of its NOR. No difference was detected
    in aggregate or host cell protein, and every result met its criterion (Section 5.5).
    Residual DNA clearance was 0.32 log10 lower at 300 cm/hr than at 100 cm/hr
    (p = 0.044), so under PCMP-001 §6.2 residual DNA is affected by flow rate. The
    difference is a factor of 2.1 in the residual DNA of the pool across the range stud-
    ied, and about 1.45 across the NOR if the effect is linear in flow rate. The drug
    substance carries 0.00017 ng per dose on average against a criterion of 0.001
    ng, with a capability index of 7.30 (Section 8). If the whole factor reached the
    drug substance, its mean would be 0.00036 ng per dose and its capability index
    2.67, still above the target of 1.33. This report therefore judges the effect too
    small to move residual DNA appreciably in the drug substance, in the sense of
    PCMP-001 §7.7. For step yield the study detected no difference, but the interval
    of the difference, −5.19 to 2.00 points, includes an effect as large as that of pro-
    tein load (−5.18 points). The study therefore does not show that flow rate leaves
    yield unchanged. For yield the class rests on the absence of a detected difference
    and on the expectation of PCP-007 §4.1 that flow rate, within its range, changes
    the sharpness of the elution peak only a little. Fragments were not measured
    in the flow-rate study, so for fragments the class rests on the mechanism alone.
    Flow rate changes the residence time and the mass transfer in the resin, and it
    changes neither the charge of the antibody nor the ionic strength that holds it.

Of the 5 parameters, 4 are critical process parameters, all of them well controlled,
and one is a general process parameter. Each WC-CPP has a proven acceptable range
equal to its range studied, and the conclusions over the NOR hold with margin for
each attribute with a criterion.

11 Discussion

A higher protein load and a higher elution buffer pH raise aggregate in the pool. More
load broadens the elution peaks, so that aggregate overlaps more of the monomer. A
higher pH lowers the positive charge that holds both species, so that they come off
closer together. A higher protein load also raises host cell protein, because more of
it is held with the bound antibody. A higher load/wash conductivity lowers host cell
protein, because a higher ionic strength strips more of the weakly bound species in
the wash, and elution buffer pH changes the size of that effect. A higher stop collect
setting raises aggregate, against the expected mechanism. The selected yield model
has no stop collect term, where a later stop was expected to recover more monomer.
Neither result is explained.
Confidence in the scale-down model rests on the offsets of Table 3.1. For aggregate
the offset is close to 1 and well determined. For host cell protein the ratio is known
only to within its interval of 0.63 to 1.28. The model is qualified for host cell protein
at the criterion in force. It would not be qualified at the a priori criterion, under 3 of
the other models within 2 AICc units of the selected one, or with the run-order drift
added at the unfavourable end of the offset interval (Section 3.1, Section 5.3.4 and
Section 5.3.2).
Screening run 6 was made with the expired buffer lot. With it deleted, the screening
models find the same significant terms. Response-surface run 14 had the tempera-
ture excursion. With it deleted, the judgement over the NOR, the design space and
the outcome of the verification are unchanged (Section 13). Deleting run 14 does
change two selected models. The aggregate model loses its conductivity term, and
the yield model replaces its protein load × load/wash conductivity interaction with a
stop collect term.
The discrepancies from the plan fall into three groups. The analysis departs from
the plan in the model selection, in the use of a host cell protein model that does
not meet the residual criterion, and in the approaches taken where the plan sets no
threshold. The selection changed the terms of the models but no conclusion over the
NOR. The run-order drift of the host cell protein residuals, a factor of 1.13 over the
study, would raise the highest single-run limit over the NOR to 326 ng/mg, below the
criterion of 418 ng/mg. The execution departs from the plan in the flow-rate study
without its set-point run, the residual DNA offset recorded without investigation, and
the fractionated runs of S-CEX-FRC. The record lacks the load results, the residual
DNA and leached Protein A results of the screening runs, the fractions and the mass
balance, the packing tests of each campaign and the feed lots. The missing load
results matter most, because every clearance factor of aggregate and host cell protein
rests on the process model at set-point instead of a measured load.
Five limitations remain.

1. Scale. The scale-down model does not reproduce the hold times, pool volumes
   or mixing of the commercial suite, and no run at manufacturing scale tests the
   edges of the design space. The process performance qualification (PPQ) batches
   of Stage 2, which have not yet been run, test the step at its set-points.
2. The corner of highest load and lowest conductivity. One screening result there

   that corner depends on the choice among the models within 2 AICc units of the
   selected one and on the run-order drift. The corner lies outside the NOR of every
   parameter.
3. The size of the flow-rate study. With two runs at each end and no set-point run,
   the study cannot exclude an effect on yield as large as that of load, and its sig-
   nificant difference in DNA clearance rests on four runs.
4. Fragments. Fragments have no adequate model and no offset. Their judgement
   rests on measured results in the scale-down model and on the assumption that
   they separate alike at both scales, and the confirmation of their platform worst
   case rests on the inadequate model.
5. Resin life cycle. The conclusions hold within the qualified life of the resin, and
   performance at the end of that life is established by the life-cycle study under
   SOP-2008, not here.

12 Conclusions

1. Objective 1 is met. The relationships between the four multivariate parameters
   and the outputs of the step were quantified over ranges wider than the NORs.
   Protein load, elution buffer pH and elution stop collect affect aggregate, and
   protein load and load/wash conductivity affect host cell protein, with pH acting
   through conductivity. The stop collect acts on aggregate in the direction opposite
   to the expectation.
2. Objective 2 is met for aggregate and host cell protein and not met for fragments.
   The design space is drawn on the single-run limit, at manufacturing scale, for
   aggregate and host cell protein. At the criteria in force it covers the whole region
   studied, and at the a priori host cell protein criterion it would cover 99.85 %.
   Host cell protein comes nearest to its criterion, at a corner outside the NOR.
   Fragments have no adequate model, so no model predicts where they meet their
   criterion. Their measured results, all below the criterion, constrain the region
   instead.
3. Objective 3 is met for aggregate and host cell protein and not met for fragments.
   Every output meets its intermediate acceptance criterion across the NOR. The
   highest single-run limits are 1.40 % for aggregate and 289 ng/mg for host cell
   protein, and fragments were 0.76 % at most. Every proven acceptable range
   of aggregate and host cell protein is the range studied. Fragments have no
   adequate model and therefore no proven acceptable range.
4. Objective 4 is met. The four multivariate parameters were classified as WC-
   CPPs and elution flow rate as a GPP, the latter by a judgement of the practical
   relevance of its effect on residual DNA.
5. Objective 5 is met. The step contributes its parameter ranges, the criteria it
   sets for the steps upstream and the criteria it hands on to the control strategy.
   No in-process control or monitoring of the pool is proposed beyond these. The
   capability indices of the drug substance attributes it clears exceed 1.33, the
   lowest 6.14 for host cell protein.
6. Objective 6 is met. The platform worst case for aggregate was not confirmed,
   and its bound of 1.40 tightened the aggregate criteria upstream. The worst case
   for host cell protein was confirmed with the offset at its estimate and would
   not be at the unfavourable end of its interval. The worst case for fragments

   PCMP-001 §7.2. The worst case for leached Protein A stands without verification

7. The runs of both study events were retained. Without screening run 6, made

13.2.1 PD-007-01 Model selection

PCP-007 prescribed the full quadratic model of each response-surface output. Each
output was analysed instead by the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3
(Section 3.5), because the full quadratic carries terms the data do not support. The
selection changed the terms of every model and put aggregate and host cell protein on
the log scale. It changed no conclusion over the NOR. With the plan’s full quadratic,
aggregate and host cell protein also meet their criteria over the NOR, and fragments
fail the limit on overfitting under either analysis. Through the anion exchange model,
the selection also widened the host cell protein criterion of this step from 400 to
418 ng/mg. That widening decides the qualification of the scale-down model for host
cell protein and whether the design space covers the whole region studied, and no
conclusion over the NOR (Section 7.1). It also moved the bound that tightened the
aggregate criteria upstream, from 1.34 under the full quadratic to 1.40.


13.2.2 PD-007-02 Conclusions on a model that does not meet the
       residual criterion

PCP-007 required models that meet every adequacy criterion of PCMP-001 §7.2. The
host cell protein model meets the first three and fails the fourth, because its residuals
fall with the order of execution (p = 0.043). It was used for the ranges and the design
space. The trend amounts to a factor of 1.13 over the study, and added in full to the
highest single-run limit over the NOR it gives 326 ng/mg against the criterion of 418
ng/mg (Section 5.3.2). No run of any model has a Cook’s distance above the median of
F(p, n − p), so no run fails the criterion. The flagged runs were each deleted and the
selection repeated, which the plan provides, and no judgement over the NOR changed
(Section 5.3.3). Outside the NOR the drift would move the edge of the design space
and the qualification for host cell protein (Section 5.3.2).

13.2.3 PD-007-03 Flow-rate study without the set-point run

PCP-007 §6.4 required the flow-rate study at the ends of the range and at the set-point.
Each end was run in duplicate, and no run was made at the set-point. Table 5.10 shows
the qualification runs at the set-point beside the flow-rate runs. They were made in
a different study, before the characterization runs, so a difference between them and
the flow-rate runs mixes any effect of flow rate with the variation between the two
studies. The comparison of the two ends does not depend on them.


13.2.4 PD-007-04 Residual DNA offset recorded, not investigated

Version 1.0 of PCP-007 accepted the scale-down model only where no compared at-
tribute differed significantly between the scales, and required the cause of a differ-
ence to be established before characterization went on. Residual DNA differed at that
level (p = 0.016), and the difference was not investigated before the characterization
runs. Under PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.1 a scale-down model may differ in mean from
manufacturing scale where the offset is known precisely enough, so the Version 1.0
test was stricter than the use of the model needs (Section 3.1).


13.2.5 PD-007-05 Load aggregate and host cell protein not recorded

PCP-007 §5.4 and §12 required aggregate and host cell protein to be measured in the
load of every run, so that each output is read as a fold across the step and a clearance
can be told apart from a low starting level. No load result for either attribute is in
the record. Version 1.0 of this report stated that the load was assayed, with no result
recorded. The clearance factors of this report are taken against the process model
at set-point, which brings 1.91 % aggregate and 9,125 ng/mg host cell protein to the
step. The pool results and the judgements against the criteria do not depend on the
load. The clearance factors and the verification of the platform worst cases do, and
a load that differed from the process model between runs would appear in them as
scatter or as a trend.


13.2.6 PD-007-06 Residual DNA and leached Protein A not measured on
       the screening runs

PCP-007 §5.4 required residual DNA and leached Protein A on every run of both de-
signs. They were measured on the response-surface runs only. The analysis of both
attributes rests on the 28 response-surface runs (Section 5.4), and the flow-rate com-
parison of residual DNA has no screening effect to set against it (Section 5.5).

13.2.7 PD-007-07 Flow-through, wash and strip fractions and the mass
       balance not recorded

PCP-007 §5.4 required the flow-through, wash and strip fractions to be sampled and
a mass balance made for every run, with the strip analysed when the mass balance
falls short. No fraction result and no mass balance is in the record, so the strip test
could not be triggered. Version 1.0 of this report stated that the flow-through and
the wash were assayed, with no result recorded. Where host cell protein is removed
within the step, in the load, the wash or the strip, therefore cannot be shown, and the
mechanism of Section 2.1 is not tested on that point.


13.2.8 PD-007-08 Packing tests not recorded for each campaign

PCP-007 §5.1 required plate count and asymmetry to be tested, and the column re-
leased against SOP-2008, before each campaign. Plate count and asymmetry are
recorded for the 3 scale-down runs of the qualification only, with no acceptance lim-
its. No packing result is recorded for the screening, response-surface and flow-rate
campaigns. Version 1.0 of this report stated that the column was released, with no
result recorded. A change in packing between or within campaigns therefore cannot
be excluded from the record.


13.2.9 PD-007-09 Fractionated runs not in the plan

The study S-CEX-FRC was made and is reported, and it is in neither version of the
plan. Its fractionated run collected the eluate in fractions to read the elution profile
against the stop collect, and its samples were analysed by SEC and by nrCE-SDS
(Section 3.3). Its results support the method decision for fragments and are not used
for any range or class of this step.


13.2.10 PD-007-10 Approaches where the plan sets no threshold, and
        step-level capability

PCP-007 §7 and §8 name the evidence for seven decisions but no threshold. This
report states and applies its own approach for each (Section 3.5.1). Three of the
decisions could not be made as planned, because the packing records, the fractions
and the load results are missing (PD-007-08, PD-007-07 and PD-007-05). PCP-007
§5.5 also asked for the capability of the step, from the fitted models over draws within
the NOR, as a Cpk for each attribute against its intermediate criterion. Capability is
given for the drug substance from the integrated model instead (Section 8), which is
the estimate PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.9 uses.

PCP-007 §6.2 required the feed lot of every run to be recorded and the lots compared
where more than one is used. The record does not name the feed lot of any run. A
lot effect therefore cannot be separated from the run-order drift of host cell protein
or from the scatter between runs.
