Executive summary

Cation exchange chromatography is Step 7 of the A-Mab drug substance process and
its principal aggregate polishing step. The antibody is bound to the resin and eluted
by a step change in pH, and the step clears host cell protein, residual DNA, leached
Protein A and part of the fragments. It sets no quality attribute of its own. This report
presents the Stage 1 characterization of the step under the plan PCP-007 (U.S. Food
and Drug Administration 2011). The studies ran between 2026-02-04 and 2026-03-
27 on a scale-down model of the commercial column, for a process that runs at a
production bioreactor scale of 15,000 L at the commercial site in Grafton, WI.
Protein load, load/wash conductivity, elution buffer pH and elution stop collect were
studied in a two-level full factorial screening design of 19 runs and in a face-centred
central composite design of 28 runs. Elution flow rate was assessed one factor at
a time. The scale-down model is qualified for aggregate and host cell protein by its
offsets from manufacturing scale, and every judgement of those two outputs is stated
at manufacturing scale. For host cell protein the qualification holds at the criterion
in force of 427 ng/mg and would not hold at the a priori criterion of 400 ng/mg.
Aggregate and host cell protein met their intermediate acceptance criteria at every
combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high. The highest 95 % single-run limit
over the NOR was 1.40 % for aggregate against 5.00 %, and 289 ng/mg for host cell
protein against 427 ng/mg. Fragments have no adequate model and were judged on
their measured results, which lay between 0.47 % and 0.76 % against 2.00 %. On
the single-run limit the design space covers the whole characterized region, and the
proven acceptable range of every parameter for aggregate and host cell protein is its
range studied.
The models of aggregate and host cell protein were selected on the log scale and meet
the first three adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2. The host cell protein model does
not meet the fourth, because its residuals drift with the order of execution. That drift
amounts to a factor of 1.13 over the study, and the highest single-run limit over the
NOR stays below the criterion with the whole drift added to it. Of the 5 parameters,
4 were classified as well-controlled critical process parameters and elution flow rate
as a general process parameter.
Within its NOR the step cleared aggregate less than the platform worst case on which
the criteria upstream rely. The lowest clearance factor predicted was 1.58 against
2.00. The one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch, 1.40, replaced the plat-
form value and tightened the aggregate criteria of the low-pH pool and the bioreactor
harvest. The platform worst case for host cell protein was confirmed. The fragment
model does not meet PCMP-001 §7.2, so the platform value for fragments stands with-
out confirmation. Leached Protein A is not a response of the designs.

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody developed on the Novacyte plat-
form (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Its drug substance is purified by Protein
A chromatography, low-pH viral inactivation, cation exchange chromatography,
anion exchange chromatography, small-virus retentive filtration and ultrafiltra-
tion/diafiltration. Cation exchange is Step 7 of this train. It receives the neutralized
pool of the low-pH viral inactivation step and passes its eluate pool to anion
exchange.
The step is operated in bind and elute mode on a resin with a sulfonate ligand. Below
its isoelectric point the antibody carries a net positive charge and binds the ligand.
After a wash at the conductivity of the load, the antibody is eluted by a step change
to the elution buffer, whose pH weakens the binding. The pool is collected between
a threshold on the ascending edge of the elution peak and the stop collect threshold
on the descending edge, both read as absorbance at 280 nm. Aggregate binds more
strongly than monomer and elutes later, so the step separates the two by where the
pool is cut. Host cell protein, residual DNA and leached Protein A are cleared in the
load and the wash.
No quality attribute is set at this step. Aggregate is formed in the production biore-
actor and added to during the low-pH hold. The derivation of the intermediate accep-
tance criteria credits no later step with any change in aggregate, so the pool of this
step is judged against the drug substance criterion itself. Host cell protein is cleared
here and again at anion exchange. No viral clearance is claimed for cation exchange.
Enveloped virus is claimed at low-pH inactivation, anion exchange and virus filtra-
tion, and small viruses at anion exchange and virus filtration (International Council
for Harmonisation 2023a).
The process is being transferred from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the
commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-
001. It operates there at a production bioreactor scale of 15,000 L. Table 1.1 lists the
documents this report depends on and the document it rolls up into.

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The cation exchange step has been operated on the Novacyte humanized IgG1 plat-
form for three earlier products, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and throughout A-Mab
clinical and engineering manufacture (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The resin
chemistry, the buffer system and the mode of operation are unchanged for A-Mab.
This experience establishes the mechanisms of the step and identified the parame-
ters worth studying. It also supplies the platform worst cases from which the criteria
are derived (Section 7.1 and Section 7.4).
Aggregate presents more positive charge to the ligand than monomer does, so it is
retained more strongly and elutes on the tail of the elution peak. The clearance of
aggregate depends on how well the two species are resolved and on where collection
stops. Most host cell proteins have an isoelectric point below the load pH and leave
in the load flow-through, and the weakly bound remainder is displaced in the wash. A
part of the host cell protein is held in association with the bound antibody and elutes
with it. DNA carries a strong negative charge and is repelled by the ligand, so its
clearance is large and is not expected to depend on the parameters studied. Free
leached Protein A does not bind under load conditions, but the leached Protein A in
complex with the antibody binds with it, which limits the clearance to a modest factor.
Fragments carry less positive charge than intact antibody and elute on the leading
side of the main peak. Yield is lost at both edges of the pool, before collection starts
and after it stops.
PCP-007 §4.1 draws four expectations from this experience, and the multivariate de-
signs were built to test them.

  1. A higher protein load occupies more of the ligand. The monomer and aggregate
     peaks broaden and overlap, and more host cell protein is held with the bound
     antibody, so aggregate and host cell protein in the pool are expected to rise and
     yield to fall as load rises.
  2. A higher load/wash conductivity is expected to strip more of the weakly bound
     host cell protein in the wash and to have little effect on the aggregate separation,
     which happens during elution.
  3. A higher elution buffer pH weakens binding and brings monomer and aggregate
     off closer together, so it is expected to raise aggregate in the pool.
  4. The stop collect point is an absorbance on the descending edge, so a lower set-
     ting stops collection later. Stopping later is expected to take in more monomer
     and the leading part of the aggregate peak, which raises both the yield and the
     aggregate of the pool, and to add eluate that carries few fragments.




                                          14
The plan adds that a higher conductivity is expected to remove more host cell protein
at a high load than at a low one, and that elution flow rate, within its range, is expected
to change the sharpness of the peak too little to move the aggregate content of the
pool. Curvature was expected in the effects of elution stop collect and elution buffer
pH, because both move the cut point against a peak that has a maximum (PCP-007
§6.3).

3.5 Statistical methods

All factors were analysed in coded units. Each factor is coded so that its set-point is
0 and the ends of its range studied are −1 and +1. At this step the set-point of every
factor is the centre of its range, so the design centre and the set-point coincide. The
letter codes of Table 4.2 are used for the model terms.
The screening design was analysed by ordinary least squares with all main effects
and all two-factor interactions. An effect is twice the coded coefficient, the change in
the response across the whole range of the factor, and a term is significant at p < 0.05
(PCMP-001 §6.2). The screening model identifies effects. It is not the predictive model
of the step, because a model without quadratic terms cannot describe curvature.
The response-surface model is the predictive model. Every judgement over the NOR,
every proven acceptable range, the design space and the verification of the platform
worst cases come from it. The plan prescribed the full quadratic model of each output,
with 14 terms besides the intercept (PCP-007 §5.5). Each output was instead analysed
by the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3, in three steps (discrepancy
PD-007-01).

  1. The scale was chosen from the Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic (Box and Cox
     1964). The log scale is used where the 95 % interval of the Box-Cox exponent λ
     contains 0 and excludes 1, and the raw scale otherwise.
  2. On that scale, every hierarchical submodel of the full quadratic was fitted, 1,337
     candidates for four factors, and the one with the lowest corrected Akaike infor-
     mation criterion (AICc) was taken. A hierarchical model keeps the main effects
     of any interaction or square it contains.




                                          22
  3. The selected model was judged against the four adequacy criteria of PCMP-001
     §7.2. An output whose selected model fails one of the first three criteria has no
     adequate model. It is judged on its measured results and sets no range.

AICc is the small-sample form of Akaike’s criterion (Hurvich and Tsai 1989), and se-
lecting a model by such a criterion is standard practice (Burnham and Anderson 2002).
It estimates how well a model would predict new runs. It rewards a close fit and pe-
nalizes every estimated term, and the residual variance is counted as one of those
terms. Its correction for small samples raises the penalty when the runs are few for
the number of terms, as they are in a design of 28 runs, so that the criterion does not
favour too many terms. The search is exhaustive because a stepwise search changes
one term at a time and stops when no single change lowers AICc, which can leave it
short of a model that only a combination of changes reaches. On the data of this step
a stepwise search, backward or forward, would have reached the selected model for
aggregate. For host cell protein a backward search would have stopped at a model
of 8 terms and a forward search at one of 3, with AICc values of −27.4 and −27.9
against −31.2 for the selected model, and for fragments both would have stopped at
a model with a higher AICc. In this report the terms of a model are counted without
the intercept.
The Box-Cox profile chose the log scale for aggregate (λ = 0.18, 95 % interval −0.40
to 0.62) and for host cell protein (λ = −0.01, interval −0.88 to 0.87). It kept the
raw scale for fragments, whose interval contains 1. On the log scale the model is
ln 𝑦 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + ⋯ + 𝜀. Transformed back, 𝑦 = 𝑒𝑏0 𝑒𝑏1 𝑥1 ⋯ 𝑒𝜀 , so each parameter
multiplies the output by a fixed factor 𝑒𝑏 per coded unit, and by 𝑒2𝑏 from the low
to the high end of its range. The scatter between runs multiplies the output too, so
its size in the unit of the output grows with the level. This is the behaviour expected
of a purification step, which removes a share of what is present, so that a parameter
changes the share rather than a fixed amount. It is also expected of a method whose
precision is a percentage of the level, as the ELISA for host cell protein is.
Least squares, and the t-tests, F-tests and prediction intervals built on it, assume
residuals that are independent and normally distributed, with mean zero and the
same variance over the whole range studied. For these two outputs the evidence for
the log scale is the Box-Cox profile. The residuals themselves do not show the scatter
growing with the level on the raw scale. For the full quadratic on the raw scale, the
residual SD in the upper half of the predictions is only 5 % larger than in the lower
half for aggregate and 6 % larger for host cell protein. On the log scale it is smaller
in the upper half (0.042 against 0.065 for aggregate). At 28 runs neither difference
is large enough to decide the scale, and the regression of the absolute residuals on
the predicted values, one of the tests of the fourth criterion, finds no trend on the log
scale for either output (Table 5.5).
The log scale has four consequences for the reader. The criteria stay in their own units,
% and ng/mg. Effects are fold changes, and Section 5.3 gives them. A prediction
interval is calculated on the log scale and transformed back, which keeps its 95 %
coverage, because the probability that ln y lies between two values is the probability
that y lies between their antilogs. The interval is asymmetric. At the least favourable
NOR combination for host cell protein the median is 227 ng/mg and the interval runs




                                           23
from 178 to 289 ng/mg, further above the median than below it. The predicted value
is the median of the output, its geometric mean, not its arithmetic mean. The upper
prediction limit, which decides against an upper criterion, is not affected by that.
Each model is reported with R², adjusted R² and predicted R², the last from the predic-
tion error sum of squares. The four adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 are applied
as PCP-007 §7.2 states them as tests.

  1. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  2. Lack of fit against the pure error of the centre points is not significant at α =
     0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20.
  4. The absolute residuals regressed on the predicted values, and the residuals re-
     gressed on the order of execution, show no slope significant at α = 0.05. A run
     with a Cook’s distance above 4/n is examined by deleting it and repeating the
     judgement, and the model fails this criterion only where a run’s Cook’s distance
     exceeds the median of the F distribution with p and n − p degrees of freedom.

A model with no term has no F-test, so the first criterion does not apply to it. An output
with a selected model that fails the fourth criterion is still used for the ranges and
the design space, and that use is discrepancy PD-007-02. For each such model this
report states the size of the trend against the criterion of its output, and it deletes each
flagged run in turn, repeats the selection and judges the output over the NOR again.
Where other models lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one, they are reported as
a sensitivity, and the selected model decides.
A quality attribute is judged on a single batch. The judgement over the NOR asks
whether the exact 95 % prediction interval of a single run, at manufacturing scale,
lies within the criterion at every one of the 81 combinations of NOR low, set-point
and NOR high of the four factors (PCP-007 §7.4). The interval is two-sided, so its
upper end is a one-sided 97.5 % limit, and since every criterion at this step is an
upper limit, the upper end decides. The design space is the set of settings within the
ranges studied at which every output with a criterion meets it at both ends of the
same interval. It is evaluated on a grid of 11 levels per factor, 14,641 points in all.
Two proven acceptable ranges are computed for each parameter and each output with
a criterion and an adequate model (PCMP-001 §6.4). The set-point analysis scans the
parameter across its range with the other factors at their set-points and judges the
mean prediction, which is the median for a log-scale model, at manufacturing scale.
The NOR-propagated analysis scans the parameter at 81 points and draws, at each,
2,000 settings of the other factors, each from a normal distribution centred on its set-
point, with a standard deviation equal to the width of its NOR divided by 6, and clipped
to the range studied. Each draw takes its own fitted coefficients from their sampling
distribution and its own residual. A setting is accepted where the 97.5th percentile
of the simulated batches, at manufacturing scale, does not exceed the criterion. The
range reported is the contiguous accepted interval that contains the set-point. The
simulation uses the fixed seed 20240724. The NOR-propagated range is the one that
supports a claim.

The verification of the platform worst cases follows PCP-007 §7.6. The clearance

4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study S-CEX-RSM ran from 2026-03-02 to 2026-03-27. It is a
face-centred central composite design in the same four factors, with 16 factorial runs,
8 axial runs and 4 centre-point runs, 28 runs in all. The axial runs sit on the faces
of the cube, so no run lies outside the range studied of any factor. The design adds
the quadratic terms that the screening design cannot estimate. All four screening
factors were carried into it, so the plan’s rule for a screening factor left out of the
response-surface design did not apply. Appendix B gives the design and its results.



4.4 Univariate assessment

The flow-rate study S-CEX-UNI ran from 2026-03-16 to 2026-03-20, within the window
of the response-surface study. Elution flow rate was run at each end of its range, 100
cm/hr and 300 cm/hr, in duplicate, with every other parameter at its set-point. The
plan also required a run at the set-point, which was not made, and the qualification
runs at the set-point serve as the reference instead (discrepancy PD-007-03). Pool
aggregate, pool host cell protein, step yield, and residual DNA and leached Protein
A in the load and the pool were measured on each run. A study of one factor at a
time cannot show whether flow rate interacts with the other parameters. That limit
was accepted because flow rate sets the residence time in the resin, and with it the
sharpness of the peak, while it changes neither the charge of the antibody nor the
ionic strength that holds it to the ligand.

5.3.2 Host cell protein

The host cell protein model on the log scale has 6 terms, protein load, load/wash con-
ductivity, elution buffer pH, protein load × elution buffer pH, load/wash conductivity
× elution buffer pH and protein load². It explains 95.1 % of the variation (adjusted

is not (p = 0.846), and predicted R² falls short of R² by 0.031. The model does not
meet the fourth criterion. Its residuals fall with the order of execution (p = 0.043),
although they show no trend against the predicted values (p = 0.242). Runs 8 and 13
have a Cook’s distance above 0.143, the largest 0.24 for run 8, and neither exceeds
the F median of 0.94.
From the low to the high end of its range, protein load multiplies pool host cell protein
by 2.12 before its curvature is added, and load/wash conductivity multiplies it by 0.50.
The two effects are of the same size within their standard errors. With the other
factors at their set-points the model gives 99.7, 159 and 211 ng/mg at manufacturing
scale for loads of 10 g/L resin, 25 g/L resin and 40 g/L resin, and 225 and 113 ng/mg
at conductivities of 3 mS/cm and 7 mS/cm. The load term has a significant negative
curvature, so host cell protein rises more slowly at high loads. Elution buffer pH has
no significant main effect (p = 0.491), but it changes the effect of conductivity (p =
0.005). From the low to the high end of the conductivity range, host cell protein falls
by a factor of 0.60 at pH 5.8 and of 0.42 at pH 6.2. The load × pH term is kept with
p = 0.075. On the log scale the full quadratic gives the load × conductivity term p =
0.988, so the interaction the screening design found on the raw scale is not found on
the log scale.

5.6 Mechanistic interpretation

Protein load and elution buffer pH acted on aggregate in the directions expected.
A higher load occupies more of the ligand and broadens the elution peaks, so the
aggregate front overlaps more of the monomer. A higher pH weakens the binding of
both species and brings them off closer together. The positive load × pH interaction
fits the same picture, since the separation that load erodes is the separation that a



                                                    39
high pH has already compressed. Within this range conductivity has no demonstrated
effect on aggregate, as expected for a parameter that acts during the wash rather than
during elution.
The elution stop collect acted on aggregate in the opposite direction to the expectation
of PCP-007 §4.1. A higher setting stops collection earlier on the descending edge and
should leave more of the aggregate tail out of the pool. In both designs aggregate
instead rose as the setting rose, by 1.37-fold across the range in the response-surface
model, with its lowest value near 0.63 AU. The model also found no effect of the stop
collect on yield, where a later stop was expected to recover more monomer. The
mechanism of Section 2.1 does not explain these results, and this report gives no
cause for them. The classification of the stop collect rests on the measured effect,
not on the mechanism (Section 9).
Protein load and load/wash conductivity acted on host cell protein in the directions
expected. More load leaves more host cell protein held with the bound antibody, and
a higher ionic strength strips more of the weakly bound species in the wash. On the
log scale each acts as a constant factor, so in ng/mg conductivity removes more host
cell protein at a high load, where there is more to remove, as the plan expected. A
change with load of the share that conductivity removes was not found. The pH ×
conductivity interaction was not expected, and the mechanism of Section 2.1 does
not explain it.
Residual DNA clearance did not depend on any of the four factors of the response-
surface design, as expected for a species repelled by the ligand. The lower clearance
at the highest flow rate is not explained by that mechanism either. Leached Protein A
was cleared by a modest factor, as expected for a species partly bound in complex with
the antibody. Yield fell with load, as expected when broader peaks lose more monomer
at their edges. For operation, aggregate control rests on protein load, elution buffer
pH and elution stop collect together, and host cell protein control rests on protein load
and load/wash conductivity, with elution buffer pH acting through conductivity.

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of the A-Mab case
study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009), as PCMP-001 §7.7 sets it out. A parameter
that has a significant effect on a quality attribute, and is held well inside its accept-
able range by the control system, is a well-controlled critical process parameter. A
parameter with such an effect whose control is narrow against its acceptable range is
a critical process parameter. A parameter that changes only yield or another perfor-
mance attribute is a key process parameter, and one that changes neither is a general
process parameter. An attribute is called affected by a parameter when a term that
contains the parameter is significant at α = 0.05 (PCMP-001 §6.2). No numerical
threshold is set for “well inside” or “narrow”, so each class below is stated with the
size of the effect across the proven acceptable range and the margin of that range to
the NOR.

  • Protein load was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects aggregate, through its main ef-
    fect and its interactions with pH and stop collect, and host cell protein, through
    its main effect and its curvature. With the other factors at their set-points, ag-
    gregate rises from 0.56 % to 1.03 % and host cell protein from 99.7 to 211 ng/mg
    across the range of 10–40 g/L resin. Its proven acceptable range is the range
    studied, 8 g/L resin beyond each end of its NOR, and the load is set by the mass
    of antibody applied per litre of resin.
  • Load/wash conductivity was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects host cell protein,
    through its main effect and its interaction with pH, and has no demonstrated
    effect on aggregate. Host cell protein falls from 225 to 113 ng/mg across 3–7
    mS/cm. Its proven acceptable range is the range studied, 1 mS/cm beyond each
    end of its NOR.
  • Elution buffer pH was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects aggregate, through its
    main effect and its interactions with load and stop collect, and host cell protein
    through its interaction with conductivity. Aggregate rises from 0.64 % to 0.90
    % across pH 5.8–6.2. Its proven acceptable range is the range studied, 0.05 pH
    units beyond each end of its NOR, the narrowest margin of the four.
  • Elution stop collect was classified as a WC-CPP. It affects aggregate, through its
    main effect, its curvature and its interactions with load and pH, in the direction
    opposite to the expectation (Section 5.6). Aggregate is 0.72 %, 0.76 % and 0.99
    % at settings of 0.5 AU, 1 AU and 1.5 AU. Its proven acceptable range is the
    range studied, 0.2 AU beyond each end of its NOR.
  • Elution flow rate was classified as a GPP. Over 100–300 cm/hr no difference was
    detected in aggregate, host cell protein or step yield, and every result met its
    criterion (Section 5.5). The comparison of residual DNA clearance between the
    two ends reached p = 0.044 on two runs at each end, for an attribute with no
    criterion at this step. Fragments were not measured in the flow-rate study, so for



                                           52
    fragments the class rests on the mechanism alone, a change in residence time
    that changes neither the charge of the antibody nor the ionic strength that holds
    it.

Of the 5 parameters, 4 are critical process parameters, all of them well controlled,
and one is a general process parameter. Each WC-CPP has a proven acceptable range
equal to its range studied, and the conclusions over the NOR hold with margin for
each attribute with a criterion.

     meets that criterion across the NOR with the highest single-run limit at 289
     ng/mg.
  4. Release testing. The drug substance criteria of Table 2.1 apply at release. For
     aggregate and fragments the register names the method, SEC and nrCE-SDS.

The conclusions of this report hold for resin within the cycle count qualified under
SOP-2008. Viral clearance and the final levels of host cell protein, residual DNA and
leached Protein A are controlled by anion exchange and virus filtration, in PCR-008
and PCR-009, and the cumulative viral clearance is consolidated in PCMR-001.

11 Discussion

The study shows how the four multivariate parameters act on the pool. Protein load,
elution buffer pH and elution stop collect govern aggregate, and protein load and
load/wash conductivity govern host cell protein, with pH acting on host cell protein
through conductivity. The main effects of load, pH and conductivity follow the phys-
ical chemistry of the step. The effect of the stop collect on aggregate does not, and
neither does the absence of a stop collect effect on yield. The classification and the
ranges rest on the measured effects, so these departures from the expected mecha-
nism do not weaken them, but they remain unexplained.
Confidence in the scale-down model rests on the offsets of Table 3.1. For aggregate
the offset is close to 1 and well determined. For host cell protein the ratio is known
only to within its interval of 0.63 to 1.28. The model is qualified for host cell protein
at the criterion in force, but not at the a priori criterion, and not under one of the
near-equivalent models (Section 3.1 and Section 5.3.4). The residual DNA offset was
recorded and not investigated, and residual DNA has no criterion at this step.
The study events changed no conclusion (Section 13). The discrepancies from the
plan are the model selection, the use for ranges and the design space of a host cell
protein model that does not meet the residual criterion, the flow-rate study without
its set-point run, and the residual DNA offset recorded without investigation. The
first changed the terms of the models but no conclusion over the NOR. The second is
bounded by the size of the trend against the criterion. The third leaves the flow-rate
study with a reference from a different study, made at a different time. The fourth
affects no criterion.
Five limitations remain, and each is managed forward.

  1. Scale. The scale-down model does not reproduce the hold times, pool volumes
     or mixing of the commercial suite, and no run at manufacturing scale tests the
     edges of the design space. The process performance qualification (PPQ) batches
     of Stage 2, which have not yet been run, test the step at its set-points.
  2. The corner of highest load and lowest conductivity. One screening result there
     exceeded the host cell protein criterion, and the edge of the design space at that
     corner depends on the choice among near-equivalent models. The corner lies
     outside the NOR of every parameter.
  3. The size of the flow-rate study. With two runs at each end and no set-point run,
     the study cannot exclude an effect on yield as large as that of load, and its sug-
     gestion of lower DNA clearance at the high end rests on four runs.
  4. Fragments. Fragments have no adequate model and no offset, and their judge-
     ment rests on measured results in the scale-down model and on the assumption
     that they separate alike at both scales.




                                           55
5. Resin life cycle. The conclusions hold within the qualified life of the resin, and
   performance at the end of that life is established by the life-cycle study under
   SOP-2008, not here.

12 Conclusions

1. The relationships between the four multivariate parameters and the outputs of
   the step were quantified over ranges wider than the NORs (objective 1). Protein
   load, elution buffer pH and elution stop collect affect aggregate, and protein
   load and load/wash conductivity affect host cell protein, with pH acting through
   conductivity. The stop collect acts on aggregate in the direction opposite to the
   expectation.
2. The design space, drawn on the single-run limit at manufacturing scale, covers
   the whole region studied (objective 2). Host cell protein comes nearest to its
   criterion, at a corner outside the NOR.
3. Every output meets its intermediate acceptance criterion across the NOR. The
   highest single-run limits are 1.40 % for aggregate and 289 ng/mg for host cell
   protein, and fragments were 0.76 % at most. Every proven acceptable range of
   aggregate and host cell protein is the range studied (objective 3).
4. The four multivariate parameters were classified as WC-CPPs and elution flow
   rate as a GPP (objective 4).
5. The step contributes its parameter ranges, the criteria it sets for the steps up-
   stream and the host cell protein criterion it hands on to the control strategy
   (objective 5). The capability indices of the drug substance attributes it clears
   exceed 1.33, the lowest 6.17 for host cell protein.
6. The platform worst case for aggregate was not confirmed, and its bound of 1.40
   tightened the aggregate criteria upstream. The worst case for host cell protein
   was confirmed. The worst cases for fragments and leached Protein A stand with-
   out confirmation by a model of this step (objective 6).
7. The runs of both study events were retained and changed no conclusion. The
   register records 4 discrepancies from the plan. The results roll up into PCMR-
   001.

13.2.1 PD-007-01 Model selection

PCP-007 prescribed the full quadratic model of each response-surface output. Each
output was analysed instead by the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3
(Section 3.5), because the full quadratic carries terms the data do not support. The
selection changed the terms of every model and put aggregate and host cell protein on
the log scale. It changed no conclusion over the NOR. With the plan’s full quadratic,
aggregate and host cell protein also meet their criteria over the NOR, and fragments
fail the limit on overfitting under either analysis. Through the anion exchange model,
the selection also widened the host cell protein criterion of this step from 400 to
427 ng/mg. That widening decides the qualification of the scale-down model for host
cell protein and no conclusion over the NOR (Section 7.1). It also moved the bound
that tightened the aggregate criteria upstream, from 1.35 under the full quadratic to
1.40.


13.2.2 PD-007-02 Conclusions on a model that does not meet the
       residual criterion

PCP-007 required models that meet every adequacy criterion of PCMP-001 §7.2. The
host cell protein model meets the first three and fails the fourth, because its residuals
fall with the order of execution (p = 0.043). It was used for the ranges and the design
space. The trend amounts to a factor of 1.13 over the study, and added in full to




                                           62
the highest single-run limit over the NOR it gives 326 ng/mg against the criterion of
427 ng/mg (Section 5.3.2). No run of any model exceeds the F median of the Cook’s
distance, so no run fails the criterion. The flagged runs were each deleted and the
selection repeated, which the plan provides, and no judgement over the NOR changed
(Section 5.3.3).


13.2.3 PD-007-03 Flow-rate study without the set-point run

PCP-007 §6.4 required the flow-rate study at the ends of the range and at the set-
point. Each end was run in duplicate, and no run was made at the set-point. The
qualification runs at the set-point serve as the reference (Table 5.10). They were
made in a different study, before the characterization runs, so a difference between
them and the flow-rate runs mixes any effect of flow rate with the variation between
the two studies. The comparison of the two ends does not depend on them.


13.2.4 PD-007-04 Residual DNA offset recorded, not investigated

Version 1.0 of PCP-007 accepted the scale-down model only where no compared at-
tribute differed significantly between the scales, and required the cause of a differ-
ence to be established before characterization went on. Residual DNA differed at that
level (p = 0.016), and the difference was not investigated before the characterization
runs. Under PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.1 a scale-down model may differ in mean from
manufacturing scale where the offset is known precisely enough, so the Version 1.0
test was stricter than the use of the model needs. Residual DNA has no criterion at
this step, and its offset is recorded only (Section 3.1).
