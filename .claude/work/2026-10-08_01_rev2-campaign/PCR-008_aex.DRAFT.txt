Executive summary

This report presents the Stage 1 process characterization of the anion exchange step,
Step 8 of the A-Mab drug substance process, for a commercial bioreactor scale of
15,000 L. The step is operated in flow-through mode and is the last chromatography
step before virus filtration. It clears host cell protein, residual DNA, leached Protein
A and virus. The register of critical quality attributes assigns to it the cumulative
clearance of minute virus of mice (MVM), and it contributes a claim to the clearance
of xenotropic murine leukaemia virus (XMuLV). The studies were run under the char-
acterization plan PCP-008 in a scale-down model qualified under SOP-1001. They
follow the lifecycle approach to process validation (U.S. Food and Drug Administra-
tion 2011), ICH Q8(R2), Q9(R1) and Q11 (International Council for Harmonisation
2009, 2023b, 2012), ICH Q5A(R2) for the viral clearance claims (International Coun-
cil for Harmonisation 2023a) and the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009). This Version 2.0 is issued under the investigations INV-001 and INV-002.
Four parameters (load pH, Equil/Wash-1 conductivity, load conductivity and protein
load) were studied in a screening design of 19 runs and a response-surface design of
28 runs. In September 2026 the response-surface design was augmented by 12 runs,
a number INV-002 calculated. Operating flow rate was assessed one factor at a time.
The first execution of both designs ran on a deamidated load and was invalidated, and
both designs were re-executed on a requalified load (study event DEV-008-01).
Each response-surface output was analysed by the model selection of INV-002 cor-
rective action CA3, and every selected model meets each adequacy criterion of the
master plan PCMP-001 §7.2 that applies to it. Pool host cell protein is modelled on
the log scale and depends on load pH and Equil/Wash-1 conductivity. The log re-
duction factors (LRFs) of both viruses depend on load pH and load conductivity. The
selected response-surface models of step yield and fragments contain no term, so they
show no effect of the parameters within the ranges studied. The screening model of
fragments carried one significant term, the interaction of load pH and protein load
(−0.064 percentage points, p = 0.029). Every output with a criterion met its inter-
mediate acceptance criterion at every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR
high, at manufacturing scale where an offset is known (pool host cell protein). The
upper 95 % limit of a single run of pool host cell protein reached at most 29.4 ng/mg
against 100 ng/mg. At the least favourable combination the mean XMuLV LRF was
5.86 log10 against the step claim of 5.5 log10, and the mean MVM LRF 4.24 log10
against 4 log10.
The design space covers 95.0 % of a grid over the characterized region and contains
the whole NOR. It is drawn on the single-batch limit for the quality attributes and on
the mean prediction for viral clearance, and MVM clearance bounds it where load
pH is low and load conductivity high. Every proven acceptable range contains its

NOR. For XMuLV and MVM the NOR-propagated range of load pH ends inside the
characterized range, 0.135 and 0.1275 pH units below the lower end of the NOR. The
scale-down model is qualified for pool host cell protein, the one compared output with
a criterion at this step.
The host cell protein clearance of the step was verified against its platform worst
case of 4-fold. At manufacturing scale, with the offset of the scale-down model at
its estimate, the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a single batch is 4.18-fold. It
replaced the platform value and widened the host cell protein criteria of the Protein
A pool and the cation exchange pool to 16,719 and 418 ng/mg. The bound does not
depend on a single run. It does depend on the model selection, because the plan’s full

the offset at the unfavourable end of its 90 % interval it would be 3.08-fold, below the

All five parameters were classified as well-controlled critical process parameters (WC-
CPP). In the integrated process model of 2,000 simulated batches, the cumulative
MVM clearance has the lowest Cpk of the attributes this step sets or clears, 1.51,
above the target of 1.33. No simulated batch fell outside any of their criteria. The
record holds three study events from the execution (DEV-008-01 to DEV-008-03) and
10 discrepancies from the plan (PD-008-01 to PD-008-10). Three of them are changes
to the work, namely the augmentation, the model selection and a flow-rate study run
without its centre run. The others record requirements of the plan that were not
met. They concern the qualification of the scale-down model, virus assay controls,
the qualification of the load, records of execution, measurements and analyses not
made and the coding of protein load. The last records the approaches this report
states where the plan sets no threshold, and the analyses it adds. The results roll up
into the master report PCMR-001, which consolidates the cumulative viral clearance
claim.

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose primary mechanism of action
is antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). It is
purified on the platform train of Protein A capture, low-pH viral inactivation, cation ex-
change, anion exchange, virus filtration and UF/DF. The process is being transferred
from the development site in Cambridge, MA to the commercial drug substance site
in Grafton, WI under the Process Transfer Plan PTP-001. The commercial process
runs at a bioreactor scale of 15,000 L.
Anion exchange chromatography is Step 8 of the train. The resin carries a quaternary
amine ligand with a fixed positive charge. At the load pH the antibody carries a net
positive charge and passes through the bed, while species that carry a net negative
charge bind. These are residual DNA, the acidic part of the host cell protein popula-
tion, leached Protein A and virus particles. The column is equilibrated, loaded with
the cation exchange pool adjusted to its target pH and conductivity, and chased with
the first wash buffer. The pool is collected from the start of the load to the end of the
chase. The step processes the cation exchange pool of a single batch at commercial
scale (PCP-008 §1).
The register of critical quality attributes assigns the cumulative MVM clearance to
this step (Table 2.1). The step also contributes a modular XMuLV clearance claim.
It clears host cell protein, residual DNA and leached Protein A, which are formed
upstream and cleared by more than one step, and fragments are judged in its pool.

1.2 Objectives

The study had six objectives, as PCP-008 §2 states them:

  1. Quantify the effect of each studied parameter on pool host cell protein, on the
     LRFs of XMuLV and MVM, and on step yield, over the ranges of Table 4.2, and
     the effect of each multivariate parameter on fragments.
  2. Define the multivariate operating region within which every governed quality
     attribute meets its criterion, a quality attribute on the single-batch limit and
     viral clearance on its mean prediction, as PCMP-001 Version 2.0 draws its design
     space, and state where the boundary of that region lies.
  3. Establish a proven acceptable range for each parameter and each attribute it
     governs, and confirm that the NOR falls inside it.
  4. Classify each parameter under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001
     from the effects demonstrated in the study and from the capability with which
     the parameter can be held.
  5. Determine whether the step meets its platform claims for both model viruses at
     the worst case within the NOR, and justify the Stage 2 ranges and this step’s
     part of the control strategy.
  6. Verify the host cell protein clearance of this step against its platform worst case,
     4-fold, on which the host cell protein criteria of the upstream steps rely.

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The platform anion exchange step has been used to manufacture three earlier human-
ized IgG1 antibodies, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and to supply A-Mab clinical studies
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). That experience established the mode of op-
eration, the buffer system and the clearance the step delivers. It also gave the two
platform worst cases of this step on which the criteria of the upstream steps rely,
a host cell protein clearance of at least 4-fold and a leached Protein A clearance of
at least 10-fold (PCMP-001 §7.3). The platform claims of the step are 5.5 log10 for
XMuLV and 4 log10 for MVM (PCMP-001 §7.4). Characterization of A-Mab therefore
confirms and bounds known behaviour.
Whether an impurity binds depends on its net charge at the load pH and on how
strongly the ionic strength of the buffer shields that charge. At a higher load pH
the pH lies above the isoelectric point of more of the host cell proteins, so more of
them carry a net negative charge and bind. A higher load pH also makes the acidic
impurities and the virus particles more negative, so they bind more strongly. Load
pH is therefore expected to lower pool host cell protein and to raise both LRFs. The
antibody, whose isoelectric point lies well above the load pH, stays positively charged
and passes through. Step yield is therefore not expected to depend on load pH within
the range studied.
A higher conductivity of the equilibration and first wash buffer shields the electro-
static interaction and lets weakly bound host cell protein and virus particles through.
Equil/Wash-1 conductivity is therefore expected to raise pool host cell protein and to
lower both LRFs. Load conductivity acts by the same shielding during the load and
is expected to act in the same directions. Load pH and the conductivities act on the
same binding equilibrium from opposite sides. For this reason PCP-008 §4.1 expected
the effect of Equil/Wash-1 conductivity on host cell protein to depend on load pH.
Protein load sets the impurity mass applied per litre of resin. Pool host cell protein is
expressed per milligram of antibody, so a higher load raises it only where the bound
impurity approaches the capacity of the ligand. Within the range studied the impurity
mass is expected to stay below that capacity, and no effect of protein load is expected.
Operating flow rate sets the residence time in the bed. Binding of the small, highly
charged impurities is fast, so only a small effect is expected. Virus particles diffuse
slowly to the ligand and are the most likely to show an effect at the shortest residence
time.
Residual DNA is a polyanion at every pH the step could run at and binds the ligand
strongly. Its clearance is expected to be large and robust to the parameters. Leached



                                          13
Protein A is acidic and binds while the antibody passes through. Fragments are ex-
pected to leave the step at the level at which they enter it, because on the platform no
step after cation exchange changes the fragment level (PCMP-001 §7.3). This study
measured fragments in the pool only, so that expectation was not tested here.
For both model viruses, clearance is expected to fall as the load pH drops toward the
point at which the particle surface loses its negative charge, and as conductivity rises.
The XMuLV claim of this step is orthogonal to that of low-pH inactivation, which acts
on the viral envelope and not on the surface charge. MVM is not inactivated by low
pH, so this step and the virus filter carry the whole MVM claim (PCMP-001 §7.4).

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The studies were executed on a scale-down model of the commercial anion exchange
column, qualified under SOP-1001 and operated under SOP-2011. The model keeps
the bed height, the linear flow velocity, the protein load in grams of antibody per
litre of resin, and the equilibration, load, wash and chase volumes in column volumes.
Scaling on these variables holds the residence time and the impurity mass per unit of
ligand equal to those of the commercial step. The resin, the buffers and the packing
method are the same as at commercial scale.
The qualification study S-AEX-SDM (2026-02-04 to 2026-02-13) compared 3 scale-
down runs at set-point with 5 batches at manufacturing scale. It ended before the
first execution of the designs began on 2026-02-16, as PCP-008 §5.1 requires. It
compared step yield, pool host cell protein, column efficiency and peak asymmetry.
It also measured residual DNA and leached Protein A in the pools, and every one of
those results was below the limit of quantitation (LOQ) at both scales, so no offset
could be estimated for either. The pressure across the bed, which PCP-008 §5.1 lists
among the compared outputs, was not measured.
The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-down runs minus the mean of the man-
ufacturing batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval, each end of which is

a scale-down value is divided by it. The model is qualified for an output when the
conclusion against its criterion over the NOR is the same with the offset at either end
of its interval. That conclusion is whether the output meets its criterion at every com-
bination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the four multivariate parameters.

3.5 Statistical methods

3.5.1 Coding and the screening model

Each multivariate parameter was coded so that the ends of its characterized range
are −1 and +1 and its middle is 0. For load pH, Equil/Wash-1 conductivity and load
conductivity the middle of the range is the set-point. For protein load the middle is
175 g/L resin, and the set-point is 200 g/L resin. PCP-008 §5.5 placed the set-point at
0, and for protein load the design was laid out about the middle of its range instead
(PD-008-09).
The screening data were fitted by ordinary least squares with every main effect and
every two-factor interaction in coded units. An effect is twice the coded coefficient,
which is the change in the response across the characterized range. A term is signifi-
cant when its p-value is below 0.05 (PCMP-001 §6.2). The screening model identifies
effects. It is not the predictive model, and no range is derived from it.


3.5.2 The response-surface models and their selection

PCP-008 planned the full quadratic model in the four multivariate parameters. Each
output was instead analysed by the model selection of INV-002 corrective action CA3
(Montgomery 2017). This report records that change as the discrepancy PD-008-02
(Section 13.2). The selection has four parts.

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic model (Box and Cox 1964)
     chooses the log scale where its 95 % interval for λ contains 0 and excludes 1,
     and the raw scale otherwise.




                                          20
  2. The model. Among all hierarchical submodels of the full quadratic, the one with
     the lowest AICc is selected. A hierarchical submodel contains an interaction
     only with both its main effects and a square only with its main effect. For four
     parameters there are 1,337 candidates, and every one was fitted.
  3. The block. Where the design has a second block, the augmentation runs, a block
     term is kept in every candidate.
  4. The check. The selected model is judged against the four adequacy criteria of
     PCMP-001 §7.2 given below.

AICc is Akaike’s information criterion with the small-sample correction (Hurvich and
Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002). It estimates how well a model would pre-
dict new runs, and it penalises every estimated coefficient. Its correction raises that
penalty when the runs are few for the number of terms, as they are in these designs,
so that the criterion does not favour too many terms. Here the residual variance is
counted as one of the estimated parameters. This report counts the terms of a model
without the intercept and the block term.
The search is exhaustive. A stepwise search changes one term at a time and stops
when no single change lowers AICc, so it stops where only a combination of changes
would lower AICc further. Across the outputs of the campaign, a backward stepwise
search would have stopped at a worse model for 9 of 21 outputs and a forward search
for 6 (INV-002). At this step a forward search would have stopped at a worse model
for XMuLV, and a backward search for MVM and for fragments. INV-002 had first
refitted the failing models it did not augment by backward elimination on p-values,
on the raw scale (its corrective action CA2). Corrective action CA3, adopted on 2026-
09-15, replaced that refit with the selection described here.
Where other models lie within 2 AICc units of the selected one, they are near-
equivalent.   The selected model decides, and the near-equivalent models are
reported as a sensitivity (Section 5.3.4). A feature that only some of them carry is
not treated as an established effect.


3.5.3 The log scale

Pool host cell protein is the one output modelled on the log scale. Its Box-Cox λ is
0.31, with a 95 % interval from −0.25 to 0.92, which contains 0 and excludes 1. On
the log scale the model is ln 𝑦 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1 𝑥1 + 𝑏2 𝑥2 + ⋯ + 𝑒. Transformed back it is
𝑦 = 𝑒𝑏0 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏1 𝑥1 ⋅ 𝑒𝑏2 𝑥2 ⋯ 𝑒𝑒 . Each parameter therefore multiplies the output by a
fixed factor, 𝑒𝑏 per coded unit and 𝑒2𝑏 from the low to the high end of its range. The
error of a single run multiplies it too, so its size in ng/mg grows with the level.
Two properties of the step and the assay make this form likely. A purification step
removes a share of what is present, so a parameter changes the share and not a fixed
amount. The precision of the host cell protein ELISA is a percentage of the level (9.5
%), so the scatter of the results grows with the level. Least squares, and the t-tests, F-
tests and prediction intervals built on it, assume residuals that are independent and
normally distributed, with mean zero and the same variance over the whole range




                                           21
studied. With the selected terms fitted on the raw scale, the residual standard devi-
ation is 2.26 ng/mg in the lower half of the predictions and 3.06 ng/mg in the upper
half. On the log scale the two halves give 0.144 and 0.127, about the same.
The criterion stays in its own unit, ng/mg, and the effects are fold changes. The
scale-down offset is a ratio of geometric means (Section 3.1). A prediction interval
is calculated on the log scale and transformed back. It keeps its 95 % coverage,
because the probability that ln 𝑦 lies between two values is the probability that 𝑦 lies
between their antilogs, and on the log scale the assumption of constant variance holds.
The interval is asymmetric. At the least favourable combination within the NOR the
predicted value is 21.9 ng/mg at manufacturing scale, and the 95 % interval of a single
run runs from 16.3 to 29.4 ng/mg, 7.5 above it and 5.6 below. The predicted value is
the median of the output, its geometric mean, and not its arithmetic mean. The upper
prediction limit, which decides against an upper criterion, is not affected by that.
Near an upper criterion, where the level is high, a raw-scale model would understate
the scatter, because its residual standard deviation is pooled over all levels.


3.5.4 Model adequacy

PCMP-001 §7.2 sets four criteria for a model to be adequate for prediction:

  1. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05.
  2. Lack of fit against pure error is not significant at α = 0.05.
  3. Predicted R² falls short of R² by no more than 0.20, the limit on overfitting.
  4. The residuals show no pattern against the predicted values or the run order, and
     no single run dominates the fit.

PCMP-001 §7.2 does not say how the first criterion applies to a model with no term.
Such a model has no F-test, and this report reads the first criterion as not applicable
to it.
The fourth criterion was tested as every Version 2.0 plan states it. The absolute resid-
uals were regressed on the predicted values, and the residuals on the order of execu-
tion, each at α = 0.05. The runs of the second block continue the order of the first. A
run with a Cook’s distance above 4/n, where n is the number of runs, was examined by
deleting it and repeating the judgement over the NOR. The model fails the criterion
only where a run’s Cook’s distance exceeds the median of the F distribution with p
and n − p degrees of freedom, where p is the number of estimated coefficients, the
intercept and the block included. Each model is reported with R², adjusted R² and pre-
dicted R², the last computed from the prediction error sum of squares (Montgomery
2017).


3.5.5 Judgement over the NOR

A quality attribute is judged on a single batch, because its criterion applies to each
batch. At every combination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the four multivari-
ate parameters (81 combinations), the exact 95 % prediction interval of a single run is




                                          22
computed from the selected model, with the coefficient and residual uncertainty. The
interval is two-sided, so its upper end is a one-sided 97.5 % limit. A quality attribute
meets its criterion over the NOR when every interval lies within it at manufacturing
scale. Viral clearance is judged on the mean predicted LRF at the least favourable
of the 81 combinations, because viral clearance is validated at worst-case conditions
(PCMP-001 §6.4). Its single-run limit is given for information only. Pool host cell pro-
tein is divided by its offset before it is judged. Fragments and viral clearance have
no offset and are judged as the scale-down model gives them.


3.5.6 Proven acceptable ranges and the design space

Two analyses were run for each pair of a parameter and an attribute with a criterion
(PCMP-001 §6.4). The first, in the column headed “PAR (set-point)”, is a univariate
range over which the mean prediction meets the criterion, scanned across the char-
acterized range. The second, the NOR-propagated analysis, scans the parameter
over 81 grid points while the other parameters vary within their NORs. For a qual-
ity attribute, each other parameter is drawn 2,000 times from a normal distribution
centred on its set-point, with a standard deviation of one sixth of the width of its
NOR, clipped to the characterized range. Each draw carries the model residual and
the uncertainty of the coefficients, and the range is where the 97.5th percentile of
the simulated batches stays within an upper criterion. For viral clearance the other
parameters are set to each of their 27 combinations of NOR low, set-point and NOR
high, and the range is where the lowest mean prediction meets the claim. A range
is reported only where it is contiguous and contains the set-point, and only from an
adequate model. The simulation uses a fixed seed (20240724). The NOR-propagated
range is the one that supports a claim.
The design space is drawn on a grid of 11 levels per parameter across the character-
ized range (14,641 points). A grid point lies inside when the 95 % prediction interval
of a single run lies within the criterion for each quality attribute and the mean pre-
diction meets the claim for each viral clearance output (PCMP-001 §6.2).

train (PCMP-001 §6.5). The model runs 2,000 simulated batches through every step,
with each process parameter drawn as in the NOR-propagated analysis. It reports

The four multivariate parameters were studied in a two-level full factorial design of

and give a test for curvature. The runs were executed in randomized order on load
lot LOT-CEX-8851 (Appendix A).



4.3 Response-surface design

The same parameters were studied in a face-centred central composite design of 28
runs (study S-AEX-RSM, 2026-04-01 to 2026-04-10), on the same load lot. The design
adds 8 axial runs at the faces of the cube to the 16 factorial runs and 4 centre points, so
every run lies inside the characterized range. Its quadratic terms allow for clearance
that rises more steeply over part of the load pH range (Appendix B).
In September 2026 the design was augmented by 12 runs in a D-optimal augmentation
(Fedorov 1972), study S-AEX-AUG (2026-09-10 to 2026-09-24), executed as a second
block and numbered 29 to 40. INV-002 decided its rule on 2026-09-02, before any
new run. It calculated the number of runs as the smallest augmentation that gives
a probability of at least 0.90 that the plan’s full quadratic model of pool host cell
protein, with a block term and fitted on all runs, meets two criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2,
the lack of fit test and the limit on overfitting. This report cites that number and does
not recalculate it. The plan did not foresee the augmentation, and it is recorded as
the discrepancy PD-008-01 (Section 13.2).

5.6 Mechanistic interpretation

Load pH and Equil/Wash-1 conductivity act on pool host cell protein, and load pH
and load conductivity act on the LRFs, in the directions that charge-based binding
predicts. A higher load pH lies above the isoelectric point of more of the host cell pro-
teins, so more of them carry a net negative charge. It also makes the virus particles
more negative, so both bind more strongly. Pool host cell protein falls and both LRFs
rise. A higher Equil/Wash-1 conductivity shields the charge of the weakly bound host
cell proteins and lets them through.
The log scale of the host cell protein model is the form a purification step gives. Each
parameter changes the share of host cell protein that passes, so its effect in ng/mg is
larger where the level is higher. That reproduces the dependence of the conductivity
effect on load pH that PCP-008 §4.1 expected. In ng/mg, Equil/Wash-1 conductivity
raises pool host cell protein more at the low end of load pH than at the high end. On
the log scale the response-surface data show no further interaction, and the screening
data do (Section 5.3.4).
Two results do not follow the expectations of Section 2.1. Load conductivity acts on
both LRFs and not detectably on host cell protein, and Equil/Wash-1 conductivity acts
on host cell protein and not detectably on either LRF. The mechanism expected each
conductivity to act on both kinds of impurity, and it does not explain this split. One



                                               41
explanation consistent with the data, which this study did not test, is that a virus
particle bound at many points of its surface is not released by the salt of the wash. Its
capture would then be decided during the load, while weakly bound host cell proteins
are displaced during the chase.
Protein load had no detected effect on pool host cell protein, as expected for an impu-
rity mass below the capacity of the ligand. The selected model of step yield contains
no term, as expected for a step in which the antibody does not bind within the load
pH range studied.
The invalidated first execution shows how the step responds to a load that is not rep-
resentative. On a deamidated load, pool host cell protein rose steeply with protein
load at high Equil/Wash-1 conductivity, and that interaction is absent from the re-
qualified data (Section 13.1.1). PCP-008 §12 expected that a load with more acidic
variants carries antibody whose net charge at the load pH is close to neutral, part
of which can bind. Bound antibody would occupy ligand that the host cell proteins
need, and the salt of the wash would displace the weakly bound proteins first. Bound
antibody would also lower the step yield. In the invalidated execution the runs at
high protein load and high Equil/Wash-1 conductivity gave a mean step yield of 96.2
% against 96.4 % for the other screening runs, and 97.5 % against 97.0 % for the
other response-surface runs. No term of either model of step yield was significant in
that execution (lowest p = 0.150). The data therefore show no loss of yield where the
interaction appeared, and this study did not test the explanation further.

6 Design space

The design space is the region of the four multivariate parameters in which every
output with a criterion meets it at once. It is drawn on the single-batch limit for pool
host cell protein and fragments and on the mean prediction for viral clearance (PCMP-
001 §6.2). On the grid of 11 levels per parameter it covers 95.0 % of the characterized
region. MVM clearance excludes 4.96 % of the grid and XMuLV clearance 1.73 %, and
pool host cell protein and fragments exclude no grid point. With every output on its
mean prediction, which serves as a comparison only, the fraction is the same, because
the quality attributes exclude nothing on either basis.
The excluded region lies at low load pH and high load conductivity. On the mean
prediction at the lowest load pH studied, 7.2, the MVM claim is not met above a
load conductivity of 6.85 mS/cm, whatever the protein load and the Equil/Wash-1
conductivity, neither of which enters the MVM model. At the same load pH and the
highest protein load studied, 300 g/L resin, the XMuLV claim is not met above 6.72
mS/cm.
The whole NOR lies inside the design space, because every combination of NOR low,
set-point and NOR high meets every criterion on its deciding statistic (Table 7.2). The
design space in turn lies inside the characterized region, the knowledge space of this
study. Within the NOR a single run can still fall below a viral claim. At the least
favourable combination the lower 95 % limit of a single run is 5.20 log10 for XMuLV
and 3.63 log10 for MVM. Viral clearance is judged on the mean (Section 3.5), and
these single-run limits are given for information.
The design space is a model of the scale-down data. It is not confirmed at scale at
its edges, and Stage 2 confirms performance inside the NOR only. Movement within
the design space is not a change under ICH Q8(R2), and any planned movement is
assessed under the change management of the pharmaceutical quality system (Inter-

9 Parameter classification

Parameters were classified under SOP-4001 by the decision logic of the A-Mab case
study as PCMP-001 §7.7 states it (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). A critical pro-
cess parameter has an effect on a critical quality attribute that is “significant and
practically relevant within the characterized range”. It is well controlled (WC-CPP)
when its risk of falling outside its acceptable range is low, and a CPP when that risk
is high. PCMP-001 §7.7 adds that “a significant effect that is too small to move the
attribute appreciably within the PAR does not by itself make a parameter critical”.
Every attribute of the register is a critical quality attribute (PCMP-001 §3), so an ef-
fect on pool host cell protein or on fragments counts in the same way as an effect on
viral clearance. An attribute is affected by a parameter when a term that contains the
parameter is significant at 0.05 (PCMP-001 §6.2), and this report applies that test to
the selected response-surface model, the predictive model. No plan sets a threshold
for practical relevance, so where it decides a class this report names its judgement
as one. No numerical threshold exists either for how far inside its acceptable range
a parameter must be held, so the margin between each NOR and its proven accept-
able range is stated. All five parameters were classified as WC-CPP. A WC-CPP is a
critical process parameter, so the step has five critical process parameters, all well
controlled.

  • Load pH (WC-CPP). Load pH acts on pool host cell protein and on both LRFs, with
    p < 0.001 in each selected model. Across its characterized range it multiplies
    pool host cell protein by 0.51 and raises the XMuLV and MVM LRFs by 1.24
    and 1.08 log10. Its NOR lies inside every proven acceptable range. The NOR-
    propagated ranges for the two viruses end 0.13 pH units below the NOR, and
    the other ranges span the characterized range. The load is adjusted to its target
    pH before it is applied (PCP-008 §5.2). Load pH was classified as a WC-CPP.
  • Equil/Wash-1 conductivity (WC-CPP). Equil/Wash-1 conductivity acts on pool
    host cell protein, a critical quality attribute of moderate to high criticality (p <
    0.001), which it multiplies by 2.13 across its characterized range. No effect on
    either LRF, on step yield or on fragments was detected. Every proven accept-
    able range spans the characterized range, which reaches 0.5 mS/cm beyond
    the NOR on each side. The conductivity is set when the buffer is prepared.
    Equil/Wash-1 conductivity was classified as a WC-CPP.
  • Load conductivity (WC-CPP). Load conductivity lowers both LRFs (p < 0.001), by
    1.11 log10 for XMuLV and 1.13 log10 for MVM across its characterized range.
    No effect on pool host cell protein, step yield or fragments was detected. Every
    proven acceptable range spans the characterized range, which reaches 1 mS/cm
    beyond the NOR on each side. The load is adjusted to its target conductivity
    before it is applied. Load conductivity was classified as a WC-CPP.

• Protein load (WC-CPP). Protein load enters the selected XMuLV model through
  its interaction with load pH (p = 0.017), which changes the LRF across the pro-
  tein load range by −0.33 to +0.44 log10, depending on load pH. That change is of
  the size of the repeatability of the XMuLV assay, and the near-equivalent XMuLV
  model without the interaction would leave protein load with no effect on any at-
  tribute (Section 5.3.4). The change is small, but it enters the least favourable
  combination for XMuLV, where the margin to the claim is smallest (Section 7.2).
  This report judges it practically relevant for that reason. No effect on pool host

  response-surface model contains no term. Every proven acceptable range spans
  the characterized range. Protein load was classified as a WC-CPP on the selected

• Operating flow rate (WC-CPP). No effect of flow rate was detected. Pool host

  which the claim was shown.

  1. Parameter ranges. Load pH, Equil/Wash-1 conductivity, load conductivity, pro-
     tein load and operating flow rate are controlled within their NORs (Table 4.2),
     which lie inside their proven acceptable ranges and, for the four multivariate
     parameters, inside the design space.
  2. Viral clearance. The step contributes 5.5 log10 for XMuLV and 4 log10 for MVM
     to the cumulative claims, which PCMR-001 consolidates with the claims of low-
     pH viral inactivation (PCR-006) and virus filtration (PCR-009). These claims hold
     on the mean at the worst case within the NOR and rest on the scale-down spiking
     model.
  3. Host cell protein. The step brings host cell protein to its drug substance level.
     The upstream criteria of the Protein A pool (PCR-005) and the cation exchange
     pool (PCR-007) rely on its verified clearance of at least 4.18-fold, and host cell
     protein is tested at drug substance release.
  4. Load material. A load deamidated during an extended hold after neutralization
     changed the host cell protein response of the step (DEV-008-01), and the charge
     variant profile by icIEF (AMV-3013) detected it. This study sets no value for the
     hold of the neutralized cation exchange pool before anion exchange. The report
     carries forward to PCMR-001 the need for such a limit, because a hold of 36 h
     produced the deamidated load.
  5. Pool collection. The trailing-edge UV set-point that ends pool collection is part
     of SOP-2011 at its corrected value (DEV-008-02).
  6. Residual DNA and leached Protein A. Both are judged at the drug substance
     against their criteria. The DNA capability of the drug substance rests on the
     DNA clearance the integrated process model assumes for this step, which this
     study cannot confirm (Section 5.5).
  7. Resin life cycle. Resin packing, sanitization and life cycle are controlled under
     SOP-2008 and are outside this study.




                                         55
11 Discussion

The step behaves as a charge-based flow-through polish. Pool host cell protein de-
pends on load pH and Equil/Wash-1 conductivity as fold changes, and both LRFs de-
pend on load pH and load conductivity. Within the NOR every output meets its cri-
terion. The viral clearance claims have the smallest margins, 0.36 log10 for XMuLV
and 0.24 log10 for MVM at the least favourable combination, both on the side of low
load pH and high load conductivity. The design space ends where those two settings
meet, outside the NOR.
Confidence in the scale-down model is greatest for pool host cell protein, for which
the model is qualified at its criterion with the offset at both ends of its interval. Frag-
ments and viral clearance were not compared at the two scales. Fragments lie far
below their criterion, but the expectation that the step leaves the fragment level of
its load unchanged was not tested, because the load was not measured. The viral
clearance results are those of a small-scale spiking study (International Council for
Harmonisation 2023a), and the record holds neither the qualification of the spiking
model nor the assay controls that PCP-008 required. The pressure across the bed was
not measured in the qualification, and the scale-down column gave fewer plates per
metre than the manufacturing columns. The pool-collection set-point of DEV-008-02
was the same in every run of the two executions of the designs, so it cannot have
created or hidden an effect within them. Its effect on the level of the outputs is not
in the record.
The verification of the host cell protein clearance widened the criteria of two upstream
steps. The widening does not depend on a single run, and the model behind it is
adequate on all four criteria. It does depend on the model selection, and it is sensitive
to the scale-down offset, at the unfavourable end of whose interval the bound would
fall below the platform value. The risk of the widening is the producer’s, because
host cell protein is tested at drug substance release.
The first execution of the designs ran on a deamidated load and was invalidated and
re-executed (DEV-008-01). Its data confirm the cause and serve no other purpose.
The augmentation of PD-008-01 was decided before any new run but made after the
planned results were known. For pool host cell protein and fragments the planned
runs alone give the same models and the same judgements, and the augmentation
changed only the model of step yield, which has no criterion. The flow-rate study ran
without its centre run (PD-008-03), so it cannot show curvature in the effect of flow
rate. The screening run of DEV-008-03 is not identified in the record. The screening
results it could affect are those of MVM, step yield and fragments, and the predictive
model of every output comes from the response-surface design, which the event did
not affect (Section 13.1.3).




                                            56
Four limitations remain. The design space is not confirmed at scale at its edges, and
Stage 2 confirms performance inside the NOR only. The univariate assessment of
flow rate cannot detect an interaction with the other parameters. The residual DNA
clearance of the step cannot be shown by an assay whose LOQ lies far above the drug
substance criterion per dose, so the capability of residual DNA rests on the process
model. The studies assumed that the resin performs within its qualified life cycle
(PCP-008 §12), and clearance on resin near the end of its life cycle is outside them
and is governed by SOP-2008.

12 Conclusions

1. Load pH and Equil/Wash-1 conductivity act on pool host cell protein, multiplying
   it by 0.51 and 2.13 across their ranges. Load pH and load conductivity act on
   both LRFs. The selected response-surface models of step yield and fragments
   contain no term. The screening model of fragments carried an interaction of
   load pH and protein load, which the response-surface model does not contain
   (objective 1).
2. The design space covers 95.0 % of the characterized region and contains the
   whole NOR. MVM clearance bounds it at low load pH and high load conductivity
   (objective 2).
3. Every proven acceptable range contains its NOR. The range of load pH carried
   into the control strategy is 7.2225–7.8, and every other multivariate parameter
   carries its characterized range. Operating flow rate is supported across its char-
   acterized range for pool host cell protein and at its upper end for viral clearance
   (objective 3).
4. All five parameters were classified as WC-CPP (objective 4).
5. The step meets its claims of 5.5 log10 for XMuLV and 4 log10 for MVM on the
   mean at the worst case within the NOR, and the NORs are justified as the Stage
   2 ranges (objective 5).
6. The host cell protein clearance of the step has a one-sided 95 % prediction bound
   of a single batch of 4.18-fold within the NOR, better than the platform worst
   case of 4-fold. It widened the Protein A and cation exchange criteria. It does
   not depend on a single run, but it depends on the model selection and on the
   scale-down offset (objective 6).
7. Every attribute the step sets or clears meets the capability target of 1.33, the
   tightest being the cumulative MVM clearance at 1.51.
8. The three study events are resolved, and the 10 discrepancies from the plan are
   recorded with what they changed. The results roll up into PCMR-001.

13.1.1 DEV-008-01, a non-representative load in the first execution

The first execution of the screening and response-surface designs (study S-AEX-SUP,
2026-02-16 to 2026-03-04) ran on a load that did not represent the process. The load
lot LOT-CEX-8842 carried 33.5 % acidic variants, against 21 % for the requalified




                                        59
lot. In that execution pool host cell protein rose steeply with protein load at high
Equil/Wash-1 conductivity. The screening effect of the interaction of protein load and
Equil/Wash-1 conductivity was 34.8 ng/mg (p = 0.002). Of the runs at high protein
load and high Equil/Wash-1 conductivity, 3 exceeded the drug substance criterion, the
highest at 150 ng/mg.
The event was detected during the charge-variant verification of the load material
by icIEF (AMV-3013). The investigation found that the cation exchange eluate had
been held for 36 h after neutralization, an extended hold that promoted asparagine
deamidation. Under PCP-008 §7, a design executed on material outside the release
profile of the cation exchange pool is invalidated for the definition of the operating
region and re-executed in full on a qualified load. Both designs were therefore inval-
idated and re-executed on the requalified lot LOT-CEX-8851, as studies S-AEX-SCR
and S-AEX-RSM. The data of the first execution are retained and were used only to
confirm the cause, and no range, class or clearance claim rests on them (Table A.2
and Table B.3).
The requalified data confirm the cause. In the re-executed screening design the same
interaction is 0.22 ng/mg (p = 0.860). In the response-surface data it is not significant
on either scale, with p = 0.361 on the log scale and p = 0.401 on the raw scale
in the full quadratic model of all 40 runs, and no near-equivalent model contains it
(Section 5.3.4). No run of the re-executed designs or of the augmentation exceeded
100 ng/mg. The interaction appeared only on the deamidated load. The first execution
measured fragments by SEC, because it ran before AMD-001.
PCP-008 §7 sets no numerical limit for the release profile of the load. The record
classes the first lot, at 33.5 % acidic variants, as not representative and the requalified
lot, at 21 %, as representative. It holds no result for the host cell protein content,
protein concentration, pH or conductivity of either lot, which the same item of PCP-
008 lists (PD-008-06).


13.1.2 DEV-008-02, the pool-collection set-point

In both executions of the designs, the trailing-edge UV set-point that ends pool col-
lection was 180 mAU, against the corrected set-point of 120 mAU. The event was
detected during the reconciliation of the pool fractions after execution, with host cell
protein measured by ELISA (AMV-3012). The cause was a set-point in the method that
was slightly permissive. The record gives the outcome as corrected by modelling and
verification runs, and it names 3 verification runs. It does not hold their results, and
this report does not use them.
The event covers the two executions of the designs, and within them the set-point was
the same in every run. It therefore acts on all those runs alike and does not enter the
estimated effects or the terms of the selected models, which come from differences
between runs. The record does not give the set-point of the augmentation runs. A
shift of level between the planned runs and the augmentation runs would enter the
block term, which was not significant for pool host cell protein (p = 0.526). It can
shift the level of an output, and the record gives no size for that shift at the corrected
set-point. For pool host cell protein the upper 95 % limit of a single run over the NOR



                                           60
leaves a factor of 3.4 to the criterion. The viral clearance claims have the smallest
margins, and the effect of the set-point on the LRFs is not in the record. The runs of
the re-executed designs are analysed as recorded.


13.1.3 DEV-008-03, an equilibration and wash buffer below its pH range

The equilibration and first wash buffer used on one screening run, lot LOT-BUF-8815,
had a pH of 7.32 at release testing under SOP-2103 (Buffer Preparation, Release and
Expiry Control), below its acceptance limit of 7.4 and its target of 7.5. The cause was
an error at the titration end point during buffer preparation. The run was retained in
the analysis.
The buffer pH is not a factor of the designs, so no setting correction applies. A lower
buffer pH weakens the binding of acidic impurities during equilibration and the chase,
in the same direction as a lower load pH. The record does not name the run (PD-008-
07), so it cannot be marked in Appendix A, analysed with its result excluded or com-
pared with its model prediction. Each of the 19 screening runs was therefore deleted
in turn. The significant terms of pool host cell protein and XMuLV did not change with
any deletion, so those screening results do not depend on the affected run, whichever
it was. For MVM, step yield and fragments the set of significant terms changed with
3, 3 and 10 of the deletions. The event affected the screening design only, which
identifies effects and sets no range. Every prediction, range and judgement of this
report comes from the response-surface design, which the event did not affect.

The model selection, PD-008-02 (Section 3.5), replaced the plan’s full quadratic model

curvature in the effect of flow rate within the range.
The qualification of the scale-down model, PD-008-04 (Section 3.1), measured no bed

runs. No conclusion of this report rests on bed pressure. The viral clearance results

not shown.
The virus assays, PD-008-05 (Section 3.4), are recorded as log reductions only, with

are not recorded, and the lot named for the designs had expired before the flow-rate
study and the augmentation began. No comparison of lots was made. For pool host

behaved as the planned load.

analysed as recorded. Without these records a run cannot be traced to its inputs, and
the effect of DEV-008-03 could only be bracketed by deleting each screening run in
turn (Section 13.1.3).
The measurements and analyses not made, PD-008-08, are residual DNA and leached
Protein A on the augmentation runs, an analysis of variance and diagnostics for the
models of step yield and fragments, which have no term, the figure of each range
against its governing factor, and a statement against the completion rule of PCP-008
§7 item 8. Residual DNA and leached Protein A were below the LOQ in every pool in

which they were measured (Section 5.5). The coefficients of the two models without

The coding of protein load, PD-008-09 (Section 3.5), placed coded 0 at 175 g/L resin,
