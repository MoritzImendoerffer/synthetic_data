Executive summary

This report presents the Stage 1 process characterization of the small virus retentive
filtration step (Step 9) of the A-Mab drug substance process. The step removes virus
by size. It is the dedicated small virus removal operation of the purification train
and the principal source of clearance of minute virus of mice (MVM), and it also car-
ries a claim for xenotropic murine leukemia virus (XMuLV). The study followed the
characterization plan PCP-009 and supports the transfer of the process from Cam-
bridge, MA, to Grafton, WI, under PTP-001. It was run on a scale-down model of the
commercial step in two designed experiments in filtration volume (load) and filtration
pressure, a screening design of 7 runs and a face-centred central composite design of
12 runs. Version 2.0 judges each output against the intermediate acceptance criteria
of the master plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0. It analyses each response-surface output
with the model that a model selection chose, in place of the full quadratic of the plan
(Section 1.4).
Filtration volume was the only parameter with a detected effect. Across the range
studied, from 50 to 140 L/m2, the mean MVM log reduction factor (LRF) fell by 1.73
log10 along a straight line. No term containing filtration pressure was significant for
any output. The selected model of MVM LRF passes the model F-test of PCMP-001
§7.2, shows no lack of fit, and meets the limit on overfitting, which allows predicted R²
to fall short of R² by at most 0.20. It fails the criterion on residuals and influential runs,
because one run moves the fit more than that criterion allows. Its Cook’s distance is
above the median of the F distribution. That run, run 5, is the run at 50 L/m2 and 19
psi, with the highest LRF of the runs at that volume. XMuLV LRF has no adequate
model and is judged on its measured results. Fragments and step yield did not depend
on either parameter.
Each output with a criterion met its intermediate acceptance criterion across the
normal operating range (NOR). The least favourable NOR combination is the com-
bination of NOR low, set-point and NOR high of the two parameters that gives the
lowest predicted LRF. There the mean predicted MVM LRF is 5.25 log10, against the
LRF claimed for this step, 4.62 log10. Every measured XMuLV LRF, from 5.90 to 6.51
log10, met its claim of 4.62 log10, and XMuLV LRF has no PAR. For fragments the
upper 95 % limit of a single run within the NOR is 0.78 %, against the criterion of 2.00
%. The proven acceptable range (PAR) of filtration volume for MVM is 50–136 L/m2
in the NOR-propagated analysis, in which the claim must hold with filtration pressure
anywhere in its NOR. It extends 31 L/m2 beyond the upper NOR edge. The PAR of
filtration pressure for MVM is the whole range studied. The design space covers 91 %
of the characterized region on the grid evaluated, and only filtration volumes above
137.6 L/m2 lie outside it. With run 5 deleted, the PAR of filtration volume and the
design space would both reach the end of the range studied, while the judgement
over the NOR and the terms of the selected model would not change.

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody whose principal mechanism of ac-
tion is antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) (CMC Biotech Working
Group 2009). It is purified by the platform train of Protein A capture, low-pH viral
inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small virus retentive filtration and ul-
trafiltration / diafiltration. The commercial process runs at a production bioreactor
scale of 15,000 L.
The small virus retentive filtration step filters the flow-through pool of anion exchange
through a membrane whose pore size distribution retains the parvoviruses while the
antibody passes. The step is run at a set filtration pressure, in psi, up to a set filtra-
tion volume, which is the volume of load filtered per square metre of membrane, in
L/m2. MVM, a parvovirus, is among the smallest viruses of concern, and this step
is the principal source of its clearance. XMuLV, an enveloped retrovirus, is many
times larger than the pores. The clearance of the step is orthogonal to that of low-pH
viral inactivation (Step 6) and anion exchange chromatography (Step 8), because it
removes virus by size and not by inactivation or by binding (International Council for
Harmonisation 2023a).
The step forms no quality attribute. It contributes a log reduction to the two cumu-
lative viral clearance requirements of the drug substance. Its filtrate is also judged
against the drug substance criterion for fragments, because no later step changes
them (Section 7). Step yield is a process performance output with no criterion.
The plan paired with this report is PCP-009. Table 1.1 lists the documents to which
this report relates.

1.2 Objectives

The objectives of the study were those of PCP-009 §2.

  1. Qualify a scale-down model of the step, and show that it represents the commer-
     cial step for virus retention, for flux decay and for step yield.
  2. Determine which of the parameters affect the two viral clearance responses and
     the step yield, in what direction and by how much.
  3. Fit a response-surface model for each response over the characterization ranges,
     and establish whether it predicts the mean response adequately.
  4. Derive a proven acceptable range for each parameter against each of the two
     viral clearance responses.
  5. Classify each parameter under SOP-4001 and define what the step contributes
     to the control strategy and to the modular viral clearance claim.

Version 2.0 of the plan adds fragments to objectives 2 to 4. The screening design
identifies the parameters that have an effect. The response-surface model is the pre-
dictive model, and every range in this report comes from it. Section 12 states the
outcome against each objective.



1.3 Regulatory and scientific basis

The study belongs to Stage 1, process design, of the lifecycle approach to process
validation (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). It follows the enhanced ap-
proach of ICH Q8(R2) and ICH Q11. Prior knowledge and risk assessment identify
the parameters that may affect quality, and designed experiments then measure and
bound their effects (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012). Criticality
is treated as a continuum, as ICH Q8(R2) describes it, and the risk assessment that
scoped the study followed ICH Q9(R1) (International Council for Harmonisation 2009,
2023b). Viral clearance is evaluated under ICH Q5A(R2). Each step that clears virus
is studied in its own spiking model, and its log reduction enters the cumulative claim
as a module (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a). The process model, the
attribute framework and the risk method follow the A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009).

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The small virus retentive filtration step has been used to manufacture three earlier
licensed humanized IgG1 antibodies of the platform, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab, and to
supply A-Mab clinical material, with the same filter type and the same mode of opera-
tion (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Their history gives the platform claim of the
step, 4.62 log10 for each of the two model viruses (PCMP-001 §7.4). The mechanism
of removal is size exclusion and does not depend on the antibody, so the platform ex-
perience gives the expected direction of each effect. How the anion exchange pool of
A-Mab fouls the membrane is specific to the product, and the effects were therefore
measured on A-Mab material.
MVM sits close to the pore size cut-off of the membrane. Its retention depends on the
fraction of pores at the large end of the distribution and on the flow through them.
As the membrane fouls with product and residual impurity, the flux shifts toward the
least fouled and largest pores, and small virus can pass. MVM retention is therefore
expected to fall as the volume filtered per unit area rises, so the claim is most at risk
at the maximum load. PCP-009 §6.3 also expected the fall to accelerate toward the
top of the volume range, as the shift of flux to the largest pores progresses.
A higher filtration pressure raises the flux. It also compacts the fouling layer, which
raises the resistance of that layer, but the flux still rises with pressure. PCP-009 ex-
pected MVM retention to fall at high pressure as more filtrate passes the largest open
pores. A low pressure or an interruption of flow lets virus held in the pore structure
diffuse through. For this reason the claim of the step is made over a pressure range
and for uninterrupted filtration. PCP-009 §4.3 reasoned that the load decides how
much of the membrane is still open and the pressure how fast the filtrate crosses
what is left. On that reasoning the plan expected the fall of MVM retention with load
to be steeper at high pressure than at low pressure.
XMuLV is many times larger than the pores. It is retained while the membrane is
intact, so its clearance is expected not to depend on either parameter. The antibody
passes the pores freely, so step yield loses only the product held up in the system and
in the fouling layer on the membrane. The hold-up of the system is fixed, so it is a
smaller share of a larger load and on its own would raise the yield as the load rises.
The fouling layer, and the product held in it, grow with the volume filtered and lower
the yield. PCP-009 expected yield to be high and to fall slowly as the load rises. That
holds only if the loss in the fouling layer grows faster than the share of the fixed hold-
up falls. Fragments, the clipped species of the antibody, are smaller than the intact
antibody and pass the pores in the same way, so the step is not expected to change
the fragment level of its load.

The step contributes log reduction to the two viral safety attributes of Table 2.1 and
is judged against the criterion for fragments. The acceptance values of the two viral

retrovirus-like particles per dose estimated by electron microscopy (CMC Biotech

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

The risk assessment RA-001 ranked every parameter of the train twice, for its poten-
tial impact on the quality attributes and for its potential to interact with other pa-

rameters (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Parameters ranked high
on either ranking were assigned to multivariate study, and parameters ranked lower
to univariate study. For this step RA-001 assigned its 2 parameters to multivariate
study and none to univariate study. Filtration volume was ranked high because foul-

3 Materials and methods

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The scale-down model uses the same filter type as manufacturing, and the load is
scaled by membrane area (PCMP-001 §5). The volumetric load in L/m2 and the filtra-
tion pressure of the model therefore correspond to the commercial step.
The qualification runs were made in study S-VF-SDM, from 2026-04-15 to 2026-04-
24, under the scale-down model procedure SOP-1001 and before the screening runs
began. The study compared 3 scale-down runs at the set-points with 5 manufacturing-
scale batches. PCP-009 §5.1 named three outputs for this comparison, the flux decay
profile, the step yield and the post-use integrity test result, and required the load
material, the filtration temperature and the hold-up volume per area to be matched.
The record holds step yield alone. The flux decay profile and the post-use integrity
test results are not recorded for either scale, and neither is the matching (PD-009-03).
Version 1.0 of this report stated that every run passed its post-use integrity test, but
no result of the test is recorded, for these runs or for the design runs (Section 3.2).
The comparison therefore rests on step yield alone.
The model is qualified by the offset rule of PCMP-001 §7.1. The offset of an output is
the mean of the scale-down runs at the set-points minus the mean of the manufactur-
ing batches, with its two-sided 90 % confidence interval. The model is qualified for
an output when the conclusion against its criterion over the NOR is the same with
the offset at either end of that interval. Step yield has no criterion at this step, so
its offset is recorded and no conclusion depends on it (Table 3.1). The mean step
yield was 97.6 % at scale-down and 98.1 % at manufacturing scale, an offset of −0.5
percentage points with a 90 % confidence interval of −1.2 to 0.3 points.

3.2 Operation

PCP-009 §5.2 specifies the flow-through pool of anion exchange as the load of every
run, and the record does not identify the pool used in each run (PD-009-06). Each
design point was run once, at the filtration pressure of the point and up to its filtration

run, and every run gave an MVM LRF and an XMuLV LRF. The order of execution of
each run is recorded and enters the residual tests (Section 3.5). The designs varied

the operation procedure SOP-2012 and were not factors of the designs.
PCP-009 §5.2 planned the step yield of each design run from the same spiked run, by
protein mass balance from the ultraviolet absorbance of the load and of the pooled
filtrate. PCP-009 §5.4 asked instead for an unspiked load and an unspiked filtrate
pool to be assayed for the yield, which cannot exist when every design run is spiked,

single value. It holds no protein concentration, no volume, no named assay and no
sample of the load taken before spiking (PD-009-05).
PCP-009 §5.2 required the filter of each run to be integrity tested before and after the
run under SOP-2012, and a run whose post-use test failed to be excluded. No integrity
test result is recorded for any design run (PD-009-04), and no run was excluded. The
claim made from these runs applies to uninterrupted filtration, and no run of the
record is described as interrupted.

3.5 Statistical methods

Both factors were coded to −1 and +1 at the ends of the range studied and to 0 at its
midpoint, so that coefficients are comparable between the two factors and between
the two designs. A coded coefficient is the change in the response over half the
range studied, 45 L/m2 for filtration volume and 11 psi for filtration pressure. Terms
significant at α = 0.05 were treated as real (PCMP-001 §6.2). In this report the terms
of a model are counted without the intercept.
The screening design was analysed by ordinary least squares with both main effects
and their interaction. An effect is twice the coded coefficient, which is the change in
the response across the whole range studied, as PCMP-001 §6.2 states it. PCP-009
§5.5 states effects as the change over half the range, which is the coefficient, and
Appendix C gives both. The screening model identifies effects. Its centre points test
for curvature through the one degree of freedom left for lack of fit, but they cannot
attribute the curvature to a factor.
Each response-surface output was analysed by model selection, the analysis that INV-
002 adopted as corrective action CA3. It replaced the full quadratic model of PCP-009
§5.5 (Section 13.2). The selection has three steps.

  1. The scale. The Box-Cox profile of the full quadratic gives the power of the re-
     sponse that best suits a least squares fit, with its 95 % interval (Box and Cox
     1964). The log scale is used where that interval contains 0 and excludes 1, and
     the raw scale otherwise. Every interval at this step contained both 0 and 1, so
     every output was modelled on the raw scale.
  2. The terms. The full quadratic in two factors has 5 terms, the two main effects,
     their interaction and the two squares. It has 13 hierarchical submodels, counting
     the intercept-only model, in which an interaction appears only with both of its
     main effects and a square only with its main effect. Every submodel was fitted,
     and the one with the lowest AICc was selected. AICc estimates how well a model
     would predict new runs and penalises every estimated term. Its small-sample
     correction raises the penalty when the runs are few for the number of terms, as
     in these designs (Hurvich and Tsai 1989; Burnham and Anderson 2002). The
     residual variance is counted as a parameter of the model. A stepwise search
     changes one term at a time and can stop where only a combination of changes
     would lower AICc further. On these data a stepwise search in either direction
     would have stopped at the same model for every output with a criterion.
  3. The check. The selected model was judged against the four criteria of PCMP-001
     §7.2 below. An intercept-only model states that the output does not depend on
     the parameters within the range studied. An output with no adequate model is
     judged on its measured results and sets no range.

PCP-009 §7 states the four adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 with their thresh-
olds.



                                          19
  1. A significant model. The model F-test is significant at α = 0.05. An intercept-only
     model has no F-test, and the criterion does not apply to it.
  2. No significant lack of fit. The lack of fit test against the pure error of the centre
     points is not significant at α = 0.05.
  3. The limit on overfitting. Predicted R², calculated from the prediction error sum
     of squares, falls short of R² by no more than 0.20 (Montgomery 2017).
  4. Residuals and influence. The absolute residuals regressed on the predicted val-
     ues, and the residuals regressed on the order of execution, show no slope signifi-
     cant at α = 0.05. A run with a Cook’s distance above 4/n is examined by deleting
     it and repeating the judgement. The model fails the criterion only where a run’s
     Cook’s distance exceeds the median of the F distribution with p and n − p de-
     grees of freedom, where p is the number of model parameters including the
     intercept and n the number of runs.

R², adjusted R² and predicted R² are reported for every model. PCP-009 §7 also keeps
the Version 1.0 conditions that the residuals show no material departure from nor-
mality and that the residual standard error is small against the distance between the
predicted response and its criterion, and it leaves the judgement of both to this report.
Normality is judged on the normal quantile plot and by the Shapiro-Wilk test at α =
0.05. The residual standard error is judged small against that distance when the end
of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run on the side of the criterion still meets
the criterion at the least favourable NOR combination. Several analyses of this re-
port are not in PCP-009 (PD-009-07). They are the deletion of every response-surface
run, the test of the curvature of the two designs against each other, the Shapiro-Wilk
test and the reading of the two Version 1.0 conditions by the prediction interval of a
single run, the comparisons with the models within 2 AICc units of the selected one
and with stepwise searches, and the capability analysis.
Each output with a criterion was judged at every combination of NOR low, set-point
and NOR high of the two parameters, 9 combinations in all. Viral clearance was
judged on the mean predicted LRF at the least favourable of these combinations,
because viral clearance is validated at worst-case conditions (PCMP-001 §6.4). The
lower end of the two-sided 95 % prediction interval of a single run there is given for
information. Fragments were judged on the exact 95 % prediction interval of a single
run at each combination, which includes the uncertainty of the fitted coefficients and
the residual. The interval is two-sided, so its upper end is a one-sided 97.5 % limit. An
output with no adequate model was judged on every measured result of both designs.
No offset was applied, because no output with a criterion has a measured offset at
this step (Section 3.1). Section 7 defines the two PAR analyses, and Section 6 the grid
on which the design space was evaluated.
Capability was estimated from the integrated model of the train (PCMP-001 §6.5) and
judged against the target and the minimum of PCMP-001 §7.9. The analyses followed
the design of experiments procedure SOP-1002. The Monte-Carlo analyses use a fixed
random seed, so every number of this report can be regenerated from the data.

4.2 Screening design

The screening study S-VF-DOE was a two-level full factorial in both factors with centre
points, 7 runs in all, of which 4 were the factorial runs at the corners of the ranges
studied and 3 were replicates of the centre point (Appendix A). With two factors the
full factorial estimates both main effects and their interaction free of aliasing. The
interaction model fits four parameters to 5 distinct design points, the corners and the
centre, so its fit statistics overstate how well it fits (PCMP-001 §6.2). The one degree
of freedom left for lack of fit tests for curvature.



4.3 Response-surface design

The response-surface study S-VF-RSM was a face-centred central composite design in
both factors, 12 runs in all, made up of 4 factorial runs, 4 axial runs and 4 replicates of
the centre point (Appendix B). The axial runs sit on the edges of the ranges studied,
because settings outside those ranges are not settings for which the step may be
claimed. The design supports the full quadratic model in both factors. Its centre
points give the pure error for the lack of fit test, with 3 degrees of freedom.



4.4 Univariate assessment

No parameter of this step was assigned to univariate study. PCP-009 §6.4 allowed a
univariate confirmation run where the designs could not separate an effect, and none
was made.

5.4 Mechanistic interpretation

The fall of MVM LRF with filtration volume is the effect that the mechanism of the
step predicts. As the membrane fouls, the flux shifts toward the largest pores, through
which the parvovirus can pass, and the shift grows with the volume filtered per unit
area. The data show the effect as a fall of 1.73 log10 across the range studied, with
a standard error of 0.19 log10.
The data do not show three other effects that PCP-009 expected from the same mech-
anism. First, the fall was expected to accelerate toward the top of the volume range.
In the full quadratic of the planned runs the square of filtration volume is positive,
0.136 log10 per coded unit squared, which is the opposite sign, and it is not signifi-
cant (p = 0.333). Within 50–140 L/m2 the decline is consistent with a straight line, and
the mechanism as PCP-009 states it does not explain the absence of the acceleration.
Second, MVM retention was expected to fall at high pressure, and at low pressure
through diffusion. The main effect of pressure in the full quadratic is −0.034 log10
per coded unit (p = 0.706). The square of pressure is negative, −0.301, which would
mean a lower LRF at both ends of the pressure range, the shape that the mechanism
predicts, but it is not significant (p = 0.058) and was not selected. Third, the fall
with volume was expected to be steeper at high pressure. The interaction in the full
quadratic is 0.028 log10 per coded unit squared, with a standard error of 0.105 (p =
0.799), and in the screening design 0.02 log10 across the range (p = 0.965). Within
the range studied no dependence of the volume effect on pressure was detected at
the precision of these runs. A term that is not significant is not shown to be absent,
and an effect of pressure of the size of these standard errors could exist within 8–30
psi.
For operation, the MVM LRF is therefore lowest at the highest volume filtered per
square metre of membrane, and the claim is most at risk there. Within the pressures
studied, pressure had no detected effect on any output, and the condition of uninter-
rupted filtration rests on the mechanism and not on these data, because the designs
did not study an interruption of flow.
The results for XMuLV LRF agree with the expectation that a virus many times larger
than the pores is retained independently of the operating parameters. No term was
significant in either design, and every measured LRF met the claim. Every LRF in
the record is a point value, and the record does not say whether any filtrate titre was
below the limit of detection (Section 3.3), so the data do not show whether retention
was complete.
Fragments did not depend on either parameter, and their level in the filtrate ranged
from 0.54 to 0.77 %, against the criterion of 2.00 %. The expectation that the step
does not change fragments cannot be tested on these data, because fragments were
measured in the filtrate and not in the load. It rests on the size of the clipped species
against the pores. Step yield did not depend on either parameter. The model with
filtration volume, the next by AICc, falls with load as PCP-009 expected, but its coeffi-
cient is not significant (p = 0.173). The data therefore do not show whether the loss in
the fouling layer grows faster than the share of the fixed hold-up falls (Section 2.1).

6 Design space

The design space of the step is the part of the characterized region in which every
output with a criterion meets it at once, each on the statistic on which it is judged
(PCMP-001 §6.2). A setting lies inside it when the mean predicted MVM LRF meets
its claim and both ends of the 95 % prediction interval of a single run for fragments
lie within their criterion. XMuLV LRF has no adequate model and draws no boundary,
because every measured result met its claim. The region was evaluated on an evenly
spaced grid of 11 by 11 points over the characterized region, 50–140 L/m2 by 8–30
psi.
Of the 121 grid points, 110 lie inside the design space, 91 % of the characterized
region. The points outside are those at 140 L/m2, the top of the volume range, where
the mean predicted MVM LRF falls below the claim at every pressure. Between the
grid points the boundary lies at 137.6 L/m2 at every pressure, because the selected
model has no pressure term. Fragments exclude no point. Drawn on the mean predic-
tion of fragments as well, the design space is the same, because MVM LRF is judged
on its mean in both and fragments exclude no point in either.
The NOR of both parameters lies inside the design space, and the design space lies
inside the characterized region, which is the knowledge space of the study. Move-
ment within the design space is not a change from the point of view of a regulatory
filing under ICH Q8(R2), but it is evaluated under the change management system
of the quality system (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2008). At the
boundary itself a single run meets the claim only about half the time if the variability
is symmetrical, because the boundary is drawn on the mean prediction, as PCMP-001
§6.2 sets it for viral clearance. At the least favourable NOR combination the lower
end of the two-sided 95 % prediction interval of a single run, a one-sided 97.5 % limit,
is 4.71 log10, above the claim. It is given for information.
Deleting run 5, the axial run at 50 L/m2 and 19 psi, or run 7, the axial run at 95 L/m2
and 8 psi, gives a selected model that meets the claim over the whole characterized
region (Table 5.9). The design space is not confirmed at manufacturing scale at its
edges, because virus cannot be spiked at that scale. The selected model of step yield
is intercept-only, so the predicted mean yield is 98.2 % at every setting in the design
space (PCP-009 §7).

9 Parameter classification

The parameters were classified under SOP-4001. Under the rule of PCP-009 §7 a
parameter affects an attribute when a term that contains the parameter is significant
at α = 0.05. The class of an affected parameter then follows from the size of its effect
across its PAR and from the margin between the PAR and the NOR. Neither PCMP-
001 nor SOP-4001 sets a number for either. The class of filtration volume below is
therefore a judgement on the values given.

  • Filtration volume (load), WC-CPP. A term containing the parameter is significant
    for MVM LRF (p < 0.001). Across its NOR-propagated PAR, 50–136 L/m2, the
    mean predicted MVM LRF falls by 1.65 log10. MVM clearance is an attribute of
    very high criticality. The PAR extends 31 L/m2 beyond the upper NOR edge, and
    its lower edge is the end of the range studied. The volumetric load is set by the
    volume of load applied to a filter of known area, so it can be held within its NOR.
    The parameter was classified as a WC-CPP on this margin and this control. With
    run 5 deleted the PAR would reach the end of the range studied, and the class
    would be the same.
  • Filtration pressure, WC-CPP. No term containing the parameter is significant
    for any output, in either design (MVM LRF p = 0.706 for the main effect and
    p = 0.058 for the square in the full quadratic, XMuLV LRF p = 0.053 in its se-
    lected model, which is not adequate). No effect of pressure on an attribute was
    therefore detected within 8–30 psi, and the class does not follow from the rule

11 Discussion

As the volume filtered per square metre rises, the membrane fouls and the flow shifts
to the larger pores, so the MVM LRF of the step falls. The study measured this fall
as a straight line from 6.31 log10 at 50 L/m2 to 4.57 log10 at 140 L/m2. The claim
of 4.62 log10 holds on the mean prediction up to 137.6 L/m2, 32.6 L/m2 beyond the
upper NOR edge of 105 L/m2. The 3 measured MVM results below the claim were
all at 140 L/m2, beyond that point. No effect of pressure was detected on any output,
and XMuLV LRF, fragments and step yield did not depend on either parameter.
The scale-down model compared step yield alone with manufacturing scale, with an
offset of −0.5 percentage points. No output with a criterion was compared at both
scales. The clearance claims therefore rest on the spiking model, as ICH Q5A(R2) pro-
vides, and the fragment results on the mechanism of the step (International Council
for Harmonisation 2023a). The flux decay profile, which PCP-009 named as the com-
parison that shows whether the model fouls like the commercial filter, is not recorded
(PD-009-03). How far the fouling of the scale-down membrane represents the com-
mercial filter is therefore not shown by these data, and the effect of filtration volume
on MVM LRF is the effect at scale-down.
Deleting run 5 or run 7 carries the upper PAR edge of filtration volume to the end of
the range studied (Table 5.9). The ranges reported come from all runs and are the
narrower ones. Under every deletion the edge lies 30 L/m2 or more beyond the NOR,
and no deletion changed the judgement over the NOR.
The data did not show the acceleration of the MVM decline at high load, the effect of
pressure or the interaction between load and pressure that the mechanism led PCP-
009 to expect (Section 5.4). The residual SD of a model sets the standard errors of its
terms. For the selected model of MVM LRF it is 0.23 log10 (Table 5.5), and the slope
of volume is 9.3 times its standard error. In the full quadratic of the planned runs the
terms that PCP-009 expected have standard errors from 0.086 to 0.129 log10 in coded
units, and none of them is significant (Table C.3). Effects of that size are not resolved
by 12 runs. Interruptions of flow were not studied, so the condition of uninterrupted
filtration rests on the mechanism.
The record lacks several results that PCP-009 required (Section 13.2). Two of them
limit the claims. The record holds no spike titre and no limit of detection, so it cannot
show whether any LRF is a lower bound. It also holds no integrity test result for any
run, so it cannot show that each membrane was intact. The MVM results depend on
that at least as much as the XMuLV results. The record names a membrane lot only
in DEV-009-02, so variation between membrane lots was not studied.
The record does not name the runs of the two study events. The deletion of each
response-surface run in turn covers an event that affected one of those runs. It does




                                           39
not cover an event that affected several runs, such as the membrane lot of DEV-009-02
(Section 13.1).

12 Conclusions

1. Scale-down model (objective 1). The qualification compared step yield alone,
   with an offset of −0.5 percentage points (90 % confidence interval −1.2 to 0.3).
   Flux decay and the post-use integrity test are not recorded, so objective 1 was
   not met as PCP-009 states it. PCP-009 §7 requires this report to state what “the
   scale-down model is qualified” means for this step, given the outputs that the
   qualification compares. Only step yield was compared, and it has no criterion,
   so no conclusion was decided by an offset. The outputs with a criterion are
   judged on the scale-down results, the LRF on the spiking model and fragments
   on the mechanism of the step.
2. Effects (objective 2). Filtration volume lowers the mean MVM LRF by 1.73 log10
   across 50–140 L/m2. No effect of filtration pressure was detected on any output,
   and no effect of either parameter on XMuLV LRF, fragments or step yield.
3. Models (objective 3). The selected model of MVM LRF passes the model F-test,
   shows no lack of fit and meets the limit on overfitting of PCMP-001 §7.2. It fails
   the criterion on residuals and influential runs on run 5, the axial run at 50 L/m2
   and 19 psi. XMuLV LRF has no adequate model and is judged on its measured
   results. The selected models of fragments and step yield are intercept-only.
4. Proven acceptable ranges (objective 4). The NOR-propagated PAR of filtration
   volume is 50–136 L/m2 for MVM LRF and the whole range studied for fragments.
   The PAR of filtration pressure is the whole range studied, 8–30 psi, for both.
   Every PAR contains the NOR. XMuLV LRF has no adequate model and no PAR,
   and every measured result met its claim across the range studied.
5. Classification (objective 5). Both parameters are WC-CPP. The class of filtration
   pressure rests on the basis that the A-Mab case study gives and on the scope of
   the viral clearance claim, not on a detected effect.
6. Criteria. Each output with a criterion met its intermediate acceptance criterion
   across the NOR, MVM LRF at 5.25 log10 against 4.62, XMuLV LRF on measured
   results from 5.90 log10 against 4.62, and fragments at 0.78 % against 2.00 %.
7. Design space. The design space covers 91 % of the characterized region and
   excludes filtration volumes above 137.6 L/m2. Deleting run 5, the run at 50
   L/m2 and 19 psi with the highest LRF at that volume, would carry the boundary
   to the end of the range studied.
8. Capability. The Cpk of the cumulative MVM clearance, 1.51, is the tightest of
   the attributes to which the step contributes, above the target of 1.33 and the
   minimum of 1.0. No simulated batch fell outside any criterion.
9. Success of the study. PCP-009 §7 asks for a qualified scale-down model, two
   viral clearance models accepted as predictive and a PAR containing the NOR

13.1.1 DEV-009-01, transient excursion of the filtration pressure

During in-process pressure monitoring the filtration pressure of one run rose for a
short time to a maximum of 22 psi. The investigation attributed the excursion to an
overshoot of the pump ramp. The record names neither the run, nor its set pressure,
nor the duration of the excursion, so the run was analysed at its design setting. Ver-
sion 1.0 recorded the peak as an excursion above the NOR, whose upper edge is 20




                                         43
psi. The reference for an excursion is the set pressure of the run, because the designs
set runs at 8, 19 and 30 psi. An overshoot rises above the set pressure, so the run was
set below 22 psi, at 8 or 19 psi in a design or at the set-point of 13 psi in qualification.
The peak lies inside the range studied, 8–30 psi, over which the designs estimated the
effect of pressure, and no term containing pressure was significant for any output. If
the run was a response-surface run, its deletion leaves the judgement over the NOR
unchanged (Table 5.9). If it was a screening run, its result enters only the screening
effects and the judgement of XMuLV LRF, every measured result of which met the
claim. If it was a qualification run, it enters only the offset of step yield, on which no
conclusion depends (Section 3.1). The run was retained in the analysis.


13.1.2 DEV-009-02, membrane lot flux below the value typical for the
       vendor

The pre-use flux verification of membrane lot LOT-VF-9331 (Meridian Membranes)
gave a flux 12 % below the value typical for the vendor. The investigation attributed
the low flux to variation of membrane permeability between lots. The record names
the MVM infectivity method (AMV-3018) with the event, but it holds no result of an
assessment by that method. Version 1.0 recorded that every run made on the lot
was retained. The lot record gives the use of the lot as “verification run”, and no
record names the runs. The lot may therefore have been used for several runs, and
the deletion of one run at a time does not bound its effect. A lower permeability
lowers the flux at a given pressure. Whether it changed the retention of MVM was
not tested, so the effect of the event on the results cannot be assessed from the record.
The record does not link the lot to run 5.
