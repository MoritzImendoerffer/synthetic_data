Executive summary

Ultrafiltration and diafiltration (UF/DF) is Step 10, the last step of the A-Mab drug
substance process. It concentrates the antibody, exchanges the process buffer for
the formulation buffer and delivers the drug substance at its target concentration
of 75 g/L. This report gives the results of the characterization of the step under the
plan PCP-010 and the master plan PCMP-001. It is part of the Stage 1 record that
supports the transfer of the process from the development site in Cambridge, MA to
the commercial drug substance site in Grafton, WI.
The step has no designed experiment. The risk assessment RA-001 assigned its three
parameters, the number of diavolumes, the transmembrane pressure (TMP) and the
final DS concentration, to univariate study. Version 1.0 of this report described the
planned univariate study S-UFDF-UNI without a result for any parameter effect. The
results reported here come from a further univariate study, S-UFDF-UNI2, made from
2026-09-19 to 2026-09-29, after the QA investigation INV-001 had closed. Each pa-
rameter was run at five levels across its range studied, in duplicate, with the other
parameters at their set-points. The study ran on a second tangential flow filtration
(TFF) skid, EQ-TFF-188. The offsets of the scale-down model from manufacturing
scale were measured on the first skid, EQ-TFF-142, and no run compared the two
skids. The offsets are applied to the results of the second skid, because the two skids
share their design and their membrane and the offsets are small against the criteria
(PD-010-01).
Every result met the drug substance criteria for aggregate (≤ 5 % HMW) and for
the acidic charge variants (18–40 %), at every level of every parameter. The proven
acceptable range (PAR) of each parameter is therefore its range studied, 7–10 DV,
10–15 psi and 65–85 g/L. Aggregate in the final retentate rose with the final DS con-
centration (p < 0.001) and with TMP (p = 0.015). Across the ranges studied the two
effects amount to 0.14 and 0.06 percentage points, and the highest result lay 4.21
percentage points below the criterion. The A-Mab case study leaves UF/DF out, so
no published source gave the size of this effect in advance. No effect of the number
of diavolumes on aggregate was detected, and no slope of the acidic charge variants
was significant.
PCMP-001 assumed that no step after cation exchange changes aggregate. A check
of that assumption on S-UFDF-UNI2 found a rise of aggregate across the NORs of
UF/DF of up to 0.104 percentage points, at the one-sided 95 % prediction bound of
a single run (PD-010-08). This prediction bound of the aggregate rise across the
step tightens the aggregate criterion of the cation exchange pool from 5.00 to 4.90
% HMW.
The residual process buffer fell by a factor of 2.70 for each diavolume, the fall ex-
pected when the buffer species pass the membrane freely. At the minimum of 7 DV



                                          7
it was at most 0.091 % of the process buffer. The buffer-exchange criterion of PCP-
010 rests on conductivity readings that are not in the study record, so it could not be
judged as planned. The buffer exchange is controlled instead by its minimum number
of diavolumes, and the formulation buffer of the drug substance is confirmed against
its release limits (PD-010-07).
All three parameters were classified as key process parameters (KPPs), and the step
has no critical process parameter. In the integrated process model of 2,000 simu-
lated batches the drug substance has a Cpk of 15.7 for aggregate, 1.05 for the acidic
charge variants and 7.3 for residual DNA, which is judged in ng per dose. The Cpk of
the acidic charge variants lies between the minimum of 1.00 and the target of 1.33 set
by PCMP-001. It is set by the distance from the simulated mean to the lower limit of
18 %. This step can only raise the acidic charge variants, by deamidation, so it moves
them away from that limit. Every result of this study lies below the simulated mean.
At the mean of the study, with the simulated standard deviation, the Cpk would be
0.63, below the minimum, so this report cannot confirm the simulated capability from
its own results (Section 8). Version 1.0 recorded two study events, a TMP excursion
to 14.6 psi (DEV-010-01) and a final concentration of 71.5 g/L before a further con-
centration (DEV-010-02). Neither changes a conclusion. The ranges and classes of
this report roll up into the master report PCMR-001.

1 Introduction

1.1 Product and unit operation

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).
Its drug substance is purified by the platform train of Protein A capture, low-pH viral
inactivation, cation exchange, anion exchange, small virus retentive filtration and
UF/DF. UF/DF is the last step of the train, and its product is the drug substance. The
commercial process runs at a production bioreactor working volume of 15,000 L.
The step works across a tangential flow membrane whose pores pass buffer salts and
retain the antibody. Ultrafiltration removes permeate and raises the antibody con-
centration in the retentate. Diafiltration then adds formulation buffer at the rate at
which permeate leaves, so that the retentate volume stays constant while the per-
meable species are washed out. A second concentration brings the retentate to the
drug substance target, and a buffer flush recovers it. The step is operated under
SOP-2013.
The step is not expected to change the glycan variants of A-Mab, and it is credited
with no clearance of host cell protein, DNA or leached Protein A. No viral clearance is
claimed for it. It can add to aggregate, which can form at the membrane wall and in
the recirculation pump. It can also add to the acidic charge variants, because deami-
dation continues while the antibody is in solution on the skid (Section 2.1). Aggregate
and the acidic charge variants are therefore the attributes measured across the step.
The residual process buffer, the step yield and the final concentration are measures
of process performance.

The study follows the lifecycle approach to process validation, and this report is part

2 Prior knowledge and quality risk basis

2.1 Platform and prior-product knowledge

The step belongs to the platform train that was used for X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab
(PCMP-001 §2). The places where aggregate can form in the step are the same for
every antibody, so that experience applies to the qualitative behaviour of the step.
How readily an antibody aggregates depends on its colloidal stability and its self-
association, which differ between antibodies. The viscosity of the retentate rises
with concentration and differs between antibodies too. For this reason the final DS
concentration was studied for A-Mab and was not carried over from the platform. The
A-Mab case study leaves the step out (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009, 114). The
expectations below therefore come from general knowledge of the tangential flow
filtration of antibodies, as PCP-010 §4.1 records them.
Each diavolume removes a fixed fraction of the permeable species that remain in
the retentate. PCP-010 §4.1 expects the residual process buffer components to fall
“roughly exponentially” with the number of diavolumes. At high antibody concentra-
tion two effects can hold the retentate off the composition of the buffer. The antibody
carries a net charge, so buffer ions of the opposite charge are enriched in the reten-
tate and ions of the same charge are depleted (the Donnan effect). The antibody also
occupies part of the retentate volume, which lowers the concentration of each excipi-
ent in the retentate as a whole (volume exclusion). Both effects can move the pH and
the excipient levels of the retentate away from the values of the buffer.
The membrane retains the antibody, so the antibody accumulates at the membrane
wall in a polarization layer, where its concentration is higher than in the bulk retentate.
The retentate also passes many times through the recirculation pump. Aggregate can
form at the wall, where the antibody is most concentrated, and in the pump, where
cavitation, air-liquid interfaces and particles shed by the pump can start it. Shear
alone causes little aggregation of an antibody. Aggregate was therefore expected
to rise with the final DS concentration, which raises the concentration in the bulk
retentate and, below the limiting flux, at the wall. The limiting flux is the flux beyond
which a higher pressure no longer raises the permeate rate. Below it, a higher TMP
drives more permeate through the membrane and raises the antibody concentration
at the wall. Above it, the concentration at the wall stays at its limiting value. A higher
TMP then compacts and thickens the polarization layer, with no gain in flux, so more
antibody is held at that concentration. Aggregate was therefore expected to rise with
TMP below the limiting flux, and it may still rise above it. It was also expected to
rise with the number of passes through the pump, which more diavolumes increase.
Within the normal operating ranges (NORs) the exposure is short, and the effects were
expected to be small. No source gave their size for A-Mab, so this study measures it



                                           13
(Section 5.3). The effect of a high final DS concentration and a high TMP on aggregate
is this campaign’s own addition to the integrated process model (Section 3.5).
Deamidation of asparagine residues continues while the antibody is in solution. It
converts a neutral residue into a charged one, so it can only raise the acidic charge
variants. The increase grows with the time in solution, at a rate that rises with pH
and temperature. The operation lasts longer with more diavolumes, with a higher
final concentration, which lowers the flux, and with a lower TMP below the limiting
flux. The acidic charge variants were therefore expected to rise, if at all, with the
number of diavolumes and the final DS concentration, and to fall with TMP.

2.3 Risk-based prioritization and parameter selection

RA-001 ranked every process parameter of the drug substance process for its poten-
tial impact on the CQAs and on process performance, and for its potential to interact
with other parameters (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b). Parameters
ranked high, or with a likely interaction, were assigned to multivariate study. Pa-
rameters ranked lower, whose range could be supported one factor at a time, were
assigned to univariate study (PCMP-001 §4). All three parameters of UF/DF were as-
signed to univariate study, and no designed experiment was planned for the step.
A higher TMP and a higher final DS concentration both raise the antibody concentra-
tion in the polarization layer at the membrane wall, or compact that layer once the
flux has reached its limit. A higher final DS concentration raises the viscosity and the
bulk concentration of the retentate, and both lower the limiting flux. The flux then
reaches its limit at a lower TMP, and above that TMP a higher pressure compacts
the layer. The rise of aggregate with TMP may therefore be larger at a high final
DS concentration than at a low one. A study that varies one factor at a time cannot
resolve such an interaction. It bounds the effect of each parameter at the edge of its
range studied, with the others at their set-points (Section 7). PCP-010 §4.3 keeps the
rule of Version 1.0 that runs with two parameters at their range edges together are
made where a measured attribute approaches its criterion at an edge. Such runs are
made before a range is claimed, and they are agreed with Statistics and QA before
they are executed.

3 Materials and methods

3.1 Scale-down model and its qualification

The bench-scale model holds the crossflow and the membrane loading at their com-
mercial values, both scaled by membrane area (PCMP-001 §5). The membrane chem-
istry, the nominal molecular weight cut-off and the feed channel geometry are those
of the commercial skid. The TMP, the number of diavolumes and the final concen-
tration do not depend on scale and were set at the values under study. One quantity
cannot be matched, the share of the batch held up in the system. With the crossflow
and the membrane loading matched per unit area, the antibody passes through the
pump as often at bench scale as at commercial scale. The bench skid, however, holds
a larger share of its batch in its recirculation loop and has a larger ratio of surface
to volume. More antibody is therefore lost to hold-up, and more of it meets surfaces
and air-liquid interfaces per gram processed. A slightly lower step yield and slightly
more aggregate were expected at bench scale.
The qualification study S-UFDF-SDM ran from 2026-02-25 to 2026-03-06 on the skid
EQ-TFF-142, under SOP-1001. It compared three runs of the model at the set-point
condition with five batches at manufacturing scale. The outputs compared were
the step yield, the aggregate content of the final retentate, the final concentration

3.2 Operation

The step receives the filtrate pool of the small virus retentive filtration step. Under
SOP-2013 the pool is concentrated by ultrafiltration, diafiltered at constant volume
against formulation buffer for the number of diavolumes of the run, concentrated a
second time to the final target and recovered with a buffer flush. At the set-point
condition the step runs 8 DV at a TMP of 12 psi to a final concentration of 75 g/L. The
composition of the formulation buffer is fixed by the drug product program and was
a constant of the studies. Under PCP-010 §5.2 every run is loaded from a single pool
of the virus filtration step, so that the variability of the load does not confound the
comparison between levels. Section 12 of the plan gives a bridging rule if one pool
is not sufficient. The study record of S-UFDF-UNI2 does not identify the load pool of
its runs, so it cannot show that one pool served the whole study (PD-010-04).

3.5 Statistical methods

For each parameter and each output, the individual results were regressed on the
setting of the parameter over the range studied, and the slope was tested against
zero at α = 0.05 (PCP-010 §5.5, PCMP-001 §6.2). Each series has 10 runs, so each
slope has 8 degrees of freedom. An attribute is called affected by a parameter when
its slope is significant at that level. The change across the range studied is the slope
times the width of the range. It is reported with its 95 % confidence interval beside the
p-value, so that statistical and practical significance are read apart. The criterion, and
not the p-value, decides whether a range is acceptable. No response-surface model
was fitted, because the study has no factorial structure and no interaction can be
estimated from it. The model-adequacy criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2 apply to response-
surface models and not to these slopes. The one fit of several parameters in this
report is the main-effects fit of aggregate used for the verification of Section 7.1. It is
judged on the first three criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2, a significant model, no significant
lack of fit and the limit on overfitting. PCMP-001 §7.5 names these three for a model
whose bound widens a criterion, and this report applies them to the fit whose bound
tightens one.
The duplicate runs at each level give the pure error of the study, the combined vari-
ability of the process and the assay, as a pooled standard deviation with 15 degrees
of freedom for each output. PCP-010 §5.5 takes the pure error from the replicated
set-point condition. This study replicates every level, so the pure error is pooled over
all of them (PD-010-06). PCP-010 §5.3 leaves to this report how its rule for small
changes and the slope test are applied together. This report applies both to aggre-
gate, the attribute whose changes the rule concerns. The slope test decides whether
aggregate is affected, and the change across the range is compared with the pure
error. For aggregate the two agreed for every parameter (Section 5.3). The acidic




                                                      20
charge variants lie near 21 %, against a limit of quantitation of 1 % for icIEF. The
rule for the low end of the method range therefore does not apply to them, and the
slope test alone decides.
The buffer exchange was analysed by fitting the exponential decay that the mecha-
nism predicts (Section 2.1), as the natural logarithm of the residual process buffer
regressed on the number of diavolumes. PCP-010 §5.5 plans that fit on the conduc-
tivity series of each run. The study record holds no such series, so the fit was made
across the runs of the diavolume series, on one result per run (PD-010-06). Com-
plete passage of the buffer species through the membrane gives a slope of −1 on that
scale.
Each attribute result was judged on its own against its criterion, as PCMP-001 §6.2
requires for a quality attribute and PCP-010 §7.2 states for this step. Aggregate
was judged at manufacturing scale and the acidic charge variants as measured (Sec-
tion 3.1). The PAR of a parameter is the contiguous span of levels over which every
attribute measured on the final retentate met its criterion, with the other parameters
at their set-points (PCP-010 §8).
The study record of S-UFDF-UNI2 does not give the order in which its runs were made
(PD-010-04). It cannot show the randomized order that PCP-010 §6.2 requires, and a
drift over the course of the study could not be separated from a slope.
The capability of the drug substance comes from the integrated process model of
PCMP-001 §6.5. The model runs 2,000 simulated batches through every step. Each
process parameter is drawn from a normal distribution centred on its set-point, with
a standard deviation of one sixth of the width of its NOR, and the draw is clipped to
the range studied. The NOR of the number of diavolumes is a minimum and has no
upper edge. The model draws it around its set-point of 8 DV with a standard deviation
of 0.33 DV, one sixth of a range of 7–9 DV, and never below the minimum of 7 DV or
above 10 DV. The model includes the effects of the UF/DF parameters on aggregate
and no effect of them on the acidic charge variants. PCMP-001 §7.9 sets a target Cpk
of 1.33 and a minimum of 1.00.

criterion. This report judges both changes small against the criterion (PD-010-06).

aggregate to rise with them. The data do not show that rise at the precision of the
study, and an effect within the interval cannot be excluded.

stand in for the conductivity. It falls by a constant fraction with each diavolume, so it
does not level off within any range that an assay can resolve.
The buffer exchange is controlled instead by its minimum number of diavolumes, 7
DV, the lower edge of a one-sided NOR. The formulation buffer of the drug substance
is confirmed against its release limits (PD-010-07). The result of this study is the
residual process buffer at each number of diavolumes, at most 0.091 % of the process
buffer at the minimum.
Objective (iii), to establish the number of diavolumes at which the retentate reaches
the formulation buffer, is not met. The study gives the residual process buffer after
each number of diavolumes, but not that number.

The step yield ranged from 91.0 to 99.2 %, with a mean of 95.7 %. No slope of the
step yield was significant. The 95 % confidence intervals of its change across the
range studied run from −4.6 to 1.1 percentage points for the number of diavolumes,

step yield has no criterion and is reported for information.
The final concentration followed its setting in the series of the final DS concentration,
with a slope of 0.998 (95 % confidence interval 0.920 to 1.075). Each result lay within
1.2 g/L of its setting. In the other two series, run at the target of 75 g/L, the final
concentration ranged from 73.3 to 76.6 g/L, inside the NOR of 70–80 g/L. The pure
error of the final concentration, 1.13 g/L, compares with a precision of the A280
method of 1.2 %, which is 0.9 g/L at the target.



5.7 Mechanistic interpretation

The rise of aggregate with the final DS concentration and with TMP is consistent
with aggregate forming in the polarization layer at the membrane wall. The final DS
concentration raises the bulk concentration at the end of the operation, and below the
limiting flux it raises the concentration at the wall too. Below the limiting flux TMP
raises the concentration at the wall by driving more permeate through the membrane.
Above it, the concentration at the wall stays at its limiting value, and a higher TMP
compacts and thickens the layer, so more antibody is held at that concentration. The
study did not measure the flux across the TMP range, so it cannot say on which side
of the limiting flux the set-point lies. Within the NOR the two effects amount to 0.07
and 0.04 percentage points.
No effect of the number of diavolumes on aggregate was detected, although more
diavolumes add passes through the pump. Diafiltration is made before the second
concentration, at a lower antibody concentration than the final one. If aggregate
forms mainly where the concentration at the wall is highest, extra diavolumes at that
lower concentration would add little. The study did not measure where in the step
aggregate forms, so this explanation is not tested.
The acidic charge variants did not change detectably over the ranges studied, al-
though each parameter changes the time the antibody spends in solution. The study
record does not give the duration of the runs, so the study cannot relate the acidic
charge variants to the time in solution. Any deamidation in the step would raise them,
toward the upper limit of the criterion.
The fall of the residual process buffer by a factor of 2.70 for each diavolume is con-
sistent with free passage of the buffer species through the membrane, which diafil-
tration at constant volume relies on. The study did not measure the pH or the excip-
ient levels of the final retentate. It therefore gives no evidence on the Donnan and
volume-exclusion effects that can move them away from the values of the buffer at
high concentration.

6 Design space

No design space is defined for UF/DF. A design space is a multivariate region (Interna-
tional Council for Harmonisation 2009). A study that varies one parameter at a time
cannot define one, because it gives no result where two parameters are away from
their set-points at the same time. The step is described instead by the univariate PAR
of each parameter, which equals its range studied (Section 7). These data support
the movement of one parameter within its PAR, with the others at their set-points.
Section 7.3 gives only the size that an interaction would need to bring aggregate to
its criterion. An excursion outside a NOR in manufacture is dispositioned against
the PAR (PCP-010 §8). A planned move of an operating range is a change, and it
is assessed under change management before it is made (International Council for
Harmonisation 2008).

7.1 Acceptance basis

No step follows UF/DF, so PCMP-001 Table 7.1 defines no IAC for it, and the drug
substance criteria apply directly (PCMP-001 §7.6). Aggregate must not exceed 5 %
HMW, and the acidic charge variants must lie between 18 and 40 %. Both criteria
come from the drug substance CQA register (PCMP-001 §3), and no result of these
studies enters them.
The a priori derivation of the criteria upstream of this step assumes that no step after
cation exchange changes aggregate, and that no step after low-pH viral inactivation
changes the acidic charge variants (PCMP-001 §7.3). On that assumption the cation
exchange pool carried the drug substance criterion for aggregate itself, 5.00 % HMW.
This study shows that two parameters of UF/DF raise aggregate within their NORs.
On the sum of the two slopes the rise at the corner of 80 g/L and 14 psi is 0.06
percentage points above the level at the set-point.
This report checks the assumption of no aggregate change after cation exchange
on the results of S-UFDF-UNI2, as PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.5 states. PCP-010 §7.2
planned no verification at this step (PD-010-08). The aggregate results, with the
aggregate offset subtracted, are fitted to the main effects of the three parameters,
with all runs pooled and no interaction term. The fit is evaluated at every combination
of NOR low, set-point and NOR high, as for a step with a response-surface model.
For the number of diavolumes, whose NOR is a minimum, the upper value of the
combinations is 9 DV, the value that also sets the spread of the simulation (Section 3.5).
At the least favourable combination the fit predicts a mean rise of 0.057 percentage
points above its value at the set-point. The one-sided 95 % prediction bound of a
single run puts the rise at 0.104 percentage points. The fit meets the first three
criteria of PCMP-001 §7.2. The model is significant (p < 0.001), it has no significant
lack of fit (p = 0.147), and R² exceeds predicted R² by 0.15, within the limit of 0.2.
PCMP-001 §7.5 rule 1 states that where the worst case predicted within the NOR is
worse than the platform worst case, the prediction bound of a single batch replaces
the platform value. Here the bound replaces the assumption of no change, and every
aggregate criterion upstream carries it. The criterion of the cation exchange pool is
tightened from 5.00 to 4.90 % HMW, and the cation exchange report PCR-007 judges
its pool against that value. The fit assumes that the parameters act independently,
and Section 7.3 gives the size that an interaction would need to matter.
For the acidic charge variants no slope was significant. The change across the step
was not measured, because no load was sampled (PD-010-03), and a rise of up to 1.4
percentage points across a range cannot be excluded (Section 5.4). The assumption of

11 Discussion

Two parameters of UF/DF, the final DS concentration and TMP, raise aggregate. Both
rises exceed the pure error of the study, and this report judges them small against
the criterion. They still matter upstream. PCMP-001 had assumed that no step after
cation exchange changes aggregate, and the bound for a single run of 0.104 percent-
age points now tightens the criterion of the cation exchange pool to 4.90 % HMW.
More diavolumes lower the residual process buffer, by the factor that free passage
of the buffer species predicts. No slope of the acidic charge variants was significant.
Every result met the drug substance criteria, and each parameter has a PAR equal to
its range studied.
The scale-down model is qualified for aggregate under the approach of Section 3.1,
which judges the aggregate results with the offset at its estimate and at either end of
its interval. Three points limit that confidence. The offsets were measured on the first
skid, and the study ran on the second. The discrepancy entry for the use of the offsets
of the first skid on the second (PD-010-01) accepts it because the skids share their
design and their membrane and the offsets are small against the criteria. The acidic
charge variants and the residual process buffer were not compared at both scales, so
their representativeness rests on the kinetics of deamidation and on the passage of
the buffer species through the membrane. The qualification record does not name
the manufacturing-scale batches (PD-010-05). The highest aggregate result lies far
below its criterion (Section 7.2), so none of these points changes a conclusion about
aggregate.
The study record of S-UFDF-UNI2 is incomplete against the plan in ways that bear
on its reading. No load was sampled, so the change across the step is unknown and
the study measures only how the parameters change the final retentate (PD-010-03).
No conductivity or pH was read, so the buffer-exchange criterion could not be judged
as planned (PD-010-02), and the buffer exchange rests on the minimum number of
diavolumes and on the release of the drug substance (PD-010-07). The order of the
runs and the load pool are not recorded, so a drift over the study cannot be separated
from a slope (PD-010-04).
The main scientific limitation is the univariate design, which cannot measure an inter-
action. TMP and the final DS concentration both act on the polarization layer at the
membrane wall, so an interaction between them would raise aggregate most where
both are high. The prediction bound of the aggregate rise in Section 7.1 assumes no
interaction, and Section 7.3 sets the size that one would need against the criterion.
Each slope rests on 8 degrees of freedom, so an effect smaller than the confidence
intervals of Table 5.2 may have gone undetected, for the acidic charge variants in
particular.

During concentration the TMP of one run rose to 14.6 psi, above the upper edge of its

the 95 % confidence interval of the slope of aggregate on TMP then puts its effect at
no more than 0.050 percentage points. It would have raised the mean of the three
scale-down runs, and with it the aggregate offset, by at most 0.017 percentage points.

would still meet the criterion.


13.1.2 DEV-010-02, final concentration below target

The final concentration check of one run measured 71.5 g/L by A280 (AMV-3019), be-
low the target of 75 g/L and inside the NOR of 70–80 g/L. The investigation attributed
the shortfall to the residual hold-up volume of the skid, the property of the bench skid
that Section 3.1 expects to lower its step yield. The record does not say how the hold-
up lowered the final concentration. The retentate was concentrated further to the
target, which kept it in the pump loop for a further period at the highest concentra-
tion of the run. The run was retained. The record gives neither the concentration
after the correction nor the run.
The correction could have raised the aggregate of that run. Had the run been one
of the scale-down runs of S-UFDF-SDM, it would enter both the offset of the final
concentration and the aggregate offset. The final concentration has no criterion at
this step. The aggregate offset of 0.020 percentage points lies between 0.010 and
0.026 when any one scale-down run is left out. With the smallest of these, the highest
aggregate result at manufacturing scale is 0.80 % HMW, 4.20 percentage points below
the criterion, so no conclusion depends on the run.

The first, PD-010-01, is work that the plan did not foresee. PCP-010 planned the uni-
variate study S-UFDF-UNI, which ran but left no result for any parameter effect. The
further study S-UFDF-UNI2 was made during the re-analysis to give the parameters
recorded results, and every result, PAR and class of this report rests on it (Section 4.2).
It ran on the second skid, with the offsets of the first applied to its results (Section 3.1).
No result for a parameter effect existed when it was made, so no conclusion rests on
runs added after a result was known.
PD-010-02 and PD-010-07 concern the buffer exchange. Without the conductivity and
pH readings the criterion of PCP-010 could not be judged as planned, and the buffer
exchange is controlled by the minimum number of diavolumes and the release of the
drug substance (Section 5.5).
The absence of load, pre-diafiltration and permeate samples (PD-010-03) leaves the
change across the step unknown for aggregate and the acidic charge variants, and the
retention of the antibody unconfirmed (Section 3.4). The missing execution records

PD-010-06 covers the rules that PCP-010 leaves to this report. They are the qualifica-

PD-010-08 is the verification of aggregate at this step, which PCP-010 §7.2 did not
plan. PCMP-001 Version 1.0 assumed that no step after cation exchange changes
aggregate, and PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.5 checks that assumption on S-UFDF-UNI2.

at the least favourable NOR combination, gave a one-sided 95 % prediction bound
