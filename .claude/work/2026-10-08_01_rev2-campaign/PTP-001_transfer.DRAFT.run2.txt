1 Purpose and scope

This plan governs the transfer of the A-Mab drug substance manufacturing process
from the development site of Novacyte Biologics in Cambridge, MA to its commercial
drug substance site in Grafton, WI. The transfer covers the manufacturing process,
the analytical methods that test the intermediates and the drug substance, and the
control strategy that connects them. Section 2 describes the process, and Section 3
names the work that each site holds.
This plan is the parent document of the A-Mab process characterization package. The
package has 22 documents, including this plan, and Table 1.1 lists the others. The
risk assessment RA-001 sets the scope of each study. Each Process Characterization
Plan (PCP-003 to PCP-010) commits the studies of its step, and each paired Process
Characterization Report (PCR-003 to PCR-010) records what they found. The master
plan PCMP-001 sets the framework that the step plans apply, and the master report
PCMR-001 consolidates the reports. Together these documents justify the operating
ranges and the control strategy that the receiving site will run. The package also
holds two investigation reports of Quality Assurance. The investigation INV-001 is
the reason for this revision (Section 1.1), and the investigation INV-002 concerns the
adequacy of the response-surface models in the step reports.

Three activities are outside the scope of this plan. Drug product manufacture, filling
and finishing are transferred under a separate plan. Cell bank manufacture and test-
ing are governed by their own records. The execution of the process performance
qualification (PPQ) campaign is also outside this plan. This plan sets the conditions
the campaign must meet before it may start and the strategy it will follow (Section 7).
The campaign will be executed under its own protocol and reported separately.
The transfer follows ICH Q10, which places technology transfer within the pharma-
ceutical quality system (International Council for Harmonisation 2008), and the life-
cycle approach to process validation (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). In
that lifecycle, process characterization is Stage 1 (process design) and process perfor-
mance qualification is Stage 2. The enhanced development approach of ICH Q8 and
ICH Q11 governs how the design space and the control strategy are defined (Interna-
tional Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012). ICH Q9 governs the risk assessment
that scopes the studies (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b), and ICH
Q5A governs the evaluation of viral safety (International Council for Harmonisation
2023a). The process model, the quality attribute framework and the risk method
follow the published A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009).

2 Product and process description

A-Mab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody. Its primary mechanism of action is
antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). An antibody without core fucose on
its N-linked glycan binds the Fcγ receptor IIIa (FcγRIIIa) of natural killer cells more
strongly, so the ADCC activity of A-Mab rises with afucosylation (CMC Biotech Work-
ing Group 2009, 38). Galactose content does not change the ADCC of A-Mab, but its
complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) rises with galactose content (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009, 38). Afucosylation and galactosylation are therefore quality at-
tributes of the drug substance. The glycan is formed in the production bioreactor.
None of the platform purification steps separates glycosylation variants under the
conditions used here (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). Protein A binds the Fc
region at the interface of the CH2 and CH3 domains. The glycan takes no part in
that binding, so Protein A captures every glycan variant alike. Fucose, galactose and
mannose carry no charge, so the two ion exchange steps, which separate by charge,
do not resolve these variants either. Control of the glycan attributes therefore sits in
cell culture. For this reason the transfer of the cell culture step carries more risk to
product quality than the transfer of any single purification step.
The drug substance is made at a production bioreactor working volume of 15,000 L.
It leaves the final formulation step at a target concentration of 75 g/L. The train has
8 unit operations, numbered Step 3 to Step 10 (Table 2.1).

The purification sequence is the platform sequence of the earlier humanized IgG1
products of Novacyte Biologics, X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab. Capture on Protein A is
followed by a low-pH hold, cation exchange, anion exchange, small virus retentive
filtration, and ultrafiltration with diafiltration (UF/DF). The low-pH hold, the anion
exchange step and the virus filter each carry a viral clearance claim. The three claims
are modular in the sense of ICH Q5A (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a).
Each step is studied and claimed on its own, and no single step report claims the
cumulative clearance of the train. The master report PCMR-001 states the cumulative
clearance.
Table 2.2 lists the 11 quality attributes that the transfer must preserve. Criticality
is a continuum, not a binary split into critical and non-critical, as in the case study
(CMC Biotech Working Group 2009) and the risk principles of ICH Q9 (International
Council for Harmonisation 2023b). The attributes ranked high and very high drive
the scope of the studies.
Afucosylation, galactosylation and high mannose depend on how far the cell processes
the glycan in the Golgi. That processing changes with the nutrient supply and with
the dissolved gas concentrations in the bioreactor. Acidic charge variants accumulate
through the deamidation of asparagine residues. The rate of deamidation rises with
pH and temperature, and the extent rises with the time the product spends at those
conditions. Aggregate forms in the culture and again at low pH, during the elution
from Protein A and the viral inactivation hold. Cation exchange reduces it, because
the resin binds the high molecular weight species more strongly than the monomer,
and these species stay on the column when the pool is cut. Fragments form in culture
and during the harvest hold, where proteases released from lysed cells clip the anti-
body. The CQA register therefore assigns them to harvest. Host cell protein, residual
DNA and leached Protein A are removed by the chromatography steps. Protein A and
cation exchange bind the antibody and elute it away from most of these impurities.
Anion exchange works the other way, binding the impurities while the antibody flows
through. The two viral clearance requirements are cumulative. The register assigns
each to one step, but each requirement is met by the sum of the claims of several
steps.

3 Sending and receiving sites

The sending site is the development site of Novacyte Biologics in Cambridge, MA. The
receiving site is the commercial drug substance site of Novacyte Biologics in Grafton,
WI. Both sites belong to the same sponsor and work under one pharmaceutical quality
system and one change control procedure. ICH Q10 places technology transfer within
that quality system (International Council for Harmonisation 2008). A change to a set-
point, a range or an analytical method during the transfer is therefore assessed once
and applied at both sites.
The development site keeps the qualified scale-down models, the execution and the
statistical analysis of the characterization studies, and the authorship of the charac-
terization plans and reports. It also keeps the process description and the justification
of the operating ranges until those are approved. The receiving site takes ownership
of commercial manufacture. That ownership covers the master batch record, the op-
eration of the equipment train at commercial scale, the engineering and qualification
batches, and the routine testing that supports batch release.
The division between the sites is one of work, not of accountability. Both sites review
and approve the process description, and Quality Assurance at both sites approves
each handover listed in Section 10. Where a characterization study supports a range
that the receiving site cannot hold, the two sites resolve it together before the range
is written into a batch record.

The largest difference between the sites is scale. The receiving site runs the produc-
tion bioreactor at 15,000 L, while the characterization studies run at the scale-down
volumes qualified under SOP-1001 (Qualification of Scale-Down Models). In cell cul-
ture, scale acts through mixing and through gas transfer. At constant power per unit
volume, mixing time rises with vessel volume. A bolus of feed therefore takes longer
to disperse, and cells near the addition point spend longer at a high local concentra-
tion. Carbon dioxide is stripped less efficiently as the liquid column deepens, because
a sparge bubble that rises through more liquid comes closer to equilibrium with the
culture before it leaves. Oxygen crosses the same interface in the other direction,
from the bubble into the culture. Carbon dioxide is far more soluble in the culture
than oxygen. Its removal is therefore limited by the volume of gas that passes through
the culture, while the oxygen supply is limited by the volumetric mass transfer coef-
ficient (kLa) of the vessel. For this reason oxygen supply and carbon dioxide removal
are matched separately when a bioreactor model is qualified.
In the purification steps, scale acts through column packing and through pressure. A
chromatography column is scaled by holding the bed height and the linear velocity
constant. This keeps the residence time of the mobile phase, but not the wall support
that stabilizes a narrow bed. A wide commercial column can therefore lose plate
count at a pressure drop that the model tolerates. A filter is scaled by membrane
area. The virus filter runs at a set filtration pressure, and the UF/DF step at a set
transmembrane pressure. At a set pressure the flux depends on how the feed builds
up at the membrane surface, so a feed that differs between the scales shows as a
difference in flux and capacity. Section 5 describes how a model is qualified against
these differences.
The second difference is process control. Automated control at the receiving site
holds a parameter closer to its set-point than manual control does, and so narrows
the distribution that the commercial process will deliver. A narrower distribution
does not invalidate a range, because every NOR lies within the characterized range
of its parameter (Table 6.1). The reverse case matters more. Where the receiving site
cannot hold a parameter inside the NOR that the characterization assumes, either the
range must be widened by further study or the equipment must be changed.
The remaining elements in Table 4.1 do not change the process itself, but each dif-
fers between the sites. The methods are transferred and requalified, the raw material
lots come from commercial supply under supplier qualification, the buffers are pre-
pared centrally, and the operators are trained on the master batch record. Buffer
preparation is the one element in that group that still needs a study, because central
preparation adds a hold before use that laboratory preparation does not have. That
study belongs to the receiving site and is listed in Section 8.

5 Scale-down model and comparability
  strategy

The characterization studies do not run at commercial scale. They run on scale-
down models, and every conclusion drawn from them reaches the commercial process
through the qualification of those models. Qualification is performed under SOP-1001
(Qualification of Scale-Down Models). The lifecycle guidance expects a manufacturer
to understand how far a laboratory model represents the commercial process (U.S.
Food and Drug Administration 2011).
A model is set up in two parts. Parameters that do not depend on scale, such as pH,
temperature and dissolved oxygen, are run at the values proposed for commercial
manufacture. Parameters that depend on scale are set so that the model reproduces
the performance of the commercial train. In the production bioreactor these are
agitation and gas flow, which are set to match the oxygen transfer and the carbon
dioxide stripping of the commercial vessel. For the chromatography steps the model
keeps the bed height and the linear velocity of the manufacturing column, and so its
residence time. Load, wash and elution volumes are normalized to column volumes,
and column efficiency is checked by plate count and peak asymmetry. The virus filter
model uses the same filter type at the same filtration pressure and load per membrane
area. The UF/DF model scales membrane loading and cross flow by membrane area at
the same transmembrane pressure. The model is then compared with manufacturing
scale on the outputs the step controls. For a chromatography step those outputs
are step yield, pool volume and the impurity levels in the pool. For the production
bioreactor they are viable cell concentration, titre and the product quality attributes
the step forms.
Each model is qualified for each output separately, by the offset rule of the master
plan PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.1. The offset of an output is the mean of the scale-
down runs at set-point minus the mean of the manufacturing-scale batches, with its
two-sided 90 % confidence interval. Until the receiving site has run the process, these
are batches of the development train at the sending site. A model may differ in mean
from manufacturing scale, provided the offset is known precisely enough. For an
output compared at both scales, every judgement of a scale-down result against a
criterion therefore subtracts the offset first, so ranges and conclusions are stated at
manufacturing scale. A model is qualified for an output when the conclusion over the
NOR is the same with the offset at either end of its interval.
The qualification runs are made, and the offset of each output is estimated, before the
first characterization run of the step. For a step with a response-surface design, the
decision whether the model is qualified for an output is made at analysis, because the
conclusion over the NOR comes from that model. The qualification does not compare

representativeness rests on the mechanism of the step for fragments and on the virus
spiking studies for viral clearance (PCMP-001 §7.1).

6.3 The control strategy

The control strategy is the set of controls that together assure the quality of the drug
substance. In this transfer it has three layers. Parameter classification places every
process parameter in a class and attaches a control to it. In-process limits bound
what each intermediate may deliver to the next step. Release testing confirms the
attributes of the drug substance against Table 2.2, with two exceptions. Residual
DNA and viral clearance are met through the clearance that the characterization
demonstrates, and no batch test measures them against their criteria.
Classification is performed under the parameter classification procedure SOP-4001
on the evidence of the characterization studies. Each report classifies the parameters
of its own step, and the master report PCMR-001 consolidates the classification for
the whole train. A parameter whose variability affects a quality attribute is a critical
process parameter (CPP). It is a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP)
where the risk of leaving the design space is low. A parameter that affects process
consistency without affecting quality is a key process parameter (KPP). The remaining
parameters are general process parameters (GPPs), which the batch record controls
without a claim of criticality (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). At this revision the
train has 1 CPP, 20 WC-CPPs, 10 KPPs and 6 GPPs (Table 6.1).
In the characterization, each step output on which a drug substance criterion depends
is judged against an intermediate acceptance criterion, where PCMP-001 Version 2.0
§7.3 defines one. The criterion is the drug substance criterion carried backward
through the train, using the worst case that each downstream step showed on the
platform products X-Mab, Y-Mab and Z-Mab. No margin is applied. The derivation
uses no data from the studies it judges. Each step report checks the platform worst
cases of its own step, and PCMP-001 §7.5 states when that check tightens or widens
a criterion upstream. The master plan gives 22 such criteria across 6 steps.
Residual DNA has a drug substance criterion but no intermediate acceptance crite-
rion (PCMP-001 §7.3). Its criterion, no more than 0.001 ng per dose, is the acceptable
range that the A-Mab case study claims, and the case study notes that DNA is consis-
tently cleared from the process (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009, 47). The criterion
is expressed per dose. The dose is the weekly A-Mab dose of 10 mg/kg (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009, 26), or 700 mg for a reference adult of 70 kg, a body weight that
this package assumes. The criterion is met through the clearance that the character-
ization demonstrates and is not tested at release. A release test could not show it,
because the qPCR method (AMV-3014) quantifies DNA only down to 1 pg/mg, which
is 0.7 ng per dose, 700 times the criterion.
Viral safety rests on three independent steps, not on any one of them. The low-pH hold
inactivates enveloped virus. The anion exchange step removes both enveloped and
small virus in flow-through mode, and the virus filter removes small and enveloped
virus by size. Inactivation by pH, removal by charge and removal by size act on
different properties of the virus particle. A particle that survives one step is therefore
no more likely to survive the next, and the log reductions are added (International
Council for Harmonisation 2023a). Each claimed step is held to its platform claim
(PCMP-001 §7.4). For XMuLV the claims sum to 16.72 log10 against a cumulative




                                           26
requirement of 16.7. For MVM they sum to 8.62 against 8.6. Both requirements are
therefore met by the claims alone, with a margin of 0.02 log10 for each virus. The
margin is smaller than the intermediate precision of the infectivity titre assays (0.3
and 0.32 log10 in Table 6.3), so a step that falls short of its claim cannot be made up
by the other steps. The load conditions on which each claim depends, such as the pH
and the conductivity of the anion exchange load, are controlled by the batch record.
The master report PCMR-001 states the cumulative clearance and compares it with
the acceptance criteria in Table 2.2.
The control strategy is transferred when PCMR-001 has been approved and the
classes it consolidates have been written into the master batch record. The engi-
neering batches and the qualification batches are both run under that batch record
(Table 10.1).

7 Process performance qualification
  strategy

Process performance qualification is the Stage 2 activity that follows this transfer
(U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011). It demonstrates that the commercial pro-
cess, run by the receiving site with the transferred methods and control strategy,
reproducibly delivers drug substance of the required quality. This plan does not ex-
ecute that demonstration. It states what must be in place before the campaign may
start and the strategy the campaign will follow.
The campaign will manufacture 5 consecutive batches at commercial scale under
the approved master batch record. Every parameter will be held at its set-point in
Table 6.1, and no deliberate excursion will be run. The campaign will not challenge
the edges of the characterized ranges. Those edges are established at small scale,
and a qualification batch is not a characterization experiment. However, a campaign
of this size cannot capture the variability that commercial manufacture will show over
its life.
The campaign may start when the following conditions are met:

  1. The characterization package is complete and PCMR-001 has been approved.
  2. Every method used for in-process and release testing has been transferred and
     requalified at the receiving site.
  3. The commercial equipment train and the utilities that serve it are qualified.
  4. The master batch record carries the confirmed set-points, the confirmed ranges
     and the in-process controls.
  5. The gaps in Section 8 are closed, or Quality Assurance has assessed and accepted
     in writing the residual risk of an open gap.

Sampling during the campaign will follow the sampling plans of the step plans. Each
intermediate for which PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.3 defines an intermediate accep-
tance criterion is then tested against it, as in the characterization. The acceptance
criteria of the campaign will be stated in the qualification protocol. The master plan
PCMP-001 plans the characterization campaign that produces the evidence, and the
master report PCMR-001 consolidates it. Both are approved before the qualification
campaign starts.
Continued process verification begins when the qualification campaign is complete.
The receiving site will draft the monitoring plan during the campaign. The plan may
not be finalized until the campaign data are available, because its control limits are
derived from the performance that the commercial process actually shows (U.S. Food
and Drug Administration 2011).
