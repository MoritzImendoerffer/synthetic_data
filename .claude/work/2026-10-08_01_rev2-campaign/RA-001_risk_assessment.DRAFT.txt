1 Purpose and scope

This document records the risk assessment made on the A-Mab drug substance pro-
cess before any characterization study began. Its output is the scope of the charac-
terization campaign. For each process parameter it names the quality attributes the
parameter could affect, the potential failure mode and its effect, an initial risk score
and the type of study assigned to the parameter. The assessment is prospective and
reports no study result.
The assessment does not classify parameters. A parameter is classified as a critical
process parameter (CPP), a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP), a
key process parameter (KPP) or a general process parameter (GPP) from the effects
that the characterization studies measure. That classification follows the parameter
classification procedure SOP-4001. It is reported in the characterization report of
each unit operation and consolidated in the master report PCMR-001. The scores
in this document are initial scores in the same sense. The risk that remains once
the studies have reported is a separate assessment, and nothing in this document
anticipates it.
The scope is the drug substance process from the production bioreactor to formula-
tion. Table 1.1 lists the 8 unit operations and the principal role of each, and the as-
sessment covers their 37 process parameters. The seed expansion train, drug product
manufacture and the analytical methods themselves are outside its scope.

In this framework risk assessment is a lifecycle activity, not a single exercise (Inter-
national Council for Harmonisation 2023b; CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). This
assessment is the first in that lifecycle. It draws on prior knowledge alone, because no
A-Mab characterization data existed when it was made. It is revisited when the char-
acterization reports are available, and again once commercial experience has been
gained. The approach follows the enhanced development approach of ICH Q8 and
ICH Q11 (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012) and supports Stage 1
of the process validation lifecycle (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011).

2 Risk assessment methodology

The method is risk ranking and filtering, applied to process parameters as in the
A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009) and within the quality risk
management principles of ICH Q9 (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b).
Each parameter is put to two questions. The first asks what the parameter could do
to a quality attribute, or to process performance, if it moved outside the range over
which the process is currently understood. The second asks whether the effect of the
parameter could depend on the setting of another parameter of the same unit oper-
ation. The first question sets the score. The second sets the study type, because an
effect that depends on a second parameter cannot be found by moving one parameter
at a time.
Severity is taken from the attribute at risk and not from the parameter. It runs from 2
to 10 and is the severity assigned to the attribute in the quality attribute assessment,
reproduced in Table 3.1. Where a parameter could affect more than one attribute,
the severity of the most severe of them is used. A parameter that puts no quality
attribute at risk takes a severity of 4, the value assigned to a process attribute such
as step yield or titre.
Occurrence and detection are assigned before any effect has been measured, and
they are assigned by class and not parameter by parameter. Table 2.1 gives each
score with its basis. Occurrence is scored at 7 for a parameter that could affect a
quality attribute, because neither the size of its effect nor the width of the range that
holds the attribute in limit is known at this point. It is scored at 4 for a parameter
that puts no quality attribute at risk, where the platform history bounds how often the
parameter leaves its range. Detection is scored on what the existing controls would
reveal. A failure that acts on viral clearance is scored at 10, since no in-process
control and no release test on the drug substance measures the log reduction a step
achieves. A failure that changes another quality attribute is scored at 8, since release
testing measures the attribute but monitoring during the unit operation does not. A
failure that puts no quality attribute at risk is scored at 6, since it shows up in the
performance of a downstream unit operation before the batch is released.

The initial risk priority number (RPN) is the product of severity, occurrence and de-
tection. Across the assessment it runs from 96 to 700 and takes 5 distinct values,
because occurrence and detection are assigned by class. Within a class of parameter
the RPN therefore differs only through severity. Priority is High where an attribute
of severity 8 or above is at risk, Medium where a quality attribute of lower severity is
at risk, and Low where only process performance is at risk. No parameter is assigned
a study type on its RPN alone.
The assessment uses 3 study types. A parameter carried into the designed experi-
ment of its unit operation is studied multivariately, which is the only way to estimate
an effect that depends on a second parameter. A parameter that could affect a quality
attribute but is not carried into the design is studied one factor at a time, and the rea-
son it was left out of the design is recorded with it. The A-Mab risk ranking and filter-
ing approach permits this substitution where the study can be justified (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009), and the assessment marks such a parameter “univariate (jus-
tified)”. A parameter that puts no quality attribute at risk is studied one factor at a
time unless it is carried into the design of its step (Section 5). The A-Mab approach
also allows a parameter to be given no new study where prior knowledge supports its
range. This assessment assigns no parameter to that outcome. Each characterization
plan states how the assigned study is executed.
Criticality is treated as a continuum and not as a binary split (International Council for
Harmonisation 2009; CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The ranking in Table 4.1
therefore orders parameters against each other. It does not divide them into a critical
and a non-critical group, and it is not a control strategy. Both follow from the studies
that this assessment scopes.

each attribute it gives the acceptance criterion, the criticality, the severity carried

Of the 11 attributes, 7 are formed in the production bioreactor. The register assigns
fragments to harvest, leached Protein A enters the process at the capture step, and
each viral clearance requirement is assigned to one of the steps that clear virus. Be-
cause most attributes are formed there, the bioreactor carries more parameters into
multivariate study than any other unit operation (Table 5.1).
The highest severity, 10, is carried by 3 attributes. High mannose has the highest Tool
#1 score in the register, 80. High mannose glycans carry no core fucose, so they are
expected to raise antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) in the same way as
afucosylation, and A-Mab acts on its target through ADCC. The impact of the attribute
is therefore scored on efficacy. Its uncertainty is scored above that of afucosylation,
because the link rests on published literature and not on assays run with A-Mab.
Both viral clearance attributes carry the highest severity for a different reason. Each
requirement is cumulative across the steps that clear the virus, which are low-pH




                                                       14
viral inactivation, anion exchange and virus filtration for XMuLV, and anion exchange
and virus filtration for MVM. The register assigns each requirement to one step, and
the claims of these steps add up to it (PCMP-001 §7.4). A shortfall at one of these
steps changes no property of the drug substance that a release test measures (In-
ternational Council for Harmonisation 2023a). Severity for these two attributes is
therefore assigned on the consequence of a shortfall and not on the probability of
detecting one.
Acidic charge variants sit at the other end of the register, with a Tool #1 score of 4
and a severity of 4. Their impact is scored low because deamidation of A-Mab occurs
at sites outside the binding region and outside the part of the Fc region that carries
effector function, so the deamidated forms keep their activity. The assessment still
scores 4 bioreactor parameters against acidic variants. The range of each parameter
over which acidic variants stay within their criterion is one of the things the bioreactor
study must establish. Residual DNA, with the lowest severity, 2, is treated the same
way. A low severity lowers the priority of a parameter and does not remove it from
study.
Fragments are new to the register at Version 2.0. The A-Mab case study lists frag-
mentation among the typical quality attributes of an antibody but gives it no limit,
and the sponsor set the criterion for fragments, ≤ 2 % (nrCE-SDS), when they joined
the register (PCMP-001 §3). Their criticality is low, with a Tool #1 score of 12. The
register assigns fragments to harvest, because proteases released from lysed cells
clip the antibody in culture and during the harvest hold. Fragments are measured
by nrCE-SDS under AMD-001, and PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.8 and Table 7.3 give the
method used in each study.
This assessment was made before fragments joined the register. No parameter is
scored against fragments and no severity is assigned to them. The study assignment
in Section 5 was made without them, and the harvest hold, during which fragments
form, is not among the parameters assessed. Whether a parameter could change
fragments is therefore outside this assessment.

4.1 Production Bioreactor

The production bioreactor forms the glycan, charge variant and aggregate attributes
of the drug substance. Of its 9 parameters, 5 are scored against a quality attribute,
all of them at a severity of 8 or above. Culture pH is the only parameter outside the

steps with a viral clearance claim that has a severity of 10, and it has the highest RPN
in the step, 560.
Culture pH and culture duration are each scored against 5 attributes, more than any
other parameter in the assessment. Culture pH takes a severity of 10 because high
mannose is one of the attributes it puts at risk. A culture pH above its range is ex-
pected to raise high mannose and to lower galactosylation. A higher pH in the medium
raises the pH in the Golgi, while the mannosidases and the galactosyltransferase work
fastest under more acidic conditions. Less mannose is then trimmed from the glycans
and less galactose is added to them. Aggregate in the harvest is expected to rise with
culture pH and afucosylation to fall. Acidic variants are expected to rise with culture
pH, because the deamidation of asparagine residues is catalysed by base.
Culture duration reaches as many attributes by a different route. A longer culture, or
one harvested after viability has fallen, is expected to raise host cell protein and DNA
in the harvest, because lysing cells release their protein and DNA into the medium.
Aggregate is expected to rise with duration too, since antibody secreted early in the
run is held at culture temperature until harvest. Galactosylation is expected to fall
late in the culture as the supply of UDP-galactose to the galactosyltransferase runs
down. Afucosylation is expected to fall with duration as well, and the study has to
bound that fall against the lower limit of the afucosylation criterion.
Culture temperature, pCO2 and osmolality are each scored against galactosylation
and acidic variants, and temperature is also scored against afucosylation. Galacto-
sylation is expected to rise with culture temperature, which raises the activity of
the galactosyltransferase, and to fall as pCO2 and osmolality rise. Temperature is
expected to act together with pH on the glycan attributes, because both act on the
activity of the same glycosyltransferases. An effect of that kind is estimated only
by moving the two parameters together. The 5 parameters scored against a glycan,
charge variant or aggregate attribute are therefore carried into one designed exper-
iment.

antibody each cell makes, and none of them is scored against a quality attribute. Ini-

with it. Dissolved oxygen raises titre through the productivity of each cell. Basal

volume is treated in the same way, since platform experience shows only a slight
effect on galactosylation. Table 4.2 gives the failure modes of the step.

4.3 Protein A Chromatography

Capture binds the antibody from the clarified harvest and removes most of the host
cell protein and DNA. Protein load and elution buffer pH are the 2 parameters scored
against a quality attribute, both against host cell protein at a severity of 6. A protein
load above its range is expected to raise host cell protein per milligram of antibody in
the pool, because the wash removes a smaller share of the host cell protein retained
on a more heavily loaded column. A lower elution pH is expected to raise it as well,
since a more acidic elution buffer releases host cell protein that the wash left bound
to the resin. The two parameters are expected to act together, and both are carried
into the capture design.
The capture eluate is the load of the low-pH hold. Elution buffer pH therefore sets
the pH of the pool before titration and the amount of acid needed to bring it to the
hold set-point.
Load flow rate and end of pool collect are scored against step yield and against no
quality attribute. Yield is expected to fall when a high load is run at a high flow rate,
because the antibody has less time to diffuse into the resin pores and part of it passes
through the column unbound. Yield is expected to rise as collection is carried further



                                            20
down the tail of the elution peak, up to the point where the pool is diluted without
gaining product. Both parameters are carried into the capture design, so that the
same design bounds step yield. Operating temperature and bed height are studied
one factor at a time.

attribute enters the process here. Leached Protein A comes from the affinity ligand

a parameter in Table 5.2. The polishing steps clear what reaches them. The capture
study measures leached Protein A in the pool across the designed experiment, and

4.4 Low-pH Viral Inactivation

Holding the pool at low pH inactivates enveloped virus by disrupting the lipid enve-
lope that XMuLV needs to enter a cell. The register assigns the cumulative XMuLV
clearance requirement to this step. Of its 4 parameters, 3 reach 700, the highest RPN
in the assessment.
Inactivation of enveloped virus at low pH is close to first order over most of the hold,
and it often tails as the titre approaches the limit of detection. Inactivation pH and
temperature set the rate constant, and the hold time sets how long that rate acts.
Where the inactivation is first order, the log reduction is proportional to the rate
constant integrated over the hold. A pH above its range lowers the rate constant and



                                             21
leaves less virus inactivated in the same hold. A hold shorter than its range leaves
less virus inactivated at the same pH. A temperature below its range lowers the rate
constant too, and this matters most at the short end of the hold and the high end
of the pH range, where the log reduction reached is smallest. The 3 parameters are
studied in one designed experiment, because the log reduction depends on all of them
together and none can be bounded with the others held at one setting.
Inactivation pH has a second failure mode in the opposite direction. A pH below its
range exposes the antibody to conditions under which aggregation and precipitation
have been seen on the platform. A hold longer than its range raises aggregate at
any pH in the range. Inactivation pH and hold time are therefore scored against
aggregate as well.
Detection scores 10 for each of these parameters. Neither an in-process control nor a
release test of the drug substance measures the log reduction that the hold achieves.
The viral clearance claim of the step is made instead from a spiking study in a qual-
ified small-scale model (International Council for Harmonisation 2023a). PCMP-001
Version 2.0 §5 states that the spiking model of each step follows the scaling principles
of its characterization model.
A-Mab concentration is scored against process performance. Prior product experi-
ence showed no effect of concentration on the rate of inactivation, on aggregation or
on charge variants over the range the pool reaches. The parameter is therefore stud-
ied one factor at a time. No parameter of this step is scored against MVM, because
a parvovirus has no lipid envelope for low pH to disrupt (PCMP-001 §7.4). Table 4.5

4.5 Cation Exchange Chromatography

The cation exchange step is the principal aggregate reduction step of the process. It
also clears host cell protein, DNA and leached Protein A, and it forms no attribute of
its own. Of its 5 parameters, 4 are scored against a quality attribute. Protein load,
elution buffer pH and elution stop collect are scored against aggregate at a severity
of 8, and protein load is scored against host cell protein as well.
Aggregate is the attribute that matters most here. No step after cation exchange
is credited with aggregate reduction, so aggregate that leaves this step in the pool
reaches the drug substance. Pool aggregate is expected to rise with protein load,
elution buffer pH and elution stop collect. Aggregate elutes after the monomer, so
collecting further down the tail of the elution peak takes more of it into the pool. A
higher load leaves less resin per gram of antibody to separate the two. Load and
elution pH are expected to act together, since the resolution between monomer and
aggregate depends on both. The conductivity of the load and wash is scored against
host cell protein. A conductivity below its range weakens the wash and leaves more
host cell protein bound, to be eluted with the product.
Elution flow rate is scored against step yield. A higher elution flow rate broadens the
elution peak, because the antibody has less time to diffuse out of the resin pores, and
a broader peak dilutes the pool. Prior knowledge places no effect of flow rate on pool
aggregate large enough to score, so the parameter is studied one factor at a time.

4.6 Anion Exchange Chromatography

The anion exchange step is run in flow-through mode. At the load pH, DNA, virus and
most host cell proteins carry a net negative charge and bind to the resin. The load
pH lies below the isoelectric point of A-Mab, which therefore carries a net positive
charge and passes through, together with the basic host cell proteins. The register
assigns the cumulative MVM clearance requirement to this step (Section 3). All 5 of
its parameters are scored against a quality attribute.
Load pH is scored against 3 attributes and reaches 700. It sets the charge on the
impurities and on the virus, so a load pH below its range weakens the binding of
host cell protein and of both model viruses at once. Clearance is also expected to fall
as load conductivity rises, because the counter-ions in the load compete with virus
and impurity for the charged groups of the resin. Load pH and load conductivity are
expected to act together, since one sets the charge on each species and the other
sets how strongly that charge is screened. The conductivity of the equilibration and
wash-1 buffers and the protein load are scored against host cell protein. A higher
wash conductivity weakens the binding of host cell protein in the same way. A higher
protein load presents more impurity to a fixed volume of resin, so its binding capacity
can be exceeded.
Operating flow rate is the one parameter in the assessment with a quality attribute
at risk that is not carried into a designed experiment. A higher flow rate shortens
the residence time of the load in the bed, which gives a virus particle less time to
reach and bind a charged site on the resin. The parameter acts on the step through
residence time alone, and prior knowledge places no interaction between residence
time and the load chemistry. It is therefore bounded by moving it one factor at a time
and is marked “univariate (justified)”. The anion exchange plan PCP-008 records the
justification with the design. Table 4.7 gives the failure modes.

4.7 Small-Virus Retentive Filtration

The virus filter removes small non-enveloped virus by size and is the principal MVM
clearance step. Both of its parameters are scored against MVM clearance, and both
reach 700. The step has no parameter that affects process performance alone.
Volumetric load is the parameter on which the step turns. The log reduction is ex-
pected to fall as the volume filtered per unit area rises, because the retentive layer
accumulates material and the chance of a parvovirus passing rises with it. Filtration
pressure is scored against both model viruses, because it sets the driving force across
that layer. Prior knowledge gives neither the size nor the direction of a pressure ef-
fect on the log reduction. The exposure is largest at high volumetric load, where the
layer is thickest. Both parameters are therefore carried into the designed experiment.

4.8 Ultrafiltration / Diafiltration

The formulation step concentrates the antibody and exchanges it into the drug sub-
stance buffer. It forms and clears no quality attribute. Its 3 parameters are scored
against process performance alone and sit at the floor of the ranking at 96. The A-
Mab case study gives no characterization of this step (CMC Biotech Working Group
2009), so the assessment rests on platform experience alone.
A number of diavolumes below its range leaves the buffer exchange incomplete. Each
diavolume removes a fixed fraction of the buffer species left in the retentate, so the
residual falls with the number of diavolumes and not with the time spent filtering. Flux

gion in which flux no longer depends on pressure. Beyond that point the polarized
layer at the membrane surface thickens and flux stops rising, so a transmembrane
pressure outside its range is expected to change the processing time. The final con-
centration is the drug substance concentration itself, which is measured and released
against a specification. A failure of this parameter is therefore seen before the batch

6 Assumptions and limitations

The assessment assumes that platform behaviour transfers to A-Mab. The mecha-
nisms it relies on were established on the earlier humanized IgG1 antibodies X-Mab,
Y-Mab and Z-Mab and on A-Mab clinical and engineering experience (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009). The assumption is that A-Mab responds to the same param-
eters in the same directions. Where it does not, the characterization study finds it,
which is one reason the ranges studied are wider than the NORs.
The parameter list is assumed complete. It is the list in the process description trans-
ferred under PTP-001, and a parameter that is not in that description is not assessed.
An equipment or facility difference at the receiving site that introduces a new param-
eter is handled under the transfer plan and requires this assessment to be revised.
Severity is taken from the quality attribute assessment and is not re-derived. That
assessment scores impact and uncertainty and deliberately excludes manufacturing
capability and detectability (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). A severity in Ta-
ble 3.1 therefore combines the impact of a failure on the patient with the uncertainty
of that impact. It says nothing about how likely the process is to produce a failure.
Occurrence and detection are assigned by class, so the initial RPN takes only 5 values
across 37 parameters. Within a class the RPN differs only through severity. Ranking
within a unit operation should therefore be read from severity, from the class of the
attribute at risk and from the reasoning in Section 4.
The judgement on which parameters act together is the weakest part of the assess-
ment. A designed experiment estimates interactions only among the parameters it
carries. An interaction between a parameter in the design and one left out of it can-
not be found by that design. The clearest exposure is the operating flow rate of the
anion exchange step, which is scored against both viral clearance attributes and stud-
ied one factor at a time. If the anion exchange study finds that residence time acts
together with the load chemistry, the univariate result for flow rate would not bound
the step, and the design would have to be augmented.
Interactions between unit operations are not scored. Each parameter is assessed
against the attributes it can affect at its own step. Material does carry properties
from one step to the next. The clearest case is the elution pH of the capture step,
which sets the starting pH of the low-pH hold. That link is handled in the receiving
step, where the pool is titrated to the set-point of inactivation pH, and not by a score
in Table 4.1. An attribute carried from one step to the next is judged against the
intermediate acceptance criteria of PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.3, which carry each
drug substance criterion back through the steps downstream.
Fragments are outside this assessment, for the reasons given in Section 3.

7 Outputs and downstream use

This assessment produces three outputs. The ranking in Table 4.1 is the pre-
characterization risk basis. The study types in Table 4.1 and the split in Table 5.1
are the scope. The parameter set in Table 5.2 is what each plan takes up.
Per unit operation, the consumer is the characterization plan named in Table 5.1.
Each plan cites this assessment as its risk basis and takes the study type and the
range to be studied. It adds the scale-down model, the analytical methods, the sam-
pling plan and the acceptance criteria. The step plans were amended under INV-001
corrective action CA2 with the criteria and the fragment method of PCMP-001 Version
2.0. Under those rules a scale-down model is qualified by its offset from manufactur-
ing scale (PCMP-001 §7.1). Each step output on which a drug substance criterion de-
pends is judged against an intermediate acceptance criterion (PCMP-001 §7.3). The
report of the unit operation measures the effects, defines the operating region and
classifies each parameter under SOP-4001.
PCMP-001 consumes the assessment at campaign level. It states once what the plans
would otherwise each restate, including the number of parameters assigned to mul-
tivariate and to univariate study across the train (PCMP-001 Table 4.1). PCMR-001
consolidates the reports and is where the risk that remains after characterization is
addressed.
This assessment is changed only by revision. A change to the parameter list, to the
severity of an attribute or to an assigned study type is made by revising this document,
and the affected plans are revised with it. Version 2.0 changes no score and no study
type (Section 1.1).
