1 Purpose and scope

This document records the risk assessment made on the A-Mab drug substance pro-
cess before any characterization study began. Its output is the scope of the charac-
terization campaign. For each process parameter it names the quality attributes the
parameter could affect, the potential failure mode and its effect, an initial risk score
and the type of study assigned to the parameter. The assessment is prospective and
reports no study result.
The assessment does not classify parameters. A parameter is classified as a critical
process parameter (CPP), a well-controlled critical process parameter (WC-CPP), a
key process parameter (KPP) or a general process parameter (GPP) from the effects
that the characterization studies measure. That classification follows the parameter
classification procedure SOP-4001. It is reported in the characterization report of
each unit operation and consolidated in the master report PCMR-001. The scores in
this document are initial scores in the same sense. The risk that remains once the
studies have reported is assessed against their data in PCMR-001, and nothing here
anticipates it.
The scope is the drug substance process from the production bioreactor to formula-
tion. Table 1.1 lists the 8 unit operations and the principal role of each, and the as-
sessment covers their 37 process parameters. The seed expansion train, drug product
manufacture and the analytical methods themselves are outside its scope.

In this framework risk assessment is a lifecycle activity, not a single exercise (Inter-
national Council for Harmonisation 2023b; CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). This
assessment is the first in that lifecycle. It draws on prior knowledge alone, because no
A-Mab characterization data existed when it was made. It is revisited when the char-
acterization reports are available, and again once commercial experience has been
gained. The approach follows the enhanced development approach of ICH Q8 and
ICH Q11 (International Council for Harmonisation 2009, 2012) and supports Stage 1
of the process validation lifecycle (U.S. Food and Drug Administration 2011).

2 Risk assessment methodology

The method is risk ranking and filtering, applied to process parameters as in the
A-Mab case study (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009) and within the quality risk
management principles of ICH Q9 (International Council for Harmonisation 2023b).
Each parameter is put to two questions. The first asks what the parameter could do
to a quality attribute, or to process performance, if it moved outside the range over
which the process is currently understood. The second asks whether the effect of the
parameter could depend on the setting of another parameter of the same unit oper-
ation. The first question sets the score. The second sets the study type, because an
effect that depends on a second parameter cannot be found by moving one parameter
at a time.
Severity is taken from the attribute at risk and not from the parameter. It is the
severity that Table 3.1 carries for the attribute, on a scale from 2 to 10. Where a
parameter could affect more than one attribute, the severity of the most severe of
them is used. A parameter that puts no quality attribute at risk takes a severity of 4,
the value given to a process attribute such as step yield or titre.
Occurrence and detection are scored before any effect has been measured, and they
are scored by class of parameter. Table 2.1 gives each score with its basis. Occur-
rence is 7 for a parameter that could affect a quality attribute, because neither the
size of its effect nor the width of the range that keeps the attribute within its criterion
is known yet. It is 4 for a parameter that affects process performance alone, because
the platform history bounds how often such a parameter leaves its range. Detection
is scored on what the existing controls would reveal. A failure that acts on viral clear-
ance scores 10, since no in-process control and no release test of the drug substance
measures the log reduction that a step achieves. A failure that changes another qual-
ity attribute scores 8. Release testing measures that attribute, but monitoring during
the unit operation does not. A failure that affects process performance alone scores
6, since it shows in the performance of the next unit operation before the batch is
released.

The initial risk priority number (RPN) is the product of severity, occurrence and de-
tection. Across the register it runs from 96 to 700 and takes 5 distinct values, because
occurrence and detection are scored by class. Severity is therefore what separates
one parameter from another within a unit operation. Priority is High where an at-
tribute of severity 8 or above is at risk, Medium where a quality attribute of lower
severity is at risk, and Low where only process performance is at risk. No parameter
is assigned a study type on its RPN alone.
The assessment uses 3 study types. A parameter carried into the designed experiment
of its unit operation is studied multivariately, which is the only way to estimate an
effect that depends on a second parameter. A parameter that could affect a quality
attribute but is not carried into the design is studied one factor at a time, and the
reason it was left out of the design is recorded with it. The A-Mab risk ranking and
filtering approach permits this substitution where the study can be justified (CMC
Biotech Working Group 2009), and the register marks such a parameter “univariate
(justified)”. A parameter that affects process performance alone is studied one factor
at a time. The A-Mab approach also allows a parameter to be given no new study
where prior knowledge supports its range. This assessment assigns no parameter to
that outcome (Section 5). Each characterization plan states how the assigned study
is executed.
Criticality is treated as a continuum and not as a binary split (International Council for
Harmonisation 2009; CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). The ranking in Table 4.1
therefore orders parameters against each other. It does not divide them into a critical
and a non-critical group, and it is not a control strategy. Both follow from the studies
that this assessment scopes.

Of the 11 attributes, 7 are formed in the production bioreactor. The register assigns
fragments to harvest, leached Protein A enters the process at the capture step, and
each viral clearance requirement is assigned to one of the steps that clear virus. The
bioreactor therefore carries more parameters into multivariate study than any other
unit operation (Table 5.1).
The highest severity, 10, is carried by 3 attributes. High mannose has the highest Tool
#1 score in the register, 80. High mannose glycans carry no core fucose, so they are
expected to raise antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) in the same way as
afucosylation, and A-Mab acts on its target through ADCC. The impact of the attribute
is therefore scored on efficacy. Its uncertainty is scored above that of afucosylation,
because the link rests on published literature and not on assays run with A-Mab.
Both viral clearance attributes carry the highest severity for a different reason. Each
requirement is cumulative across the steps that clear the virus, which are low-pH viral
inactivation, anion exchange and virus filtration for XMuLV, and anion exchange and



                                                     12
virus filtration for MVM. A shortfall at one of these steps changes no property of the
drug substance that a release test measures (International Council for Harmonisation
2023a). Severity is therefore assigned on the consequence of a shortfall and not on
the chance of detecting one.
Acidic charge variants sit at the other end of the register, with a Tool #1 score of 4
and a severity of 4. Their impact is scored low because deamidation of A-Mab occurs
at sites outside the binding region and outside the part of the Fc region that carries
effector function, so the deamidated forms keep their activity. The assessment still
scores 4 bioreactor parameters against acidic variants. The range of each parameter
over which acidic variants stay within their criterion is one of the things the bioreactor
study must establish. Residual DNA, with the lowest severity in the register, 2, is
treated the same way. A low severity lowers the priority of a parameter. It does not
remove the parameter from study.
Fragments are new to the register at Version 2.0 (Section 1.1). The register gives
them a criterion of ≤ 2 % (nrCE-SDS), low criticality with a Tool #1 score of 12, and
assigns them to harvest. Proteases released from lysed cells clip the antibody in
culture and during the harvest hold, and the register places the attribute at harvest
for that reason (PCMP-001 §3). From the date of AMD-001, fragments are measured
by nrCE-SDS (AMV-3020, under SOP-3015) in every study at every step.
This assessment was approved before fragments were in the register. No parameter
in Section 4 is scored against fragments, and the study assignment in Section 5 was
made without them. The severity that Table 3.1 shows for fragments therefore enters
no score.
PCMP-001 Version 2.0 states which studies measure fragments. They are a response
of the response-surface designs at Protein A, low-pH viral inactivation, cation ex-

4.1 Production Bioreactor

The production bioreactor forms the glycan, charge variant and aggregate attributes
of the drug substance, so it carries the highest severities of the train. Of its 9 pa-
rameters, 5 are scored against a quality attribute. The highest RPN in the step, 560,
belongs to culture pH.



                                                       16
Culture pH and culture duration are each scored against 5 attributes, more than any
other parameter in the register. Culture pH takes a severity of 10 because high man-
nose is one of the attributes it puts at risk. A culture pH above its range is expected to
raise high mannose and to lower galactosylation. A higher pH in the medium raises
the pH in the Golgi and moves the mannosidases and the galactosyltransferase away
from the acidic conditions in which they work fastest. Aggregate in the harvest is
expected to rise with culture pH, and afucosylation and acidic variants are expected
to fall.
Culture duration reaches as many attributes by a different route. A longer culture, or
one harvested after viability has fallen, is expected to raise host cell protein and DNA
in the harvest, because lysing cells release their protein and DNA into the medium.
Aggregate is expected to rise with duration too, since antibody secreted early in the
run is held at culture temperature until harvest. Galactosylation is expected to fall
late in the culture as the supply of UDP-galactose to the galactosyltransferase runs
down. Afucosylation is expected to fall with duration as well, and the study has to
bound that fall against the lower limit of the afucosylation criterion.
Culture temperature, pCO2 and osmolality are each scored against galactosylation

Harvest separates the cells from the culture and clarifies the supernatant for capture.
Its 3 parameters are scored against process performance alone, and all of them sit

The register as it now stands assigns fragments to this step (Table 3.1), because the
antibody is clipped by proteases released from lysed cells during the harvest hold.
Fragments were not in the register when this assessment was made. None of the
harvest parameters was scored against them, and the duration of the harvest hold is
not among the parameters assessed.
The failure modes are a centrifugal force outside its range, a depth filter loaded be-

4.3 Protein A Chromatography

Capture binds the antibody from the clarified harvest and removes most of the host
cell protein and DNA. Protein load and elution buffer pH are the 2 parameters scored
against a quality attribute, both against host cell protein at a severity of 6. A protein
load above its range is expected to raise host cell protein per milligram of antibody in
the pool, because the wash removes a smaller share of the host cell protein retained
on a more heavily loaded column. A lower elution pH is expected to raise it as well,
since a more acidic elution buffer releases host cell protein that the wash left bound
to the resin. The two parameters are expected to act together, and both are carried
into the capture design.
The capture eluate is the load of the low-pH hold. Elution buffer pH therefore sets
the pH of the pool before titration and the amount of acid needed to bring it to the
hold set-point.

4.4 Low-pH Viral Inactivation

Holding the pool at low pH inactivates enveloped virus by disrupting the lipid enve-
lope that XMuLV needs to enter a cell. The register assigns the cumulative XMuLV
clearance requirement to this step. Of its 4 parameters, 3 reach 700, the highest RPN
in the assessment.
Inactivation of enveloped virus at low pH follows first order kinetics. Inactivation
pH and temperature set the rate constant, and the hold time sets how long that rate
acts. The log reduction of the step is therefore proportional to the rate constant



                                             20
integrated over the hold. A pH above its range lowers the rate constant and leaves
less virus inactivated in the same hold. A hold shorter than its range leaves less virus
inactivated at the same pH. A temperature below its range lowers the rate constant
too, and this matters most at the short end of the hold and the high end of the pH
range, where the log reduction reached is smallest. The 3 parameters are studied in
one designed experiment, because the log reduction depends on all of them together
and none can be bounded with the others held at one setting.
Inactivation pH has a second failure mode in the opposite direction. A pH below its
range exposes the antibody to conditions under which aggregation and precipitation
have been seen on the platform. A hold longer than its range raises aggregate at
any pH in the range. Inactivation pH and hold time are therefore scored against
aggregate as well.
Detection scores 10 for each of these parameters. Neither an in-process control nor a
release test of the drug substance measures the log reduction that the hold achieves.
The claim is made instead from a spiking study in a qualified small-scale model (In-
ternational Council for Harmonisation 2023a). PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §5 sets out the
spiking models, which follow the scaling principles of the characterization model of
each step.
A-Mab concentration is scored against process performance. Prior product experi-
ence showed no effect of concentration on the rate of inactivation, on aggregation or
on charge variants over the range the pool reaches. The parameter is therefore stud-
ied one factor at a time. No parameter of this step is scored against MVM, because
a parvovirus has no lipid envelope for low pH to disrupt (PCMP-001 §7.4). Table 4.5

The cation exchange step is the principal aggregate reduction step of the process. It
also clears host cell protein, DNA and leached Protein A, and it forms no attribute of

elution buffer pH and elution stop collect are scored against aggregate at a severity

Aggregate is the attribute that matters most here. No step after cation exchange
is credited with aggregate reduction, so aggregate that leaves this step in the pool
reaches the drug substance. Pool aggregate is expected to rise with protein load,
elution buffer pH and elution stop collect. Aggregate elutes after the monomer, so
collecting further down the tail of the elution peak takes more of it into the pool. A
higher load leaves less resin per gram of antibody to separate the two. Load and
elution pH are expected to act together, since the resolution between monomer and
aggregate depends on both. The conductivity of the load and wash is scored against
host cell protein. A conductivity below its range weakens the wash and leaves more
host cell protein bound, to be eluted with the product.
Elution flow rate is scored against step yield. A higher elution flow rate broadens the
elution peak, because the antibody has less time to diffuse out of the resin pores, and
a broader peak dilutes the pool. Prior knowledge places no effect of flow rate on pool
aggregate large enough to score, so the parameter is studied one factor at a time.

4.6 Anion Exchange Chromatography

The anion exchange step is run in flow-through mode. At the load pH, host cell protein,
DNA and virus carry a net negative charge and bind to the resin. The load pH lies




                                             22
below the isoelectric point of A-Mab, which therefore carries a net positive charge and
passes through. The register assigns the cumulative MVM clearance requirement to
this step. All 5 of its parameters are scored against a quality attribute.
Load pH is scored against 3 attributes and reaches 700. It sets the charge on the
impurities and on the virus, so a load pH below its range weakens the binding of
host cell protein and of both model viruses at once. Clearance is also expected to fall
as load conductivity rises, because the counter-ions in the load compete with virus
and impurity for the charged groups of the resin. Load pH and load conductivity are
expected to act together, since one sets the charge on each species and the other
sets how strongly that charge is screened. The conductivity of the equilibration and
wash-1 buffers and the protein load are scored against host cell protein. A higher
wash conductivity weakens the binding of host cell protein in the same way. A higher
protein load presents more impurity to a fixed volume of resin, so its binding capacity
can be exceeded.
Operating flow rate is the one parameter of the register with a quality attribute at
risk that is not carried into a designed experiment. A higher flow rate shortens the
residence time of the load in the bed, which gives a virus particle less time to reach
and bind a charged site on the resin. The parameter acts on the step through resi-
dence time alone, and prior knowledge places no interaction between residence time
and the load chemistry. It is therefore bounded by moving it one factor at a time and
is marked “univariate (justified)”. PCP-008 records the justification with the design.

4.8 Ultrafiltration / Diafiltration

against process performance alone and sit at the floor of the register at 96. The A-

6 Assumptions and limitations

The assessment assumes that platform behaviour transfers to A-Mab. The mecha-
nisms it relies on were established on the earlier humanized IgG1 antibodies X-Mab,
Y-Mab and Z-Mab and on A-Mab clinical and engineering experience (CMC Biotech
Working Group 2009). The assumption is that A-Mab responds to the same param-
eters in the same directions. Where it does not, the characterization study finds it,
which is one reason the ranges studied are wider than the NORs.
The parameter list is assumed complete. It is the list in the process description trans-
ferred under PTP-001, and a parameter that is not in that description is not assessed.
An equipment or facility difference at the receiving site that introduces a new param-
eter is handled under the transfer plan and requires this assessment to be revised.
Severity is taken from the attribute assessment and is not derived again here. Tool
#1 scores impact and uncertainty and leaves out manufacturing capability and de-
tectability (CMC Biotech Working Group 2009). A severity in Table 3.1 therefore says
what a failure would mean for the patient. It says nothing about how likely the process
is to produce one.
Occurrence and detection are scored by class, so the initial RPN takes only 5 values
across 37 parameters. The number separates classes of parameter. It does not sepa-
rate parameters within a class, and the ranking within a unit operation is read from
severity and from the reasoning in Section 4.
The interaction judgement is the weakest part of the assessment. A designed exper-
iment estimates interactions only among the parameters it carries. An interaction
between a parameter in the design and one left out of it cannot be found by that de-
sign. The clearest exposure is the operating flow rate of the anion exchange step,
which is scored against both viral clearance attributes and studied one factor at a
time. If the anion exchange study finds that residence time acts together with the
load chemistry, the univariate result for flow rate would not bound the step, and the
design would have to be augmented.
Interactions between unit operations are not scored. Each parameter is assessed
against the attributes of its own step. Material does carry properties from one step
to the next. The clearest case is the elution pH of the capture step, which sets the
starting pH of the low-pH hold before the pool is titrated to the hold set-point. An
attribute carried from one step to the next is judged against the intermediate ac-
ceptance criteria of PCMP-001 Version 2.0 §7.3, which carry each drug substance
criterion back through the steps downstream. Such links are handled there and not
by a score in Table 4.1.
Fragments were added to the register after this assessment was approved. Whether
a parameter could change fragments is therefore not assessed here, and no study

type was assigned on that ground. The harvest hold, during which fragments form,
is not a parameter in this assessment.
Finally, no number in this document is a measurement of A-Mab process behaviour.
The scores are judgements, the studies had not been run when the assessment was
made, and the ranges in Table 5.2 are ranges to be studied and not ranges shown to
be acceptable.

7 Outputs and downstream use

This assessment produces three outputs. The ranking in Table 4.1 is the pre-
characterization risk basis. The study types in Table 4.1 and the split in Table 5.1
are the scope. The parameter set in Table 5.2 is what each plan takes up.
Per unit operation, the consumer is the characterization plan named in Table 5.1.
Each plan cites this assessment as its risk basis and takes the study type and the range
to be studied. It adds the scale-down model, the analytical methods, the sampling plan
and the acceptance criteria. Under INV-001 corrective action CA2 the plans were
amended with the criteria and the fragment method of PCMP-001 Version 2.0. Under
those rules a scale-down model is qualified by its offset from manufacturing scale
(PCMP-001 §7.1), and each step output is judged against an intermediate acceptance
criterion (PCMP-001 §7.3). The report of the unit operation measures the effects,
defines the operating region and classifies each parameter under SOP-4001.
PCMP-001 consumes the assessment at campaign level. It states once what the plans
would otherwise each restate, including the number of parameters assigned to mul-
tivariate and to univariate study across the train (PCMP-001 Table 4.1). PCMR-001
consolidates the reports, and it is where the risk that remains after characterization
is addressed.
This assessment is changed only by revision. A change to the parameter list, to the
severity of an attribute or to an assigned study type is made by revising this document,
and the affected plans are revised with it. Version 2.0 changes no score and no study
type (Section 1.1).
