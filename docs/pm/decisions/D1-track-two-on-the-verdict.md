# D1 — if the stopping rule holds, does Track 2 start without another decision?

**What is being asked.** TASK-008 applies a stopping rule fixed in advance: mid-sentence `, so `
at or under 1.0 % of sentences and sentence-initial connectives at or above 3.0 % in both
`PCP-003` and `PCR-003`, with topic chaining and copula not more than 2.0 points worse than round
one. If it holds, the proposal says "go to Track 2" — the remaining eighteen documents, at roughly
40 re-anchored annex spans and two explicit renders each, so about 720 spans and 36 renders in
all. That is nine times the pilot's cost, and the pilot's own lesson was that nobody knew what an
amendment did until a document was written and measured. The question is whether the round-two
numbers alone are enough to commit that, or whether the owner reads the pair first and decides.

**Option A — the numbers decide.** If the rule holds, `/ship` rewrites the proposal to Track 2 and
the next `/explore` opens it. Fast, and the rule was fixed before the round for exactly this
purpose. Risk: the pair clears every number and the owner still reads it as machine-written, which
is what happened to the possessive result — a measure that moved and a reader who did not notice.

**Option B — the numbers and the reading decide.** The rule must hold *and* the owner's reading
(TASK-008 records it) must say the pair is no longer *immediately* recognisable. If the numbers
clear and the reading does not, the owner's quoted sentences become the next target and Track 2
stays blocked. Slower by one reading; it is the reading the owner has already said is the real
test.

**What the plan assumes meanwhile.** Option B. TASK-008 records both the verdict and the reading;
TASK-009 prepares both shapes of the proposal (Track 2, or the new target) so `/ship` does not
improvise. Nothing in this epic is blocked by the answer.
