# D2 — does round three still read as machine-written, and which sentences give it away?

**What is being asked.** The numbers half of round three is complete and every one of the eight
lines of the stopping rule holds, including the one that decided the round: the passive moved from
35.4 % to 57.4 % of sentences with a root and a subject, from twenty points below every human
source to inside their range. The balanced `, and ` second clause went 22.6 % → 0.5 % and the
`, not ` tail 4.3 % → 0.0 %. The full page is
[`docs/results/2026-08-18-register-round-three.md`](../../results/2026-08-18-register-round-three.md).

By standing owner decision (`state.json` → `decisions.human_check`) the numbers are only half the
verdict. The other half is a person reading `pc_package/PCR-003_bioreactor.pdf` — 56 pages,
rendered 2026-08-18 — and saying whether it is still *immediately* recognisable as machine-written,
and which sentences give it away. Rounds two and three both got their targets that way: a reader
finds it, the count confirms it, in that order. Nothing else in this project has produced a target.

The reading is **not blind**. This is the fourth read of the same document by the same reader. That
was accepted when the check was set up, because no blind reader is available and one consistent
reader across four points is worth more to the series than a fresh reader at one point. The page
says so in its own words.

**Option A — the reading names specific sentences.** Whatever it quotes is counted afterwards
against the four human sources, and becomes round four's measure. The count either confirms the
reading, as it did for all three faults named after round two, or it does not, which is itself the
result. This is the path the last two rounds took.

**Option B — the reading says the document no longer gives itself away.** Then the register
campaign has reached its stopping point on this genre, and round four is the both-genres check that
this round gave up: does the same instruction move `PCP-003`, which was held at round two as the
control and is untouched?

**What the plan assumes meanwhile.** Two candidates are already on the page, found by measuring
what paid for the round's own wins rather than by reading, and either would survive a reading that
names nothing:

- `, which` rose 9.50 % → **15.33 %** of sentences, against 0.60–2.35 % in the four sources. The
  coordination the author was told to stop writing came back as subordination.
- `its` rose 0.51 → **1.66** per 1000 words, four times the highest source, having been driven to
  near zero in round one when it was the measure being watched.

Both are unprinted measures, which is what the three-round pattern predicts will drift.

**Settled 2026-08-18 by the project owner**, verbatim:

> The document reads better. Not perfect but ok to me.

This is **option B**, and it is the first reading in the series that names no sentence. The
consequence for the campaign is that the reading has stopped producing targets on this genre, so
the rule the series ran on — a reader finds it, the count confirms it — has no input this round.

The owner then asked where the numbers say it got worse. That question was answered the other way
round, from the measures, and the answer is on the page under *Where it got worse, count-led*: six
measures moved away from the four human sources in the same re-author that moved the three targeted
ones toward them, and two crossed from inside the source range to outside it. The strongest are a
new staccato — zero runs of three or more consecutive short sentences at rounds one and two, eight
at round three against 1 to 10 in the sources — and `, which` at 15.33 % of sentences against
0.60–2.35 %.

Those are **count-led candidates and the page says so**. Whether round four targets them, or spends
itself on the both-genres check this round gave up, is the scope question for `/ship` (TASK-007) and
is not settled here.
